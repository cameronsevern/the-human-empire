Title: THE SCROLL. Date line: about 5,000 years ago. An ancient Near Eastern scroll lying partly unrolled on a plain surface, seen from above and slightly to the front, large, in the centre. Both ends are rolled up into plain cylinders of the same pale skin, with no wooden rods or handles, and a stretch about four columns wide lies open between them. The material is thin, pale, cream-brown animal skin. The open stretch carries four narrow columns of writing in black ink, side by side: show the writing only as rows of small generic ink strokes that suggest letters, not real letters of any script and no readable words. Between the second and third columns a vertical seam runs from top to bottom where two sheets are sewn together with a line of small stitches. The stitched seam is the terracotta-accented part. Below the scroll lie a cut reed pen with a dark tip and a small round clay pot of black ink. No people, no hands, no ornaments, no religious symbols; a plain, respectful study of the object. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps the scroll, the pen, the pot or another label. Callouts: 1 'Sheets of skin or papyrus'; 2 'The sheets are joined edge to edge: skin is sewn, papyrus is glued' (pointing to the seam); 3 'Narrow columns of writing, side by side'; 4 'A reed pen, and ink of soot, gum and water'; 5 'The reader unrolls with one hand and rolls up with the other' (pointing to the rolled-up left end).
