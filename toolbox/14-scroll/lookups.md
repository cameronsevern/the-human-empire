# Lookups: the scroll

1. "First made about 5,000 years ago, in Egypt."
   - Verdict: correct. The oldest known roll of papyrus, blank, comes from the tomb of Hemaka at Saqqara, First Dynasty, about 2900 BCE; the oldest written rolls are of the Fourth and Fifth Dynasties.
   - Sources: https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?entryid=2357 ; https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/papyrus-in-ancient-egypt
2. Papyrus "a kind of coarse paper". Codex: papyrus is laid strips, not paper. Reworded as a comparison, as in the chapter. https://exhibits.library.cornell.edu/Paper-exhibit/feature/funerary-text-on-papyrus
3. Papyrus sheets "glued": they were glued with overlapping edges of 1 to 2 cm. Added. https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/papyrus-making-in-egypt
4. "The story of Gilgamesh takes twelve" tablets.
   - Verdict: correct for the standard Babylonian version. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_of_Gilgamesh
5. "A single roll that weighed less than a loaf of bread": not verified. Replaced with the chapter's "carried in one hand".
6. The book with pages "about 2,000 years ago"; overtook the scroll.
   - Verdict: correct. The codex appears in the first century CE and reaches parity with the roll about 300 CE. First draft's "within four centuries" replaced by "by about 1,600 years ago". https://www.litteraturogmedieleksikon.no/gallery/papyrus.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex
7. "The word outlived the object for everybody else." Codex: wrong as an absolute (medieval rolls, Chinese handscrolls). Softened to "most other readers". https://medievalscrolls.fas.harvard.edu/intro/
8. Torah scrolls still written by hand on skin and read weekly: correct, as in the chapter's lookups. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefer_Torah
9. Reed pen with a 5,000-year date line: the earliest Egyptian scribes wrote with a rush chewed into a brush, and the cut reed pen came later. The page keeps the chapter's "pen cut from a reed", which is right for the scrolls of Judah. Weak point: the picture's date line is the date of the first rolls, while the pen and sewn skin belong to the later scroll.
10. Photo: Temple Scroll, late 1st century BCE to early 1st century CE, on very thin parchment. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Scroll
