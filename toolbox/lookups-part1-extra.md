# Fact check: Part I extra tool pages (01 to 07)

Verdicts: correct, wrong, imprecise, could not verify. "Replacement" gives the corrected text where one is needed.

## 01 The hearth

1. "Common by about 400,000 years ago" (First made). **Correct.** Habitual fire use in Europe is clear only after about 400,000 years ago; Qesem Cave (Israel, occupied 420,000 to 200,000 years ago) has a central hearth used again and again by about 300,000. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1018116108 (Roebroeks and Villa 2011); https://popular-archaeology.com/?p=17831 (Qesem).
2. "traces of campfires go back a million years and more" (First made). **Correct.** Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa: burnt bone and ash about 1 million years old (Berna 2012); a 2026 study reports fire traces from 1.07 to 1.79 million years. Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, Israel: burnt flint and wood about 790,000 to 800,000 years old (Goren-Inbar 2004). https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109 ; https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/333702
3. "Made of: Fire, a ring of stones, and dry wood". **Imprecise.** The early hearths at Wonderwerk, Gesher Benot Ya'aqov and Qesem are patches of ash and burnt material on the cave floor or lake shore; stone-lined or stone-ringed hearths belong mostly to much later periods. Replacement: "Fire and dry wood, on a patch of bare ground, later ringed with stones." https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1018116108
4. "A fire keeps the cold off, and that let a tropical ape live in places with winters." **Imprecise.** People were living in cold climates before there is any trace of fire (Dmanisi, Georgia, about 1.8 million years ago; Happisburgh, England, about 800,000 years ago), so fire did not come first. Replacement: "A fire keeps the cold off, and that made life in places with winters far easier for a tropical ape." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans
5. "It keeps lions and hyenas back at night." **Could not verify** as a measured fact for early humans. It is the usual inference, and ethnographic accounts of hunter-gatherers support it. No page found that states it for Palaeolithic sites. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans
6. "Fire was also the first fuel that any animal put to work." **Correct.** No other species is known to use fire; it is the first fuel in human use. (General statement; no dispute found.)

## 02 The voyaging canoe

7. "First made: More than 1,000 years ago, in the islands of the western Pacific." **Imprecise.** Linguistic evidence puts double canoes after the Lapita settlement of West Polynesia (Tonga, Samoa, about 2,800 years ago); the great voyages to East Polynesia, Hawaii and New Zealand were made in them between about AD 1000 and 1300. Replacement: "More than 1,000 years ago, in the islands of Polynesia." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_navigation ; https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/high-precision-radiocarbon-dating-shows-recent-and-rapid-initial-/
8. "Made of: Wood, cord and woven matting." **Correct.** Planks and hulls lashed with coconut-fibre sennit; sails of pandanus matting (flax in New Zealand). https://archive.hokulea.com/hawaiiloa.html ; https://www.pilotguides.com/articles/hawaiian-voyaging-canoes/
9. "Before it: Rafts and single-hulled canoes." **Correct.** Single-hull and outrigger canoes preceded the double canoe. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_navigation
10. "Two of them lashed together, with a deck between, will stand up to an ocean swell and carry a heavy load." **Correct.** Two equal hulls lashed side by side with a platform between them. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_navigation
11. "large enough for several families along with pigs, dogs, chickens and the seedlings of the plants they meant to grow." **Correct.** The Polynesian "canoe plants" and the pig, dog and chicken went with the settlers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_navigation
12. "Hawaii, nearly four thousand kilometres from the nearest islands that had people." **Correct.** Honolulu to Nuku Hiva (Marquesas) is 3,864 km; the Marquesas are the nearest settled group. https://files.hawaii.gov/dbedt/economic/databook/2019-individual/05/050119.pdf
13. "They found Easter Island." **Correct.** Settled about AD 1200 (Wilmshurst 2011). https://www.world-archaeology.com/issues/issue-47/late-date-for-pacific-expansion/
14. "About seven hundred years ago they reached New Zealand." **Imprecise.** Radiocarbon dating of short-lived plant remains gives AD 1230 to 1282, about 750 years ago. Replacement: "About 750 years ago they reached New Zealand." https://www.world-archaeology.com/issues/issue-47/late-date-for-pacific-expansion/ ; https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/65209/pacific-colonisation-more-recent-than-thought
15. "the last large land on Earth to be settled." **Correct.** New Zealand was the last large landmass reached by people (Wilmshurst 2011). https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/high-precision-radiocarbon-dating-shows-recent-and-rapid-initial-/

## 03 The bead

16. "First made: More than 140,000 years ago, in North Africa." **Correct.** Bizmoune Cave, Morocco: 33 pierced sea-snail shell beads from deposits at least 142,000 years old (Sehasseh 2021, Science Advances). https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/206409/1/sciadv.abi8620.pdf ; https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/929149
17. "Made of: Sea shells, and later ivory, bone, teeth and stone." **Correct.** Shell beads at Bizmoune and Blombos (about 75,000 years); mammoth ivory at Sungir and elsewhere about 30,000 years ago and later. https://www.archaeology.org/news/10013-210927-morocco-shell-beads
18. "Before it: Nothing that survives." **Correct** as far as known; no ornament older than Bizmoune has been reported. https://leakeyfoundation.org/archaeologists-find-the-oldest-known-shell-beads/
19. "this person is one of us, or is married, or is someone of importance." **Imprecise.** The excavators suggest the Bizmoune beads signalled identity or group membership; marriage and rank are not shown for beads of that age. Replacement: "…to mean something to them: this person is one of us, or has a place in the group." https://www.archaeology.org/news/10013-210927-morocco-shell-beads
20. "At one burial ground in Russia, three people were buried with about thirteen thousand beads of mammoth ivory sewn onto their clothes." **Correct.** Sungir, about 30,000 years ago: an adult man with about 3,000 to 3,500 beads and two children with about 5,000 each; over 13,000 in all. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungir ; https://sapiens.org/archaeology/paleolithic-burial-sunghir
21. "Each bead took most of an hour to make." **Imprecise.** The usual estimate is 10,000 hours for all the beads, about 45 minutes a bead; a lower estimate of 2,500 hours covers only the ivory beads. Replacement: "Making them all is estimated to have taken about 10,000 hours, around three-quarters of an hour a bead." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungir ; https://www.discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/how-we-know-ancient-humans-believed-in-the-afterlife

## 04 The spear

22. "First made: More than 400,000 years ago." **Imprecise.** Stone points hafted as spear tips at Kathu Pan 1, South Africa, about 500,000 years ago (Wilkins 2012); the Clacton yew spear tip about 400,000 years ago; the Schöningen throwing spears, the best-preserved, about 300,000 (the older figure of 400,000 for Schöningen has been replaced). Replacement: "About 500,000 years ago for stone points on spear shafts; wooden spears survive from 400,000 and 300,000 years ago." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23161998/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%B6ningen_spears ; https://phys.org/news/2024-04-early-humans-wood-years-animals.pdf
23. "Made of: A wooden shaft, and later a point of stone held with glue." **Correct.** Wooden spears came first; the oldest known tar adhesive on stone flakes is at Campitello Quarry, Italy, dated only by fauna and geology to the late Middle Pleistocene, about 200,000 years ago (Mazza 2006). Hafting at Kathu Pan was earlier, with no adhesive recorded. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2006.01.006 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6586293
24. "Before it: The club and the thrown stone." **Could not verify.** No source ranks these; clubs and thrown stones do not survive. Plausible inference only.
25. "Among chimpanzees, a powerful male can terrorise the others, because none of them can stand up to him alone." **Correct** as a summary of dominance among chimpanzees; coalitions of males can still bring an alpha down (Boehm 1999, "Hierarchy in the Forest"). Could not retrieve a page; no counter-evidence found.
26. "Among the hunters of northern Australia a century ago, most of the killing in war was done with spears, by raiders who surrounded a camp while it slept." **Could not verify.** Warner's 1931 "Murngin Warfare" (Oceania) and "A Black Civilization" (1937) describe warfare in Arnhem Land with spears and surprise attacks, but the searches did not reach the passage that gives "most of the killing" or the sleeping-camp raid. Check the passage before it stands. https://opus4.kobv.de/opus4-Fromm/files/22540/Warner_L_1931_Q-056-Ex.pdf

## 05 The grinding stone

27. "First made: More than 30,000 years ago." **Correct.** Grotta Paglicci, Italy: a grinding stone with starch from wild oats, more than 32,000 years old (Mariotti Lippi 2015). The stone may also have been used as a pestle. https://www.mprnews.org/story/2015/09/15/npr-paleo-people-were-making-flour-32000-years-ago ; https://atlasofscience.org/?p=67
28. "used on wild cereals by 23,000 years ago." **Correct.** Ohalo II, Israel: a ground stone with 150 starch grains, 127 of them from grass seeds including barley and probably wheat; later work adds oats (Piperno 2004; Nadel 2012). https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/processing-of-wild-cereal-grains-in-the-upper-palaeolithic-reveal/ ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/new-evidence-for-the-processing-of-wild-cereal-grains-at-ohalo-ii-a-23-000yearold-campsite-on-the-shore-of-the-sea-of-galilee-israel/3F1C519692D8923D4FD321001CB87359
29. "Made of: A slab of hard stone and a smaller rubbing stone." **Correct.** Saddle quern and hand stone. https://www.scientificamerican.com/author/theya-molleson/
30. "People were grinding wild grain twelve thousand years before anyone farmed." **Correct.** The Nature paper states routine processing of wild cereals at least 12,000 years before domestication. https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/processing-of-wild-cereal-grains-in-the-upper-palaeolithic-reveal/
31. "Before it: Chewing, pounding and soaking." **Could not verify** as a sequence; pounding in mortars is attested (Natufian mortars), the others are inference.
32. "a woman knelt on the ground with her toes curled under her, and pushed a stone back and forth across a slab, throwing her whole weight forward and dragging it back." **Correct.** Molleson's Abu Hureyra work: kneeling with toes tucked under, bent-back big toes, damaged lower back. https://www.scientificamerican.com/author/theya-molleson/ ; https://engines.egr.uh.edu/episode/960
33. "Feeding a family took hours of this every day, from girlhood." **Imprecise.** The skeletons show daily grinding from adolescence; the "hours a day" figure comes from ethnographic comparison, not the bones. Replacement: "Feeding a family took hours of this every day, from adolescence." https://www.scientificamerican.com/author/theya-molleson/
34. "The skeletons from one early farming village on the Euphrates show what it cost them, in toes, knees and backs." **Correct.** Abu Hureyra: enlarged knee bones, arthritic big toes, collapsed lower vertebrae, mostly in women. https://engines.egr.uh.edu/episode/960 ; https://www.scientificamerican.com/author/theya-molleson/

## 06 The ration bowl

35. "First made: Nearly 6,000 years ago, on the plain of Iraq." **Correct.** Early Uruk period, about 3900 to 3600 BC (5,900 to 5,600 years ago), southern Mesopotamia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveled_rim_bowl
36. "Made of: Coarse clay mixed with straw." **Correct.** Chaff or straw tempered clay (Goulder 2010, "Pottery is king"). https://core.ac.uk/works/74257583
37. "What it does: Serves out food to workers, a bowl apiece." **Imprecise.** The ration reading is the long-standing explanation but is disputed; other proposals are bread baked or rationed in the bowl, votive offerings, and, at Shakhi Kora, reuse for meat stew. Replacement: "What it does: Probably served out a portion of food, perhaps a worker's ration." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveled_rim_bowl ; https://the-past.com/news/meat-stews-were-on-the-menu-in-mesopotamia-study-finds/
38. "This bowl was shaped in a few moments without a wheel." **Imprecise.** The method is not known; pressing in a mould is the most accepted theory, hand forming the other. Replacement: "This bowl was pressed in a mould, or shaped by hand, in a few moments, without a wheel." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveled_rim_bowl
39. "It is the commonest thing that comes out of the ground in the oldest cities of the Middle East." **Imprecise.** It is the commonest pottery at Uruk-period sites, not the commonest find. Replacement: "It is the commonest pottery that comes out of the ground in the oldest cities of the Middle East." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveled_rim_bowl
40. "At some sites, three out of every four pieces of broken pottery come from bowls of this one kind." **Correct** on the figure given: roughly three quarters of ceramics at Uruk sites. The source is Wikipedia and carries no citation; no excavation report found. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveled_rim_bowl
41. "their sign for a ration looked just like this bowl." **Imprecise.** The proto-cuneiform sign NINDA (bread, food) resembles the bowl, but a derivation from the bowl is an early suggestion, not settled. Replacement: "The sign for bread or food looks like a bowl of this shape; some scholars think it was drawn from it." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveled_rim_bowl
42. "their sign for eating was the same sign set against a human head." **Correct.** The proto-cuneiform "eat" sign (GU7) is the bowl sign combined with the head sign; the British Museum tablet BM 140852 (about 3300 to 3100 BC) is described as a ration allocation identified by that sign. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ration_distribution_BM_140852.jpg
43. "A bowl of one kind, made by the thousand, means that somebody was feeding a great many workers out of a storehouse." **Imprecise.** Depends on the ration reading in item 37. Replacement: "A bowl of one kind, made by the thousand, points to food being served out in standard portions, perhaps to a great many workers from a storehouse." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveled_rim_bowl

## 07 The Nile boat

44. "First made: More than 5,000 years ago, in Egypt." **Correct.** Boats with sails on Naqada II pottery, about 3500 to 3200 BC (British Museum jar); a sailing boat on the Qustul censer, 3200 to 3000 BC; the Abydos boats, about 3000 BC. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_boat ; https://egyptology.yale.edu/node/56
45. "Made of: Planks tied with rope." **Correct.** The Abydos boats, First Dynasty, are the oldest known planked hulls, planks lashed with rope through mortises, seams packed with reeds (Ward). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abydos_boats ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/boatbuilding-and-its-social-context-in-early-egypt-interpretations-from-the-first-dynasty-boatgrave-cemetery-at-abydos/6611A06E237D29258D98E9043488A421
46. "a mast and a square sail." **Correct.** A square sail on a single-pole mast near the bow on a Naqada II jar. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_boat
47. "Before it: Rafts of bundled reeds, paddled." **Correct.** Reed and papyrus boats; the earliest Nile boat image is a pebble from the Khartoum Mesolithic. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_boat
48. "The Nile's current flows north... The wind blows the other way, from the north, on most days of the year." **Correct.** Prevailing north wind, current north. https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Sail_(hieroglyph)
49. "the sign for travelling north was a boat with its sail down, and the sign for travelling south was a boat with its sail up." **Imprecise.** Gardiner P1, a boat with no sail, is the determinative of "sail downstream" (north); P2, a boat under sail, of "sail upstream" (south). Replacement: "the sign for travelling north was a boat with no sail, and the sign for travelling south was a boat under sail." https://ganino.com/anteanus/ships-and-parts-of-ships ; https://ipfs.aleph.im/ipfs/QmXoypizjW3WknFiJnKLwHCnL72vedxjQkDDP1mXWo6uco/wiki/List_of_hieroglyphs_P.html
50. "a valley a thousand kilometres long." **Correct** (rounded). The Nile in Egypt from Aswan to the Mediterranean is about 1,000 km. Could not retrieve a page giving the figure directly. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile
51. "it stayed one, with interruptions, for most of three thousand years." **Imprecise.** Unification about 3100 BC to Alexander in 332 BC is about 2,800 years. Replacement: "…and it stayed one, with interruptions, for most of the next 2,700 years." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt
