# The spy plane

| | |
|---|---|
| **First made** | Lockheed's Skunk Works, California, 1955, designed by Kelly Johnson |
| **Made of** | Thin aluminium skin over a very light frame, and one jet engine |
| **What it does** | Photographs a strip up to 200 km wide from 21 km up |
| **Before it** | Converted bombers such as the RB-47, open to Soviet defences |
| **After it** | The A-12 of 1962, flying at three times the speed of sound |

## Why it mattered

The plane was close to a glider with a jet engine. To climb above 21 kilometres it was stripped of everything heavy. Its wheels ran in a single line down the belly, and small wheels on poles held the long wings up on the ground, then fell away at take-off. In the thin air its top speed and its stalling speed were only about 18 km an hour apart. Pilots called that narrow band the coffin corner.

The camera was the point of it. Its lens swung across seven windows in the belly. Two reels of film, each nearly two kilometres long, turned in opposite directions, so that the moving film never upset the plane's balance. One flight could photograph a strip 3,500 kilometres long.
