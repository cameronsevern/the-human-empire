Title: THE SLEDGE. Date line: about 4,600 years ago. Ancient Egyptian workers hauling a large squared block of limestone on a wooden sledge with upturned runners. The whole scene is ON a long, gentle ramp that rises from lower left to upper right: the sledge and block are tilted on the slope, part-way up it, exactly as in the attached schematic, not on level ground. The ramp surface is sand over rubble and mud brick, and its built side is visible along the bottom edge. A line of about six men in plain white linen kilts, higher up the ramp at the right, leans into two ropes that run straight back to the front of the sledge. One man stands beside the track, to the near side of the ropes and clear of them, pouring pale blue-grey water from a clay jar onto the sand directly in front of the runners. The damp patch of sand in front of the runners is the only terracotta in the picture. No wheels, no rollers, no pyramids in the background. Callouts: 1 'A block of stone that weighs as much as two cars'; 2 'A wooden sledge with upturned runners'; 3 'Ropes, and a team of about twenty'; 4 'Water is poured on the sand in front. Damp sand is firm, and the pull needed is halved'; 5 'A ramp of rubble and mud brick'.
