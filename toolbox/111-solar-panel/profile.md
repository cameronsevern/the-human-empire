# The solar panel

| | |
|---|---|
| **First made** | Bell Labs, New Jersey, 1954, turning about 6 per cent of sunlight |
| **Made of** | Thin wafers of pure silicon under glass, in an aluminium frame |
| **What it does** | Turns sunlight straight into electric current, with no moving parts |
| **Before it** | Selenium cells coated with gold, 1883, about one per cent efficient |
| **After it** | Perovskite layered on silicon, first sold by Oxford PV in 2024 |

## Why it mattered

Bell Labs had wanted a power source for telephone lines in remote, damp places. Instead, the cells made their name in space. The American satellite Vanguard 1, launched in 1958, carried six small banks of solar cells, and its sun-powered radio kept sending until 1964. It is still in orbit, the oldest human-made object there.

Each cell gives only about half a volt, so a panel joins sixty or more in a row, adding up their voltage. Panels on sale in 2025 turned more than a fifth of the sunlight falling on them into electricity. In 2022 more than four fifths of the world's panels, at every stage of making them, came from China.
