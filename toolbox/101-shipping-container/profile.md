# The shipping container

| | |
|---|---|
| **First made** | Port Newark, 1956, for Malcolm McLean; world standard sizes from 1968 |
| **Made of** | Corrugated steel walls and a plywood floor on a steel frame |
| **What it does** | Carries goods sealed from factory to warehouse, unpacked nowhere between |
| **Before it** | Sacks, crates and barrels, carried aboard one at a time |
| **After it** | Rotterdam's terminal of 1993, with driverless carriers and stacking cranes |

## Why it mattered

Every box is the same at its corners. Each of the eight corners is a steel casting with an oval hole. A crane's spreader drops a twistlock into each top hole and turns it a quarter turn, and the box is held. The same locks pin it to a lorry, a railway wagon, a ship's deck or the box beneath. Their designer, Keith Tantlinger, gave up his patent royalties, so that anyone could use them.

As a result, almost nobody looks inside. A box is sealed at the factory and opened at a warehouse on another continent, and the people who lift, stack and carry it never learn what it holds. By 2011 there were about twenty million of them in the world.
