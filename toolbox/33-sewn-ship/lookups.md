# Lookups: the sewn ship

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea (first century) describes sewn boats at Omana in Arabia and on the East African coast; Rhapta took its name from them (Greek "sewn") | correct | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhapta ; Schoff translation, Periplus §15, §16, §36 |
| Western Indian Ocean sewn hulls: planks edge to edge, coir cord, a wadding roll over the seams, holes plugged and sealed with lime or resin putty with fish oil | correct | Vosmer, "Building the Jewel of Muscat", in Shipwrecked (Smithsonian, 2010), https://asia.si.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/shipwrecked-08-vosmer.pdf ; Ghidoni and Pavan, PSAS 2022 |
| Belitung wreck: planks sewn with coconut-fibre cord through holes 5 to 6 cm apart, wadding under the stitching inside and outside | correct | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belitung_shipwreck ; Flecker, in Shipwrecked (Smithsonian, 2010) |
| X-shaped stitches alternating with vertical ones; pairs of workers, one inside and one outside; stitch hammered tight over the wadding | correct | Vosmer, above |
| Jewel of Muscat: more than 37,000 holes, more than 120 km of coconut cordage, much of it handmade | correct | Vosmer, above |
| Hull gave a little rather than splitting on reefs | correct as the traditional and the builders' explanation (the designer argues sewing spreads loads across the hull) | Vosmer, above; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewn_boat |
| Marco Polo thought the ships of Hormuz poor ("wretched"), many were lost; sewn with coconut twine, rubbed with fish oil | correct (wording varies between translations, so not quoted) | Polo, Book I (Yule); summarised in Pedersen, BAR 31:3 (2005) |
| Before it: Khufu's ship beside the Great Pyramid, planks tied with rope, no nails | correct | https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/pharaoh/expl-06.html |
| After it: nailed hulls spread after Portuguese ships entered the ocean (da Gama reached India 1498); sewn boats lingered, and small ones are still built in India | correct | https://archive.archaeology.org/9705/abstracts/dhow.html ; Commons "Sewn boats in Vizag" description; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewn_boat |
