Title: THE EDICT PILLAR. Date line: about 2,270 years ago. One free-standing stone pillar of the Indian emperor Ashoka, shown whole and upright in the centre of the picture, in side view, with the ground cut away in section so that the buried part can be seen. The shaft is a single round, slightly tapering column of pale buff sandstone, very tall and slender, with no base and no joints, polished so smooth above ground that it has a soft gloss. On top of the shaft sits the capital, in grey-buff stone: a bell shaped like a lotus flower turned upside down, with long petals running down it, then a low round platform, and on the platform one seated lion in profile, in the stiff, formal style of ancient Indian sculpture. About a quarter of the way up the shaft, beginning just above the height of a standing person's head, a band of some seven close rows of tiny cut letters runs round the stone; show them as generic small marks, not real writing. This band of inscription is the terracotta-accented part. Below the ground line the earth is shown in section as a plain tan wash, with a narrow pit: the bottom fifth of the shaft stands in the pit, its surface a little rougher than the polished stone above, and its foot rests on a flat square slab of stone. A small plain figure in a simple wrapped cloth stands on the ground beside the pillar for scale, about one seventh of the pillar's height above ground, with no detailed face. No buildings, no railings, no other objects. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps the pillar, the figure, the ground or another label. Callouts: 1 'The shaft: one piece of sandstone, rubbed until it shines'; 2 'The bell of the capital, shaped like a lotus turned upside down'; 3 'An animal on top, most often a lion'; 4 'The king's words, cut into the stone in neat rows'; 5 'The buried foot stands on a slab of stone'.
