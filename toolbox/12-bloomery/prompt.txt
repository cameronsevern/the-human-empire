Title: THE BLOOMERY. Date line: about 3,000 years ago. An Iron Age iron-smelting furnace of the kind called a bloomery, seen from the side and cut open from top to bottom so that the inside shows, large, in the centre, standing on bare ground. It is a slightly tapering shaft of thick baked clay about a metre tall, open at the top. Inside, the shaft is filled with alternating layers of black charcoal and rust-brown broken ore, glowing dull orange towards the bottom. Near the base on the left, a clay pipe passes down through the wall, and a pair of simple bag bellows made of animal skin is fitted to its outer end, lying on the ground. At the very bottom of the shaft, sitting in a pool of dark slag, is the bloom: a rough, spongy, glowing lump about the size of a loaf of bread. The bloom is the terracotta-accented part. At the base on the right, a small hole in the wall lets a tongue of dark molten slag run out onto the ground. No people, no tools, no buildings. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps the furnace, the bellows, the slag or another label. Callouts: 1 'A clay shaft about as tall as a child'; 2 'Charcoal and broken ore go in at the top, in layers'; 3 'Bellows blow air in through a clay pipe'; 4 'Molten slag runs out through a hole at the base'; 5 'The bloom: a spongy lump of iron, left at the bottom'.
