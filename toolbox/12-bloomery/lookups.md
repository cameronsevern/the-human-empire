# Lookups: the bloomery

1. Furnace heat about 1,200 °C; iron melts at just over 1,500 °C.
   - Verdict: correct. Slag at Tell Hammeh indicates temperatures above 1,200 °C; pure iron melts at 1,538 °C.
   - Sources: https://archive.archaeology.org/0001/newsbriefs/iron.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Hammeh ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomery
2. "First made more than 3,000 years ago, in the lands between Turkey and Iran."
   - Verdict: safe. The earliest excavated smelting workshop, Tell Hammeh in Jordan, dates to about 930 BCE; smelted iron objects are older. The card avoids a single place of invention.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Hammeh ; https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1446144/
3. Slag is tapped through a hole at the base.
   - Verdict: correct for slag-tapping furnaces, which include the early Near Eastern ones (tap slag at Tell Hammeh). The chapter has the slag trickling to the bottom; no conflict.
4. Slag as "sand and clay ... melt". Codex: wrong, the silica combines with iron oxide. Fixed to match the chapter.
5. "Copper and tin lie in a few places." Codex: wrong for copper. Fixed: only tin is called scarce.
6. "A heap of charcoal that took a stand of trees." Unverifiable scale. Replaced with "several times its own weight in charcoal" (experimental smelts: about 50 kg charcoal and 50 kg ore for about 12 kg of metal before forging losses). https://www.wealdeniron.org.uk/Expt/Owens%20Smelt.htm
7. Blast furnace "hot enough to melt iron". Codex: incomplete; the iron takes up carbon, which lowers its melting point to about 1,150 °C. Fixed. https://www.intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue69/14/full-text.html
8. Modern blast furnace "as tall as a ten-storey building", "runs for years".
   - Verdict: correct. Large furnaces stand 30 m and more; campaigns between relinings last 10 to 20 years. https://globalenergymonitor.org/?p=11367
9. "Most of the world's new steel begins in one."
   - Verdict: correct. The blast furnace and oxygen converter route makes about two thirds of crude steel. Not found: an exact figure from the World Steel Association in this search.
10. "Could build a bloomery in a couple of days, and knock it down afterwards": not verified, cut.
11. Photo caption: Banjeli (Bassar region, Togo) furnaces were natural-draught, with no bellows. Correct per the standard literature on Bassar smelting (de Barros); not rechecked online. The postcard is undated; "before 1914" rests on its German caption and the end of German rule in Togo.
