Title: THE BAMBOO BOOK. Date line: about 2,300 years ago. A Chinese book of the Warring States period, made of bamboo strips, lying partly unrolled on a plain surface and seen from above. The left end of the book is still rolled up into a tight roll; from it about nineteen narrow strips of pale yellow bamboo lie open side by side, each strip as narrow as a pencil and about as long as a forearm, all the same length. Down each strip runs one single column of small black brush marks. The marks are generic dabs and short strokes of ink that only suggest writing; do not attempt real Chinese characters or any readable script. Two thin cords run across all the strips, one about a quarter of the way down and one about three quarters of the way down, each knotted round every strip, with loose ends hanging at the right-hand edge; the two cords are the only terracotta-accented part of the picture. At the lower right, separate from the book, lie the writing things: a slim brush with a bamboo handle and a fine dark hair tip, a flat stone slab with a pool of black ink on it and a small rubbing stone beside the pool, and a small bronze knife with a ring at the end of its handle. No paper, no bound book with pages, no silk, no people. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps an object or another label. Callouts: 1 'Bamboo is split into strips, scraped smooth and dried over a fire'; 2 'One column of writing runs down each strip'; 3 'Cords tie the strips side by side, in order'; 4 'The book rolls up like a mat'; 5 'A brush, ink made of soot, and a knife to shave off mistakes'.
