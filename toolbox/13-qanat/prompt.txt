Title: THE QANAT. Date line: about 3,000 years ago. A cross-section through dry ground in Iran, seen from the side, filling the width of the picture. The ground surface slopes down from the foot of bare hills at the left to a flat dry plain at the right. Under the hills at the left, the lower part of the earth is wet gravel, tinted pale blue-grey, with a dashed line marking the top of the buried water. A narrow tunnel runs underground from the wet gravel at the left, sloping very gently down to the right, until it comes out at the surface on the plain; it carries a thin stream of pale blue-grey water. The tunnel is the terracotta-accented part: its outline is drawn in terracotta. A row of seven narrow vertical shafts goes straight down from the surface to the tunnel, the deepest at the left under the hills and the shallowest at the right; each shaft mouth has a small ring of dug earth heaped around it. Where the tunnel reaches the surface at the right, the water runs on in an open channel into a small plot of green crops. No people, no buildings, no animals.
