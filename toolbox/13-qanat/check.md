I found five factual or logical issues, plus several design/style issues. No files were edited.

1. **Continuous flow depends on groundwater, not just maintenance.** [Line 23](toolbox/13-qanat/profile.md:23):
   > “Once it is dug, the slope of the ground does the work, day and night, for as long as the tunnel is kept clear.”

   A clear tunnel can stop flowing when groundwater falls below its collecting section. Seasonal drying, drought and over-extraction are documented causes. The correct condition is that the tunnel remains clear **and its source remains supplied**. This also breaks the style rule against absolutes with real exceptions. [Source: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, water supply](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kariz_3/).

2. **The canal comparison overstates evaporation losses.** [Line 25](toolbox/13-qanat/profile.md:25):
   > “In a desert, a canal loses much of its water to the sun before the water arrives.”

   Evaporation occurs, but it does not generally account for a large proportion of canal water: seepage commonly dominates conveyance losses. FAO describes canal evaporation as commonly only a small percentage of diverted water. The defensible point is that underground conveyance reduces evaporation, without claiming that open canals generally lose “much” to it. [Source: FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/t0667e/t0667e0b.htm).

3. **“The last few metres” is an unsupported distance.** [Line 25](toolbox/13-qanat/profile.md:25):
   > “A qanat keeps its water in the cool and the dark until the last few metres.”

   Water stays underground until the outlet; surface channels then distribute it to agricultural land. Their length is not necessarily a few metres. Replace the distance with the outlet as the boundary. [Source: UNESCO’s description of qanat distribution](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1506/).

4. **1858 marks replacement beginning, not use ending.** [Line 31](toolbox/13-qanat/profile.md:31):
   > “Madrid drank from them until 1858.”

   Madrid’s new Canal de Isabel II supply opened in 1858, but the *viajes de agua* continued serving parts of the city well into the twentieth century. “Until 1858” misleadingly supplies a terminal date. [Source: Madrid City Council](https://www.madrid.es/portales/munimadrid/es/Inicio/Buscador/Viajes-de-agua-en-Madrid/?vgnextchannel=7db8fc12aa936610VgnVCM1000008a4a900aRCRD&vgnextfmt=default&vgnextoid=c2c61a824d4ae210VgnVCM2000000c205a0aRCRD).

5. **The purpose has not been outlived.** [Line 31](toolbox/13-qanat/profile.md:31):
   > “The method outlived the purpose, too.”

   Qanats still supply settlements and agriculture. Railway tunnelling illustrates another application of shaft construction, not survival after the original purpose disappeared. This contradicts the immediately preceding sentence, “Thousands in Iran still run,” and conflicts with the chapter’s account of continued use. [Source: UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1506/).
