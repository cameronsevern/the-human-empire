# Lookups: the qanat

1. "First made about 3,000 years ago, in Iran or eastern Arabia."
   - Verdict: correct as a rounded figure. Settlements depending on qanats (aflaj) in Oman and the Emirates date from about 1000 BCE; the older view places the invention in Iran in the early first millennium BCE. The origin is disputed, so the card names both.
   - Sources: https://www.worldhistory.org/qanat/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qanat
2. Gonabad mother well "about 300 metres deep".
   - Verdict: correct. UNESCO nomination dossier: the Doolab branch of the Qasabeh qanat has a mother well 300 m deep (the other branch 200 m). https://whc.unesco.org/document/152821
3. "Madrid drank from them until 1858."
   - Verdict: imprecise (Codex). The viajes de agua supplied Madrid until the Canal de Isabel II arrived on 24 June 1858, and some stayed in use afterwards. Reworded. https://es.hispanopedia.com/wiki/Viajes_de_agua_en_el_Madrid_hist%C3%B3rico
4. Early railway tunnels dug from a row of shafts; the brick towers above them are those shafts.
   - Verdict: correct. Kilsby Tunnel (1830s): working shafts sunk along the line, tunnel driven both ways from their bottoms, ten or more kept as ventilation shafts. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilsby_Tunnel ; https://www.railengineer.co.uk/stairway-to-heaven-shaft-repairs-at-kilsby-tunnel/
5. "A canal loses much of its water to the sun." Codex: overstated; seepage is the larger loss. Reworded to say only that the canal lies in the sun and the qanat does not.
6. "For as long as the tunnel is kept clear." Codex: flow also needs the groundwater to last. Fixed.
7. "Tens of thousands in Iran still run": matches the chapter (about 37,000 in use in 2015).
