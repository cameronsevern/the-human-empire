Title: THE SHADOW STICK. Date line: about 2,250 years ago. A clear explanatory diagram, not a landscape. The lower part of the sheet is filled by the top of the Earth, drawn as one large smooth curve of sandy ground that is highest right of centre and falls away to both sides. From the top of the sheet seven thin straight golden rays of sunlight come straight down, all exactly parallel and evenly spaced. Two plain wooden sticks stand on the curve, each at a right angle to the ground beneath it. The first stick, right of centre at the very top of the curve, stands exactly upright under a ray, so the ray runs straight down its length and it casts no shadow at all; the place is named 'Syene' in small italics on the ground beside it. The second stick stands well to the left, further round the curve, and so leans away to the left; it is the only terracotta-accented part of the picture. A ray passes its tip and meets the ground a little to the left of its foot, and a short dark shadow lies on the ground between the foot and that point; the place is named 'Alexandria' in small italics on the ground below. A small arc at the tip of the leaning stick marks the narrow angle between the stick and the ray. A dotted line follows the ground from the foot of one stick to the foot of the other. No sun disc, no buildings, no people, no well, no degree figures or numbers other than the ringed callout numbers. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps a ray, a stick or another label. Callouts: 1 'Sunlight reaches the Earth in parallel rays'; 2 'At Syene the noon sun is overhead, and a stick casts no shadow'; 3 'At Alexandria a stick casts a short shadow'; 4 'The stick meets the sunlight at one fiftieth of a circle'; 5 'The towns are 5,000 stades apart, so the Earth is fifty times that around'.
