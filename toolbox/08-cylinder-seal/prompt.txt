Title: THE CYLINDER SEAL. Date line: about 5,500 years ago. An ancient Mesopotamian cylinder seal being rolled across clay. The seal is a small cylinder of dark stone LYING ON ITS SIDE at the right-hand end of a long flat strip of damp clay, so that its curved side touches the clay like a rolling pin, with a hole through its length (visible at the near end) and a short cord threaded through the hole. Carved into the curved side of the cylinder, in the fine style of early Mesopotamian seals, is a scene of two horned goats rearing up on either side of a small tree; those carved lines are the only terracotta in the picture. On the clay strip to the left of the cylinder, the same scene of goats and tree stands out in raised relief and is repeated three times in a continuous band, showing where the seal has rolled. A small arrow above the strip points left to show the direction of rolling. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps the seal, the clay or another label. Callouts: 1 'A cylinder of stone, the size of a thumb joint'; 2 'The picture is cut into the stone, back to front'; 3 'Rolled across damp clay, it leaves the picture standing up'; 4 'The picture repeats for as long as the roll goes on'; 5 'A hole for a cord, worn at the neck or wrist'.
