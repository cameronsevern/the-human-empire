Title: THE SPOKED WHEEL. Date line: about 4,000 years ago. A single Bronze Age wooden chariot wheel seen straight on, large, a little left of centre. It has a turned wooden hub at the centre with a round hole for the axle, ten slender wooden spokes of equal length, and a rim made of wood bent into a circle, with one visible scarf joint where the ends of the bent wood overlap and are bound with rawhide. The spokes are the terracotta-accented part. At the lower right, much smaller, for comparison, is the older kind of wheel: a heavy solid disc made of three thick planks side by side, held together by two cross-battens, with a hole in the middle. No vehicle, no people, no animals. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps a wheel or another label. Callouts: 1 'The hub: a block of wood with a hole for the axle'; 2 'Ten or twelve thin spokes, all the same length'; 3 'The rim: wood soaked or steamed and bent into a circle'; 4 'Each spoke sits in a socket in the hub and another in the rim'; 5 'The solid wheel it replaced weighs several times as much'.
