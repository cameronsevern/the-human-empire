Title: THE COIN DIE. Date line: about 2,600 years ago. The tools for striking a coin by hand in an ancient Greek or Lydian mint, seen from the side and arranged in a vertical line, a little left of centre. At the bottom is a heavy block of wood serving as an anvil, cut away at the front to show the lower die set into it: a short thick cylinder of bronze sunk into the block, with a small hollow picture cut into its upper face. The lower die is the terracotta-accented part. Resting on the lower die is the blank: a small, thick, bean-shaped lump of pale gold metal. A short gap above it hangs the upper die: a short bronze bar held upright, its lower end cut with a small hollow picture, its top end flattened by hammering. Above that is a hammer with a plain iron head and a wooden handle, poised to strike, with a small arrow showing the blow going downwards. At the right, separately, lies one finished coin, shown larger than life: a thick, slightly irregular round coin with a simple lion's head on it. No people, no hands, no modern tools, no writing on the coin. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps the tools, the coin or another label. Callouts: 1 'The lower die, set in an anvil, its picture cut hollow and back to front'; 2 'The blank: a weighed lump of metal'; 3 'The upper die, held upright like a chisel'; 4 'One hard blow of the hammer'; 5 'The coin comes out with a picture on each face'.
