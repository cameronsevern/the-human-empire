1. **“Silver weighed out on scales at every sale”** — [card, line 8](toolbox/15-coin-die/profile.md:8)

   **Wrong, and contradicts the chapter.** Before coinage, payments also used barley and other commodities, and purchases could be made on credit. Silver was not physically weighed at every sale. The chapter explicitly says: “The actual payment was often made in barley, or wool, or labour. Very often it was not made at once.” Correct the card to something like “Metal weighed out for payment.” This also breaks the style rule against exception-breaking absolutes. [Source: Bank of Italy’s Mesopotamian tablets](https://www.bancaditalia.it/servizi-cittadino/musei-e-visite/museo-moneta/tavolette-mesopotamiche/index.html?com.dotmarketing.htmlpage.language=1&dotcache=refresh).

2. **“The blank is a lump of gold or silver that has already been weighed, so the coin's value is fixed before it is struck.”** — [step 2, line 16](toolbox/15-coin-die/profile.md:16)

   **Two problems.** For the early Lydian setting, the material should include **electrum, an alloy of gold and silver**. The gold-or-silver description omits precisely the material central to the preceding chapter and the pale-gold illustration. [Source: British Museum’s electrum coin description](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/electrum-1-6-stater/gAHZKMNvhGBtrg?hl=en).

   More substantially, weighing fixes **weight**, not automatically value: composition and the issuer’s valuation also matter. The chapter correctly says, “From that moment the coin's weight was fixed,” and later distinguishes the king’s promised value from the metal’s worth. Label 2 likewise says only “a weighed lump of metal.” The profile adds an erroneous inference. [Source: Austrian Archaeological Institute’s research on electrum composition and coin valuation](https://www.oeaw.ac.at/en/oeai/research/classical-studies/numismatics/early-lydian-coinage-and-chronology).

3. **“He strikes the top of it with a hammer, once and hard.”** — [step 4, line 18](toolbox/15-coin-die/profile.md:18)

   **Doubtful as a general instruction.** Hand-striking could require several blows. Sellwood’s experiments reproducing Greek minting required two blows to fill the deeper designs. The correction is to describe striking hard without specifying exactly once. This problem is shared by prompt label 4, “One hard blow of the hammer”; it is not a mismatch between text and label. [Source: D. Sellwood, “Medieval Minting Techniques,” p. 65](https://www.britnumsoc.org/publications/Digital%20BNJ/pdfs/1962_BNJ_31_10.pdf).
