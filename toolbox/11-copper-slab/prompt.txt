Title: THE COPPER SLAB. Date line: about 3,500 years ago. A Late Bronze Age copper ingot of the kind archaeologists call an oxhide ingot, seen from above and slightly to one side, large, in the centre. It is a flat rectangular slab of dull reddish-brown copper with a green-grey patina, about 60 cm long, its four long sides curving inwards and its four corners drawn out into short blunt handles, so that the outline looks like a stretched animal hide. Its upper face is rough and blistered. A simple mark like a small cross is cut into the upper face. The four corner handles are the terracotta-accented part. At the right, much smaller, a man in a plain kilt walks carrying one such slab on his shoulder, steadying it with one hand, as in Egyptian tomb paintings. Below the slab is a thin scale bar labelled 'about 60 cm'. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps the slab, the man or another label. Callouts: 1 'A slab of nearly pure copper, as heavy as a full suitcase'; 2 'Four corners drawn out into handles'; 3 'One face is rough and blistered, where the metal cooled in an open mould'; 4 'A mark cut in by the maker or the shipper'; 5 'One man carries it on his shoulder'.
