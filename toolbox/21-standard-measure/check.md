1. **“First made” confuses this specimen with the invention of standard measures.**

Identify the date as that of this Qin example. 4. **The explanation turns differing standards into an absence of standards.**  
   > “In either case nobody could prove anything, for as long as each scoop was its own standard.”

   [Profile, line 23](toolbox/21-standard-measure/profile.md:23). As a hypothetical this works, but the paragraph presents it as the historical situation before Qin’s reform. Earlier reference measures and enforcement existed: the Qi example above expressly regulates dishonest officials. The sound distinction is between differing local or state standards and a common standard, not between proof and complete impossibility of proof. This also breaches “No absolutes that one exception breaks”; the objection is the historical premise, not the confident voice. [National Museum of China](https://en.chnmuseum.cn/collections_577/collection_highlights_608/artifacts_handed___down_from_ancient_times_612/202008/t20200811_246873.html).

5. **The ending confuses brim measures, filling lines and certification marks.**

   Compare the card:
   > “The official measures of every later Chinese dynasty, and the stamped line on a beer glass”

   [Profile, lines 9 and 31](toolbox/21-standard-measure/profile.md:31). A pint glass can measure a pint **at its brim or at a marked line below it**. Also, glasses used after a metered dispense need not themselves be stamped measuring vessels.
