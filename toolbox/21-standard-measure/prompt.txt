Title: THE STANDARD MEASURE. Date line: about 2,370 years ago. A bronze grain measure of the Chinese kingdom of Qin, seen from the front and a little above, large, a little left of centre, standing on a plain surface. It is a small, shallow, straight-sided rectangular box of dull green-brown bronze, open at the top, about 12 cm long, 7 cm wide and only 2.5 cm deep, with a short, stubby bronze handle sticking out horizontally from the middle of its left end, so that the whole thing looks like a flat scoop. On its long front side are three short rows of small cut characters; show them as generic angular marks, not real writing. This band of inscription is the terracotta-accented part. The box is full of pale golden millet grain. A plain straight wooden stick lies across the rim and is being drawn along it from left to right, with a small arrow showing the direction: behind the stick the grain is perfectly level with the rim, and ahead of it the grain is still heaped up. No hands, no people. To the right, smaller, stands a bronze weight of the same period: a low dome like a small bell with a flat base and a half-ring loop on top, with columns of the same generic cut marks on its side. Below the box is a thin scale bar labelled 'about 12 cm'. No coins, no balance scales, no other objects. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps the measure, the weight or another label. Callouts: 1 'A bronze box, open at the top. Filled to the brim it holds one measure'; 2 'A short handle at one end'; 3 'The ruler's order and the size of the box, cut into the metal'; 4 'A straight stick sweeps the grain level with the rim'; 5 'A bronze weight carries the same order'.
