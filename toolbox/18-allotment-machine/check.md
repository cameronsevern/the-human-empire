I found the following issues. No files were edited.

1. **The machine is credited with selecting a particular trial’s jury.** [Profile, lines 7 and 23](toolbox/18-allotment-machine/profile.md:7):

   > “Picks a jury by chance, in public, a few minutes before the trial”
   >
   > “The machine removed that knowledge.”
   >
   > “Until the cubes dropped, nobody in Athens could say who would judge that day's cases, and that included the officials who ran the courts.”

   The cubes selected men for service that day. Each selected man then drew a separate token assigning him to a court. Omitting that stage makes the explanation misleading: dropping the cubes did not establish who would judge each case. The chapter explicitly says, “A second draw then decided which court each chosen man would sit in.” [Aristotle, *Athenian Constitution* 64.4–65](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/athenian_const.3.3.html).

2. **Rows of five did not guarantee equal representation in every jury.** [Line 27](toolbox/18-allotment-machine/profile.md:27):

   > “Each row held one man from each letter, so that every jury was drawn evenly from all the sections into which the jurors were divided.”

   Each row contained five of the ten letter groups within **one tribe**. Selection by rows balanced those five groups at that stage; subsequent individual court assignments did not preserve rows or guarantee equal numbers from every letter group in every jury. This also conflicts with the chapter’s separate court-assignment draw. [Aristotle, 63.4–64.4](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/athenian_const.3.3.html).

3. **The loading procedure leaves out an essential randomisation step.** [Line 16](toolbox/18-allotment-machine/profile.md:16):

   > “The tickets are pushed into the column under their letter, from the top down, in the order in which they come to hand.”

   The tickets first went into lettered boxes and were shaken before being drawn and inserted. Without that explanation, “come to hand” can mean arrival order or an operator’s choice. The chapter supplies the boxes and drawing step. This is a consequential omission under the style rule requiring readers to understand how the device works. [Aristotle, 64.1–2](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/athenian_const.3.3.html).

4. **Advance knowledge of the actual jury is not a necessary condition for bribery.** [Line 23](toolbox/18-allotment-machine/profile.md:23):

   > “A jury can be bought only if the buyer knows who is on it.”

   A briber could approach prospective jurors before knowing which would serve. Random assignment made targeted bribery harder and more expensive; it did not make such knowledge logically necessary. Douglas MacDowell discusses precisely this problem and proposes organised groups spanning the possible panels as one historical method—explicitly a hypothesis. This is a substantive problem with “only,” also covered by the style rule against exception-breaking absolutes, rather than a complaint about confidence. [MacDowell, “Athenian laws about bribery,” pp. 226–27](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PHIL959/MacDOWELL%20Studies.pdf).

5. **The “before” entry collapses earlier changes in jury selection.** [Line 8](toolbox/18-allotment-machine/profile.md:8):

   > “Juries whose members were known in advance”

   This describes an earlier arrangement, but is doubtful as the immediate predecessor of the machine. Earlier procedures already assigned panels to courts by a daily draw; the later system randomised individual jurors more thoroughly. The contrast should distinguish fixed panels from individual allotment, rather than imply that the machine introduced all uncertainty about court assignments. [MacDowell, pp. 226–27](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PHIL959/MacDOWELL%20Studies.pdf).

6. **The illustration contradicts the stated cube count.** [Line 17](toolbox/18-allotment-machine/profile.md:17):

   > “The white ones have been counted beforehand, one for every five jurors needed.”

   The generated picture has eight occupied rows but visibly more than eight white cubes, including the released cube and those entering the funnel. Under the stated procedure, that load cannot correspond to the tickets shown. The schematic also contains too many. The sentence’s rule is correct; the picture needs to follow it.

This is an internal presentation mismatch, not grounds for declaring either rounded date historically wrong.
