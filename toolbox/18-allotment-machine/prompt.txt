Title: THE ALLOTMENT MACHINE. Date line: about 2,400 years ago. An ancient Athenian allotment machine seen straight on: a tall upright slab of pale grey marble, about the height of a man, standing on a plain stone base. Its face is cut with exactly five columns of short horizontal slots, eleven slots to a column, forming neat rows of five. At the head of each column one Greek capital letter is cut into the stone, reading from left to right Α, Β, Γ, Δ, Ε. The top eight rows of slots each hold a small flat rectangular ticket of bronze, the size of a stick of chewing gum, scratched with a few fine lines that only suggest a name; the bottom three rows of slots are empty dark slits. Down the left-hand side of the slab runs a narrow upright tube with a funnel at its top; the tube and funnel are the only terracotta-accented part of the picture. There are exactly eight small cubes in the whole picture, one for each row of tickets, and no more. The tube is shown cut away and is mostly empty: only four cubes sit in a single stack at its foot, reading from the bottom up black, white, white, black. Three more cubes, two white and one black, are falling into the funnel. A small stop closes the foot of the tube, and one white cube, the eighth, has just dropped out below it. A small bracket marks the top row of five tickets. No people, no handle or wheel, no modern machinery. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps the slab or another label. Callouts: 1 'Five columns of slots, each under its own letter'; 2 'A juror's bronze ticket goes into the column for his letter'; 3 'Black and white cubes are poured into the funnel'; 4 'The tube lets them out at the bottom, one at a time'; 5 'A white cube picks the whole row. A black one sends it home'.
