# The small drone

| | |
|---|---|
| **First made** | Hobby racing drones of the 2010s, armed in Ukraine from 2022 |
| **Made of** | A carbon-fibre frame, four electric motors, a camera and a battery |
| **What it does** | Flies a charge into a target, steered through a pilot's goggles |
| **Before it** | Big military drones such as Turkey's Bayraktar TB2, 12 metres across |
| **After it** | Drones that lock on to a target and finish alone |

## Why it mattered

The pilot does not watch the drone. Instead, goggles show the pilot, live, what its camera sees, so it is flown as if from a seat in its nose. That is what the hobbyists' name for it, first-person view, means. The flight goes one way: it ends in the target, and the charge goes off.

It is not a reliable weapon. A British defence institute reported in 2024 that only about one in five reached a target, because of cheap parts, the skill a pilot needs, and enemy jamming. Most of the parts came from China, which tightened its exports of some of them in 2023. The drone's strength is its price: a hundred misses cost less than one tank.
