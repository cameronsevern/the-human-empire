1. **Doubtful explanation of backward recitation — line 25.**

   > “A pattern recited backwards also makes no sense as a sentence.”
   > “The reciter cannot be carried along by the meaning, and so he has to know each word for itself.”

   Reversing Sanskrit words does not necessarily destroy their meaning: its inflections permit considerable freedom of word order. The braid interrupts ordinary continuous recitation, but that is different from making meaning unavailable. [Kulkarni and colleagues](https://sanskrit.uohyd.ac.in/faculty/amba/PUBLICATIONS/papers/sktsynOffprintAKulkarnietal.pdf) demonstrate grammatical rearrangements, including reversed order.

   The chapter supplies a better explanation: “Whenever the order of two words is switched, moreover, the sounds where they join have to be worked out afresh.” A [practitioner’s account, page 7](https://ghanapati.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/About-Vedas-and-Vedic-Recitation.pdf) likewise specifies the sound joins required throughout the braid. This also breaches the style rule against exception-breaking absolutes; the problem is the linguistic claim, not confidence of tone.

2. **The “After it” entry confuses analogy with descent — line 9.**

   > “The check digit, and every code that says a thing twice to catch a slip”

   Later the page says:

   > “Engineers, meanwhile, arrived at the same principle on their own.”

   These do not fit the design’s definition of **After it** as “what it turned into.” The prose describes independent invention, not a descendant of Vedic recitation. Also, a check digit encodes a calculated check rather than saying the original information twice. [GS1 explains](https://www.gs1ie.org/tools-services/tools/check-digit-calculator/) that it is calculated from the other digits. Present this as a modern parallel, not a historical succession.
