Title: THE MEMORISED VERSE. Date line: about 2,500 years ago. A clear teaching diagram of a method of memorising, not a picture of an object. At the left, small and quiet, an Indian teacher sits cross-legged on a mat, seen from the side, and a boy pupil sits cross-legged facing him; both wear a plain cloth wrap, their faces are not detailed, and there is no book or writing anywhere near them. The right two thirds of the sheet holds three rows of plain numbered blocks, like small tiles of unfired clay. Each block carries only a single digit and no other writing, and stands for one word of a verse. Top row: five separate blocks numbered 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 with gaps between them. Middle row: four joined pairs of smaller blocks reading 1 2, then 2 3, then 3 4, then 4 5. Bottom row: six joined pairs of smaller blocks reading 1 2, then 2 1, then 1 2, then a slightly wider gap, then 2 3, then 3 2, then 2 3, followed by three dots to show that the pattern goes on. Every block numbered 3, in all three rows, is the terracotta-accented part; every other block is pale bone colour. The digits must be exactly as given, in exactly that order. No Sanskrit, no Indian script, no religious symbols, no letters on the blocks. Place the labels in clear space so that no label overlaps a block, a figure or another label. Callouts: 1 'The teacher chants a line and the pupil chants it back'; 2 'The verse is learned again, one word at a time'; 3 'The step pattern: each word with the next'; 4 'The braid: each pair forwards, backwards, forwards'; 5 'Word three is said twice in the step pattern and six times in the braid'.
