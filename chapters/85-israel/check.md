I found the following issues in [chapter 85](chapters/85-israel/chapter.md), ranked roughly by seriousness. I found no clear direct contradiction with chapters 74, 82 or 84; one continuity problem with chapter 74 is noted below. No files were edited.

1. **Factual overstatement and an absolute — line 5.**  
   > “Israel was created in 1948 by a Jewish national movement that had been settling in Palestine for sixty years, after the murder of the Jews of Europe had left the survivors with nowhere else to go.”

   “Nowhere else” makes restricted alternatives into nonexistent ones. Between December 1945 and July 1948, the United States admitted approximately 35,000–40,000 displaced persons under Truman’s directive, most of them Jewish. The defensible claim is that many survivors could not safely return home and faced severe immigration barriers elsewhere. **Confidence: high.** [USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/united-states-immigration-and-refugee-law-1921-1980)

2. **Doubtful land-ownership statistic — line 51.**  
   > “They were two thirds of the population and owned more than nine tenths of the privately held land, and they were being offered less than half the country.”

   The private-land denominator needs checking. A UN historical study gives approximately 13 million dunams of Arab-owned land against 1.49 million Jewish-owned dunams in 1945: roughly 90%, but not *more* than 90%, even before subsequent Jewish purchases. The familiar figure that Jews owned about 6–7% concerns **all Palestine**, including public land; its complement is not automatically Arab private ownership. Retain the population and partition figures, but substantiate or remove this ownership fraction. **Confidence: medium-high that the stated fraction is unreliable; exact replacement depends on date and land categories.** [UN land-ownership study](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-208638/)

3. **Factual error with consequences for the partition argument — line 45.**  
   > “The plan gave the Jewish state fifty-six per cent of the land, including most of the coast and the empty southern desert.”

   The Negev was sparsely populated, not empty. UNSCOP explicitly counted approximately 90,000 Bedouins in the proposed Jewish state, describing them as cultivators and stock owners. “Sparsely populated southern desert” preserves the geographical point without erasing its inhabitants. **Confidence: high.** [UNSCOP report](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-179435/)

4. **Wrong date and geographical coverage — line 33.**  
   > “In the summer of 1945 there were about a quarter of a million Jews in camps in Germany and Austria, guarded by Allied soldiers.”

   Approximately 250,000 is the **1947** peak, covering Germany, Austria **and Italy**. JDC records approximately 75,000 in those countries’ camps by late 1945, followed by a large influx from eastern Europe. This also means the next sentence’s account of who the refugees were needs room for Jews returning from wartime refuge in the Soviet Union. **Confidence: high.** [JDC Archives](https://archives.jdc.org/topic-guides/jdc-in-the-displaced-persons-dp-camps-1945-1957/), [USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/displaced-persons-administration)

5. **Wrong person in the account of Haifa’s evacuation — line 63.**  
   > “Its mayor and its wealthier families had left during the winter, as such families had done in earlier troubles, expecting to come back when things were calm.”

   Haifa’s mayor was **Shabtai Levy**, a Jew who remained and appealed to Arab residents to stay. Some Arab leaders and wealthy families departed, but the mayor cannot be included among them. **Confidence: high.** [Contemporary report of Levy’s appointment](https://www.jta.org/archive/jew-replaces-arab-as-mayor-of-haifa), [*Haifa Before & After 1948*](https://ihjr.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Haifa-Before-and-After-1948.pdf)

6. **Factual error and prohibited absolute — line 23.**  
   > “There was only one place where the state could be.”

   Herzl himself considered alternatives. *The Jewish State* contains a section explicitly weighing **Palestine against Argentina**. Palestine’s historical and religious importance explains why it prevailed; it does not make alternatives nonexistent. **Confidence: high.** [Herzl’s text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/25282/25282-h/25282-h.htm)

7. **Two separate Iraqi laws collapsed into one — line 97.**  
   > “A hundred and twenty thousand left Iraq, after a law that allowed them to go on condition that they gave up their citizenship and their possessions.”

   The March **1950** law required relinquishing citizenship. A separate measure in March **1951** froze the assets of Jews who had registered to leave. Presenting property confiscation as an original condition conceals the sequence in which people surrendered citizenship and subsequently lost control of their assets. **Confidence: high.** [UN account of the legislation](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-210477/)

8. **The final image uses the wrong calendar — line 107.**  
   > “On the following day, in the camps of Lebanon and Gaza and the towns of the West Bank, people walk in procession and hold up the keys.”

   Nakba Day is **15 May**. Israeli Independence Day follows **5 Iyar in the Hebrew calendar**, with adjustments around the Sabbath. They therefore do not normally occur on successive days. This is worth correcting because the closing scene depends on the relationship between the dates. **Confidence: high.** [Knesset](https://m.knesset.gov.il/en/about/pages/independence.aspx), [Palestinian Nakba commemoration at the UN](https://www.un.org/unispal/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/15-May-2025-Presidents-Statement-Nakba-English-Final-.pdf)

9. **Wrong places and season — line 73.**  
   > “The fighting ended in a series of armistices, signed that spring on a Greek island.”

   The agreements extended from **February to July 1949**, and were not all signed on Rhodes. Lebanon’s was signed at **Ras en Naqoura on 23 March**; Syria’s was signed near the Israeli–Syrian frontier on **20 July**. Egypt’s and Jordan’s were signed on Rhodes. **Confidence: high.** [UN treaty record for Lebanon](https://treaties.un.org/pages/showDetails.aspx?objid=080000028015c467), [Syria agreement record](https://www.ecf.org.il/issues/issue/174)

10. **Anachronistic city — line 95.**  
    > “Jews had lived in Baghdad since the Babylonian exile, twenty-five centuries before, and in Egypt, Yemen and Morocco since before the coming of Islam.”

    The ancient continuity belongs to **Babylonia/Mesopotamia**, not Baghdad. Baghdad was founded as the Abbasid capital in **762 CE**. Replace the city with the region. **Confidence: high.** [Cambridge history of Baghdad](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-world-history/baghdad-an-imperial-foundation-762836ce/BF772E2AAF4E5677A881AB43FFBB79F2)

11. **Invented Ottoman administrative unit — line 23.**  
    > “It was then a quiet province of the Turkish empire called Palestine, with about half a million inhabitants.”

    Palestine was a geographical region, not a single Ottoman province. In Herzl’s period, Jerusalem was administered separately, while Acre and Nablus belonged to Beirut province. “A region of the Ottoman empire” avoids giving readers the wrong institutional picture. **Confidence: high.** [Alan Dowty, *Arabs and Jews in Ottoman Palestine*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Dowty-Alan-Arabs-and-Jews-in-Ottoman-Palestine_-Two-Worlds-Collide-Indiana-University-Press-2019.pdf)

12. **Doubtful conversion story presented as settled — line 19.**  
    > “He concluded that if they were not safe there, they would never be safe anywhere as a minority.”

    In context, this presents the Dreyfus degradation and the crowd’s cries as the decisive cause of Herzl’s Zionism. That familiar account is disputed by historians; his developing views and experience of Austrian antisemitism cannot safely be reduced to this single revelation. The problem is the asserted causal sequence, not the confidence or political position of the sentence. **Confidence: high that the causal account is disputed; medium on the precise role of the Dreyfus episode.** [University of Haifa Herzl research publication](https://herzl.haifa.ac.il/images/2023-HRZL-PDF-web.pdf)

13. **Overstated originality; continuity problem with chapter 74 — line 17.**  
    > “The man in the portrait was Theodor Herzl, a newspaper correspondent from Vienna, and the state had been his idea.”

    Jewish national restoration had advocates before Herzl, including Moses Hess and Leon Pinsker. Herzl’s distinctive achievement was organising and internationalising political Zionism. Chapter 74 has already introduced Jewish settlement beginning in the 1880s; this formulation makes the reader wonder how that movement preceded its supposed inventor. This is a misleading simplification rather than a strict contradiction between chapters. **Confidence: high.** [Historical introduction accompanying Herzl’s text](https://home.uncg.edu/~jwjones/islamicworld/readings/herzl.html)

14. **Wrong duration and prohibited absolute — line 25.**  
    > “A scholar in Jerusalem had resolved that the new nation must speak Hebrew, which had been a language of prayer and study, used in daily life by nobody, for two thousand years.”

    Hebrew remained a spoken vernacular into the early centuries CE, and continued to have occasional spoken and intercommunity uses before its modern revival. The distinction needed here is between a **community’s everyday mother tongue** and a language nobody spoke outside prayer or study. “Nobody” and “two thousand years” both overstate the case. **Confidence: high.** [Academy of the Hebrew Language](https://eng.hebrew-academy.org.il/overview-of-hebrew/hebrew-through-the-ages/), [Jewish Language Project](https://www.jewishlanguages.org/hebrew)

15. **Incorrect description of the convoy’s dead — line 61.**  
    > “Seventy-eight doctors, nurses and patients were shot or burned to death.”

    The 78 included university personnel and Haganah fighters as well as medical staff and patients. The death toll is defensible; assigning all the dead to those three categories is not. “Seventy-eight people” would avoid the error. **Confidence: high.** [Israel’s heritage-site account of the Hadassah convoy](https://shimur.org/signs/%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%93%D7%A1%D7%94/)

16. **Reader loses track of the armed organisations — line 59.**  
    > “On 9 April about a hundred and twenty fighters from the two extreme underground groups attacked Deir Yassin, a village of stonecutters on a hill at the western edge of Jerusalem.”

    The reader has encountered “an underground army,” “the Jewish underground,” “the most extreme of its groups,” and “Jewish forces,” but has not been told how these relate. “The two” consequently points to a distinction never established. Briefly distinguish the main Haganah militia from Irgun and Lehi before this passage; the distinction also explains why “the main Jewish leadership” could condemn the attack.

17. **Geography needs explanation or the planned map — line 45.**  
    > “The Arab state would have forty-three per cent, in three pieces joined at their corners.”

    Readers have no way to picture those pieces or understand how such a state would function. Name their broad locations—western Galilee, the central hills and the southern coastal area—and ensure the outline’s partition map is present. The later Jerusalem supply-road fighting likewise needs its relationship to the Jewish coastal settlements made explicit.

18. **Signpost sentence — line 85.**  
    > “The decision that mattered most is not in dispute.”

    This announces the importance and evidential status of the next sentence instead of stating the decision. It falls under style.md’s prohibition on announcing that a point is coming. Begin directly with the cabinet’s decision, with whatever chronological precision the account needs.
