# Chapter 85 — How was Israel created?

**Line under the title:** Palestine and Israel, from 1896 to 1949.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. The same events are a founding to one people and a catastrophe to another; tell both from the ground, in the same plain voice, and state disputes as disputes.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Israel was created in 1948 by a Jewish national movement that had been building a society in Palestine for sixty years, at the moment when the murder of Europe's Jews had left the survivors with nowhere to go and the world unwilling to refuse them. The United Nations voted to divide the country. The Arabs of Palestine, who were two thirds of its people, refused to give up more than half of it, and lost the war that followed. About seven hundred thousand of them fled or were driven out, and the new state did not let them return. One people remembers 1948 as independence and the other as the catastrophe, and both are describing the same year.

## The story

1. **Opening: thirty-two minutes.** Answer first. Tel Aviv, 14 May 1948, four in the afternoon.
2. **A journalist in Paris.** Herzl, the idea of a Jewish state, and a prediction in a diary. A language brought back to life; a town on the dunes.
3. **Survivors.** 1945: the camps of displaced persons; a closed door; a ship turned back. A hotel blown up. Britain gives up.
4. **Thirty-three to thirteen.** The United Nations plan and how each side saw it.
5. **The roads.** The civil war of the winter. A village on the edge of Jerusalem in April; a convoy four days later. Haifa empties.
6. **Five armies.** The war after 15 May: who had the numbers, and what the kings wanted. The armistice lines.
7. **Keys.** The seven hundred thousand: how they left, town by town, and the argument about why. The decision that they would not return. Those who stayed.
8. **The other exodus.** The Jews of the Arab countries. A state that doubled in three years.
9. **Ending: two words for one year.**

## Left for later

The wars of 1956, 1967 and 1973. The occupation and the Palestinian national movement. Oil and the superpowers.
