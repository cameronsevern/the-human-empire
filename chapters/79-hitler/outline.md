# Chapter 79 — How did Hitler come to power?

**Line under the title:** Germany, from 1918 to 1939.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Hitler did not seize power and was not voted into it by a majority. He was appointed chancellor, legally, by conservative politicians who thought that they could use him. The Depression had made his party the largest in Germany, with a third of the vote, and the republic it faced had few defenders: it was born of a defeat that many Germans refused to believe in, its elites despised it, and its left was split. Once in office he needed six months. An emergency decree after a fire suspended civil rights, a law passed under armed guard let him rule without parliament, and by the summer of 1933 every other party, every union and every free newspaper was gone. Most Germans went along, because there was work again, because the radio told them one story, and because the price was paid by other people.

## The story

1. **Opening: torches.** Answer first. Berlin, 30 January 1933: a parade past the chancellery window, and a politician's boast that he has hired the new chancellor.
2. **A corporal in hospital.** Hitler before politics: a failed painter in Vienna, a soldier, the news of the armistice. The legend of the stab in the back.
3. **The beer hall.** A new party, a talent for speaking, a failed uprising in 1923, and a comfortable prison where he wrote down what he meant to do.
4. **Twenty governments.** The republic: its constitution, its enemies in the army and the courts, its good years on borrowed money. Two and a half votes in a hundred.
5. **Work and bread.** The Depression. Six million unemployed and the Nazi vote. Who voted for him and who did not. An election campaign by aeroplane. Why his opponents did not combine.
6. **"We have hired him."** The intrigue of January 1933.
7. **For the protection of people and state.** Six months: the fire, the decree, the last election, the vote in the opera house, the first camp, the unions, the books.
8. **The people's receiver.** Radio and film. Jobs and bloodless victories. Neighbours who informed. A forged book.
9. **Grandparents.** The Jews of Germany: the laws of 1935, the night of November 1938, and the conference where no country would take them.
10. **Ending: a pastor.** The words of a clergyman who had voted for him.
