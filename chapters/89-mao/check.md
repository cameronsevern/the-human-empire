1. **Factual error; absolute — high confidence.** “In 1946 the government had four times as many soldiers and all the American weapons it could use.”

   American support was substantial, but weapons were not unlimited: Washington suspended arms shipments from July 1946 until May 1947 while trying to broker peace. Keep the military advantage; remove the claim of unrestricted supply. [US State Department memorandum, 1948](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1948v07/d373).

2. **Factual error; internal contradiction; absolute — high confidence.** “For the first time in forty years, one authority ruled the whole country, and its officials did not take bribes.”

   Both halves overreach. The preceding section establishes that Taiwan remained under Chiang. Communist officials also committed corruption: the party’s own November 1951 report documents embezzlement, illicit business dealings and gifts to officials in the Liu Qingshan–Zhang Zishan case. Stronger central control and a campaign against corruption are defensible claims; complete territorial unity and incorruptibility are not. [Central Committee report](https://commonprogram.science/documents/Corruption%20Case%20.pdf).

3. **Factual error affecting the explanation — high confidence.** “The Communists had no navy with which to follow him.”

   They had established a navy in April 1949, initially using a few former Nationalist warships. What they lacked was sufficient capability to conquer Taiwan. For the subsequent survival of Chiang’s government, American intervention in the strait from June 1950 also matters. [Chinese Ministry of National Defense’s founding account](https://eng.mod.gov.cn/xb/News_213114/NewsRelease/4884413.html); [US Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1953-1960/taiwan-strait-crises).

4. **Factual error and direct contradiction with chapter 75 — high confidence.** “In 1921 he was one of thirteen delegates who met in a girls’ school in Shanghai to found the Chinese Communist Party.”

   The congress met at Li Hanjun’s residence; several delegates **lodged** at Bowen Girls’ School. Chapter 75 correctly says they “met in a house in the French quarter of Shanghai.” The school and meeting venue have been conflated. [Shanghai’s official account, distinguishing both buildings](https://www.meet-in-shanghai.net/en/guide/quintessential-ishikumeni-buildings-woven-into-the-fabric-of-modern-chinese-history-215130/).

5. **Chronological and geographical error — high confidence.** “An American journalist reached them there in 1936 and wrote a book that described a band of cheerful, honest young men who shared everything and meant to fight Japan.”

   “There” refers to Yan’an, but Edgar Snow visited the Communist headquarters at **Bao’an** in 1936. The headquarters moved to Yan’an in January 1937. This also affects “The survivors settled around the dusty town of Yan’an” and the December 1936 campaign described as being “against Yan’an.” Distinguish the initial northwestern refuge from the later capital. [National Museum of China’s account of Snow’s visit](https://www2.chnmuseum.cn/collections_577/collection_highlights_608/objects_of_chinese_revolutionary_history_610/201911/t20191121_172651.html); [headquarters chronology](https://chinesehistoryforteachers.omeka.net/exhibits/show/chinese-communist-party/chinese-communist-party-overvi).

6. **Factual error; absolute — high confidence.** “An army of several million had to be kept in the field with no industry and little revenue.”

   Nationalist China lost much of its industrial base, but retained inland industry and relocated coastal factories into the interior. It also developed new wartime enterprises. “No industry” erases a significant part of the war effort; “much of its industry lost” preserves the point. [National Memorial account of wartime economic policy](https://www.cngongji.cn/english/2015-11/02/c_134776109.htm).

7. **Misleading claim of a new right — high confidence.** “A woman could now ask for a divorce, and in the next few years more than a million did.”

   Women already had the legal right to initiate divorce under the Nationalist Civil Code, implemented in 1931, although enforcement and access were limited. The 1950 law changed the grounds, reach and practical politics of divorce; it did not introduce women’s legal ability to petition for it. [Gail Hershatter, “Regulatory Regimes, 1928–37,” p. 131](https://cnm2606.yilu.org/files/readings/s07/hershatter-regulatory-regimes.pdf).

8. **Overcompressed leadership chronology — high confidence on the distinction.** “During the march Mao became the leader of the party, and he remained so until his death.”

   The march greatly strengthened Mao, but did not immediately establish the uncontested leadership implied here. Zhang Wentian took overall responsibility for the party, while military leadership was shared. Mao’s supremacy developed over subsequent years. Chapter 75 uses a similar compression, so this is a shared problem rather than a contradiction. [Academy of Chinese Studies account](https://chiculture.org.hk/index.php/en/photo-story/2780); [scholarly discussion of Zunyi as one stage in Mao’s rise](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/abs/zunyi-conference-as-one-step-in-maos-rise-to-power-a-survey-of-historical-studies-of-the-chinese-communist-party/21CD8FB5BA16B3BD0BE8537463C4913C).

9. **Misleading military generalisation — medium confidence on the generalisation; high on the counterexample.** “In April the Communists crossed the Yangtze, and the great cities of the south fell one after another, usually without a shot.”

   “Usually” allows exceptions, but the sentence leaves out the conspicuous one: Shanghai, China’s largest city, required a sixteen-day campaign in May, including fierce fighting. Describe the rapid collapse while acknowledging Shanghai, rather than leaving the reader with an essentially bloodless southern conquest. [Shanghai Archives Bureau account](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Latest-WhatsNew/20240527/7374bb72330d4d9e9efef7ad5e67e636.html).

10. **Factual overstatement; absolute — high confidence.** “By 1956 the government had gathered the fields of every village into collective farms, and the deeds were waste paper.”

    Collectivisation was overwhelmingly extensive, but not universal. By the end of 1956, about 96 percent of peasant households had joined cooperatives; about 88 percent were in the advanced cooperatives that replaced individual landownership with collective ownership. “Every village” also obscures that distinction. [University of California Press, *China’s Continuous Revolution*](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/china-s-continuous-revolution-the-post-liberation-epoch-1949-1981.html).

11. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.** “The Communists had been slaughtered because they had no army.”

    They lacked a sufficiently strong, independent party army, but Communist officers already commanded troops within the Nationalist revolutionary army—notably Ye Ting’s regiment. Chapter 75 has already introduced Communist participation in the military academy and Northern Expedition, making the categorical wording especially confusing. [Mao’s own retrospective discussion and its notes on Ye Ting’s regiment](https://openstax.marxists.info/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-2/mswv2_12.htm).

12. **Unverified scene presented as representative fact — medium confidence; provenance needed.** “Then someone would begin: an old woman whose daughter he had taken in payment of a debt, a man whose father had been beaten to death for a missed rent.”

    These are specific accusations embedded in an otherwise generic reconstruction. The reader cannot tell whether they come from a documented meeting, different villages, or an invented composite. Under the style guide’s insistence on reliable concrete examples, use one traceable village account or remove the particular biographies. I cannot establish their provenance from the supplied chapter and outline; this is a verification gap, not a finding that such abuses never occurred.

13. **Reader loses the causal thread.** “The Communists, meanwhile, had gone back to the weapon that they had laid aside during the war with Japan.”

    The reader must infer both that “the weapon” means land confiscation and when the policy changed. More importantly, land redistribution is the opening explanation for victory, yet the wartime expansion just described occurred while confiscation had stopped. State when redistribution resumed and briefly connect the earlier rent reductions, organisation and wartime resistance to the support already accumulated.

14. **Unexplained term at the ending; style guide’s plain-language rule.** “By 1956 the government had gathered the fields of every village into collective farms, and the deeds were waste paper.”

    Beyond the factual overstatement above, “collective farms” is never explained. A general reader needs one sentence establishing that families lost individual ownership and farmed jointly under cooperative management. Otherwise the final reversal—the destruction of the ownership promise that helped win the revolution—lands before its mechanism is clear.

I found no clear contradictions with chapters 80 or 88, and no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-references, prohibited cross-references or clear announcement-style signpost sentences. No files were edited.
