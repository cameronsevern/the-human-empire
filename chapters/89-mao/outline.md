# Chapter 89 — Why did China become communist?

**Line under the title:** China, from 1927 to 1952.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

China became communist because the war with Japan destroyed the government that the Communists were fighting, and because the Communists offered the peasants, who were four fifths of the country, the one thing they wanted, which was land. The Nationalists lost their best soldiers, their richest provinces and their honesty in eight years of war, and paid their bills by printing money until it was worthless. The Communists spent those years behind the Japanese lines, building an army out of villagers. When the two met in 1946 the Nationalists had four times the soldiers and American weapons, and they collapsed in three years. The new government ended the inflation and the warlords, gave the land to those who farmed it, and killed perhaps two million people in doing so.

## The story

1. **Opening: a balcony.** Answer first. Beijing, 1 October 1949.
2. **A librarian's assistant.** Mao: a farmer's son, a new party, a report from the countryside, and a conclusion about guns.
3. **The long way round.** The base in the hills, the encirclement, a year's retreat, the caves of the north-west. An American journalist's book.
4. **Kidnapped.** December 1936: a general arrests his own leader to make him fight Japan.
5. **Bleeding.** What the war with Japan did to the Nationalists: lost armies, a flood, press gangs, a famine, a tax paid in grain.
6. **Not a needle or a thread.** What the Communists did in the same years: guerrillas, rules for soldiers, rent cuts, and a campaign to remake the minds of the party.
7. **The transport officer.** The civil war: Soviet tanks in Manchuria, an American mediator, an army that sold its guns, money by the brick.
8. **Speak bitterness.** Land reform in a village: the meeting, the division, and why it won the war. Wheelbarrows.
9. **Across the strait.** 1949: the cities fall, Chiang leaves for Taiwan with the gold and the palace treasures. "Who lost China?"
10. **New China.** The first three years: the marriage law, the end of inflation and opium, the land deeds, the executions.
11. **Ending: a deed with a red seal.**
