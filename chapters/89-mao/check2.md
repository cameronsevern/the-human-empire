## Factual flags

1. **Major — newly introduced claim about landlord alliances.**
   
   > “There was no longer any need to keep the landlords friendly.”
   
   Communist policy still sought cooperation from selected landlords after the war against Japan. Mao’s directive of 1 March 1948 expressly advocated maintaining alliances with sympathetic landlords and rich peasants who supported land reform, partly to win other social groups and isolate Chiang.
   
   **Correct fact:** The Communists resumed confiscation but continued cultivating selected landlord allies. Ending the wartime protection of landlord property did not end every political reason to retain their cooperation. [Mao, “On the Question of the National Bourgeoisie and the Enlightened Gentry”](https://marxists.info/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-4/mswv4_32.htm).

2. **Minor — new wording reinforces an existing geographical overstatement.**
   
   > “What had been done in the northern villages was now done everywhere. Land reform was carried to the whole country.”
   
   “Everywhere” and “the whole country” erase explicit regional exceptions. Article 36 of the 1950 Agrarian Reform Law excluded national-minority areas from its general application; Article 35 also provided separate arrangements for areas adjoining large cities.
   
   **Correct fact:** Land reform spread across most of rural China, with regional exceptions and differing arrangements. It was not one uniform operation applied everywhere. [1950 Agrarian Reform Law, Articles 35–38](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/Rural/AgrarianReformLawOfPRC-1950.pdf).

3. **Minor — existing factual error, not raised in the earlier check.**
   
   > “The Communists had a little over a million men and no aircraft.”
   
   They possessed aircraft, including former Japanese machines used by their aviation school established in March 1946. The distinction needed here is between possessing aircraft and having an effective combat air force.
   
   **Correct fact:** The Communists lacked an effective operational air force comparable to Chiang’s, but did not lack aircraft altogether. [Chinese Ministry of National Defense’s account of the Northeast Aviation School](https://www.mod.gov.cn/gfbw/gfjy_index/16128976.html).

## Clarity flags

- **Unsupported explanation of the inflation figure:**  
  > “At that pace, money lost its value faster than people could spend it.”
  
  A tenfold price increase over roughly ninety days corresponds to an average daily increase of about 2.6%. That is disastrous inflation, but the stated figure alone does not establish that money became unusable between payday and noon. The next sentence already supplies the vivid behaviour; this addition makes the numerical explanation less precise.

- **An announcement before the explanation:**  
  > “The reasoning was cold and simple.”
  
  This announces the reasoning that the next sentences then provide. It falls under the style sheet’s prohibition on signpost sentences.

- **Vague forward reference:**  
  > “The party that came out of it was disciplined and obedient to one man, which would matter a great deal once it held power.”
  
  The added clause promises later significance without explaining it. “Would matter a great deal” is padding and functions as an implicit “as we will see.”

- **Unclear referent and change of subject:**  
  > “It was also enormous. In 1937 the Communists had about forty thousand soldiers.”
  
  “It” refers grammatically to the party, but the evidence measures the army and the population governed. Party membership, military strength, and territorial reach are different things.

- **Immediate repetition:**  
  > “What had been done in the northern villages was now done everywhere. Land reform was carried to the whole country.”
  
  These sentences perform the same job consecutively, besides the factual problem above.

- **Repeated explanatory formula:**  
  > “A defeat, in other words, was turned into a story of triumph.”  
  > “The American aid, in other words, was arming the other side.”  
  > “Killing, in other words, had become a matter of planning.”
  
  All three newly added sentences use the same summarising device after concrete evidence. Individually they are intelligible; collectively they give the revision a repetitive explanatory rhythm.

- **Metaphor that could read as medical fact:**  
  > “A disease of the skin can wait; a disease of the heart cannot.”
  
  The preceding sentence clearly attributes the metaphor to Chiang; this addition sounds like the narrator endorsing its literal premise. Its intended function is to explain Chiang’s priorities.

I found no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-mentions, or prohibited chapter cross-references. Most of the added transitions satisfy the style sheet’s instruction to give readers room.

## Overall

The revision preserves the original factual particulars, but it is not entirely fact-neutral: several connecting sentences introduce broader causal or absolute claims. The landlord-alliance assertion needs correction, and the nationwide land-reform wording needs narrowing. The main stylistic weakness is repeated explanation after evidence that already makes the point.
