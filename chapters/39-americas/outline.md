# Chapter 39 — What was America like before Columbus?

**Line under the title:** Central America, the Andes and the Mississippi valley, from about the year 250 to about 1400.
**Length:** 4,400 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The Americas held tens of millions of people, with cities, kings, wars, writing, a calendar and a zero, all of it worked out with no contact with the rest of the world. That makes the Americas the nearest thing history has to a second experiment, and the result is that farming people left to themselves build much the same things: states, taxes, monuments, records. They built them here with a different kit. There were no horses, cattle, sheep or pigs, so there was no plough, no cart and no cavalry, and every load moved on a human back, a llama or a canoe. The Maya wrote the day of a king's death in stone; Andean states kept their accounts on knotted strings and stored potatoes dried by frost; a town on the Mississippi raised a hill of earth by the basketload. When the Maya cities of the southern forest were abandoned, the Maya themselves did not vanish. Millions of them still speak the language.

## The story

1. **Opening: the day a king died.** Answer first. A stone that records the capture and beheading of a king in 738. How the writing was read, and what readers had believed before.
2. **Three sisters.** Maize, beans and squash, and the trick with lime. What the Americas did not have: animals to pull, ride or milk, and iron.
3. **Holy lords.** The Maya cities in the forest and what the laser surveys found. Kings who gave their own blood. Dozens of states and no empire. The great city to the west whose name nobody knows.
4. **A shell for nothing.** The calendar, the count of days, and a sign for zero. The books that were burned.
5. **The last date.** The cities fall silent between 800 and 900. Drought, war, worn-out land, and kings who could not bring rain. The people who stayed.
6. **Potatoes left out in the frost.** The Andes: a country stacked on its side. Freeze-dried food, llamas, storehouses. Accounts kept in knots.
7. **Fifteen million baskets.** The earth city on the Mississippi.
8. **Ending: a toy on wheels.**
