# Chapter 99 — Why did Iran have an Islamic revolution?

**Line under the title:** Iran, Afghanistan and the Muslim world, from 1963 to 1989.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Iran had a revolution because its king, put back on his throne by a foreign coup, spent twenty-five years forcing the country to become modern at speed with oil money and a secret police, and closed every outlet for complaint except one. The mosque was the only institution he could not shut. When almost every class of Iranian turned against him in 1978, it was an exiled cleric who spoke for them all, and once the king had gone that cleric and his followers pushed aside their liberal and socialist allies and built something new: a modern state ruled by a religious jurist. The same year a mosque was seized in Mecca and the Soviet army entered Afghanistan. Together those events made religion the main language of politics across the Muslim world, in forms that disagreed with one another and often fought.

## The story

1. **Opening: "Nothing."** Answer first. An aircraft from Paris, 1 February 1979.
2. **The king of kings.** The shah after 1953: reform from above, oil, a secret police, a party in the desert. An "island of stability".
3. **The cleric.** Khomeini in 1963 and 1964: a speech about an American dog. Exile. What Shia clergy were and why the king could not shut them down. A new doctrine in a lecture hall.
4. **Forty days.** 1978: an insulting article, a cycle of mourning, a cinema fire, a square in September, cassette tapes, the oil workers.
5. **Everyone's revolution.** Who marched and what each wanted. The king leaves.
6. **The guardian.** How the clerics won: a referendum, courts, a guard, a constitution. The embassy: 444 days. The veil.
7. **Poison.** The war with Iraq: eight years, gas, a million casualties, an airliner. What it did for the regime.
8. **The solution.** The wider turn to religion in politics: a schoolteacher in Egypt in 1928, a writer hanged in 1966, the defeat of 1967, Saudi money. Mecca, November 1979. Sunni and Shia.
9. **The bleeding wound.** Afghanistan: a coup, an invasion at Christmas, the villages, the refugees, the paymasters, the foreign volunteers, a bridge in 1989.
10. **Ending: hair.**
