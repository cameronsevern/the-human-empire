I found no direct contradictions with chapters 97–98, and no hedging stacks or explicit chapter cross-references. The substantive findings below are ranked by seriousness. Line numbers refer to [chapter 99](chapters/99-iran/chapter.md). No files were edited.

1. **The central explanation makes the mosque both exclusive and untouchable.**  
   > “Iran had a revolution because its king spent twenty-five years forcing the country to become modern at great speed, with oil money and a secret police, and closed every outlet for complaint except the mosque.”  
   > “One kind of institution lay beyond the shah’s reach.”  
   
   **Lines 5, 27 — factual overstatement and style.** Religious networks retained unusual independence and became crucial to mobilisation, but secular opposition survived through universities, writers, lawyers and professional organisations. Religious institutions also suffered direct repression: security forces attacked Qom’s principal seminary in 1963. The defensible distinction is their resilience and reach, not immunity or a monopoly on dissent. **Confidence: high.** Sources: [Library of Congress country study](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/ir/irancountrystudy00curt_0/irancountrystudy00curt_0.pdf), [Encyclopaedia Iranica on the bazaar and opposition](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bazar-index/bazar-iii/).

2. **The war narrative omits the decision that explains six of its eight years.**  
   > “The war lasted eight years, longer than either of the world wars, and it resembled the first of them.”  
   
   **Line 71 — consequential omission.** The surrounding account moves directly from Iraq’s invasion to Iranian defensive sacrifice and eventual ceasefire. By summer 1982 Iran had recovered most occupied territory; its leaders then carried the war into Iraq, seeking, among other conditions, Saddam’s removal. Without this turn, readers cannot understand why accepting peace was so bitter for Khomeini. **Confidence: high.** Source: [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Iran–Iraq War”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iraq-vii-iran-iraq-war/).

3. **The present-tense claim about Iran’s leader is outdated.**  
   > “The supreme leader who succeeded Khomeini has held the post for more than thirty-five years.”  
   
   **Line 105 — factual error as of November 2026.** Ali Khamenei died on 28 February 2026; Mojtaba Khamenei was selected as his successor in March. Either give the epilogue an explicit earlier date or update this sentence. **Confidence: high.** Sources: [AP reporting on Ali Khamenei’s death](https://apnews.com/article/a689242aa635c103033790bacbff7f07), [report of the succession](https://www.euronews.com/2026/03/08/kuwait-city-tower-on-fire-as-iranian-air-strikes-continue-across-gulf-countries).

4. **Afghanistan’s chronology jumps over the surviving government.**  
   > “The Afghans who had beaten him then turned their guns on one another.”  
   
   **Line 101 — misleading sequence.** After the Soviet withdrawal in February 1989, the insurgents continued fighting Najibullah’s Soviet-supported government, which survived until April 1992. Factional fighting existed earlier, but the struggle among victorious factions for Kabul belongs chiefly after that collapse. The sentence makes withdrawal, victory and the subsequent struggle appear simultaneous. **Confidence: high.** Source: [Congressional Research Service, Afghanistan background](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R45818.html).

5. **The new constitution is described inaccurately.**  
   > “A council of clerics would decide who was permitted to stand for election and could strike down any law.”  
   
   **Line 59 — factual error and overcompression.** The Guardian Council contains six religious jurists and six other legal specialists, not twelve clerics. Its legislative review concerns compatibility with Islam and the constitution. Its extensive candidate-vetting system also developed over time; the important expansive interpretation of electoral supervision came in 1991. The sentence projects the mature system straight into 1979. **Confidence: high.** Sources: [constitutional Articles 91–99](https://constituteproject.org/constitution/Iran_1989), [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Guardian Council”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/guardian-council/).

6. **The account of who armed Iraq leaves a materially false impression.**  
   > “The Arab oil states paid for Iraq’s weapons, and the Western powers, which feared Iran more, supplied them and looked away from the gas.”  
   
   **Line 71 — misleading generalisation.** Western support, including major French arms sales, belongs here. But the Soviet Union was Iraq’s largest arms supplier, with China and other communist states also important. Presenting the supply relationship simply as Arab money buying Western weapons obscures a central feature of the war. **Confidence: high.** Sources: [CSIS study of wartime arms supplies](https://csis-website-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/legacy_files/files/media/csis/pubs/9005lessonsiraniraqii-chap03.pdf), [SIPRI Yearbook 1987](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/SIPRI%20Yearbook%201987.pdf).

7. **The women’s protests are placed after the wrong event.**  
   > “Women were ordered to cover their hair, and when thousands marched against it they were attacked by gangs with knives.”  
   
   **Line 63 — chronology.** Following the hostage-crisis paragraph and “The new order then turned on its former allies,” this reads as an event after November 1979. The major protests began on 8 March 1979. Workplace enforcement followed in 1980; the penal law covering public noncompliance came in 1983. Distinguish the initial order and protests from subsequent nationwide enforcement. **Confidence: high.** Source: [Iran Human Rights Documentation Center, *Silencing the Women’s Rights Movement in Iran*](https://www.justice.gov/file/273001/dl?inline=).

8. **The destruction of the left is dated too early.**  
   > “Within three years the left-wing parties that had helped to make the revolution had been crushed, and several thousand of their members executed.”  
   
   **Line 63 — factual overstatement.** Extensive repression and executions occurred in 1981–82, but the Tudeh Party remained active until the major crackdown of 1983. “Within three years” cannot cover all the parties described. **Confidence: high.** Sources: [Amnesty International’s account](https://www.amnesty.org/zh-hans/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/mde130211990en.pdf), [contemporary Keesing’s chronology](https://web.stanford.edu/group/tomzgroup/pmwiki/uploads/2590-1984-K-a-RHR.pdf).

9. **The chemical-warfare comparison needs evidence or removal.**  
   > “Iraq used poison gas, which no army had done on that scale since 1918, against Iranian soldiers and against a Kurdish town of its own, where five thousand people died in an afternoon.”  
   
   **Line 71 — doubtful comparative claim.** Japan conducted more than 2,000 chemical attacks in China during 1937–45, producing many thousands of casualties. “On that scale” needs a defined measure and a defensible comparison; it risks erasing substantial intervening chemical warfare. Describing Iraq’s extensive use directly would preserve the point. **Confidence: high that the comparison needs substantiation; medium that it is false under every possible measure.** Source: [OPCW medical guide, historical overview](https://www.opcw.org/sites/default/files/documents/2019/05/Full%20version%202019_Medical%20Guide_WEB.pdf).

10. **Twelver belief is presented as the belief of all Shia Muslims.**  
    > “Shia Muslims hold that the rightful line of leaders ended with a twelfth, who vanished in the ninth century and will return at the end of time.”  
   
    **Line 29 — factual overgeneralisation.** This describes Twelver Shiism, Iran’s predominant tradition. Ismaili and Zaydi Shia have different accounts of the succession. Specify Iran’s Twelver Shia here. Also, “ended” is awkward for a doctrine in which the twelfth imam remains alive but hidden. **Confidence: high.** Source: [Encyclopaedia Iranica on Shia traditions](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/shiite-doctrine-iii/).

11. **Black Friday’s citywide death toll is assigned to one square.**  
    > “On 8 September, the morning after martial law was declared, soldiers fired on a crowd in a square in Tehran and killed about ninety people.”  
   
    **Line 41 — factual precision.** The often-cited reconstruction gives 64 deaths in Jaleh Square and 88 across Tehran that day. “About ninety” is defensible for the citywide toll, not specifically the square. **Confidence: high about the distinction; medium about any final exact count.** Source: [“Black Friday revisited,” discussing Baghi’s reconstruction](https://lup.lub.lu.se/search/files/234861949/Black_Friday_revisited_disinformation_misinformation_and_the_politics_of_memory_at_Tehran_s_Jaleh_Square.pdf).

12. **The reader never sees the monarchy actually fall.**  
    > “He appointed a mild, elderly engineer as prime minister, and for a few months it looked as if they were right.”  
   
    **Line 55 — missing explanatory step.** The shah’s departure did not immediately dissolve his government. Khomeini appointed a rival prime minister while the shah-appointed government still existed; the latter collapsed after armed fighting and the army’s declaration of neutrality on 11 February. One bridging sentence would explain how an arriving exile acquired the power exercised here. **Confidence: high.** Source: [Library of Congress country study](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/ir/irancountrystudy00curt_0/irancountrystudy00curt_0.pdf).

13. **The constitution appears settled before the embassy seizure.**  
    > “The constitution that followed was Khomeini’s lecture turned into law.”  
   
    **Line 59 — chronology and reader orientation.** The constitutional drafting process was underway before November, but the constitution was approved by referendum on 2–3 December 1979, after the embassy seizure. Its placement before “an event in November” suggests that legal consolidation was already complete. Supply the date or distinguish drafting from ratification. **Confidence: high.** Source: [US National Archives hostage-crisis chronology](https://www.archives.gov/research/foreign-policy/iran-hostage-crisis).

14. **Tehran’s growth has no timeframe and appears attached to the wrong one.**  
    > “Tehran grew from a city of one million to five.”  
   
    **Line 19 — reader confusion and potentially misleading statistic.** The preceding sentences establish 1963 and then the 1973 oil boom, inviting readers to place this fivefold growth within that period. Tehran already had about 2.7 million inhabitants in 1966 and 4.5 million in 1976. Give the longer interval intended, or use figures matching this paragraph’s timeframe. **Confidence: high.** Source: [research on the 1968 Tehran master plan](https://doi.org/10.1080/02665433.2018.1468805).

15. **The crowd superlative is an unresolved minor claim.**  
    > “It may have been the largest crowd that had ever gathered anywhere.”  
   
    **Line 7 — doubtful claim and style.** Earlier gatherings have larger reported estimates: about five million at the 1954 Kumbh Mela. **Confidence: medium on comparative crowd estimates; high that this record claim is insufficiently secure.** [Source](https://www.indiatoday.in/india-today-insight/story/how-railways-is-keeping-mahakumbh-rush-on-track-2665765-2025-01-16). Under “Settle doubt before writing,” retain the crowd’s scale and drop the speculative record. The problem is not the single hedge itself.

16. **Several universal statements break the explicit rule against exception-fragile absolutes.**  
    > “His secret police, trained by the Americans and the Israelis, had informers in every office and classroom, and tortured those it arrested.”  
    > “It was the work of religious militants, and the whole country believed that the secret police had done it.”  
    > “He was hanged in 1966, and his book has been read by every militant since.”  
   
    **Lines 21, 41, 79 — style.** “Every office and classroom,” “the whole country” and “every militant” make literal claims the narrative cannot sustain. The first sentence also implies that every arrested person was tortured. Widespread surveillance, torture, belief and influence can all be stated forcefully without universality.

17. **The Afghanistan transition is an outline signpost.**  
    > “The third event of that year took place in Iran’s eastern neighbour.”  
   
    **Line 89 — style and orientation.** It announces an event rather than telling it, contrary to “No signpost sentences.” “That year” also requires the reader to recover 1979 after a substantial historical detour. Start with Afghanistan and the date or event directly.

18. **The name needed to understand the ending of the Afghanistan section is withheld.**  
    > “They called it the Base.”  
   
    **Line 99 — reader confusion.** A general reader may not recognise that “the Base” translates *al-Qaeda*. Give the familiar name alongside the translation; otherwise the paragraph’s significance depends on knowledge the intended reader may not have. Likewise, “mullahs” at line 91 needs a brief explanation on first use, under the style guide’s rule for historical terms.
