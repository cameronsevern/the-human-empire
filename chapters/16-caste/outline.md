# Chapter 16 — Where did the caste system come from?

**Line under the title:** Northern India, from about 3,500 years ago to about 2,500 years ago.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. A living religion and a live social question: describe accurately, without mocking or endorsing, and stay out of modern politics.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The caste system came in two steps a thousand years apart. Herders descended from the steppe people came into the north-west of India about 3,500 years ago, composed hymns to their gods, and kept them by a method of memorising so exact that it worked like a recording. Their priests controlled the sacrifices, and about 3,000 years ago one hymn set out four ranked orders of society, made from the body of the first man, with priests at the top. As the hymn people moved east into the Ganges plain, with rice, iron, towns and kings, teachers asked what lay behind the ritual and arrived at a set of linked ideas: a self that is reborn, deeds that decide the next birth, duty according to one's place, and release from the round of births. Those ideas explained rank and made it deserved. Even so, ancient DNA shows that people went on marrying across the lines for a thousand years after the hymn. Strict marriage inside thousands of small birth groups set in about 1,900 years ago. The order that resulted needed no state and no church to run it, which is why it outlasted every empire. "Hinduism" as one name for these traditions is much later, and how far British rule hardened caste is debated.

## The story

1. **Opening: books without writing.** Answer first. The manuscripts in Pune, 560 years old, of hymns five times older. For most of their life the hymns existed only in memory.
2. **Cattle, horses and fire.** Who composed the hymns: herders of steppe descent in the Punjab. The Indus cities already empty. What the hymns ask for. No writing.
3. **Thirteen times each.** How the memorising worked: the run-together text, the word-by-word text, the pair pattern, the braid and the dense pattern, and why they act as an error check. Why exactness mattered: the wrong accent that killed the wrong party.
4. **The human gods.** The sacrifices grow; the horse set loose for a year; the bird altar of a thousand bricks; fees; priests as gods on earth.
5. **Mouth, arms, thighs, feet.** The hymn of the first man. Four orders, three of them twice-born, and the people placed below all four.
6. **East to the rice.** Fire carried east in a story; the Ganges plain, iron, rice, towns and kings.
7. **Salt in water.** The teachers of the Upanishads. The self, rebirth, deeds and their consequences, duty by place, release. How these ideas made rank deserved.
8. **A thousand years of mixing.** Who gained. Law books as wishes. Varna and jati. What ancient DNA shows: mixing until about 1,900 years ago, then walls. An order with no state behind it.
9. **Two late names.** "Caste" is Portuguese, "Hinduism" is about two centuries old. The British census and the debate. Close on those who walked away from the priests.
