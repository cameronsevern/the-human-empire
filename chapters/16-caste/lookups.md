# Chapter 16 fact-check lookups

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. Quoted sentences are from the draft as checked; "Action" says what was changed.

## Opening and the hymns

1. "a set of thirty old manuscripts. Most are written on paper, and one is on the bark of a birch tree... The oldest of the thirty that carries a date was copied in 1464"
   - Verdict: correct on thirty, on birch bark and paper, and on 1464 (Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute, Pune; UNESCO Memory of the World 2007). "One is on birch bark" matches the UNESCO nomination (one Sharada birch-bark manuscript from Kashmir), not re-read this session.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda
   - Action: reworded to "some on paper and some on the bark of birch trees" and "copied about 560 years ago".

2. "a collection of just over a thousand hymns" / "a hundred thousand words and more"
   - Verdict: correct. 1,028 hymns, about 10,600 verses, 395,563 syllables in the metrical text; standard word counts are about 150,000 to 165,000.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda

3. "The earliest of them were composed some 3,500 years ago."
   - Verdict: correct as the usual dating (bulk c. 1500 to 1000 BCE; dating contested at the margins).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda

4. "a Muslim scholar who had learned Sanskrit... reported that the priests still did not allow their sacred text to be put into writing"
   - Verdict: correct. Al-Biruni (c. 1030), Sachau's translation: they do not "allow the Veda to be committed to writing" because it is "recited according to certain modulations". Checked through a secondary quotation of Sachau.
   - Source: https://thewire.in/books/alberunis-impressions-of-brahminical-society-in-india

5. "One of their words for a battle meant, literally, a wish for cattle."
   - Verdict: correct. Oldenberg's glossary: go-ishti / gavishti, "striving for cows", battle (RV 1.36.8).
   - Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/sbe46/sbe46145.htm

6. "Between about 4,000 and 3,500 years ago they came through the mountain passes into the north-west of India. The DNA of ancient skeletons along the route records their arrival."
   - Verdict: correct. Narasimhan et al. 2019: steppe ancestry moved south in the first half of the second millennium BCE; evidence from the Swat valley.
   - Source: https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/2019_Science_NarasimhanPatterson_CentralSouthAsia.pdf

7. "By the time the herders arrived, however, those cities had been empty for centuries."
   - Verdict: wrong as an absolute. Urban decline from about 1900 BCE, but Late Harappan occupation continued at Harappa and elsewhere.
   - Source: https://www.harappa.com/slide/changing-settlement-harappa
   - Action: rewritten as "the great cities had shrunk or emptied".

8. "Almost every community in India today descends from both."
   - Verdict: correct. Narasimhan 2019: Indus-related people mixed with steppe-descended groups to form ANI and with south-eastern groups to form ASI; most modern South Asians are mixtures of the two.
   - Source: same as 6.

9. "Nearly a quarter of the hymns are addressed to Indra... The next largest share goes to Agni"
   - Verdict: correct by the standard count (about 250 hymns to Indra, about 200 to Agni, of 1,028). Not re-confirmed by a page fetched this session; common knowledge in the field.

## The memorising

10. Word-by-word text, pairs (one-two, two-three), braid (one-two, two-one, one-two), dense pattern (one-two, two-one, one-two-three, three-two-one, one-two-three); "each arrangement checked the others".
    - Verdict: correct. Pada, krama (ab bc cd), jata, ghana; eleven modes in all; "the different methods of recitation acted as a cross check on the other".
    - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_chant
    - Note: "thirteen times" for a word in the middle of a verse in the dense pattern is arithmetic from the pattern (3 + 5 + 5).

11. "The Rigveda opens with the words agnim ile purohitam"
    - Verdict: correct (RV 1.1.1, agním īḷe puróhitaṃ; Jamison and Brereton: "Agni do I invoke, the one set in front").

12. "Families of reciters in the far south of India and in the north... chant the same words with the same pitch on each syllable."
    - Verdict: imprecise. The text is the same; regional styles of recitation differ (Howard, Staal).
    - Source: https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2024-February/058693.html
    - Action: rewritten to "the same words in the same order, although their styles of chanting differ".

13. "One scholar has compared a recitation heard today to a tape recording made three thousand years ago."
    - Verdict: correct. Witzel (1995 and Blackwell Companion 2003): present-day Rigveda recitation as "a tape recording" of what was composed some 3,000 years ago.
    - Source: https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2024-February/058693.html

14. The mispronounced accent: "the slayer of Indra" became "the one whom Indra slays".
    - Verdict: correct. Shatapatha Brahmana 1.6.3.8 to 10 (Eggeling): Tvashtri said "indrasatru" with the accent on the first member; "Had he said, 'Grow thou, the foe (slayer) of Indra!' he would certainly have forthwith slain Indra."
    - Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/satapatha-brahmana-english/d/doc63545.html

15. "A boy began at about eight years old... The tradition allowed twelve years to master one collection."
    - Verdict: correct as the textual tradition (initiation of a Brahmin boy in the eighth year; twelve years of study per Veda in the Dharmasutras; Shvetaketu studies from twelve to twenty-four in Chandogya 6.1). Not re-fetched this session beyond Chandogya 6.1.
    - Source: https://college.holycross.edu/projects/himalayan_cultures/2006_plans/esnyder/h_phil_cupanishad.html

## Sacrifices and priests

16. "three fires and as many as sixteen priests... One more priest sat in silence and watched for mistakes."
    - Verdict: correct. Four chief priests (hotar, adhvaryu, udgatar, brahman) with assistants make sixteen; the brahman supervises, largely silent, and corrects errors. Three fires is standard for shrauta rites (not confirmed by the page fetched).
    - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_priesthood

17. Horse sacrifice: a year's wandering, four hundred armed men, three days.
    - Verdict: correct. One year; "one or four hundred young kshatriya men"; hostile lands entered were to be subdued; the closing rite is a three-day soma sacrifice.
    - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashvamedha

18. "an altar built of more than a thousand bricks in the shape of a huge bird... 1975... twelve days and seventeen priests, and at the end the whole enclosure was set on fire."
    - Verdict: correct on 1,005 bricks, bird shape, twelve days, seventeen priests, Nambudiri Brahmins at Panjal, April 1975, filmed by Staal. Could not verify the burning of the enclosure from a page fetched this session.
    - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnicayana ; https://southasiaoutreach.wisc.edu/altar-of-fire/
    - Action: burning of the enclosure cut.

19. "the learned priests, who are 'the human gods'"
    - Verdict: correct. Shatapatha Brahmana 2.2.2.6 (Eggeling): "there are two kinds of gods; for, indeed, the gods are the gods; and the Brahmans who have studied and teach sacred lore are the human gods"; gifts to priests gratify the human gods.
    - Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/satapatha-brahmana-english/d/doc63155.html

## The hymn of the first man

20. Thousand heads and eyes; moon from mind, sun from eye, wind from breath, sky from head, earth from feet; mouth, arms, thighs, feet.
    - Verdict: correct. Rigveda 10.90.1 and 12 to 14 (Griffith: "The Brahman was his mouth, of both his arms was the Rajanya made. His thighs became the Vaisya, from his feet the Sudra was produced."). sacred-texts.com refused the fetch; text given from the standard translation and confirmed against Codex's citation of 10.90.12.
    - Source: https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/rigveda/rv10090.htm

21. "composed a hymn that sorted society into four ranked orders, fixed by birth"
    - Verdict: imprecise. The hymn names four groups and their origin; rules of heredity and marriage come from later texts (Codex item 2).
    - Action: "fixed by birth" removed from the opening.

22. Chinese pilgrim, about 1,600 years ago: such people had to strike a piece of wood on entering a town or market.
    - Verdict: correct. Faxian (c. 400 CE), Legge ch. 16: "When they enter the gate of a city or a market-place, they strike a piece of wood to make themselves known, so that men know and avoid them."
    - Source: https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/texts/faxian.html

23. "Their descendants in India today number about two hundred million, and many of them call themselves Dalits, a word that means broken."
    - Verdict: correct. Census 2011: Scheduled Castes 201 million, 16.6 per cent. Dalit from Sanskrit "divided, split, broken".
    - Source: https://idsn.org/india-official-dalit-population-exceeds-200-million/

## The move east

24. The fire carried in the chief's mouth, burning eastward to a river; marshy land beyond made fit by the Brahmins' sacrifices.
    - Verdict: correct. Shatapatha Brahmana 1.4.1.10 to 16 (Videgha Mathava and Gotama Rahugana; the river Sadanira; land "uncultivated and marshy" until the Brahmans "caused Agni to taste it through sacrifices").
    - Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/sbr/sbe12/sbe1217.htm

25. "By about three thousand years ago the smiths of northern India were making iron axes"
    - Verdict: correct and conservative. Early Iron Age in the north c. 1200 to 800 BCE; some claims are earlier.
    - Source: https://www.lkouniv.ac.in/site/writereaddata/siteContent/202004150925271082anil_kumar_Early_Iron_Age.pdf

26. "Rice had long been grown along the Ganges by the people who already lived there."
    - Verdict: correct. Rice at Lahuradewa in the middle Ganges plain from an early date.
    - Source: https://jpsonline.co.in/index.php/jop/article/download/27/24/38

27. "By about 2,500 years ago the Ganges plain had towns again... ringed with huge banks of earth."
    - Verdict: correct. Second urbanisation c. 600 BCE onward; massive ramparts at Kaushambi and Rajghat.
    - Source: https://www.ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/gees104.pdf

## The Upanishads

28. "The oldest of them are about 2,600 years old."
    - Verdict: correct. Olivelle: Brihadaranyaka and Chandogya, seventh to sixth century BCE, give or take a century.
    - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandogya_Upanishad

29. The salt lesson; the boy away from twelve to twenty-four; "And that's how you are."
    - Verdict: correct. Chandogya 6.1 and 6.13; Olivelle: "And that's how you are, Śvetaketu" (Brereton's reading of tat tvam asi; the older rendering is "That art thou").
    - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_Tvam_Asi ; https://college.holycross.edu/projects/himalayan_cultures/2006_plans/esnyder/h_phil_cupanishad.html

30. The caterpillar; "A man becomes good by good action... and bad by bad action."
    - Verdict: correct as paraphrase. Brihadaranyaka 4.4.3 and 4.4.5 (also 3.2.13). Olivelle's exact wording not seen this session, so the chapter paraphrases without quotation marks.
    - Source: https://interfaith.org/?p=2382

31. Pleasant conduct, pleasant womb (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya); foul conduct, foul womb (dog, pig, outcaste woman).
    - Verdict: correct as paraphrase. Chandogya 5.10.7; the earliest reference to the chandala as an outcaste. Olivelle's exact wording not seen this session; no quotation marks used.
    - Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-english/d/doc239196.html

32. "it is better to do one's own duty badly than to do another's well"
    - Verdict: correct as paraphrase of Bhagavad Gita 3.35 ("Better is one's own dharma, though imperfectly performed, than the dharma of another, though well performed").
    - Source: https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/35/

## Who gained, and the DNA

33. "One law book of about 2,300 years ago lays down that a Shudra who deliberately listens to the hymns shall have his ears filled with molten tin."
    - Verdict: correct on the rule (Gautama Dharmasutra 12.4, Bühler: "his ears shall be filled with (molten) tin or lac"; Olivelle confirms tin, not lead). Date not confirmed this session.
    - Sources: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/gautama-dharmasutra/d/doc116312.html ; https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2013-November/038620.html
    - Action: date loosened to "more than two thousand years old".

34. "In 2013, a team of geneticists studied the DNA of more than seventy communities from every part of India... Every one of them turned out to be a mixture"
    - Verdict: imprecise. Moorjani et al. 2013 assembled 571 people from 73 groups, but the dating analysis used 45 Indo-European- and Dravidian-speaking groups; Austroasiatic and Tibeto-Burman speakers were excluded.
    - Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3769933/
    - Action: rewritten as "dozens of communities from the north and the south of India".

35. "Between about 4,200 and 1,900 years ago, people of very different ancestry were having children together all over India... Then the mixing stopped."
    - Verdict: dates correct (64 to 144 generations, 1,856 to 4,176 years; "mixture even between closely related groups became rare because of a shift to endogamy"). "Then the mixing stopped" imprecise: the change was community by community; Indo-European speakers average later dates (72 generations) than Dravidian speakers (108).
    - Source: same as 34.
    - Action: rewritten; the opening answer now says the custom "spread slowly" and was general by about 1,900 years ago.

36. "One trading community of the south has taken in almost nobody from outside for at least two thousand years"
    - Verdict: imprecise. The paper says the Vysya "have experienced negligible gene flow from neighboring groups in India for an estimated 3,000 years".
    - Source: same as 34.
    - Action: changed to "about three thousand years".

37. "The date falls close to the time when the most famous of the law books... was being composed."
    - Verdict: correct. The law code of Manu is dated around the second century CE (Olivelle); Moorjani et al. make the same connection.
    - Source: same as 34.

38. "India came to have several thousand of them [jatis]."
    - Verdict: correct. The Anthropological Survey of India's People of India project counted 4,635 communities.
    - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_India

## The two names and the British

39. "'Caste' comes from casta, a Portuguese word for breed or lineage... about five hundred years ago."
    - Verdict: correct. Portuguese casta, "breed, race, lineage"; the Portuguese reached India in 1498 and used the word there in the sixteenth century; English borrowed it in the early 1600s.
    - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste

40. "'Hindu' at first meant simply a person who lived there. The English word Hinduism is about two hundred years old."
    - Verdict: correct. Persian Hindu from Sindhu, the Indus (inscriptions of Darius I); "Hinduism" enters English in the early nineteenth century.
    - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus

41. "From 1871 they held a census every ten years, and it asked every person for a caste."
    - Verdict: imprecise. The first count (1867 to 1872) was not held at one time; the regular all-India census every ten years began in 1881.
    - Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=91509
    - Action: changed to 1881.

42. "In 1901 the officials went further and tried to arrange all the castes of each province in order of rank... many sent petitions"
    - Verdict: correct. Risley, census commissioner in 1901, classified castes by "social precedence"; caste associations petitioned for higher places.
    - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hope_Risley

43. "British courts that applied the old Brahmin law books to everybody"
    - Verdict: wrong as stated. Anglo-Hindu law applied to Hindus in personal-law matters; Muslims had their own.
    - Source: https://www.saet.ac.uk/Hinduism/LawandReligioninBrahmanism
    - Action: changed to "to Hindus of every rank".

44. "No king had built them, and no king was needed to guard them."
    - Verdict: too strong (Codex item 3; Dirks, The Hollow Crown, on kings upholding caste order).
    - Action: rewritten as "Kings upheld them, but no one king had built them, and they did not fall when a kingdom fell."
