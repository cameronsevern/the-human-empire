# Held for a later chapter

Cut from the end of chapter 1. Intended for the chapter on the spread out of Africa, where our ancestors meet the other humans.

## The others

One thing about that camp in Africa has been left out. The people in it were not the only humans on Earth.

Upright apes had been walking out of Africa for nearly two million years, long before our own species existed, and they had settled across Europe and Asia. Cut off from one another by deserts, mountains and seas, they had gone their separate ways. A hundred thousand years ago there were at least five kinds of human alive at once.

In Europe and western Asia lived the Neanderthals. They were shorter and broader than us and a great deal stronger, with heavy brows and brains at least as large as ours. They kept fires, made fine stone tools, hunted big animals at close quarters and looked after their injured. At least sometimes, they buried their dead.

Further east lived a people we know almost entirely from their DNA. A finger bone and a few teeth were found in a cave in Siberia, and the genes inside them belonged to no human anyone had heard of. They are called the Denisovans, after the cave, and nobody yet knows what their faces looked like.

On the island of Flores, in Indonesia, there were people about a metre tall, with brains the size of a chimpanzee's, who made stone tools and hunted dwarf elephants. Others lived in the Philippines, and another small-brained kind had been living in southern Africa.

All of them were human. They walked upright, used their hands and lived in groups. Several of them had fire. If the story so far has been about what makes a human, it has been about them as well, and for most of the past two million years any visitor to Earth would have found several sorts of human and seen no reason to bet on ours.

Today there is one.

About sixty thousand years ago, some bands of our kind were living at the north-eastern corner of Africa. They had spears, fire, language and everything their grandparents had taught them. Ahead of them lay Arabia, and beyond it the rest of the world, with its cold forests, its islands and its giant animals. Other people were already living in parts of it. Other parts had never seen a human of any kind.

The bands began to walk.
