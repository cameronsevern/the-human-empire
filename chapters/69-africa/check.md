1. **Faulty central explanation — high confidence.** “The Europeans had won everywhere else because of their weapons, and where an African ruler had the same weapons, they lost.” Modern weapons mattered enormously, but possessing them did not guarantee independence. Samori Touré acquired modern repeating rifles and nevertheless fell to the French in 1898. Ethiopian numbers, mobilisation, terrain, diplomacy and Italian mistakes also mattered—as the preceding paragraph itself shows. This is an unsustainable causal absolute, not merely a confident interpretation. [UNESCO, *General History of Africa*, VII](https://peachf.org/images/GeoRest/AfricaHistoryGeneral7UNESCO-1-405.pdf).

2. **Contradiction with chapter 52; factual overstatement — high confidence.** “For four centuries, fever and African armies had kept Europeans on the coast.” Chapter 52 explicitly supplies an exception: “It was the one place on the Atlantic side of Africa where they tried to conquer the interior,” referring to Portuguese Angola and Njinga’s resistance. The new sentence erases that qualification. “Mostly confined to coastal footholds in tropical Africa” would preserve the argument without contradicting the earlier account. Source: [chapter 52, “The kings’ choice”](chapters/52-slave-trade/chapter.md).

3. **Arms restrictions misrepresented — high confidence.** “In 1890 the European powers agreed among themselves that none of these weapons was to be sold to Africans.” The Brussels Act regulated imports within a specified African zone, rather than imposing a universal racial ban throughout Africa. It provided exceptions for government forces and authorised individuals; its signatories also included non-European states. Explain the restrictions’ geographical scope and exceptions sufficiently to make Menelik’s subsequent purchases intelligible. [General Act of Brussels, Articles VIII–IX](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/General_Act_of_Brussels).

4. **False discovery claim — high confidence.** “The man who first understood what was happening had never been to Africa.” Congolese victims already understood it, and foreign denunciations preceded Morel’s campaign. George Washington Williams visited the Congo and published an open letter exposing violence, deception and slave trading in 1890. Morel’s distinctive achievement was using commercial evidence and organising a sustained campaign, not discovering the abuses first. [Williams’s 1890 open letter](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/s3-euw1-ap-pe-ws4-cws-documents.ri-prod/9780415844550/Document_12-1.pdf).

5. **The ending’s election date is wrong — high confidence.** “A child born in a West African village in 1885, the year the diplomats went home from Berlin, could have seen the first soldiers arrive as a teenager and, at seventy-two, have voted in the election that made the Gold Coast, under the name of Ghana, the first of the tropical colonies to become independent.” The relevant general election took place in July **1956**, when this person would have been seventy or seventy-one. Independence followed in March **1957**; there was no corresponding independence election that year. The lifetime illustration works if it distinguishes voting from witnessing independence. [Parliamentary account of the election and independence timetable](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/1956-12-11/debates/7fff498d-64f3-42f8-88ee-447dc8e87c57/OrdersOfTheDay). “First of the tropical colonies” also needs a narrower definition: Sudan became independent in 1956. **Medium confidence on that wording objection**, because classifications of Sudan vary. [CFR’s regional history](https://www.cfr.org/education/learn/reading/modern-history-and-us-foreign-policy-sub-saharan-africa).

6. **Tariff claim contradicts the arrangements being described — high confidence.** “Each was sure that it could not afford to let a rival have it, since a territory once taken would be closed behind a wall of tariffs.” Colonial annexation did not automatically produce an exclusive tariff zone. The Berlin Act expressly established freedom of international trade in the conventional Congo basin. Fear of commercial exclusion is defensible; its presentation as an inevitable consequence everywhere is not. [Berlin General Act, Article 1](https://worldjpn.net/documents/texts/pw/18850226.T1E.html).

7. **Berlin’s coastal rule becomes a continent-wide requirement — high confidence on the legal scope.** “A government that wanted a territory now had to plant a flag and a garrison on it before a rival did.” Articles 34–35 addressed new coastal acquisitions and the establishment of sufficient authority there. They did not prescribe a flag-and-garrison requirement for every inland territory. The preceding sentence correctly mentions the coast, but this one silently broadens the rule. Distinguish the agreement’s wording from its subsequent political effects. [Berlin General Act, Articles 34–35](https://worldjpn.net/documents/texts/pw/18850226.T1E.html).

8. **Share ownership confused with control — high confidence.** “Britain, which had bought control of the new canal through Egypt because it was the short road to India, occupied Egypt itself in 1882 to keep the canal safe, and then felt obliged to secure the Nile behind it.” Britain bought the Egyptian ruler’s approximately **44 per cent shareholding** in 1875, not outright control of the canal. The occupation also involved British financial interests and an Egyptian political crisis. Name the **Suez Canal** and explain that it connected the Mediterranean to the Red Sea; otherwise a general reader cannot see why Egypt shortened the journey to India. [Purchase agreement](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/39-40/67/pdfs/ukpga_18760067_en.pdf?view=extent); [National Army Museum’s account](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/egypt-and-sudan).

9. **Railway generalisation creates a false distinction — high confidence.** “They ran from a mine or a plantation district to the nearest port, and almost never from one African town to another.” Export-oriented railways also connected African towns. Dakar–Saint-Louis and the inland Kayes–Bamako line are straightforward examples. The defensible distinction is between networks prioritising extraction and networks designed around local transport needs—not between export routes and routes connecting towns. [World Bank railway history, Annex 1](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08ca3e5274a27b2001313/C21-TP8.pdf).

10. **Mortality figure loses its population, place and period — high confidence that qualification is needed.** “The fevers of tropical Africa killed between a third and a half of the Europeans who went there within a year.” Chapter 52 attaches a similar figure to men sent to English trading forts in **West Africa**. Here it becomes a statistic for Europeans throughout tropical Africa. Historical mortality varied sharply by location, period and group: Curtin reports substantially different rates even for officers and enlisted men on the same coast. Restore a specific population and period rather than generalising the figure. [Philip Curtin, “‘The White Man’s Grave’: Image and Reality, 1780–1850”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3D5A35C18B1B4D2A4956641007FB4572/S0021937100031245a.pdf/aoethe_white_mans_gravea_image_and_reality_17801850.pdf).

11. **Invention attribution is wrong — high confidence.** “In 1888 a Scottish vet invented the air-filled rubber tyre, and within a decade the factories of the world wanted all the rubber they could get.” Robert William Thomson invented and patented a pneumatic tyre in **1845**. Dunlop independently developed a practical bicycle tyre in 1888. “Developed a practical pneumatic bicycle tyre” preserves the story without claiming the original invention. [National Museums Scotland](https://www.nms.ac.uk/discover-catalogue/dunlop-tyre-air-apparent).

12. **Casualty comparison is ambiguous — high confidence.** “The British and Egyptians had lost forty-eight men.” Forty-eight refers to deaths, whereas the preceding sentence gives both Sudanese deaths and wounded. Hundreds on the British-led side were also wounded. Say “forty-eight killed” so readers do not compare total Sudanese casualties with British-led fatalities. [National Army Museum](https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=1985-05-13-14).

13. **An absolute exceeds the participant list — high confidence.** “Every power in Europe was there, with the United States and the Ottoman empire.” Fourteen states participated, but not every European state: Switzerland and Greece, for example, were absent. “All the major European powers” would make the intended claim accurately. This is also a direct instance of style.md’s rule against exception-breaking absolutes. [Berlin General Act’s list of participating governments](https://worldjpn.net/documents/texts/pw/18850226.T1E.html).

14. **The sentence appears to reverse its intended meaning.** “The chief was paid, and backed with soldiers if he was obeyed.” Readers will expect military backing when the chief was **disobeyed**, or when enforcement was needed. As written, it suggests obedience was the condition for sending soldiers. Clarify whether this means enforcing the chief’s orders or rewarding his obedience to colonial officials.

15. **The promised second exception disappears.** “Thirty years later they ruled all of it except two countries.” Neither country is named here, and Liberia never appears anywhere in the chapter. Later, “One African state came through, and the reason is instructive” appears to reduce two exceptions to one. Name Ethiopia and Liberia at the first mention, then distinguish Ethiopia’s successful military resistance from Liberia’s different circumstances.

16. **Technical terms arrive without explanation.** “They adopted rifles loaded at the breech, then rifles with magazines that fired a dozen shots without reloading.” A reader without firearms knowledge needs to know that the breech is the rear of the barrel and a magazine holds cartridges ready to feed into the gun. Otherwise the technological progression—the chapter’s central explanation—is vocabulary rather than understanding. This breaks style.md’s requirement to explain terms when introduced.

17. **An important political term is unexplained.** “On the strength of that one verb, Italy informed the world that Ethiopia was its protectorate.” Explain what Italy was claiming: control over Ethiopia’s foreign relations despite the continued existence of its ruler. Without that distinction, readers may not understand why the treaty dispute mattered or how a protectorate differed from outright annexation.

18. **Clear signpost sentence.** “It also made a plain point about the rest of the continent.” This announces the lesson before stating it, exactly the structure style.md prohibits. Remove the announcement and state the corrected historical point directly. By contrast, ordinary transitions such as “The means were now to hand” are not inherently violations.

19. **Statistics crowd out the intended contrast.** “A British and Egyptian army of twenty-five thousand men, with gunboats on the river and forty-four Maxim guns, faced the army of the Sudanese state, about fifty thousand strong.” Three figures in one sentence are followed immediately by a clock time and three casualty figures. This breaks “one number per point.” Retain the roughly two-to-one disparity and one clear comparison of deaths; the exact machine-gun count and finishing time add little explanatory value.
