# Chapter 69 — How did Europe take over Africa?

**Line under the title:** Africa, from 1870 to 1914.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

For four hundred years Europeans had been confined to the edges of Africa by fever and by African armies. In about thirty years after 1880 they took nearly all of it. Three inventions removed the old barriers: quinine against malaria, the river steamboat, and guns that fired hundreds of rounds a minute against muskets. The rush itself was set off by rivalry, since each European government feared that the others would take whatever it left. They divided the continent by drawing lines on maps of places they had never seen. The conquest was resisted nearly everywhere and succeeded nearly everywhere, with one great exception, Ethiopia, which had bought modern rifles in time. Colonial rule was run cheaply, for the benefit of the rulers, and at its worst, in the Congo, it killed millions for rubber. It lasted about one lifetime. The borders are still there.

## The story

1. **Opening: a map on a wall.** Answer first. Berlin, November 1884: fourteen governments meet to settle the rules for taking a continent, with no African in the room.
2. **The barrier.** The map of 1870: a few footholds on the coast. Why the interior was closed. A river voyage in 1854 on which nobody died.
3. **Steam and the Maxim.** Boats that can go up rivers. The gun that loads itself. A battle beside the Nile in 1898.
4. **The king's company.** Why the rush began when it did: gold, diamonds, markets, a canal, and governments watching each other. A king who wanted a colony of his own, and the explorer he hired. The rules made at Berlin, and lines drawn with a ruler.
5. **Red rubber.** The Congo: the bicycle tyre, the quotas, the severed hands. A shipping clerk who read the cargo lists.
6. **Spears, water and rifles.** Resistance: a Zulu victory, a rising that trusted in sacred water, and an order to exterminate a people.
7. **Adwa.** The exception: an emperor who bought a hundred thousand rifles, and the day an African army destroyed a European one.
8. **On the cheap.** How the colonies were run: a few officials, the chiefs, a tax payable in money, railways from the mine to the port. The mission schools.
9. **Ending: one lifetime.** How short it was, and what stayed.
