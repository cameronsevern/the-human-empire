# Chapter 19 — Why is Alexander called the Great?

**Line under the title:** Greece, Egypt and western Asia as far as India, from about 2,360 to about 2,200 years ago.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Alexander won his name by taking, in eleven years, the largest empire the world had yet seen, with an army his father had built. He did not replace that empire so much as take it over: he kept its provinces, governors, roads, taxes and treasuries, and sat on its throne in its king's dress, to the anger of the Macedonians who had won it for him. His own rule ended the day he died, and his generals tore the conquests into rival kingdoms. What lasted was something he did not plan in any detail: Greek cities, Greek schools and a common Greek language from Egypt to Afghanistan, and in Alexandria a set of scholars on a king's payroll who put geometry in order and measured the Earth with a stick and its shadow. The accounts we have were written three to four and a half centuries later, so what he did is fairly sure and what he meant is not.

## The story

1. **The king died.** Answer first. A Babylonian clerk's clay diary records the death in three words. Almost everything else was written centuries later.
2. **The long pike.** The Greek cities in brief. Macedon, Philip, the five-metre pike, the heavy cavalry, full-time pay. The Greek cities beaten. Philip murdered.
3. **Five thousand camels.** The Persian empire in brief. The crossing, three battles, Egypt, Babylon, the treasure of Persepolis, the march to India and the river where the army refused.
4. **A Macedonian in Persian dress.** He kept the provinces, the governors, the taxes. Mazaeus. The royal dress. The killing of Cleitus. The weddings at Susa, the thirty thousand boys, the mutiny at Opis.
5. **The soldier, the noble and the priest.** What each made of the new ruler: the Silver Shields and their baggage; the nobles who served, the later Persian memory of "the accursed"; Egypt's priests, Luxor and the Rosetta Stone.
6. **The stolen body.** Death at thirty-two, no usable heir, forty years of war, the family wiped out, the funeral carriage hijacked to Egypt. Three kingdoms.
7. **Greek on the Oxus.** Cities, settlers, common Greek, coin. A thin ruling layer. The city in Afghanistan with a theatre and the sayings from Delphi.
8. **The king's scholars.** Alexandria, the Museum and the library, books taken from ships, Euclid.
9. **A stick and its shadow.** Eratosthenes measures the Earth, step by step. Ending: the soldiers stopped Alexander at a river; a librarian found the size of the world without leaving Egypt.
