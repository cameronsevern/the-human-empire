# Chapter 19 fact-check lookups

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. "Fixed" means the chapter was changed. Where a source was read on the web during the check, the URL is given. Where a claim rests on a standard ancient reference that was not re-fetched, the passage is named and marked "not fetched".

## Opening (the diary; the sources)

1. "The entry for the twenty-ninth day ... says: 'The king died.' Then it mentions clouds."
   - Verdict: correct. Astronomical Diary for 323 BCE: "The twenty-ninth: The king died. Clouds." 29 Aiaru = 11 June 323 BCE. Tablet in the British Museum.
   - Source: https://www.livius.org/sources/content/oriental-varia/a-contemporary-account-of-the-death-of-alexander/
2. "they had been doing it for more than three hundred years"
   - Verdict: correct. Oldest dated diary is 652 BCE; 652 to 323 is 329 years. (Sachs and Hunger, Astronomical Diaries; not fetched.)
3. "He was thirty-two years old ... born more than two thousand kilometres away"
   - Verdict: first draft said "two and a half thousand", wrong. Pella to Babylon is about 2,150 km in a straight line (computed from coordinates). Fixed.
4. "The oldest full account that survives was written about three hundred years after his death, and the one that historians trust most ... more than four hundred and fifty years after."
   - Verdict: correct. Diodorus book 17, written about 60 to 30 BCE (about 270 to 290 years after 323); Arrian's Anabasis, about 130 to 160 CE (450 to 480 years after). Ptolemy (later king of Egypt), Aristobulus, Nearchus and Callisthenes wrote accounts that are lost. (Standard; not fetched.)

## The long pike

5. "As a teenager he had spent three years as a hostage in Thebes"
   - Verdict: correct (first draft "as a boy": imprecise, fixed). Hostage about 368 to 365 BCE, aged about 14 to 17; king 359 BCE.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon
6. "a pike more than five metres long ... took both hands ... The points of the first five rows all stuck out beyond the front rank"
   - Verdict: correct after fix. Sarissa about 5 to 7 m, Connolly about 5.8 m for the 4th century; two-handed; five rows projecting (Polybius 18.29). First draft said "about five metres" and that the shield "hung from the neck"; the neck strap is uncertain, so reworded to a strap over the shoulder. Depth of sixteen is the standard file (Polybius 18.30; not fetched).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarissa
7. "Philip's men were paid all year round, with gold from mines that he had seized"
   - Verdict: correct. Philip took Crenides (Philippi) and its mines, which paid for his campaigns.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon
8. "He made them carry their own food and gear ... cut down the trail of carts and servants"
   - Verdict: correct. Frontinus, Stratagems 4.1.6 (not fetched). First draft said the army "moved faster than any other": an absolute, reworded.
9. "Most Greek soldiers were farmers who went to war for a few weeks in summer."
   - Verdict: first draft said "Greek citizens were farmers": too sweeping (Theban Sacred Band, mercenaries). Fixed to "most".
10. "About 2,360 years ago he beat the combined armies of Athens and Thebes ... Alexander, who was eighteen, led the cavalry."
   - Verdict: correct. Chaeronea, 338 BCE; Alexander born 356. His command of the left is from Diodorus 16.86 (not fetched).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon
11. "Two years later, at his daughter's wedding, one of his own bodyguards stabbed him to death at the entrance to the theatre."
   - Verdict: correct. 336 BCE, Aigai, Pausanias, one of the seven bodyguards, at the wedding of his daughter Cleopatra.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon
12. "taught for a few years by the philosopher Aristotle"; Thebes destroyed and its people sold.
   - Verdict: correct (Aristotle at Mieza about 343 to 340; Thebes razed 335, Arrian 1.9, Diodorus 17.14). Not fetched.

## Five thousand camels

13. The Persian empire "from the coast of Turkey to the river Indus, and from Egypt to Central Asia", satraps, royal road with relays.
   - Verdict: correct; common knowledge in the field (Herodotus 5.52 to 53, 8.98).
14. "A hundred and fifty years earlier, a Persian army had invaded Greece and burned Athens."
   - Verdict: correct. 480 BCE; 146 years before 334.
15. "about forty thousand men"
   - Verdict: correct as a round figure. Arrian 1.11: over 30,000 foot and 5,000 horse, plus the advance force already in Asia.
16. "In the two largest, the method was the same ... both times Darius turned his chariot and fled."
   - Verdict: first draft said "the method was the same each time", wrong for the Granicus. Fixed to the two battles Darius commanded (Issus, Gaugamela).
17. "Their diary says that the king's troops deserted him. Three weeks later it records that Alexander, whom it calls 'king of the world', entered Babylon."
   - Verdict: correct. "The king, his troops deserted him"; "Alexander, king of the world, came into Babylon". Battle 1 October 331 BCE; entry about 20 to 21 October.
   - Source: https://www.livius.org/sources/content/oriental-varia/a-contemporary-account-of-the-battle-of-gaugamela/
18. "one ancient writer says it took five thousand camels and twenty thousand mules to carry it away"
   - Verdict: correct. Plutarch, Alexander 37: "ten thousand pairs of mules and five thousand camels".
   - Source: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Lives/Alexander*/5.html
19. "close to five thousand tonnes of silver, or its worth in gold"
   - Verdict: correct as arithmetic on the ancient figure. 180,000 talents gathered at Ecbatana (Strabo 15.3.9; Diodorus 17.80; not fetched) at about 26 kg a talent is about 4,700 tonnes.
20. "A few months later he burned the palace at Persepolis."
   - Verdict: correct. First draft discussed whether it was a drunken whim; cut as source commentary.
21. "his own governors stabbed him and left him in a cart by the road"
   - Verdict: correct. Bessus and fellow satraps, 330 BCE (Arrian 3.21; not fetched).
22. "defeated an Indian king who brought a line of war elephants against him"
   - Verdict: first draft said "two hundred" (Arrian's figure; Curtius says 85, Diodorus 130). Number removed.
23. The refusal at the river in the Punjab; three days in his tent; weeks of rain.
   - Verdict: correct. Hyphasis (Beas), 326 BCE; Arrian 5.25 to 28; seventy days of rain in Diodorus 17.94 (not fetched). First draft called it "the only defeat of his life": an absolute (he had been repulsed at the Persian Gates). Reworded.

## A Macedonian in Persian dress

24. Mazaeus: commanded a wing at Gaugamela, surrendered Babylon, made governor; a Macedonian commanded the troops and another collected taxes; coins in his own name.
   - Verdict: correct. First draft added that he kept "the palace": not verified, cut. "Only the man at the top had changed" was too strong and was reworded.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazaeus
25. "most of the governors Alexander appointed in the east were Persian nobles ... he executed them and put Macedonians in their place"
   - Verdict: correct (Badian; Bosworth, Conquest and Empire; purge of 325 to 324). Not fetched.
26. Darius's body sent for royal burial; the chief murderer punished in the Persian way.
   - Verdict: correct. Arrian 3.22 and 4.7 (not fetched).
27. "He put on the tunic, the sash and the headband of a Persian king, though he drew the line at trousers."
   - Verdict: correct. Plutarch, Alexander 45: he did not adopt "trousers, or sleeved vest, or tiara"; Diodorus 17.77 lists diadem, tunic and sash.
   - Source: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0243:chapter=45
28. Cleitus: a cavalry commander, saved Alexander's life in the first battle, killed at a banquet in Samarkand after praising Philip; three days without food.
   - Verdict: correct after fix (first draft: "who commanded the cavalry"; he was one of two commanders). Three days: Arrian 4.9 (not fetched).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleitus_the_Black
29. "he and about eighty of his officers married the daughters of Persian and other Asian nobles. He himself took a daughter of Darius."
   - Verdict: first draft said "about ninety" (Chares gives 92); Arrian 7.4 gives eighty. Fixed to eighty.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susa_weddings
30. "thirty thousand young Asians ... He called them the Successors."
   - Verdict: correct. Epigonoi; Arrian 7.6, Plutarch 47 and 71 (not fetched).
31. The mutiny: ten thousand veterans sent home; thirteen executed; commands to Persians; Macedonians at his door without weapons within three days.
   - Verdict: correct. Arrian 7.8 (thirteen led off to execution), 7.11 (third day; weapons thrown down at the gates), 7.12 (about 10,000 sent home).
   - Sources: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Anabasis_of_Alexander/Book_VII/Chapter_VIII ; https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Anabasis_of_Alexander/Book_VII/Chapter_XI

## The soldier, the noble and the priest

32. The Silver Shields: three thousand veterans; camp, families and loot captured; they handed over their general.
   - Verdict: correct. Battle of Gabiene, 316 BCE; Eumenes given up to Antigonus. Number 3,000 from Diodorus 19.28 (not fetched).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argyraspides
33. "They called him 'the accursed Alexander'."
   - Verdict: correct. Book of Arda Viraf 1: "the accursed Alexander, the Roman".
   - Source: https://www.avesta.org/mp/viraf.html
34. "the last conquest was only about ten years old when Alexander arrived"; governor handed Egypt over without a fight.
   - Verdict: correct after fix (first draft "eleven years"; reconquest dated 343 or 342, Alexander arrived late 332).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-first_Dynasty_of_Egypt
35. Offerings to the sacred bull at the old capital; the shrine at Luxor rebuilt in his name showing him as pharaoh.
   - Verdict: correct. Arrian 3.1 (Apis at Memphis; not fetched). Luxor: chapel of Alexander behind the Amenhotep III rooms (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxor_Temple); reliefs confirmed by looking at the Commons photographs.
36. Rosetta Stone: decree of a council of priests for a thirteen-year-old king; gifts and tax favours; three scripts.
   - Verdict: correct. Memphis, 27 March 196 BCE; Ptolemy V about 13; hieroglyphic, demotic, Greek. First draft did not name the stone and placed it "in Egypt"; fixed. A sentence on the revolt in the south (about 206 to 186 BCE) was cut for length.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
37. Cut: Darius's mother starving herself (Diodorus 17.118; Curtius 10.5). True to the sources but cut for length.

## The stolen body

38. Death after a drinking party, fever, eleven days.
   - Verdict: correct. Plutarch 75 to 76 and Arrian 7.25 to 26 from the royal diaries (not fetched).
39. Wife pregnant; half-brother; joint kings; mother, half-brother, wives and son murdered; "the boy was about thirteen".
   - Verdict: correct. Philip III killed 317, Olympias 316, Alexander IV and Roxane about 310.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_the_Diadochi
40. "The fighting lasted forty years."
   - Verdict: correct on the usual reckoning (323 to 281 BCE, Corupedium). Wikipedia's wider span is 322 to 272.
41. Ptolemy seized the funeral carriage in Syria and took the body to Egypt; his family ruled for nearly three centuries.
   - Verdict: correct. Late 322 or early 321 BCE (Diodorus 18.28); dynasty to 30 BCE.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_the_Diadochi
42. "Seleucus gave up the Indian provinces ... in exchange for five hundred war elephants."
   - Verdict: correct. Strabo 15.2.9.
   - Source: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/15B*.html

## Greek on the Oxus

43. "Alexander had planted cities along his march, most of them named Alexandria."
   - Verdict: first draft said "more than a dozen"; the secure count is debated (Fraser accepts far fewer than Plutarch's seventy). Number removed.
44. Ai Khanoum: found 1961 when the Afghan king was shown a column capital on a hunting trip; dug 1964 to 1978; theatre for five to six thousand; gymnasium 100 m across.
   - Verdict: correct after fix ("a hundred metres long" changed to "across": final phase 390 by 100 m).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ai-Khanoum
45. The Clearchus inscription and its maxims; "four thousand kilometres away".
   - Verdict: correct. Stele in the shrine of Kineas just north of the palace (first draft said "in the middle of the city"; fixed to "near the palace"). Last maxim is about being without grief, so "die without regret" was changed to "without sorrow". Delphi to Ai Khanoum is about 4,080 km in a straight line (computed).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ai-Khanoum
46. Common Greek ("the common tongue"), 24 letters.
   - Verdict: correct (koine dialektos). First draft said a child could learn the alphabet "in weeks" (unverifiable, cut) and that courts judged in Greek and that anyone wanting "a contract or a hearing" needed it (wrong for Egypt, where Egyptian-language contracts and courts continued). Fixed.
47. Persian silver turned into coin; coins of one design from Greece to Afghanistan.
   - Verdict: correct; standard (Alexander's Heracles and Zeus silver struck at some two dozen mints).

## The king's scholars

48. The Museum: salary and meals from the king.
   - Verdict: correct. Strabo 17.1.8. "Paid no taxes" cut because Strabo does not say it.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Alexandria
49. Books from ships seized and copied, the copy returned.
   - Verdict: correct. Galen, commentary on Hippocrates' Epidemics III.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Alexandria
50. Size of the library.
   - Verdict: ancient figures run from 40,000 to 700,000 rolls and are unreliable. The chapter gives no number.
51. Euclid in Alexandria under the first Ptolemy; thirteen parts; first postulate.
   - Verdict: correct (Proclus; Elements I, postulate 1). First draft "proved everything else ... resting only on" was an overstatement; reworded.

## A stick and its shadow

52. "About 2,270 years ago, the third Ptolemy appointed a new head of the library ... from a Greek city on the coast of Libya."
   - Verdict: correct after fix (first draft 2,260). Born Cyrene about 276 BCE; summoned after Ptolemy III's accession in 246.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eratosthenes
53. No shadow at Syene at noon on the longest day; a shadow at Alexandria; one fiftieth of a circle; five thousand stades from the king's surveyors; 250,000 stades.
   - Verdict: correct. Cleomedes; the distance from royal pacers (bematists) is in Martianus Capella. First draft said Egypt "had been measured for tax since the days of the pyramids" and spoke of "the road": replaced. A sentence was added that Syene lies almost due south, which the reasoning needs.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eratosthenes
54. "educated Greeks had known for more than a century that the Earth is a ball ... the edge of that shadow is always curved"
   - Verdict: correct. Aristotle, On the Heavens 2.14, about 350 BCE.
55. "On the shortest stade that scholars have proposed ... about 39,000 kilometres ... on the common Athenian stade about 46,000 ... within one sixth"
   - Verdict: correct arithmetic. 250,000 x 157.5 m = 39,400 km; x 185 m = 46,250 km; true meridian circumference 40,008 km; 46,250 is 15.6 per cent over. First draft said "the longest" stade and "within one fiftieth": imprecise, fixed.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eratosthenes
56. "Syene does not lie exactly south of Alexandria, and the midsummer sun was not quite overhead there."
   - Verdict: correct. Syene is about 3 degrees east of Alexandria and a little north of the tropic. Straight-line distance about 844 km (computed).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eratosthenes
57. "A little over two centuries after that noon ... a city in Italy had swallowed every one of them west of the Euphrates"
   - Verdict: correct after fix (first draft "within two centuries"; about 240 to 30 BCE is 210 years).
