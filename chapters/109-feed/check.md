Read the style guide and outline first, then chapter 109 and the earlier chapters. No files changed. Findings below are ranked by seriousness; confidence refers to the factual correction.

1. **False claim about the Slovak election.** “There was no time to disprove it, and he lost.”

   AFP published its debunk on **28 September 2023**, before the election on 30 September; both people impersonated also publicly denied the recording. Its electoral effect is unestablished. The sentence falsely rules out a timely rebuttal and implies an unsupported explanation for the result. **Confidence: high.** [AFP’s dated fact-check](https://fakty.afp.com/doc.afp.com.33WY9LF)

2. **Overstates the UN’s finding.** “In 2018 a United Nations mission that had investigated the killings in Myanmar reported that Facebook had played a determining role in them.”

   The mission chairman’s “determining role” formulation concerned the spread of hatred and conflict. The written report described social media’s role as significant, but explicitly called for further independent examination of how Facebook posts translated into real-world violence. Presenting this as an established determination about the killings goes beyond that finding. **Confidence: high.** [UN report, paragraph 74](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?l=en&s=a%2Fhrc%2F39%2F64&t=pdf)

3. **Misleading picture of the remaining Rohingya population.** “By then there were hardly any Rohingya left in the villages.”

   Approximately **600,000 Rohingya remained in Myanmar** after the expulsions. Many villages were emptied or destroyed, but this unqualified sentence implies near-total removal of the population. Specify the devastated villages or northern areas intended. **Confidence: high on the population; medium on the intended geographical scope.** [UN fact-finding mission’s 2019 assessment](https://myanmar.un.org/en/17572-myanmar%E2%80%99s-rohingya-persecuted-living-under-threat-genocide-un-experts-say)

4. **Legal protection becomes an absolute.** “A social network cannot be sued for spreading one.”

   Section 230 generally protects services against US claims treating them as publishers of **content supplied by someone else**. It is neither worldwide immunity nor protection for defamatory content the service itself creates or develops. “Cannot be sued” also confuses protection against liability with impossibility of bringing proceedings. This breaks the rule against exception-breaking absolutes. **Confidence: high.** [Section 230, especially subsections (c)(1) and (f)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230)

5. **A survey is presented as a causal finding.** “The company's own research on its photograph-sharing service, which it did not publish, found that it made about one teenage girl in three who already felt bad about her body feel worse.”

   The girls **reported that Instagram made their body-image problems worse**. That is meaningful evidence of their experience, but not an experimental finding that the service caused the deterioration. The denominator is appropriately qualified; the missing qualification is that these were respondents’ reports. **Confidence: high.** [Original research deck](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Instagram-Teen-Annotated-Research-Deck-1.pdf)

6. **Election research is given too broad a scope.** “The Russian posts were a tiny fraction of what Americans saw that year, most of it went to people whose minds were made up, and researchers who have looked for an effect on votes have not found one.”

   The relevant study examined **exposure to Internet Research Agency accounts on Twitter**, finding concentrated exposure among strongly partisan users and no detectable relationship with changes in voting behaviour. It did not establish these conclusions for the entire Russian operation across platforms. “Minds were made up” also substitutes an inference for the measured partisan characteristics. **Confidence: high.** [The study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9)

7. **Incorrect account of TikTok’s inputs.** “A newer service, owned by a Chinese company, shows films a few seconds long, one after another, and learns from nothing more than how long the viewer pauses on each.”

   Watch time is important, but TikTok also uses likes, shares, comments, follows, video information and account/device settings. “Nothing more” is demonstrably false and breaks the absolutes rule. **Confidence: high.** [TikTok’s explanation of recommendations](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-foryou?lang=en-AU)

8. **Unsupported comparison presented as measurement.** “Within an hour it knows a teenager's tastes better than her parents do.”

   The documented claim is that recommendations adapt to viewing and interaction signals. Neither the one-hour threshold nor the comparison with parents is established by that evidence. This needs a specific comparative study or replacement with an observable claim about how quickly the feed changes. **Confidence: high that the cited mechanism does not substantiate this comparison.** [TikTok’s account of learning preferences](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-foryou?lang=en-AU)

9. **Wrong apparent recipient and basis of the fine.** “The company paid a fine of five billion dollars.”

   The immediately preceding company is the political consultancy, Cambridge Analytica. **Facebook** paid the $5 billion penalty in 2019, settling charges that it violated a 2012 FTC privacy order. Name Facebook here and distinguish the broader privacy settlement from a simple fine for the quiz incident. **Confidence: high.** [FTC announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/07/ftc-imposes-5-billion-penalty-sweeping-new-privacy-restrictions-facebook)

10. **Two distortions of the false-news study.** “Three researchers at an American university studied every disputed news story that had spread on one of the short-message services over eleven years, a hundred and twenty-six thousand of them.” “They found that false stories reached people about six times as fast as true ones, and were passed on far more often.”

    These were roughly **126,000 sharing cascades** associated with stories classified by fact-checking organisations, not every disputed story on Twitter. The sixfold comparison specifically concerned the time taken to reach **1,500 people**, rather than a universal speed ratio. The broader finding that falsehood spread faster is supported. **Confidence: high.** [MIT’s account of the study](https://news.mit.edu/2018/study-twitter-false-news-travels-faster-true-stories-0308)

11. **Incorrect date.** “In 2017 the company added a row of little faces with which users could respond to a post, and for some time its system counted an angry face as worth five times an ordinary sign of approval.”

    Facebook launched Reactions globally on **24 February 2016**. The sentence conflates their introduction with the later change in ranking weights. **Confidence: high.** [Facebook’s launch announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2016/02/reactions-now-available-globally/)

12. **Incorrect blanket timeline for Trump’s suspensions.** “In January 2021, after a crowd of his supporters stormed the American Congress, the outgoing president was removed from all the main networks within two days, on the judgement of three or four executives.”

    YouTube suspended his channel on **12 January**, six days after the attack, and initially prevented new uploads rather than removing the channel. “All” is false; the exact tally of decision-makers also needs evidence. **Confidence: high on the timeline and YouTube action.** [Google’s congressional response](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/IF/IF16/20210325/111407/HHRG-117-IF16-Wstate-PichaiS-20210325-SD004.pdf)

13. **Australia’s restriction is overstated.** “Australia has banned children under sixteen from social media altogether.”

    From **10 December 2025**, covered platforms had to take reasonable steps to prevent under-sixteens from creating or retaining accounts. This is not a prohibition on every social service or all access to publicly viewable material; exemptions apply. “Altogether” breaks the absolutes rule. **Confidence: high.** [Australian regulator’s explanation](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions)

14. **False absolute about payment.** “None of these services charges the people who use them.”

    Their basic services are generally free and advertising remains central, but paid subscriptions exist. Facebook and Instagram themselves introduced European ad-free subscriptions in 2023. The business-model argument needs “basic access is usually free,” not “none.” **Confidence: high.** [Meta’s subscription announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/11/facebook-and-instagram-to-offer-subscription-for-no-ads-in-europe/)

15. **False absolute about China’s foreign services.** “The foreign search engines and social networks are blocked, and their Chinese equivalents, which are as large and as inventive, are required by law to police what their users say.”

    Major foreign services including Google and Facebook are blocked, but **Microsoft Bing operates in China with censorship**. Identify the blocked services or qualify the category. **Confidence: high.** [Citizen Lab’s comparative testing](https://citizenlab.ca/research/a-comparison-of-search-censorship-in-china/)

16. **An estimate becomes a count.** “A medical charity counted at least six thousand seven hundred killed in the first month.”

    Médecins Sans Frontières **estimated** at least 6,700 violent deaths from retrospective household mortality surveys. It did not count 6,700 individually identified deaths. This is a substantive distinction in how the evidence was obtained, not unnecessary hedging. **Confidence: high.** [MSF’s explanation of the surveys](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/latest/no-one-was-left-violence-against-rohingya-myanmar)

17. **The military operation’s personnel are described too precisely.** “Many, it emerged later, came from a secret unit of seven hundred army officers who ran fake pages devoted to pop stars and beauty tips, built up large followings, and then used them to spread invented stories of Muslim atrocities.”

    The underlying reporting described **as many as 700 people/military personnel**, not an established unit of exactly 700 commissioned officers. “Hundreds of military personnel” preserves the force without inventing rank or numerical precision. **Confidence: high.** [Paul Mozur’s investigation](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/10/15/technology/myanmar-facebook-genocide.html)

18. **Likely confusion between internet and mobile penetration.** “Fewer than one person in a hundred had a mobile telephone.”

    For 2011, the reported figures are **internet penetration below 1%** and about **1.3 million mobile subscriptions**—roughly 2–3 subscriptions per hundred inhabitants. Subscriptions are not unique owners, but the available evidence does not establish the sentence’s ownership figure. **Confidence: medium-high.** [Reporting citing ITU figures](https://www.wired.com/story/how-facebooks-rise-fueled-chaos-and-confusion-in-myanmar/)

19. **The average lacks its population.** “People now spend, on average, nearly two and a half hours a day on such services.”

    This is a survey-based average for **internet users in the covered markets and age groups**, not humanity as a whole. DataReportal’s 2025 figure is 2 hours 21 minutes. State whose average this is and anchor “now” to the chapter’s period. **Confidence: high.** [DataReportal’s 2025 analysis](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-sub-section-state-of-social)

20. **The opening blurs the distinction established in chapter 107.** “A piece of software decides, and it belongs to one of a handful of companies.”

    This works as an answer about a recommended social-media feed, but the title asks about everything seen online. Chapter 107 distinguishes the open network and Web from the companies through which many people use them. Establish that the present subject is the feed. This is a **scope and reader-orientation problem**, rather than a direct contradiction.

21. **An absolute contradicts the chapter’s own mechanism.** “Any of them can publish a sentence, a picture or a film that can be seen an hour later by a farmer in Brazil or a minister in Delhi, and nobody stands in between.”

    The ranking systems, moderators and platform owners subsequently described stand between publication and distribution. The defensible distinction is that users usually need no editor’s advance permission to publish. “Nobody” both breaks the style rule and obscures the central argument.

22. **Avoiding names makes the companies impossible to track.** “Within a year the same idea had been applied to videos, and the year after that to short messages.” “The two largest of these companies took in more than four hundred billion dollars from advertising in 2024.”

    Readers must infer YouTube and Twitter, then work out that the advertising comparison concerns Google/Alphabet and Meta rather than the unnamed services just introduced. The same difficulty recurs with Instagram, WhatsApp and TikTok. *Style.md* permits names needed to follow the story: these qualify. Introduce the services and establish the important ownership relationships once.

23. **A compressed transition conceals the mechanism.** “Other governments have reached for cruder tools.”

    This introduces internet shutdowns, EU transparency/risk-assessment requirements and Australia’s age restrictions as one category. The reader can understand why switching off access is “cruder” than covert distraction, but not why disclosure requirements belong in the same category. Explain the different approaches rather than making this sentence carry an unstated comparison.

24. **The ending’s chronology implies the wrong order.** “It hired a hundred reviewers who could read Burmese and removed the pages of the army's commanders.”

    Following the November admission, this reads as subsequent action. Facebook removed senior military accounts in **August 2018**; its August staffing report described 60 language experts and a target of at least 100 by year-end. Distinguish completed removals from the staffing target and place them correctly relative to the admission. **Confidence: high.** [August removal report](https://www.investing.com/news/technology-news/facebook-removes-top-myanmar-military-official-others-from-website-1587553), [commissioned assessment’s staffing account](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/bsr-facebook-myanmar-hria_final.pdf)

I found **no direct contradictions with chapters 103, 107 or 108**, and no clear hedging stacks, chapter self-mentions, explicit cross-references or prohibited answer-announcing signposts. The principal style violations are the absolutes identified above.
