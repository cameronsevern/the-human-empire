## Factual flags

1. **Major — mismatched years in the new moderator comparison.**

   > “Four people, for a country of eighteen million users.”

   In context, this describes **2015**. Reuters reports approximately **four Burmese-speaking reviewers and 7.3 million active users in 2015**. The **18 million** figure belongs to **2018**, when the outsourced operation had about 60 reviewers. The new sentence substantially exaggerates the contemporary users-per-reviewer ratio. [Reuters investigation, archived copy](https://mmlibrary.uwazi.io/api/files/16324894623010ic3k9cap0zt.pdf)

2. **Major — the disappearance of checks contradicts both evidence and the chapter.**

   > “The internet removed the cost, and with it the filter.”  
   > “The cost of reaching the world has fallen to nothing, and so has the check.”

   Publishing without an editor’s advance approval does not mean checks disappeared. The chapter subsequently describes moderation, removal and fact-checking. Facebook began its third-party fact-checking programme in December 2016. The correct distinction is that **editorial approval ceased to be a necessary condition of publication**, not that checking fell to zero. [Facebook’s original announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2016/12/news-feed-fyi-addressing-hoaxes-and-fake-news/)

3. **Minor — newspaper copies were not universally identical.**

   > “A newspaper sold the same page to everyone who bought it.”

   Newspapers sold geographically differentiated editions and advertising. For example, the *San Francisco Chronicle*’s publisher statement explicitly records additional advertising in its Wednesday zoned editions. The defensible comparison is between **broad audience segments in print and individual targeting online**. [Chronicle publisher statement](https://extras.sfgate.com/chronicle/adsite/downloads/auditstatement.pdf)

4. **Minor — the smartphone chronology remains wrong.**

   > “In 2007 came the telephone with a screen, which meant that people carried all of it with them.”

   Phones with screens, touchscreen smartphones and mobile web access all predate 2007. That year marks the **iPhone’s introduction**, not the arrival of these categories. The Computer History Museum documents Simon’s touchscreen and successful web-capable smartphones from 1999. This is an existing error reinforced by the new pocket-versus-desk sentences. [Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/mobile-computing/18/341)

5. **Minor — recommendation traffic does not establish what viewers were seeking.**

   > “Most of what its viewers see, in other words, they did not go looking for.”

   The preceding percentage concerns how viewing is generated, not whether viewers wanted or sought the subject or video. Recommendations can help someone find precisely what they are looking for; YouTube’s own explanation gives that example. The correct fact concerns **viewing attributable to recommendations**, without the additional claim about intention. [YouTube’s explanation](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)

6. **Minor — an earlier flag remains only partly fixed.**

   > “Three researchers at an American university studied a hundred and twenty-six thousand news stories that had spread on Twitter over eleven years and had been checked by independent fact-checkers.”

   The study tracked approximately **126,000 sharing cascades**, not 126,000 distinct fact-checked stories. A cascade is a chain of sharing; one story can produce multiple cascades. The earlier problem with “every disputed news story” has been fixed, and the 1,500-person qualification has been added, but the unit being counted remains wrong. [MIT’s account of the research](https://news.mit.edu/2018/study-twitter-false-news-travels-faster-true-stories-0308)

## Clarity flags

- **Explicit signposts:** “That cost had a side effect.” and “The effect of those words is easiest to see by comparison.” announce the explanation instead of giving it. These directly conflict with the style sheet’s prohibition on signpost sentences.

- **Repeated explanation:** “Part of it, in the end, depends on people.” is immediately followed by “What the software cannot judge is passed to people, because some decisions still need a human eye.” The second sentence explains the point; the first and the added “because” clause repeat it.

- **Low-information additions:** “The business is vast.” precedes the revenue figure; “Some of them are matters of life and death.” precedes the killings; “The work leaves its mark.” precedes the trauma. These add little orientation beyond what the next sentence supplies.

- **Unclear comparison:** “Video works the same way, only more so.” More so in what respect—engagement, personalisation, outrage or influence? The next sentence does not resolve it.

- **Exception-breaking absolutes:** “A reader who had followed a page for its gossip had no reason to doubt it when it turned to politics”; “a rumour had to get past them before it could reach a crowd”; and “The mere possibility of forgery protects everybody who has something to hide.” Familiarity need not eliminate doubt, rumours can reach crowds outside edited media, and the possibility of forgery does not protect everybody.

- **Overliteral account of learning:** “The loop never stops. Every swipe and every pause becomes a lesson for the system.” These absolutes imply uninterrupted learning from every event. The preceding feedback explanation already makes the useful point without that claim.

- **Unclear scope of reinstatement:** “A decision by a few executives had been reversed by one owner.” The preceding suspension concerned several networks; the purchase concerned one. Identify the reversal as occurring on Twitter.

- **Potentially misleading contrast:** “Other harms are quieter.” introduces body-image problems, depression and self-harm after the deaths in India. “Quieter” is imprecise and can suggest lesser severity rather than less public visibility.

No hedging stacks, chapter self-mentions or explicit cross-references stood out. Many of the new transitions appropriately follow the instruction to give readers room; they should not all be cut simply for being explanatory.

## Overall

The revision is **not entirely fact-neutral**. Its clearest new error combines Myanmar figures from different years, while several other additions broaden limited claims into absolutes. The chapter’s chosen interpretation can remain confident; the needed corrections concern chronology, scope and measurement. No files were edited.
