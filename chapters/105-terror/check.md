I found the following issues, ranked by seriousness. I found no clear contradiction with chapters 98, 99 or 104, and no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-references or body cross-references. No files were edited.

1. **Misleading central claim — high confidence.**  
   > “It overthrew two governments, one that had sheltered the attackers and one that had not, and could build a lasting state in neither place.”

   This equates two different outcomes. Afghanistan’s new republic collapsed; Iraq’s post-invasion constitutional system survived, despite severe failures and violence, and held another parliamentary election in 2025. Failure to achieve the state Washington wanted is defensible; failure to establish anything lasting is not. The outline makes that distinction more accurately. Sources: [Iraq’s constitution](https://peacemaker.un.org/en/documents/constitution-iraq-current), [UN’s 2025 Iraq report](https://iraq.un.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/UN%20Iraq%202025%20annual%20results%20report_complete9.pdf).

2. **Wrong endpoint for Islamic State — high confidence.**  
   > “It took a coalition of seventy countries, Kurdish and Iraqi soldiers on the ground, and three years to destroy it.”

   Three years takes the reader to 2017, when Mosul and Raqqa fell. Islamic State’s last territorial enclave fell at Baghuz in **March 2019**, nearly five years after the caliphate’s proclamation. Its organisation survived that defeat. Specify the destruction of its territorial state and distinguish that from eliminating the movement. Source: [UN assessment of Islamic State after territorial defeat](https://www.un.org/securitycouncil/ctc/news/tenth-sg-report-states-isil-continues-adapt-and-evolve-despite-death-its-leader).

3. **Misstated war-cost estimate — high confidence.**  
   > “By their count the United States spent eight trillion dollars on them.”

   Brown’s estimate includes money already spent **and future obligations**, notably roughly $2.2 trillion for veterans’ care. It is not eight trillion dollars already spent. Name the Costs of War project and give the estimate’s scope and date. Source: [Brown’s explanation of the estimate](https://www.brown.edu/news/2021-09-01/costsofwar).

4. **Incorrect legal description — high confidence.**  
   > “It had been chosen because it lay outside the reach of American courts.”

   That was the administration’s intended legal advantage, not an enduring fact about Guantánamo. In *Rasul v. Bush* (2004), the Supreme Court held that federal courts could hear the prisoners’ habeas petitions. Attribute the belief to the officials who selected the site. Source: [*Rasul v. Bush*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/542/466/).

5. **False absolute about Iraqi weapons — high confidence.**  
   > “There were no weapons.”

   No active WMD programmes or stockpiles matching the invasion’s central claims were found. However, investigators did recover old, abandoned chemical munitions. The sentence both overstates the finding and breaks the prohibition on absolutes that exceptions invalidate. Source: [Congressional Research Service’s account of the findings](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL32379.html).

6. **The resolution was not one sentence — high confidence.**  
   > “Three days after the attacks, Congress passed a resolution a single sentence long.”

   > “The resolution was sixty words long.”

   The famous sixty words are the operative authorisation in section 2(a), not the entire resolution. The law also contains a preamble, a short-title section and War Powers provisions. This matters particularly because the closing image depends on it. Source: [Public Law 107–40](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-107publ40/html/PLAW-107publ40.htm).

7. **Misleading Afghanistan chronology — high confidence.**  
   > “Afghanistan was given a new government and a constitution, and for a few years it seemed a success.”

   > “Then the attention of Washington moved elsewhere.”

   Followed immediately by the Iraq section, this makes constitutional reconstruction and several successful years precede the turn towards Iraq. Afghanistan’s constitution was adopted in **January 2004**, after the March 2003 invasion of Iraq. The diversion of attention happened during reconstruction, not after this sequence. Source: [UN account of the constitution’s adoption](https://www.un.org/sg/en/content/former-secretary-general/statements/2004-01-15/secretary-generals-remarks-the-security-council-afghanistan).

8. **Destruction and damage conflated — high confidence.**  
   > “Most of the housing had been destroyed, and nearly the whole population had been driven from place to place, many of them several times.”

   The housing figures distinguish **destroyed** from **damaged**. OCHA’s August 2025 figures put 160,000 units in the former category and 276,000 in the latter; its October assessment likewise reports extensive damage and destruction together. “Most … destroyed” needs separate supporting evidence; “most … damaged or destroyed” is supported. Sources: [OCHA housing figures](https://www.un.org/unispal/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Gaza_Reported_Impact_Snapshot_20_August_2025.pdf), [October UNOSAT assessment](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/unosat-gaza-strip-damage-assessment-31oct25/).

9. **Court proceedings compressed into a misleading sequence — high confidence.**  
   > “The international criminal court issued warrants for the Israeli prime minister and for the military chief of Hamas.”

   This omits the warrant for former Israeli defence minister **Yoav Gallant**. It also leaves the reader thinking the Hamas warrant remained active at the chapter’s endpoint: proceedings against Mohammed Deif ended in February 2025 after confirmation of his death. Explain that these were individual war-crimes/crimes-against-humanity proceedings, separate from South Africa’s genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice. Sources: [ICC’s Israeli warrants](https://www.icc-cpi.int/news/situation-state-palestine-icc-pre-trial-chamber-i-rejects-state-israels-challenges), [termination of Deif proceedings](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/CourtRecords/0902ebd180ad67a3.pdf).

10. **Wrong tower timings and overstated entrapment — high confidence.**  
    > “Each burned for about an hour, with its upper floors cut off.”

    The South Tower stood for **56 minutes** after impact; the North Tower for **102 minutes**. Moreover, one South Tower stairwell remained passable, allowing people from at or above the impact area to escape. Sources: [NIST’s findings](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-towers-investigation), [9/11 Memorial account of the escape route](https://www.911memorial.org/node/105371).

11. **The firefighter total is turned into an invented common circumstance — high confidence.**  
    > “Three hundred and forty-three firefighters who had climbed the stairs to reach the trapped were inside.”

    The 343 is the total number of FDNY members killed, not a count of firefighters simultaneously inside the towers after climbing their stairs. It includes a chaplain and emergency medical personnel; deaths also occurred outside. State the departmental loss without assigning everyone the same final location and activity. Sources: [New York’s departmental accounting](https://www.nyc.gov/html/ops/downloads/pdf/mmr/0202_vol1.pdf), [9/11 Commission reconstruction](https://9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch9.htm).

12. **Islamic State’s area exaggerated — high confidence that the comparison needs changing.**  
    > “The Islamic State, as it called itself, soon held a territory the size of Britain with eight million people.”

    Territorial estimates vary, but the coalition’s figure was approximately **110,000 square kilometres**, around half the area of Great Britain. “The size of Britain” substantially overstates that estimate. Source: [Global Coalition statement](https://2017-2021.state.gov/joint-statement-by-the-political-directors-of-the-global-coalition-to-defeat-isis/).

13. **Unsupported breadth of surveillance — high confidence.**  
    > “At home, the agency that intercepts foreign signals was turned, in secret, on everyone.”

    > “In 2013 a young contractor fled to Hong Kong with a cache of its documents, which showed that it was collecting the records of nearly every telephone call made in the United States and tapping the cables that carry the world's internet traffic.”

    “Everyone” is an unsustainable absolute, and “nearly every” overstates the demonstrated coverage. Official reviews described collection from a limited set of providers; contemporary estimates suggested substantially incomplete coverage, particularly of mobile calls. Bulk collection on an enormous scale is supported without claiming near-total coverage. Source: [UK independent reviewer’s examination of the US programme, drawing on American oversight reports](https://terrorismlegislationreviewer.independent.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Bulk-Powers-Review-final-report.pdf).

14. **Two different Afghan force totals conflated — high confidence.**  
    > “The Taliban had come back from their sanctuaries across the border, and no number of foreign troops, which reached a hundred thousand, could hold the countryside for a government that its own people regarded as corrupt.”

    > “In the summer of 2021, as the last troops packed, the Afghan army, three hundred thousand strong on paper, dissolved in eleven days.”

    Roughly 100,000 describes the **American** peak, not all foreign troops: ISAF exceeded 130,000. The approximately 300,000 Afghan figure included **police and other security personnel**, not just the army. “On paper” addresses reliability but not the category error. Sources: [NATO’s force history](https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/operations-and-missions/isafs-mission-in-afghanistan-2001-2014), [breakdown of the Afghan total](https://www.factcheck.org/2021/08/factchecking-bidens-statements-about-afghanistan/).

15. **Disputed bank seizure presented as established fact — medium confidence.**  
    > “The attackers captured hundreds of armoured vehicles and the contents of the central bank.”

    The celebrated Mosul bank robbery was challenged by bankers interviewed shortly afterwards, who disputed that the reported money had been taken. Later Islamic State appropriation of banking assets does not establish this specific event at Mosul’s capture. Retain the captured military equipment; the bank detail needs stronger evidence. Source: [Financial Times investigation, syndicated](https://www.euro2day.gr/ftcom_en/article-ft-en/1237531/iraqi-bankers-say-400m-isis-heist-in-mosul-never.html).

16. **Refugee composition misstated — high confidence.**  
    > “In 2015 a million refugees, most of them Syrian, crossed to Europe in rubber boats.”

    UNHCR counted roughly a million **refugees and migrants arriving by sea**; Syrians constituted about **49 per cent**, the largest group but not a majority. The total also cannot uniformly be described as travelling in rubber boats. Source: [UNHCR’s Mediterranean arrivals account](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/legacy-pdf/5c4710d84.pdf).

17. **Two caliphate events merged — high confidence.**  
    > “A month later their leader stood in the pulpit of the city's great mosque and declared himself caliph, the successor of the Prophet and ruler of all Muslims.”

    The caliphate and Baghdadi’s appointment were announced in an audio statement on **29 June 2014**. His Mosul sermon followed in early July. The public appearance should not become the original proclamation. Source: [Brookings chronology](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/key-dates-in-the-rise-of-the-islamic-state-isis/).

18. **Uncertain deaths described too precisely — high confidence about the wording problem.**  
    > “Several hundred people trapped above the fire jumped.”

    “Several hundred” is not a securely established count, and “jumped” assigns the same action to people who may have fallen or been forced out by conditions. The Commission uses “jumped or fell.” A non-numerical description would preserve the horror without manufacturing certainty. Source: [9/11 Commission](https://9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch9.htm).

19. **A missing explanation at a major turning point.**  
    > “The Syrian government lasted until December 2024, when it fell to a rebel offensive in eleven days.”

    The preceding account says Iran and Russia kept the government in power. The reader then gets its collapse without knowing who defeated it or why its protectors could no longer save it. One or two bridging sentences are needed. Also distinguish the fall of **Assad’s regime** from the disappearance of government altogether.

20. **An unnamed person becomes an ambiguous reference.**  
    > “This was done to the planner of the attacks a hundred and eighty-three times.”

    Bin Laden is the only attack organiser the narrative has identified, so a general reader may reasonably assume this means him. It means **Khalid Sheikh Mohammed**. Identify him as the operational planner and distinguish him from bin Laden before giving the torture figure.

21. **An unexplained technical term at the centre of the scene.**  
    > “In February 2003 the American secretary of state, a former general whom the world trusted, sat before the United Nations Security Council and held up a small glass vial to show how little anthrax would be needed to kill thousands.”

    “Anthrax” needs a short explanation—disease-causing bacterial spores used as a biological weapon—and the vial should be identified as a **prop**, not actual anthrax. This is where `style.md`’s instruction to explain a term on first appearance matters: the reader needs to understand what the object demonstrates.

22. **An absolute that creates an unnecessary historical claim.**  
    > “The oldest conflict of the region had seemed, to many governments, to have been put aside.”

    “Oldest” is neither defined nor established; the preceding chapters themselves describe much older religious and political antagonisms. The point concerns governments sidelining the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. Its supposed seniority adds an avoidable claim and breaks the rule against fragile absolutes.
