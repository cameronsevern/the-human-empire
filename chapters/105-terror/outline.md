# Chapter 105 — What did 9/11 change?

**Line under the title:** The United States and the Middle East, from 2001 to 2025.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,800 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. Several of these events are recent and bitterly disputed; give figures with their source, state disagreements as disagreements, and write the last sections so that they stay true.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Nineteen men with knives killed three thousand people in a morning, and the most powerful country in the world answered with twenty years of war. It overthrew two governments, one that had sheltered the attackers and one that had not. In neither place could it build the state that it wanted, and each war left behind an armed movement, a grievance or a ruined country that fed the next: the occupation of Iraq produced the Islamic State, the Islamic State and a failed Arab uprising destroyed Syria, and the Taliban walked back into Kabul. At home and abroad the campaign added things that have not gone away: imprisonment without trial, killing by remote control, and the routine collection of everyone's messages. The oldest quarrel of the region, between Israelis and Palestinians, exploded again in 2023 on a scale that neither had seen.
