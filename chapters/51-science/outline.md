# Chapter 51 — How did science begin?

**Line under the title:** Europe, with debts to Cairo, Persia and Samarkand, from 1543 to 1758.
**Length:** 4,300 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Science as we know it began when arguments about nature stopped being settled by what the great books said and started being settled by measurements that a stranger could repeat. Nobody decided this on a particular day. It grew over about a century and a half in Europe, out of new instruments that showed things no ancient author had seen, printed reports that let people far apart check one another, and societies whose rule was to take nobody's word. It was built on older work, much of it written in Arabic, and it was paid for in part by navies and gunners who needed better mathematics. The church's trial of Galileo was a fight about who had the right to say what is true, at a time when the church had just lost half of Europe over that question.

## The story

1. **Opening: four nights in January.** Answer first. Galileo, a home-made telescope and the little stars beside Jupiter.
2. **The man who would not look.** Why sensible people doubted the tube. The old picture of the heavens and why it had lasted.
3. **Borrowed circles.** Copernicus's book, and the Persian and Syrian astronomers whose geometry is in it. The Cairo scholar who tested light in a dark room.
4. **Eight minutes.** A Danish nobleman's twenty years of measurements, and a German mathematician who threw away his own theory because it missed by a hair.
5. **The trial.** Venus, the warning of 1616, the book of 1632, and why Rome could not let it pass.
6. **Take nobody's word.** Experiments before witnesses, the first journal, and a Dutch cloth merchant who saw living things in a drop of water.
7. **Gunners and navigators.** Who paid, and what they wanted.
8. **The comet comes back.** Newton, a book nearly unpublished for want of money, and a prediction kept after the men who made it were dead.

## Left for later

Chemistry, medicine and the Enlightenment; the steam engine; longitude solved by a clock.
