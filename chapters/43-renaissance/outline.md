# Chapter 43 — What was the Renaissance?

**Line under the title:** Florence and the cities of Italy, from about 1340 to 1527.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The Renaissance was a movement among a few thousand people in the rich trading cities of northern Italy, who decided that the Greeks and Romans had done most things better than anyone since, and set out to match them. Merchants and bankers paid for it, because their cities had no kings, competed with one another and ran on reading and arithmetic. Scholars hunted down the old books and learned to date a document by its language. Artists learned to paint the world as the eye sees it, using geometry, and turned from craftsmen into celebrities. The movement left the idea that the past was a different country that could be studied, that a person could be measured by talent, and that the present could equal the ancients. It ended in Italy when the country's wealth drew in foreign armies with cannon. The word itself was coined afterwards.

## The story

1. **Opening: a hole in the roof.** Answer first. Florence's cathedral, planned with a dome that nobody knew how to build. The contest of 1418 and the goldsmith who won it.
2. **Four million bricks.** What Brunelleschi had learned among the ruins of Rome, and how the dome went up with no frame under it.
3. **A city of bookkeepers.** Why Florence: cloth, the gold florin, banks, children at school, no king. The Medici.
4. **Letters to a dead man.** Petrarch and Cicero. The book hunters in the monasteries. Greek comes back. Reading a document's language to find its date.
5. **A window in the wall.** Perspective: the mirror at the cathedral door, the painted chapel. Bodies studied from corpses. Painters stop being tradesmen.
6. **The banker's conscience.** Why rich men paid: sin, rivalry and advertisement. The giant in the square. A ceiling in Rome.
7. **Ten kinds of weapon.** Leonardo's letter asking for a job, and his notebooks.
8. **Things as they are.** The French guns of 1494, Machiavelli's little book, the sack of Rome. Ending: the new St Peter's and its bill.

## Left for later

Gutenberg's press and Erasmus. Luther. Columbus and Vespucci. Copernicus and Galileo. The painters of Flanders and Germany.
