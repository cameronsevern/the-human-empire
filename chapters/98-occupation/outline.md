# Chapter 98 — Why can't Israelis and Palestinians make peace?

**Line under the title:** Israel and the Palestinian territories, from 1967 to 2007.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. Two peoples tell this story differently; where they disagree about what happened, set out both accounts plainly and side by side.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

In six days in 1967 Israel took the rest of the land that both peoples claim, and with it more than a million Palestinians who were given no citizenship of any state. That turned a quarrel between Israel and its neighbours into a struggle over one small territory and the people living on it. Peace proved possible where land could be handed back to a state in return for recognition, as with Egypt. It has not been possible where two national movements want the same hills and the same city, where one of them has gone on building towns on the disputed ground, and where, each time leaders came close, men on both sides who rejected any compromise answered with murder. Each people has its own account of why the talks failed, and each account contains things that are true.

1. **Opening: six days.** Answer first. June 1967: fear, a morning's air strike, and soldiers at a wall.
2. **Land for peace.** What Israel now held and who lived there. A resolution with a missing word; three refusals.
3. **A hotel at Passover.** Military rule. The first settlers, and the belief that drove them. The numbers, decade by decade, and the law.
4. **The gun and the olive branch.** The Palestinian movement in exile: Arafat, hijackings, Munich, the United Nations, Lebanon. A massacre in two camps and a protest in Tel Aviv.
5. **A flight to Jerusalem.** 1973, Sadat, Camp David: the peace that held, and what it cost the man who made it.
6. **Stones.** The uprising of 1987. A new movement in Gaza.
7. **A handshake.** Oslo, 1993: what was agreed and what was left for later. A doctor in Hebron; bombs on buses; a rally in Tel Aviv.
8. **Two accounts of a summer.** Camp David, 2000. The second uprising. A barrier.
9. **Gaza.** The withdrawal of 2005, the election of 2006, and a people split in two.
10. **Four questions.** Borders, Jerusalem, refugees, security: why each is hard.
11. **Ending: thirty-five acres.**
