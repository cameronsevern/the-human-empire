1. **Factual error and contradiction with chapter 85 — high confidence.**  
   > “It also held about a million Palestinian Arabs, who were now citizens of nowhere.”

   West Bank Palestinians generally retained Jordanian citizenship after Israel occupied the territory in 1967. Jordan’s withdrawal of nationality from West Bank residents came in **1988**. Gaza’s inhabitants had a different citizenship history. Chapter 85 explicitly says: “The Arab states, except Jordan, declined to make the refugees citizens.” The new sentence erases that distinction. [Source: Human Rights Watch’s nationality investigation](https://www.hrw.org/report/2010/02/01/stateless-again/palestinian-origin-jordanians-deprived-their-nationality).

2. **Factual error affecting the central explanation — high confidence.**  
   > “The last serious attempt was made in July 2000, when another American president brought an Israeli prime minister and Arafat to the same retreat in Maryland for two weeks.”

   Serious negotiations continued after Camp David, notably at **Taba in January 2001**, still within this chapter’s period. The negotiators’ joint statement said they had never been closer to agreement. Omitting Taba makes the failure of July 2000 look like the end of diplomacy rather than one stage in it. [Source: Israeli–Palestinian joint statement](https://heskem.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/Taba-Declaration.pdf).

3. **Unsupported absolute — high confidence.**  
   > “Each time the two sides came close, men who rejected any compromise answered with murder.”

   Rabin’s assassination and attacks intended to derail negotiations support a narrower argument. They do not establish this explanation for **every** near-agreement. Taba, for example, ended ahead of Israel’s election, with both delegations proposing renewed negotiations. This breaks the rule against absolutes that one exception defeats; the problem is the universal causal claim, not the narrator taking a position. [Source: Taba joint statement](https://heskem.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/Taba-Declaration.pdf).

4. **Misleading ending — high confidence.**  
   > “That arrangement, made in an afternoon, has held for more than fifty years.”  
   > “It is among the few things in that city that both sides have agreed to leave alone.”

   Muslim administration and restrictions on Jewish prayer survived as broad principles, but the arrangements governing access, policing and worship repeatedly changed and were fiercely contested. A detailed historical study identifies a breakdown of the earlier understanding during **1996–2003**, followed by further erosion. Even within the chapter, the account of Sharon’s visit makes “agreed to leave alone” difficult to sustain. [Source: Jerusalem Institute, *The Eroding Status-Quo*](https://jerusaleminstitute.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/PUB_ERODINGSTATUSQUO-2017_eng.pdf).

5. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.**  
   > “The world's governments refused to deal with a party that would not recognise Israel.”

   The boycott was not universal. Russia received a Hamas delegation in Moscow in March 2006. The Quartet’s conditions also included renouncing violence and accepting previous agreements, not recognition alone. Identify the governments imposing restrictions rather than making a worldwide claim. [Sources: Russian foreign minister’s statement](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-194155/), [Quartet conditions](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-195262/).

6. **Factual error — high confidence.**  
   > “Israel built a barrier seven hundred kilometres long, of fence and in the towns of concrete slabs eight metres high.”

   Roughly 700 kilometres describes the **planned route**, not the completed barrier. Even in September 2017, approximately 460 kilometres—65% of the planned 712 kilometres—had been completed. The sentence substantially overstates construction during the period being narrated. [Source: B’Tselem, using OCHA construction figures](https://www.btselem.org/separation_barrier).

7. **Misleading account of border control — high confidence.**  
   > “Israel kept control of Gaza's borders, its coast and its sky.”

   Israel retained extensive external control, but the Egyptian crossing needs distinguishing. Under the November 2005 agreement, **Rafah was operated by the Palestinian Authority and Egypt, with European monitoring**, alongside agreed Israeli monitoring and coordination provisions. The sentence implies identical Israeli control over every border crossing. [Source: Agreement on Movement and Access and Rafah principles](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-197357/).

8. **Wrong signatories — high confidence.**  
   > “On 13 September 1993, on the lawn of the White House, Rabin and Arafat signed the result.”

   **Shimon Peres and Mahmoud Abbas** signed the Declaration of Principles for Israel and the PLO. Rabin and Arafat attended and shook hands. Their political responsibility for the agreement does not make them its signatories. [Source: declaration and signature block](https://mideastinfo.com/documents/Oslo.htm).

9. **Incorrect negotiating timetable — high confidence.**  
   > “Everything difficult was put off for five years: the borders, the settlements, Jerusalem and the refugees.”

   Oslo established a five-year transitional period, but permanent-status negotiations were supposed to begin **no later than the beginning of its third year**. The sentence incorrectly makes five years a waiting period before tackling those questions. [Source: Declaration of Principles, Article V](https://www.gov.il/en/pages/declaration-of-principles).

10. **Conflated attacks and misleading chronology — high confidence.**  
    > “Six weeks later Hamas sent its first suicide bomber onto an Israeli bus.”

    The attack six weeks after the Hebron massacre was the **6 April 1994 Afula bombing: a car bomb beside a bus**, not a bomber boarding it. Hamas had also carried out a suicide bombing in April 1993. The present wording encourages the mistaken inference that Hamas first adopted suicide bombing after Hebron. [Sources: contemporary Israeli report to the UN](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-179341/), [ICCT historical account](https://icct.nl/sites/default/files/2023-01/Handbook_Schmid_2020.pdf).

11. **Incorrect historical geography and a brittle absolute — high confidence.**  
    > “Tel Aviv and the coast, where most Israelis lived, had never been part of the ancient kingdoms.”

    “The coast” is too sweeping. **Dor**, on the Mediterranean coast, was controlled by the kingdom of Israel during the ninth and eighth centuries BCE. The distinction between the biblical hill country and modern coastal population centres can stand without claiming that the ancient kingdoms never possessed coastal territory. [Source: archaeological research in *Antiquity*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/iron-age-ship-cargoes-from-the-harbour-of-dor-israel/D44019724498E1221A1E026E25FFEA04).

12. **Wrong territorial multiplier — high confidence on the correction, approximate on the rounded figure.**  
    > “When it ended, Israel held three times as much land as it had a week earlier.”

    Including Sinai, the territory under Israeli control became roughly **four times** its prewar extent. The sentence appears to confuse acquiring about three times the original area with holding three times the original total. [Source: Smithsonian’s historical account](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/what-six-day-war-tells-us-about-cold-war-180963590/).

13. **Wrong identification of the Western Wall — high confidence.**  
    > “It was the last remnant of the Temple that the Romans had destroyed.”

    The Western Wall is part of the **retaining wall of the enlarged Temple Mount**, not the Temple building itself; other retaining walls also survive. The chapter’s final section correctly calls it a retaining wall, creating an internal inconsistency. [Source: archaeological explanation in *Biblical Archaeology Review*](https://www.biblosfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/9BAR.Article.TempleMt.pdf).

14. **Unmarked shift in date — high confidence.**  
    > “Gaza, a strip forty kilometres long with two million people, was sealed off by Israel and Egypt.”

    In the immediately preceding **2007** setting, Gaza had about **1.4 million inhabitants**, not two million. A later population figure needs an explicit later date. The subsequent references to “thirty years,” “now nearly six million,” and “more than fifty years” further blur where the narrator is standing in time. [Source: Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics, 2007 census](https://www.pcbs.gov.ps/Downloads/book1824.pdf).

15. **Compressed chronology obscures a crucial intervening event — high confidence.**  
    > “A few days after they had left, with the Israeli army ringing the area and lighting it with flares, a Lebanese Christian militia allied to Israel entered two Palestinian refugee camps on the edge of the city.”

    The PLO evacuation ended on **1 September 1982**; the massacre began on **16 September**. Between them came the assassination of president-elect Bashir Gemayel and Israel’s entry into West Beirut. “A few days” should be about two weeks, and the intervening change matters to understanding why the militia entered then. [Source: Sciences Po’s documented chronology](https://www.sciencespo.fr/mass-violence-war-massacre-resistance/en/document/sabra-and-chatila.html).

16. **Overstatement of military destruction — high confidence.**  
    > “The air forces of Syria and Jordan were destroyed that afternoon.”

    Jordan’s small air force was destroyed, but Syria lost **most**, not all, of its fighter aircraft. “Crippled” would preserve the decisive military point without claiming total destruction. [Source: contemporary US intelligence assessment](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v19/d414).

17. **Incorrect description of the victims — high confidence.**  
    > “At the Olympic Games in Munich in 1972, a Palestinian squad took the Israeli team hostage, and eleven athletes were killed.”

    The eleven murdered Israeli delegation members included **athletes, coaches and officials**. The attackers also did not take the entire Israeli team hostage. [Source: International Olympic Committee](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/munich-1972-era-defining-games-of-joy-and-tragedy).

18. **Reader loses track of the organisations.**  
    > “Leadership of their national organisation passed in 1969 to the head of its largest guerrilla group, a short, unshaven engineer in a checked headscarf named Yasser Arafat.”  
    > “Arafat's old movement, which ran the authority, had become known for corruption, and voters gave a majority of the seats to Hamas.”

    Neither **the Palestine Liberation Organization, the umbrella organisation, nor Fatah, Arafat’s faction**, is named or clearly distinguished. By the election, the reader cannot tell whether “old movement,” “national organisation” and “authority” mean the same institution. These names earn their place because the distinction drives the story.

19. **Reader cannot reliably identify the recurring man.**  
    > “Two months later the leader of the Israeli opposition, the former defence minister, walked onto the mosque compound in Jerusalem with a thousand policemen.”

    Ariel Sharon has never been named. The reader must connect him to the unnamed minister in the 1982 massacre passage, across intervening appearances by other defence ministers, then recognise him again as the prime minister withdrawing from Gaza. Naming him at his first consequential appearance would fulfil, rather than violate, the style guide’s allowance for recurring people.

20. **Missing geographical handoff.**  
    > “The West Bank and the strip of coast around Gaza were placed under military government, which was expected to be brief.”

    The conquest narrative never says that Israel **captured Gaza from Egypt**. Chapter 85 leaves Gaza under Egyptian control, so this sentence silently changes its ruler. The earlier conquest paragraph also leaves the Golan Heights unnamed, making the later references to returning “the heights” harder to place.

21. **Unsupported absolute that erases Palestinian political activity — high confidence.**  
    > “Neither the resolution nor anyone else had much to say about the Palestinians, who appeared in it only as a refugee problem.”

    The criticism of Resolution 242’s treatment of Palestinians is defensible. “Nor anyone else” is not: the PLO had existed since 1964, and Palestinian representation and national claims were already subjects of organised politics and diplomacy. Confine the observation to the resolution or the particular diplomatic framework. [Source: contemporary State Department account of Palestinian organisation](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v18/d356).

22. **Unnecessary numerical claim that fails on the page.**  
    > “Their commander sent a radio message of five words: the Temple Mount is in our hands.”

    The English rendering supplied contains **eight words**, not five. The famous Hebrew phrase is commonly transliterated *Har habayit beyadenu*. The word count contributes nothing necessary and should be removed rather than defended through translation conventions. [Source: historian Gershom Gorenberg’s account](https://sites.google.com/site/ithacaseminarjerusalem/home/temple-mount-who-owns-it/2000-8-14-gershon-gorenberg-on-the-heart-of-the-matter).

23. **Signpost sentence and inconsistent enumeration.**  
    > “The uprising had two results.”

    This announces the explanation instead of stating it, contrary to the prohibition on signpost sentences. The next paragraph then adds “It also produced a rival,” effectively introducing a third result. Beginning directly with what the uprising persuaded Israelis or the exiled leadership to believe would resolve both problems.
