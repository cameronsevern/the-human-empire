# Chapter 27 — Why did the Roman Empire fall?

**Line under the title:** The Roman world from Britain to Syria, from about the year 235 to 565.
**Length:** 4,800 to 5,400 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Only half of it fell. The Roman Empire was, in the end, a machine for collecting taxes and paying soldiers. In the third century the machine nearly broke, and the emperors who repaired it made it bigger, harsher and split in two. In the fifth century the western half lost its richest provinces to armed peoples it had let in and could not expel, above all the grain and gold of North Africa. With no money there were no soldiers, and with no soldiers the landowners of Gaul, Spain and Italy made their own terms with whichever king was nearest. The eastern half kept Egypt, kept its taxes, paid its army, bought off or outlasted the same enemies, and went on for another thousand years. People at the time did not see a fall. They saw a series of deals.

## The story

1. **Opening: the unpaid garrison.** Answer first. Soldiers on the Danube send men to Italy to fetch their pay; the bodies float back down the river. What an army costs: the empire as a payroll.
2. **Bad silver.** The third-century crisis: fifty years, twenty-odd emperors, Persia reborn under the Sasanians, an emperor captured, plague. The coin debased to nothing. Why raising pay was the one thing every emperor did.
3. **Four emperors and a price list.** Diocletian's repair: a bigger army, taxes in kind, people tied to their jobs, the edict on prices. Constantine's new capital and gold coin. An empire that now has two halves and two courts.
4. **The other empire.** Persia as an equal: the rock carving of the kneeling emperor. Why the east could never strip its frontier.
5. **The river crossing.** The Huns push the Goths. 376: a whole people is let across the Danube and starved. Adrianople. The new kind of treaty: a people with its own king inside the frontier.
6. **Three days in Rome.** Alaric wants a command and pay. 410. Britain told to look after itself. The Rhine crossing.
7. **The grain ships stop.** The Vandals cross to Africa; Carthage, 439. What Africa was to the western treasury. The failed armada of 468. Attila as a business. The landowners' choice: a bishop dines with a Gothic king.
8. **A boy sent to the country.** 476, and how little it changed on the day. What did change over two centuries: pots, roof tiles, cows, reading.
9. **The half that lasted.** Constantinople's walls, Egypt's grain, gold enough to pay everyone. Justinian reconquers, builds, codifies the law, and the plague arrives.
