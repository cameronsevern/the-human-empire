# Chapter 59 — How did Napoleon conquer Europe?

**Line under the title:** Europe, from 1793 to 1815.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Napoleon conquered Europe with the army that the French Revolution had made: enormous, conscripted from a whole nation, and led by men promoted for ability. He used it faster than armies had ever been used, by splitting it into self-contained bodies that marched separately, fed themselves from the country and came together to fight. Wherever he won he abolished the privileges of birth and installed one law for everyone, and he also took the money, the paintings and the young men. He could not reach Britain, which ruled the sea and paid his enemies. He was beaten when the peoples he had conquered learned his own lesson, that a nation in arms fights harder than a king's hired soldiers. That lesson, the nation, outlasted him and remade the map of Europe.

## The story

1. **Opening: a captain of artillery.** Answer first. The siege of a French port in 1793 and an officer of twenty-four.
2. **A Corsican.** Where he came from, what the Revolution offered a poor officer, how a general became first consul and then emperor. The crown and the pope. How tall he was.
3. **March divided.** How the army worked: conscripts, sergeants who became marshals, the corps, the speed. An Austrian army captured by marching. The battle of December 1805.
4. **One law.** What he did with what he conquered: the code, the end of feudal dues, careers by merit. Brothers on thrones, and the horses of Venice.
5. **The whale.** Britain: a sea battle, a blockade, and a government that could borrow.
6. **The little war.** Spain, 1808: priests, peasants and a new word.
7. **A nation of Germans.** What occupation taught the conquered. Lectures in Berlin. A beaten kingdom frees its serfs and drafts its men.
8. **Moscow.** 1812: six hundred thousand men go in.
9. **The hundred days.** The battle of the nations, the island, the return, Waterloo, the second island. What it cost.
10. **Ending: what stayed.** The kings come back, the code remains, and the nation in arms becomes everyone's method.
