# Chapter 1 fact-check

Method note: items marked [searched] were checked against web results this session. Items marked [background] were judged from well-established literature I know but did not re-fetch; spot-check them if the stakes are high.

## Summary
- WRONG: 20 ("all seven came home")
- PARTLY: 11, 21, 23, 29 (plus 22 NOT FOUND, 18 shaft-heating detail)
- CONFIRMED: the rest (22 of 30)

## 1. Split "some seven million years ago" - CONFIRMED [background]
Genetic and fossil estimates run about 6-8 million years. "Some seven million" is a fair round figure. Britannica / Smithsonian Human Origins "Human Family Tree" give 6-7+ mya.

## 2. Laetoli / Lucy - CONFIRMED [searched]
Tracks dated about 3.66 mya, Tanzania, ash wetted by rain, attributed to Australopithecus afarensis. Trackmaker height estimates range from about 1.0 m (G1) to 1.3-1.5 m, with one 2016 estimate of 1.65 m for a new individual. "A little over a metre" is on the small side but within the range for the original three. Lucy is from Hadar, Ethiopia. Brain about 400-500 cc, chimp-sized.
https://elifesciences.org/articles/19568 ; https://www.pubmed.gov/27964778/
Optional tweak: "They stood roughly a metre to a metre and a half tall" if you want to cover the range.

## 3. Upright two million years before brains grew; tripled - CONFIRMED [background]
Bipedalism is evidenced from at least 4-6 mya (Ardipithecus, Orrorin, Australopithecus), brains stayed near 400-500 cc until early Homo about 2-2.5 mya, then rose to about 1,350 cc (about 3x). "At least two million years" is true (it was nearer four).

## 4. "No other animal throws both hard and straight" - CONFIRMED (fair) [background]
Roach et al. 2013 (Nature) show human throwing relies on shoulder energy storage; chimps throw far slower and less accurately. Fine as written.

## 5. Sweat, antelope panting, Kalahari persistence hunt - CONFIRMED [background]
Bramble and Lieberman 2004 (Nature) on endurance running; quadrupeds' breathing is locked to gait when galloping so they cannot pant effectively. Persistence hunts by San hunters were filmed (Attenborough, The Life of Mammals; Liebenberg). Fine.

## 6. Brain 1/50 of weight, 1/5 of energy - CONFIRMED [background]
About 2% of body mass, about 20% of resting energy.

## 7. Newborn brain a quarter; birth harder; chimp mothers alone - CONFIRMED [background]
Newborn human brain about 25-30% of adult size (chimp newborn about 40%). Obstetric difficulty and assisted birth: Rosenberg and Trevathan. Chimpanzee females typically give birth alone. Fine.

## 8. Chimp weaning at 4-5; forager children net consumers into teens - CONFIRMED [background]
Wild chimp weaning about 4-5 years. Kaplan et al. 2000 show forager children produce less than they consume until about age 18-20.

## 9. Birth intervals - CONFIRMED [background]
Chimps about 5.5-6 years (Gombe, Taï); Ju/'hoansi about 4, Hadza about 3.3.

## 10. Chimp mothers rarely let others hold the baby - CONFIRMED [background]
Hrdy, Mothers and Others: chimp infants are held by others very little in early months. Fine.

## 11. Female mammals / grandmothers / Hadza - PARTLY [searched]
- "Almost every other female mammal goes on having young until she dies": mostly right, but killer whales, pilot whales and a few other toothed whales have a post-reproductive life, and in 2023 Wood et al. (Science) found wild Ngogo chimpanzees with menopause-like decline and about a fifth of adult years post-reproductive. "Almost every" survives, but "every other ... until she dies" is no longer clean, and the chapter elsewhere says humans are unique among apes.
- Hadza: Hawkes's work shows older Hadza women forage hard and that child growth tracked the foraging of the mother, then the grandmother once the mother had a new baby. "Dig the most roots" is stronger than the evidence I could confirm.
https://news.arizona.edu/news/researchers-find-first-proof-menopause-wild-chimpanzees ; https://csbs.utah.edu/news/grandmothers-influence-human-evolution.php
Suggested replacement: "Among nearly all other mammals, a female keeps having young until she dies, and the few exceptions are whales. Women stop in middle age and then live for decades more. Among the Hadza, who still forage in Tanzania, grandmothers dig roots as hard as anyone in the camp, and a toddler whose mother has a new baby grows faster if its grandmother is working hard."

## 12. Flakes about 3 mya; hand axe over a million years - CONFIRMED [background]
Lomekwi, Kenya, 3.3 mya (Harmand 2015); Oldowan about 2.6 mya (Gona, Ethiopia). "About three million years ago, in East Africa" fits. Acheulean hand axes about 1.7 mya to about 0.3 mya, over a million years.

## 13. Wonderwerk and 400,000-year hearths - CONFIRMED [background]
Berna et al. 2012 (PNAS): burnt bone and plant ash at Wonderwerk Cave, about 1.0 mya. Roebroeks and Villa 2011: regular hearths in Europe from about 400 kya. Fine.

## 14. Chewing; raw food and weight loss - CONFIRMED [background]
Organ et al. 2011 (Nature): chimps spend about 37% of the day chewing (about 6 h), humans about 5%. Koebnick et al. 1999 (Giessen Raw Food Study) found weight loss on raw diets. Wrangham, Catching Fire.

## 15. Ju/'hoansi day and night talk - CONFIRMED [background]
Wiessner 2014 (PNAS, "Embers of society"): about 34% of daytime talk was economic and much criticism/gossip; about 81% of night talk was stories.

## 16. Vervet alarm calls - CONFIRMED [background]
Seyfarth, Cheney and Marler 1980: leopard call, run into trees; eagle call, look up and run into bushes; snake call, stand and scan the ground. Amboseli, Kenya.

## 17. Most conversation about people - CONFIRMED [background]
Dunbar, Duncan and Nettle 1997: about 65% of conversation time on social topics.

## 18. Sibudu glue / heated shafts - PARTLY [background]
Wadley, Hodgskiss and Grant 2009 (PNAS): compound adhesive of plant gum and red ochre at Sibudu, about 70 kya, with hafted stone points. Confirmed. But wooden shafts do not survive, and I know of no direct African evidence from that date of shafts straightened by heat. Stone-heating (Pinnacle Point, about 160 kya) is evidenced instead.
Suggested replacement: "Take a spear with a stone point. The point is a flake, shaped to be thin at the base. It is fixed on with glue, cooked from plant gum and mixed with powdered red earth so that it does not crack, and then bound tight. The stone itself was often heated first to make it flake better."

## 19. Franklin expedition - CONFIRMED [background]
Erebus and Terror, sailed May 1845, 129 men, steam engines, provisions for three years, beset off King William Island in September 1846, abandoned April 1848 (about 19 months, so "a year and a half" is fair). McClintock found a boat with silver cutlery. All died. Netsilik territory. Britannica "Sir John Franklin".

## 20. Amundsen - WRONG on "all seven came home" [searched]
Gjøa expedition 1903-06: Amundsen plus six men (seven in all), small herring sloop, two winters at Gjøa Haven beside a Netsilik camp (1903-05), then a third winter. Second engineer Gustav Juel Wiik fell ill and died on 31 March 1906, aged 27, and was buried at King Point. Six came home. "Sixty years later" is 58 years: fine. South Pole 1911: fine.
https://frammuseum.no/polar-history/explorers/gustav-juel-wiik-1878-1906/ ; https://amundsen.mia.no/en/expeditions/gjoa-expedition/
Suggested replacement: "He spent two winters next to a Netsilik camp and used the time to learn how they dressed, how they drove dogs and how they built with snow. Six of the seven came home; the one who died was killed by illness, not by the cold. A few years after that, Amundsen used what the Netsilik had taught him to become the first man to reach the South Pole."
(Shorter: "Six of the seven came home.")

## 21. Herrmann 2007 and Horner and Whiten 2005 - PARTLY [searched]
- Herrmann et al.: children were 2.5 years old, the apes were chimpanzees AND orangutans. Children and chimps scored similarly on physical tasks; children more than twice as good on social tasks. "Two-year-old" should be "two-and-a-half-year-old". Fine otherwise.
- Horner and Whiten: chimps skipped the irrelevant step only when the box was clear (causal information visible). With an opaque box they copied everything. Children (3-4 years old) copied everything in both conditions. So "a chimpanzee skips the step" is true only for a see-through box.
https://courses.washington.edu/ccab/Herrmann%20et%20al%20-%20human%20social%20cog%20-%20Science%202007.pdf ; https://www.emory.edu/LIVING_LINKS/publications/articles/Horner_Whiten_2005.pdf
Suggested replacement: "Give a two-and-a-half-year-old child and an adult chimpanzee the same puzzles about space, numbers and cause, and they score about the same. ... Shown a clear box with a pointless extra step in the way of opening it, a chimpanzee skips the step and goes for the reward. A child copies everything, pointless step included."

## 22. Bands of about thirty; community of "perhaps a thousand" - PARTLY / NOT FOUND [searched]
Band of about thirty: confirmed. Hill et al. 2011 (Science), 32 foraging societies, mean band about 28 adults (note: adults, so total about 40-50 with children; "about thirty" is a fair round figure for camp size in many surveys). The "thousand" figure is not from Hill. Hill et al. 2014 show adults from many bands meet regularly; they estimate foragers see 300+ other men's tool-making in a lifetime. The "about 1,000" figure matches Dunbar-style ethnolinguistic-group estimates, not Hill.
https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/311043 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4105570
Suggested replacement: "There are about thirty people in it ... Beyond the camp there are other bands who speak the same language, some hundreds of people in all, and a person will know most of them by sight or by name before he dies." Also change the chapter's closing "a few hundred familiar faces" (already consistent with this).

## 23. Women bring home most food; hunters empty-handed; large kill once in weeks - PARTLY [searched/background]
True for the Ju/'hoansi (gathering about 60% of calories by weight) and Hadza (women supply most calories; hunters succeeded on about 3.4% of excursions). Not true across all foragers: across Marlowe's sample, hunting provides more calories in colder places. As a scene set in Africa it is fair, but the sentence is stated as universal.
Suggested replacement: "In a warm country like this the women bring home most of what the camp eats, and they bring it home every day. The men go looking for meat, and most days they come back with nothing. A large animal is killed perhaps once in several weeks."

## 24. Insulting the meat - CONFIRMED [background]
Richard Lee, "Eating Christmas in the Kalahari" (Natural History, 1969), and The !Kung San (1979).

## 25. Bullies mocked, deserted, sometimes killed - CONFIRMED [background]
Boehm, Hierarchy in the Forest (1999); Boehm 1993 Current Anthropology "Egalitarian behavior and reverse dominance hierarchy".

## 26. Child mortality; survivors reach 60-70 - CONFIRMED [background]
Gurven and Kaplan 2007 (Population and Development Review): about 48% of foragers die before 15; modal adult age at death about 68-78. Volk and Atkinson 2013 (Evolution and Human Behavior): about 49% die before age 15 in 10 hunter-gatherer groups.

## 27. The clock - CONFIRMED (arithmetic checked)
300,000 years = 1,440 minutes, so 1 minute = 208 years. Farming at 12,000 years = 57.6 min before midnight, i.e. about 11:02 pm ("a little after eleven"). Cities at 5,000 years = 24 min before midnight, i.e. 11:36 pm. The text says "about twenty-five to midnight"; 24 is closer, but "about" covers it. Industry from about 1760 (about 260 years) = 1.25 min. All correct.

## 28. Cars vs snakes - CONFIRMED [searched]
WHO 2023: about 1.19 million road deaths a year. WHO snakebite: 81,000-138,000 deaths a year. Cars kill roughly ten times as many.
https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240086517 ; https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/snakebite-envenoming

## 29. Ultimatum game - PARTLY [searched]
Henrich et al. 2001 (15 small-scale societies): behaviour varied widely and rejection of low offers was often rare (Machiguenga mean offer 26%, overall rejection 4.8%; some groups rejected generous offers). Rejection of very low offers is the norm in industrial societies (US, Europe, Japan, Israel etc.), but "in most places" overstates the small-scale picture.
https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/mic/papers/0305/0305009.pdf
Suggested replacement: "If the share is insultingly small, people in countries all over the world turn it down."
Also soften the follow-on slightly: keep "They would sooner lose money than let a cheat get away with it."

## 30. Minimal groups - CONFIRMED [background]
Tajfel, Billig, Bundy and Flament 1971; Billig and Tajfel 1973 (explicitly random assignment). Group favouritism appears at once.

## Other statements to consider
- "Our closest living relatives are the chimpanzees": bonobos are equally close. Suggested: "Our closest living relatives are the chimpanzees and bonobos."
- "A newborn chimpanzee can cling to its mother within days": chimp newborns grip from birth. Suggested: "can cling to its mother from birth."
- "Fear of snakes, spiders, heights ... We fear what killed our ancestors": spiders very rarely killed anyone. Suggested: "We fear snakes, heights and the dark, which were real dangers to our ancestors."
- "The oldest lesson we have any trace of was taught in stone": no evidence of teaching at 3 mya, only of toolmaking. Suggested: "The oldest skill we have any trace of was worked in stone."
- "No expedition had ever been better equipped": superlative; suggested "Few expeditions had been better equipped."
- "Hand axe ... Knowledge was being handed down, but hardly anything was being added": fair simplification, leave.
- "Stone Age" band 100,000 years ago: scene is fine as framed.
