# Chapter 1 — What is a human?

**Line under the title:** Africa, from about seven million years ago to about 50,000 years ago.
**Length:** 5,000 to 7,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md` at the repository root.

The movements below are the story, not section headings. Items marked **[CHECK]** need a reference before drafting.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

A human is an ape that cannot survive alone. Our bodies are weak, our children are helpless for years, and almost nothing we know was worked out by the person who knows it. We live by cooperating and by passing on what we learn. Much of what we feel today, from the pull of sugar to the sting of being left out, was shaped by several hundred thousand years of life in small bands where food was uncertain and other people were both the greatest help and the greatest danger.

## The story

1. **The animal that took over.** Open in the present. One species has spread to every continent, and humans and their farm animals now far outweigh all the wild mammals on Earth. **[CHECK: biomass shares]** Then go back 100,000 years. The same species is a minor animal in Africa, not the strongest or the fastest, and few in number. **[CHECK: population estimates]** What happened in between is the subject of the book. What kind of animal this was is the subject of the chapter.

2. **An ape that stood up.** We are apes. Our line and the chimpanzees' split several million years ago in Africa. **[CHECK: date range]** Follow the body as it changed: walking upright, hands free to carry and throw, a body that sweats and can outlast an antelope on a hot day, and a brain that grew to three times the size of a chimpanzee's. The brain is expensive: it burns about a fifth of the body's energy while doing nothing visible. **[CHECK]** Say briefly how any of this is known: bones, footprints, and the DNA of living people.

3. **The helpless child.** A large head and a narrow upright pelvis meant babies had to be born early and unfinished. A foal walks within hours. A human child needs feeding for well over a decade. No mother could do that alone, so fathers, grandmothers, siblings and neighbours fed and carried children who were not their own. From the first, humans depended on each other.

4. **Stone and fire.** A sharp stone flake did the work of teeth and claws. **[CHECK: earliest tools]** Fire did more. It gave light, warmth and safety at night, and cooking let the gut take far more energy from the same food, which helped pay for the brain. **[CHECK: earliest secure evidence of controlled fire]** Fire is the first energy source outside the body. The hearth also made a place where people sat together after dark with nothing to do but talk.

5. **Talk.** Other animals signal. Humans describe. With language a person can say where the water is, who shared and who cheated, what happened to a grandfather, and what might happen tomorrow. People can talk about things that do not exist: plans, promises, spirits. Nobody knows when language began, since words leave no fossils. Its effect is clear: a group that can gossip can keep track of who is trustworthy, and a group that can tell stories can agree on what it is.

6. **The ratchet.** No one alive could make a bow, the glue that holds its arrowhead, the poison on the tip and the fire to cook the catch, starting from nothing. Each of these was worked out once, taught, and improved. Knowledge piles up across generations in a way it does not in any other animal. This is the central idea: humans are not individually clever enough to survive in the wild. We are clever together, over time. Set a stranded explorer who starves beside local people who thrive in the same country. **[CHECK: a documented case, such as Burke and Wills]**

7. **A day in the band.** Now slow down and live there. A band of a few dozen people, mostly relatives, moving with the seasons, knowing perhaps a few hundred others in a lifetime. **[CHECK: band and network sizes from studies of recent foragers]** Tell the hard facts through one day and one year:
   - **Hunger.** Food was not stored. A good week was followed by a bad one. Sweet fruit and fat were rare and worth gorging on.
   - **Danger.** Predators, snakes, falls, infected wounds. Darkness was dangerous.
   - **Children.** Perhaps close to half died before growing up. **[CHECK]** Every adult had buried a child.
   - **Sharing.** A hunter who killed an antelope gave most of it away, because next month he would be the one who came home with nothing. Hoarders and boasters were mocked.
   - **Reputation.** Everyone knew everyone. To be thought lazy, stingy or dangerous could mean being left behind, and being left behind meant death.
   - **Strangers.** Another band might mean a marriage partner, a trade, or a fight.
   Note in a sentence that this picture leans on foragers studied in recent centuries, who are not living fossils, and on what bones and campsites show.

8. **What that life left in us.** Hundreds of thousands of years of this, against a few thousand of anything else. The traces, each tied to a pressure from movement 7:
   - The craving for sugar, fat and salt, in a world that now sells them on every corner.
   - Fear that comes easily for snakes, heights and the dark, and hardly at all for cars.
   - The pain of exclusion, the hunger for approval, and the pleasure of gossip.
   - A quick sense of fairness, and anger at cheats.
   - Easy loyalty to "us" and suspicion of "them".
   - The pull of status, and resentment of those who claim too much of it.
   Say plainly that this kind of explanation is easy to push too far. People also learn, and cultures differ widely. But the starting equipment was built there. Plant what later chapters use: these are the instincts that rumour, advertising, armies, religions and drugs all work on.

9. **Not the only humans.** End by widening the view. For most of this time we were one of several kinds of human. Neanderthals in Europe and western Asia were strong, big-brained, used fire and buried their dead. Others lived in Asia and on its islands. **[CHECK: which species, and dates]** Today there is only us. Close on that fact, and on the people at the edge of Africa about to walk into the rest of the world, where they would meet the others.
