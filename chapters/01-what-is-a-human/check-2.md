## Factual flags

1. **Major — “A newborn chimpanzee can cling to its mother from its first day.”**  
   The later sentence, “A newborn chimpanzee can cling to its mother's fur while she climbs,” repeats the error. Newborn chimpanzees have a grasping reflex but cannot support their weight reliably; mothers physically support them for roughly their first two months. The contrast exaggerates the difference between human and chimpanzee newborns. [Wisconsin National Primate Research Center](https://primate.wisc.edu/primate-info-net/pin-factsheet-chimpanzee/).

2. **Major — “However, their tricks stay at about the level a clever animal could invent for itself.”**  
   The revision acknowledges chimpanzee traditions, fixing part of the earlier objection, but retains an experimentally challenged boundary. In a 2024 study, chimpanzees learned a complex task socially after none had solved it during three months of independent access. Human cumulative culture is much more extensive; chimpanzee learning is not reliably limited to skills they readily reinvent alone. [Van Leeuwen and colleagues, *Nature Human Behaviour*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01836-5).

3. **Minor — “The closest that other animals come is a handful of calls.”**  
   Animal communication extends beyond a few isolated calls. Researchers documented 390 distinct vocal sequences in chimpanzees, with ordering and recombination patterns. These are not equivalent to human language, but they contradict the restricted repertoire described here. [Girard-Buttoz and colleagues, *Communications Biology*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-022-03350-8).

4. **Minor — “The only written record the expedition left behind, found in a pile of stones on King William Island.”**  
   This caption incorrectly makes the Victory Point note unique. Another expedition note was recovered at Gore Point. The distinctive importance of the Victory Point note is its added account of deaths and the abandonment of the ships. [Stenton, “Franklin’s ‘Cemented Tomb’: The Jamme Report of 1928 Revisited”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/polar-record/article/franklins-cemented-tomb-the-jamme-report-of-1928-revisited/FAF78A01D19278125DC82E5E1B442D3B).

## Clarity flags

- **“But the walkers were not people.”**  
  The chapter still leaves *human*, *people*, and *our species* undefined. A general reader cannot tell whether “human” means *Homo sapiens*, the genus *Homo*, or the broader ancestral lineage. This becomes particularly important in “Squeeze the whole history of our species into a single day.”

- **“As a result, she is soon responsible for a baby, a toddler and an older child who are all hungry and none of whom can feed themselves.”**  
  “Feed themselves” sounds like an inability to eat independently. The preceding argument concerns obtaining enough food to support themselves. Those are different kinds of dependence.

- **“Third, it let people talk about each other, and this may matter most of all.” / “Finally, it let people talk about things that are not there.”**  
  The categories overlap with the earlier example of reporting a distant dry waterhole. The final step appears to introduce talking about absent things, although the reader has already encountered that capacity. The intended progression toward plans, promises and shared beliefs needs a clearer distinction.

- **“Turning down free money makes no sense for a single deal.”**  
  The previous sentence supplies an intelligible motive: refusing unfair treatment. Specify that rejection sacrifices immediate monetary gain; otherwise “makes no sense” appears to contradict the explanation just given.

- **“Nevertheless, they are the starting point for everybody, and they do not go away.”**  
  This and the following final sentence recap the chapter’s argument. That conflicts with the style sheet’s instruction to end on an image, consequence or open question rather than a summary.

## Overall

The revision resolves many earlier problems and reads coherently. Four factual flags remain worth addressing, especially the newborn-chimpanzee comparison and the proposed limit on chimpanzee learning. The chapter’s confident evolutionary interpretations are not, by themselves, grounds for further flags. No files were edited.
