# Chapter 65 — Why did China lose the Opium Wars?

**Line under the title:** China, from 1793 to 1864.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

China lost because its armed forces were two centuries out of date and its government was too small and too sure of itself to see the danger in time. The Qing had beaten every enemy on land and had never faced one from the sea. Britain, hardened by constant European wars and newly armed with steam, wanted Chinese tea and had only one thing the Chinese would buy in quantity: opium grown in British India. When China tried to stop the drug, a few thousand British and Indian troops with iron steamships sailed up its rivers and cut the canal that fed its capital. The treaties that followed opened China by force. The defeat helped to set off the bloodiest civil war in history, begun by a failed examination candidate who believed he was the younger brother of Jesus. China has not forgotten any of it.

## The story

1. **Opening: the trenches at the river mouth.** Answer first. June 1839: an imperial commissioner destroys a thousand tonnes of British-owned opium and apologises to the sea.
2. **We possess all things.** China's trade with Europe before the war: one port, licensed merchants, and a British embassy of 1793 with clocks nobody wanted. The tea problem.
3. **Forty thousand chests.** The solution: opium from Bengal, smuggled in, and silver flowing out of China for the first time in three centuries.
4. **A letter to the queen.** The commissioner's campaign. The merchants go home to demand a war. A vote in London.
5. **Nemesis.** An iron steamship on a Chinese river. Why Qing forts and junks could not answer it. The canal. The treaty of 1842.
6. **Twenty thousand officials.** The deeper reasons: an empire with no naval enemies, a tiny government, and a court that took the British for pirates.
7. **God's Chinese son.** The Taiping: a failed candidate's visions, a kingdom at Nanjing, twenty million dead.
8. **The garden of perfect brightness.** The second war. The emperor's summer palace burned. Russia helps itself.
9. **Ending: what China remembers.** Half-measures, a hundred years of humiliation, and a flag lowered in 1997.

## Left for later

Japan's different response. The fall of the Qing in 1911. The Communist revolution.

- China's population and number of officials.
