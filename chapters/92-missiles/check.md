1. **The central submarine scene is presented as settled when its crucial details are disputed.**  
   > “On this boat it needed the agreement of three officers.”  
   > “The captain was for it, and so, after a moment, was the political officer.”  
   > “Arkhipov did not give way, and B-59 came up into the glare of American searchlights.”

   The three-person vote, the political officer’s agreement and the sequence before surfacing are not securely established. The National Security Archive’s updated reconstruction places the decisive intervention **after surfacing**, when Savitsky mistook American actions for an attack. Arkhipov helped calm him; accounts differ about the precise orders. Preserve that supported core without staging an undocumented vote. **Confidence: high that the passage overstates certainty; moderate on the exact sequence.** [National Security Archive’s reconstruction and eyewitness records](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/russia-programs/2022-10-03/soviet-submarines-nuclear-torpedoes-cuban-missile-crisis).

2. **The Guatemalan death toll is assigned to the wrong period and an oversimplified succession of governments.**  
   > “The military governments that followed ruled for forty years and killed some two hundred thousand of their own people.”

   The approximately 200,000 figure concerns people killed or disappeared during the **1960–1996 armed conflict**, not forty uninterrupted years of military government following 1954. Civilian presidency returned in 1986, although military violence continued. The truth commission attributed 93% of documented violations to state forces and associated groups; that is not the same as assigning the entire estimated death toll to them. **Confidence: high.** [Truth-commission findings](https://www.ghrc-usa.org/our-work/themes/truth-justice-and-historic-memory/); [civilian presidency and continuing abuses](https://apnews.com/article/408d2fe091307a6ef68cc7f4a4fbfa60).

3. **The test ban did not end radioactive fallout.**  
   > “That summer the two countries and Britain signed a treaty banning nuclear tests in the air, in space and under the sea, which ended the fallout that had been turning up in children's milk teeth all over the world.”

   It ended those countries’ atmospheric testing and substantially reduced additional fallout. France continued atmospheric tests until 1974 and China until 1980; existing contamination also persisted. “Ended” is both factually wrong and an absolute prohibited by the style guide. **Confidence: high.** [United Nations history of nuclear testing](https://www.un.org/en/observances/end-nuclear-tests-day/history).

4. **The assassination examples conflate proposals, attempts and dates.**  
   > “His government went on trying to get rid of Castro by other means, including sabotage and attempts to have him killed with poisoned cigars and an exploding seashell.”

   The seashell was an impractical proposal explored in **1963**, not an executed attempt preceding the missile crisis. The poisoned-cigar scheme originated in 1960, before Kennedy’s presidency. Actual assassination plotting supports the point, but these examples distort its chronology and implementation. **Confidence: high.** [CIA inspector general’s account](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/jfk/releases/2022/104-10057-10270.pdf); [Church Committee findings, section on early 1963](https://rapeutation.com/churchcmte.rep5.htm).

5. **The executions are inaccurately compressed into stadium trials of policemen.**  
   > “He shot several hundred of Batista's policemen after trials in a sports stadium.”

   Those executed included soldiers and officers as well as police. Trials occurred in multiple places, notably La Cabaña fortress. The conspicuous Sports Palace trial of army officer Jesús Sosa Blanco cannot stand for the process involving hundreds of defendants. **Confidence: high.** [Contemporary reporting on La Cabaña and Sosa Blanco](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C825589-2%2C00.html).

6. **The quarantine’s scope is overstated.**  
   > “The navy would stop and search every ship bound for Cuba and turn back any that carried weapons.”

   The quarantine targeted specified **offensive military equipment**, not all weapons, and ships were not universally boarded and searched. Kennedy’s announcement explicitly distinguished this from stopping ordinary necessities. This also makes the opening’s “cut the island off” misleading. **Confidence: high.** [Kennedy’s announcement](https://microsites.jfklibrary.org/cmc/oct22/doc5.html); [Navy operational history](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/n/the-naval-quarantine-of-cuba/quarantine-22-26-oct.html).

7. **The alert passage blurs both the force involved and the date.**  
   > “For the only time in their history, American nuclear forces were moved to the last stage of alert before war.”

   **Strategic Air Command** went to DEFCON 2 on **24 October**; the sentence’s placement immediately after the 22 October speech makes it appear simultaneous and applicable to all nuclear forces. Name the command, explain it briefly, and place the escalation two days later. **Confidence: high.** [Smithsonian account](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/cuban-missile-crisis); [official military chronology](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/432140).

8. **Arkhipov did speak publicly about the incident.**  
   > “He served on, rose to vice-admiral, and never spoke about that afternoon in public.”  
   > “What he had done became known four years later, when one of his fellow officers described it to a conference of old men from both sides.”

   Arkhipov discussed the episode at a Moscow conference on **14 October 1997**, although he avoided explicitly acknowledging his own intervention. Reporting based on Orlov’s recollections had appeared in **1995**. The 2002 conference brought much wider attention, rather than the first disclosure. **Confidence: high.** [His presentation and its publication history](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/russia-programs/2022-10-03/soviet-submarines-nuclear-torpedoes-cuban-missile-crisis).

9. **Only three submarines were forced up.**  
   > “The submarine commanders had been ordered to stay hidden, and all four had been forced to the surface.”

   B-36, B-59 and B-130 were surfaced under American pressure; **B-4 escaped that outcome**. “All four” is a straightforward factual error and a broken absolute. **Confidence: high.** [National Security Archive’s submarine history](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB75/).

10. **The Turkish bargain’s secrecy is overstated.**  
    > “The Turkish half of the bargain stayed secret for more than twenty-five years.”

    Public disclosure was gradual. Former Turkish prime minister İsmet İnönü publicly referred to the bargain in **1970**; stronger American documentary confirmation emerged later. Distinguish prolonged official concealment from complete public ignorance. The following claim about what an entire generation of politicians “therefore learned” needs that distinction too. **Confidence: high that the categorical chronology is wrong.** [Wilson Center’s documentary history](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/jupiter-missiles-and-endgame-cuban-missile-crisis-sealing-deal-italy-and-turkey).

11. **The cause of Arkhipov’s cancer is asserted without adequate support.**  
    > “He died in 1998 of a cancer brought on by radiation from a reactor accident on another submarine years before.”

    His radiation exposure and subsequent kidney cancer are reported, but the causal connection is generally presented as suspected, not established. Keep the death and exposure as separate facts, or omit the medical explanation. This is precisely where the style guide recommends removing a weak detail rather than strengthening its certainty. **Confidence: moderate; the causal assertion is unsupported by the source located.** [National Geographic’s explicitly qualified account](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/you-and-almost-everyone-you-know-owe-your-life-to-this-man-1).

12. **Árbenz’s election year is wrong.**  
    > “In 1951 the people of Guatemala, in Central America, elected a young army officer named Jacobo Árbenz as president.”

    He was elected in **November 1950** and took office in March 1951. **Confidence: high.** [Declassified chronology](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP79T00826A003200110001-8.pdf).

13. **“The only port” is another avoidable factual absolute.**  
    > “Most of the country's farmland belonged to a tiny number of owners, and the largest of them was an American banana company, which also owned the railways and the only port, and left most of its land unplanted.”

    Puerto Barrios was the principal Caribbean port, not Guatemala’s only port. San José and Champerico operated on the Pacific coast. The company’s transport dominance can be stated without erasing them. **Confidence: high.** [World Bank description of Guatemala’s ports and their development](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/589111468045234292/pdf/multi0page.pdf).

14. **The thirteen-day frame needs an explicit starting point.**  
    > “The two governments settled the matter in thirteen days.”

    Coming after the reference to the “last week of October,” this leaves a general reader unsure where thirteen days fit. They conventionally run from **Kennedy’s briefing on 16 October to the agreement on 28 October**, not from the blockade or the beginning of the Soviet deployment. Physical withdrawal and further negotiations continued afterward. **Confidence: high.** [State Department chronology](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/cuban-missile-crisis).

15. **The survival calculation needs one bridge to what chapter 88 established.**  
    > “A leader who believed that he was being attacked would have a quarter of an hour in which to decide whether to fire his own before they were destroyed.”

    Chapter 88 has already explained survivable retaliation: “Once each side could destroy the other even after being struck first, neither could start a war and survive it.” This is **a tension in explanation, not a direct contradiction**. Distinguish vulnerable missiles from surviving bombers and submarine weapons, and explain that warning time varied. Otherwise the reader may conclude that the deterrent described earlier has suddenly ceased to exist.

16. **The missile totals have no clear date.**  
    > “The pictures showed that the Soviets had only a few dozen long-range missiles.”

    The preceding dates are 1960; the following paragraph moves to 1961; the intended comparison appears to concern the crisis-era balance. Date the comparison. Also distinguish missiles from total nuclear warheads, since the next sentence changes the thing being counted. This is a reader-orientation problem even before the figures are checked.

17. **An unexplained naval term carries the opening’s crucial mechanism.**  
    > “Soviet rules allowed the weapon to be used without permission from Moscow if the hull was holed.”

    “Holed” means that an attack had punctured the submarine’s outer structure; a general reader may not know this. Explain the physical condition plainly. More importantly, distinguish the reported emergency authorization from the disputed claim about a mandatory three-officer vote.

18. **A minor signpost can be removed.**  
    > “Cuba's part in the story began in another country.”

    This announces a narrative detour instead of making the connection. The Guatemala episode can open directly, with its relevance established through Guevara. This is a mild breach of the ban on signpost sentences, not a problem with the narrator’s confidence.
