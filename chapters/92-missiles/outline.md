# Chapter 92 — How close did the world come to nuclear war?

**Line under the title:** Cuba, the United States and the Soviet Union, from 1954 to 1963.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The world came closest in October 1962, when the Soviet Union secretly put nuclear missiles in Cuba and the United States blockaded the island. For thirteen days the two governments bargained, and they settled it: the missiles left Cuba, America promised not to invade, and American missiles quietly left Turkey. Both leaders wanted a way out from the first day. What nearly caused a war was what neither of them controlled: a local commander who shot down a plane, a pilot who lost his way, and a submarine captain who thought the war had begun. Cuba, whose fear of invasion had started it, was not consulted about the ending. The outcome owed as much to luck and to one naval officer as to statesmanship.

## The story

1. **Opening: a submarine.** Answer first. 27 October 1962, in the Sargasso Sea.
2. **Thirty minutes.** The rocket that crosses an ocean and cannot be called back; the satellite that counts them. A "missile gap" that ran the other way. Missiles in Turkey.
3. **Bananas.** Guatemala, 1954: an elected government, a fruit company, and a young Argentine doctor who watched.
4. **Eighty-two men in a boat.** Cuba under Batista; Castro's landing and victory; what he did and what Washington did. The beach landing of 1961. Plots.
5. **A hedgehog.** Why Khrushchev sent the missiles, and how.
6. **Photographs.** 14 to 22 October: what the spy plane saw; the generals' advice; the choice of a blockade; the speech.
7. **Eyeball to eyeball.** The ships turn. Two letters from Moscow.
8. **Saturday.** One day: a plane shot down, a plane astray, a cable from Havana, the submarine, and a meeting at night.
9. **What was not said.** The settlement and its secret half. Castro's rage. What the generals never knew was on the island.
10. **Afterwards.** A teleprinter line, a test ban, the fall of Khrushchev, the arms race resumed.
11. **Ending: the officer who said no.**

## Left for later

The space race. Vietnam. Chile in 1973. Later false alarms and arms control. The end of the Soviet Union.
