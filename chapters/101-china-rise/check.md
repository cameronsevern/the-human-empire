1. **Factual error affecting the main explanation — high confidence.**  
   > “Nothing was added except the incentive.”

   Agricultural growth also benefited from improved seeds, fertiliser, irrigation, and changes in procurement prices and markets. Household incentives mattered greatly, but this sentence excludes other documented causes. [FAO’s study of China’s agricultural reforms](https://www.fao.org/4/ag088e/AG088E03.htm) identifies these contributions explicitly. This also breaks the rule against absolutes that one exception defeats.

2. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.**  
   > “A migrant registered in a village had no right to send his children to a city school or to use a city hospital.”

   The registration system restricted access to local public schooling and subsidised healthcare; it did not categorically prohibit attending city schools or receiving hospital treatment. Migrants could face fees, documentation requirements and exclusion from local insurance. The [World Bank describes limited public-school access](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/297421610599411896/pdf/From-Recovery-to-Rebalancing-China-s-Economy-in-2021.pdf), while [research on migrant healthcare](https://www.ccpr.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Niu-Jianlin.pdf) distinguishes hospital access from insurance coverage.

3. **Misleading statistical benchmark — high confidence.**  
   > “In 1981 nearly nine Chinese in ten lived on less than two dollars a day at today's prices.”

   These figures refer to the old **$1.90 poverty line in 2011 purchasing-power-parity dollars**, not two dollars at today’s prices or ordinary exchange rates. Explain that this measures what the money could buy locally. The [World Bank’s poverty report](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/bdadc16a4f5c1c88a839c0f905cde802-0070012022/original/Poverty-Synthesis-Report-final.pdf) specifies the benchmark.

4. **Unsupported certainty at the ending — high confidence about the distinction.**  
   > “The state that would have executed them for it displays it as the founding document of the new China.”

   The pact establishes that the farmers **feared** imprisonment or execution, not that execution was their certain fate. Earlier, “could be shot” preserves that distinction; the ending discards it. The [account of the pact and its contingency clause](https://www.northcountrypublicradio.org/news/npr/145360447/the-secret-document-that-transformed-china) supports their fear. Make the ending about the punishment they risked.

5. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.**  
   > “Its goods now entered the markets of the rich countries on the same terms as anyone's, and foreign firms could invest there in the knowledge that those terms would last.”

   WTO accession strengthened predictable market access, but China faced special transitional safeguards, including a China-specific mechanism lasting twelve years. Membership also did not guarantee permanently unchanged trading conditions. The [WTO’s accession announcement](https://www-server1.wto.org/english/news_e/pres01_e/pr243_e.htm) spells out these exceptions. The useful point is greater security of access, not identical or immutable terms.

6. **Doubtful precision and population definition — high confidence.**  
   > “In the far western region of Xinjiang, from 2017, more than a million members of a Muslim people, the Uyghurs, were held in camps for what the government called re-education.”

   The widely cited estimates encompass **Uyghurs and other predominantly Muslim minorities**, and are estimates rather than a verified count exclusively of Uyghurs. “An estimated million or more Uyghurs and other Muslim minorities” would preserve the scale without changing the population being counted. The [US intelligence assessment](https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-Report-Uyghur-Genocide-Concentrated-Reeducation-Camps-China-Oct2024.pdf) discusses the differing estimates and populations.

7. **Farming-policy chronology — high confidence.**  
   > “Their county secretary protected them, and within two years what they had done in secret was national policy.”

   Central acceptance in 1980 was initially restricted, especially to poor areas. General endorsement came with the January 1982 central document—just over three years after the pact. A [study tracing the policy decisions](https://journal.ccnu.edu.cn/sk/CN/Y2018/V57/I2/19) distinguishes those stages. The sentence conflates permission for selected experiments with nationwide endorsement.

8. **One-child policy presented too uniformly — high confidence.**  
   > “Those who conceived a second time were pressed, and often forced, to have an abortion, sometimes late in pregnancy, and in a single year twenty million people were sterilised.”

   Coercive abortions are well documented, but second pregnancies were not uniformly prohibited. Rules varied by place, ethnicity and period; many rural couples could have a second child, especially after a daughter. [Demographic research summarising policy coverage](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1081602X.2016.1213179) shows how extensive these exceptions were. Specify **unauthorised pregnancies** and give the year for the sterilisation figure.

9. **Earlier-chapter continuity problem — high confidence.**  
   > “In America he toured a car plant and the space centre, and at a rodeo in Texas he put on a ten-gallon hat, which did more than any speech to tell the Chinese that the Americans were no longer the enemy.”

   [Chapter 91]( chapters/91-cultural-revolution/chapter.md) has already described Nixon’s 1972 visit and concluded: “They were now, in effect, partners against the Soviet Union.” The hat can symbolise the deepening rapprochement, but this wording makes it sound like the original reversal. Also give **1979**: the preceding “That year” currently anchors the passage in 1978.

10. **Internal contradiction and break of the absolute rule — high confidence.**  
    > “They could get rich, travel, and live as they pleased.”

    The chapter itself has just described reproductive coercion and soon describes restrictions on migrants’ residence-related rights. “Live as they pleased” therefore contradicts its own evidence. Describe the expansion of private choices without implying unrestricted personal freedom.

11. **Numerical claim needs correction or a consistent series — medium-high confidence.**  
    > “In 2000 it made about a twentieth of the world's manufactured goods.”

    Five per cent is below the estimates in the sources checked: [GAO gives 6.8 per cent](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a319409.html), while a [Federal Reserve study gives just under nine per cent](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/2011/1033/ifdp1033.htm). Different valuation methods produce different shares. Use one consistent manufacturing-value-added series for both 2000 and 2020, and describe it as a share of manufacturing output **by value**.

12. **Incorrectly broad railway claim — high confidence.**  
    > “China had no high-speed railway in 2007, and now has more than the rest of the world put together.”

    China introduced high-speed services on upgraded conventional lines in April 2007. The Beijing–Tianjin line opened in 2008 as the first of the new generation of dedicated high-speed lines. Specify the dedicated network rather than denying all high-speed rail in 2007. [World Bank railway history](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/933411559841476316/pdf/Chinas-High-Speed-Rail-Development.pdf).

13. **Wrong date for the fence — high confidence.**  
    > “In 1980 Shenzhen was fenced off and declared a special zone.”

    The zone dates from 1980; construction of its control boundary followed. Shenzhen’s own history dates the construction organisation and approval to **1982**, and acceptance of the completed project to **1985**. Separate designation from fencing. [Shenzhen municipal history](https://www.sz.gov.cn/szstory/202212/content/mpost_10330832.html).

14. **Ambiguous office and constitutional change — high confidence.**  
    > “Since 2012 the country has been led by Xi Jinping, the purged official's son who spent his teens in a cave village, and he has removed the limit on his term of office.”

    Xi became party general secretary in 2012 and state president in 2013. The change in 2018 removed the presidency’s two-consecutive-term limit; the general secretaryship had no equivalent formal limit. Identify the presidency so a general reader does not merge the offices. [Congressional Research Service explanation](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF10854/IF10854.2.pdf).

15. **Container capacity confused with box count — high confidence.**  
    > “The largest ships now carry twenty-four thousand of them.”

    The capacity is roughly 24,000 **twenty-foot equivalent units**, not necessarily 24,000 individual containers. A forty-foot container counts as two units. Say “the equivalent of twenty-four thousand twenty-foot containers.” [Maersk’s definition](https://www.maersk.com/support/faqs/what-is-teu); [shipbuilder’s capacity specification](https://www.yzjship.com/en/newsdetails/72/582.html).

16. **Unsupported demographic superlative — high confidence that qualification is needed.**  
    > “China today has some thirty million more men than women, and a population that is ageing faster than any large country's has before.”

    “Large” and “ageing faster” have no defined measures here. South Korea aged faster on the standard measure of the time taken for the over-65 share to rise from seven to fourteen per cent. The [comparative demographic study](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/12/12/5086) explicitly identifies China’s slower pace relative to South Korea. “Ageing rapidly” makes the supported point.

17. **Unanchored present-day statistic — high confidence.**  
    > “The party has ninety-eight million members and no rivals.”

    Approximately 98 million is the **end-2022** figure, beyond the advertised 2020 endpoint. Date it explicitly or use a figure appropriate to the endpoint. The [official membership report](https://www.moj.gov.cn/pub/sfbgw/gwxw/ttxw/202306/t20230630_481751.html) supplies the date. More generally, the repeated “today” and “now” leave readers unsure when the historical narrative becomes a present-day comparison.

18. **Unsupported expansion of a supported claim — high confidence.**  
    > “In forty years, eight hundred million Chinese climbed out of extreme poverty, which is the largest improvement in human welfare ever recorded.”

    The poverty reduction is supported; the ranking of **all improvements in human welfare** is not established by that statistic. Welfare includes health, survival and other changes that cannot simply be ranked against an income threshold. The [World Bank supports the scale of poverty reduction](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2022/04/01/lifting-800-million-people-out-of-poverty-new-report-looks-at-lessons-from-china-s-experience). Keep the superlative specific to poverty reduction.

19. **Chronological ambiguity for the reader — high confidence.**  
    > “The next morning a column of tanks rolling down the avenue was stopped by a single man in a white shirt, with a shopping bag in each hand, who stepped in front of the leading tank and moved from side to side to block it.”

    “The next morning” can be read as the morning after the night of 3 June, although the preceding paragraph has already reached dawn on 4 June. Tank Man’s confrontation was on **5 June**. Give the date directly. The [National Gallery of Art’s record of the photograph](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/218541-tank-man) confirms it.

20. **Missing explanation at an important political turn.**  
    > “The party's general secretary wanted to talk to them.”

    Readers know Deng decides, but are suddenly introduced to a different apparent party leader without an explanation of their relationship. Briefly identify the general secretary as the formal party chief, with Deng retaining ultimate authority. Because this man returns in the house-arrest sentence, naming Zhao Ziyang would satisfy the style guide’s rule about recurring people.

21. **Missing explanation of the mechanism driving the protests.**  
    > “Officials and their children used their positions to buy goods at the low state price and sell them at the market one.”

    The reader has not been told why the same goods have two prices. Add a short explanation that some goods still had to be sold at government-set prices while additional production could be sold at market prices. That makes the corruption understandable rather than merely asserted.
