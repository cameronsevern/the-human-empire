# Chapter 101 — How did China get rich?

**Line under the title:** China, from 1978 to 2020.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

China got rich by letting its people work for themselves while its ruling party kept every lever of political power. The first step was taken by peasants who broke the law to divide up their collective farm, and by leaders, themselves victims of Mao, who chose to copy them instead of shooting them. The second was to invite foreign firms to make goods for export in walled-off zones on the coast, with the labour of two hundred million migrants from the villages. A steel box that made shipping almost free, and the country's admission to the world trading system in 2001, turned it into the workshop of the world. Eight hundred million people left extreme poverty, the largest such change in history. The freedom that many outsiders expected to follow did not, and the one time the people demanded it, in 1989, the army was sent in.

## The story

1. **Opening: eighteen thumbprints.** Answer first. A village in Anhui, the winter of 1978, and a contract hidden in a roof.
2. **The survivor.** Deng Xiaoping: a teenager in a French factory, three falls, and a meeting in December 1978. A bullet train and a cowboy hat. Black cats.
3. **Farming for yourself.** What happened to the harvest, and to the communes.
4. **A fishing town.** The zones: Shenzhen, Hong Kong's factories, and the neighbours who had shown the way.
5. **One child.** A policy from the same years, and its cost.
6. **The square.** 1989: prices, corruption, students, a statue, the night of 3 June, a man with shopping bags. The bargain that followed.
7. **Southern tour.** An old man restarts the reforms. State factories shut; the villages empty into the cities. Who the migrants are and what they may not have.
8. **A box.** How a trucker's idea in 1956 made distance almost free, and what that did to where things are made.
9. **Made in China.** 2001: the trading system. What it did to China, and to a furniture town in America.
10. **The reckoning.** What was gained: poverty, railways, cement. What it cost: air, inequality, debt. What did not change: the party, the firewall, the camps in the west.
11. **Ending: a museum case.**
