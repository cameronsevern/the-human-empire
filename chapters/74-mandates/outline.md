# Chapter 74 — Who drew the borders of the Middle East?

**Line under the title:** The Ottoman lands, from 1914 to 1939.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. This chapter covers ground that is still fought over: state what each party was promised, wanted and feared, in its own terms, and keep the narrator's judgements to what the documents show.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Most of the borders were drawn by British and French officials between 1916 and 1923, when they divided the Arab provinces of the defeated Ottoman empire between themselves. To win the war, Britain had made three promises about the same land: independence to the Arabs, a share to France, and a national home in Palestine to the Jewish people. They could not all be kept. The Arabs who had revolted against the Turks got kingdoms under British supervision in place of the single state they had expected. The Kurds got nothing. The Armenians had been destroyed during the war by the Ottoman government. The Turks alone refused the terms, fought, and drew their own border. In Palestine, Britain governed for a quarter of a century between two peoples who each held one of its promises.

5. **The revolt in the desert.** The Arab rising, a British officer in Arab dress, Damascus. The Bolsheviks publish the secret treaty.
6. **Mandates.** The peace conference. France takes Syria by force. Iraq rises and is bombed. A conference in Cairo hands out thrones. The Kurds.
7. **The father of the Turks.** The treaty that carved up Turkey, the general who tore it up, the exchange of populations, and the end of the caliphate.
8. **A national home.** Palestine under British rule: immigration, land, riots, a revolt, and a plan to divide the country.
9. **Ending: the door.** 1939, and what has lasted.
