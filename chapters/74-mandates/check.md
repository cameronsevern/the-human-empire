I found no clear contradictions with chapters 29, 68, 72 or 73, and no hedging stacks, explicit cross-references or chapter self-mentions. The findings below are ranked by seriousness. No files were edited.

1. **Wrong: the proportion of Hebron’s Jews killed.**  
   > “There were riots in 1920 and 1921, and worse in 1929, when Arab mobs killed a hundred and thirty-three Jews, among them most of an old religious community in the town of Hebron, and about as many Arabs were killed, mostly by British police.”

   About 67 Jews were killed in Hebron, whose Jewish population numbered several hundred. The massacre and subsequent evacuation destroyed the community’s presence; **most of its members were not killed**. The Palestine-wide figure of 133 is sound. **Confidence: high.** [Account and demographic context](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Hebron_massacre).

2. **Wrong: Turkey’s present borders were not fixed in 1923.**  
   > “It fixed the borders that Turkey has today.”

   Lausanne explicitly left the Iraqi frontier for subsequent negotiation or League arbitration. Hatay became part of Turkey in 1939. These are substantial exceptions in a chapter whose central subject is borders. **Confidence: high.** [Lausanne, Article 3](https://www.mfa.gov.tr/lausanne-peace-treaty-part-i_-political-clauses.en.mfa); [Hatay’s history, Turkish Ministry of Culture](https://antakyatarihikentmerkezi.ktb.gov.tr/EN-348066/history.html).

3. **Misleading: the Kurdish provision was conditional, not an unconditional promise of statehood.**  
   > “The Kurds, who live in the mountains where Turkey, Iraq, Syria and Iran now meet, had been promised a state of their own in the first peace treaty.”

   Sèvres proposed local autonomy in a defined part of Ottoman territory, with independence conditional on demonstrated majority support and League approval. It did not promise a state encompassing the Kurdish populations of all four countries, and it never entered into force. Naming Sèvres here would also help: “the first peace treaty” has not yet been introduced. **Confidence: high.** [Treaty of Sèvres, Articles 62–64](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres/All_parts#Article_62).

4. **False absolute, also undermined by the chapter’s own Saudi story.**  
   > “The one people of the region who drew their own frontier were the Turks, who fought for it.”

   Ibn Saud’s conquests also shaped an independent state’s territory, although British negotiations helped determine its external boundaries. The Turks were not the region’s sole actors in making their own territorial settlement. The later paragraph about Ibn Saud makes the opening particularly difficult to sustain. **Confidence: high.** [British Library/Qatar Digital Library historical profile](https://qdl.qa/en/historical-profile-saudi-arabia).

5. **Misleading chronology: bombing during the Iraqi revolt becomes the later system of economical air control.**  
   > “They used a new economy measure, bombing rebellious villages from the air, which was far cheaper than sending soldiers.”

   Aircraft participated in suppressing the 1920 revolt, but substantial ground forces were essential. The cheaper system in which the RAF assumed primary responsibility for control followed the revolt and took effect in 1922. As written, the sentence makes that later arrangement appear to have been the method already used to defeat the uprising. **Confidence: high.** [David Omissi, *Air Power and Colonial Control*](https://archive.globalpolicy.org/component/content/article/169-history/36386-british-air-power-and-colonial-control-in-iraq-1920-1925.html); [RAF Museum, 1922](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/research-enquiries/history-of-aviation-timeline/british-military-aviation/1922-2/).

6. **Wrong or substantially overstated: the Sèvres territorial settlement.**  
   > “A treaty signed by the sultan's government in 1920 gave the western coast to Greece, set aside zones for France and Italy, and left the Turks a rump of the interior.”

   The Smyrna district was placed under Greek administration while formally remaining under Turkish sovereignty, with a later mechanism for possible incorporation into Greece. “The western coast” is too extensive, and the Turkish remainder included Constantinople and substantial coastal territory, not merely an inland rump. **Confidence: high.** [Treaty of Sèvres, Articles 27, 36, 69 and 83](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres/All_parts).

7. **Misleading: the 1908 revolution and 1913 seizure of power are collapsed.**  
   > “In 1908 a group of young army officers had seized power, and they wanted a modern, centralised state, more Turkish in character.”

   The 1908 revolution restored constitutional and parliamentary government. The CUP consolidated its dictatorship through the 1913 coup and subsequent events. Because the next sentences take these same “officers” directly into the wartime alliance and genocide, the missing distinction materially distorts who governed and how they acquired power. **Confidence: high.** [Erik-Jan Zürcher, “Young Turk Governance … during the First World War”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00263206.2019.1590820).

8. **Doubtful: freezing accounts for most of the army.**  
   > “Most of it froze to death.”

   The Sarıkamış army suffered catastrophic losses through combat, exposure, disease and capture. Estimates are disputed, but they do not justify confidently assigning most of the entire army’s deaths to freezing. State the destruction of the army and the contribution of exposure without making that unsupported majority claim. **Confidence: high that the sentence overstates the evidence; moderate on precise casualty totals.** [*International Encyclopedia of the First World War*, Sarıkamış](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/sarkams-battle-of-1-1/?format=pdf).

9. **Wrong chronology: the empire’s remaining lifespan.**  
   > “It had another three years to live.”

   Following the agreement of May 1916, Ottoman military defeat came in 1918; the sultanate survived until November 1922. Neither event falls three years later. Specify whether the sentence means defeat, loss of the Arab provinces or abolition of the empire. **Confidence: high.** [Turkish parliament’s history of the abolition](https://cdn.tbmm.gov.tr/TbmmWeb/Yayinlar/Dosya/b6aa0899-396d-4442-9b1f-17be2445d948.pdf).

10. **Wrong sequence: abolition of the sultanate follows Lausanne in the narrative.**  
    > “He abolished the sultanate and declared a republic.”

    Following “Kemal then remade what was left,” this places both acts after the 1923 treaty. The sultanate was abolished in November 1922, before Lausanne; the republic was proclaimed in October 1923. **Confidence: high.** [Parliamentary account](https://cdn.tbmm.gov.tr/TbmmWeb/Yayinlar/Dosya/b6aa0899-396d-4442-9b1f-17be2445d948.pdf).

11. **Overbroad historical absolute: population exchange had precedents.**  
    > “The treaty contained a provision that had no precedent.”

    Earlier Ottoman–Bulgarian and Greek–Bulgarian agreements provided precedents for population exchange. The distinctive claim concerns the compulsory character and international framework of the 1923 settlement, not exchange without any precedent. This falls directly under style.md’s prohibition on absolutes that an exception breaks. **Confidence: high.** [Oxford account of the legal distinction and the 1919 precedent](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62359/reference-article-abstract/561713421).

12. **Misleading: the population figures imply all these people were physically moved after the agreement.**  
    > “About one million two hundred thousand Orthodox Christians were removed from Turkey to Greece, and about four hundred thousand Muslims from Greece to Turkey.”

    Many of the Christians counted in the exchange had already fled or been expelled during the war. The convention explicitly included earlier emigrants retrospectively. Distinguish the total displacement recognised by the settlement from the transfers carried out under it. **Confidence: high.** [Population-exchange convention, Article 3](https://www.mfa.gov.tr/lausanne-peace-treaty-vi_-convention-concerning-the-exchange-of-greek-and-turkish-populations-signed-at-lausanne_.en.mfa).

13. **Overstatement and reader confusion: the proposed division becomes an unrestricted territorial handover.**  
    > “Everything north of it would go to France, and everything south of it to Britain.”

    The eventual agreement distinguished direct control from privileged influence over an Arab state or confederation, and provided for international administration in Palestine. “Everything” gives the reader a misleading map that the later explanation must undo. Clarify that this was a proposed division of predominance within the territories under discussion. **Confidence: high.** [Sykes–Picot text, Annex I](https://www.un.org/unispal/es/history/origins-and-evolution-of-the-palestine-problem/part-i-1917-1947/).

14. **Reader gap: a central mechanism remains unexplained.**  
    > “France was to control the Syrian coast and to have a zone of influence inland.”

    A general reader needs one plain distinction between direct government and a “zone of influence”: local Arab government would nominally remain, while France enjoyed privileged advisory and economic rights. Without that explanation, the reader cannot see precisely how this agreement conflicted with the independence promise. **Confidence: high as an editorial finding.** [Agreement’s provisions](https://www.un.org/unispal/es/history/origins-and-evolution-of-the-palestine-problem/part-i-1917-1947/).

15. **Style: the final paragraph retreats from the concrete ending into summary.**  
    > “The lines that the officials drew in those years have proved remarkably lasting.”

    The preceding image—the immigration restriction followed by the invasion of Poland—already provides the concrete consequence that style.md asks an ending to land on. This sentence starts a retrospective summary, precisely the ending form the guide prohibits. **Confidence: high as a style finding.**
