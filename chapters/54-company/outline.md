# Chapter 54 — How did a company conquer India?

**Line under the title:** Amsterdam, the Spice Islands, Bengal and London, from 1600 to 1858.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The East India Company conquered India with Indian soldiers, paid from Indian taxes and financed by Indian bankers, at the moment the Mughal empire fell apart. It brought two inventions of its own. One was the company owned by shareholders, a Dutch idea that let thousands of strangers pool money for years at a time and that made a trading firm richer than most kings. The other was the drilled infantryman with a musket, who could beat cavalry many times his number. Once the Company won the right to collect the land tax of Bengal, the province's own revenue paid for the army that took the next province. A business answerable to investors in a London street ruled a hundred million people, with results that included a famine and, in the end, a rebellion by its own soldiers that finished it.

## The story

1. **Opening: an ambassador with poor presents.** Answer first. An English envoy at the Mughal court in 1615, begging leave to trade.
2. **A maid's hundred guilders.** The Dutch company of 1602: why a voyage to Asia needed a new kind of firm, what a share was, and the market where shares were sold.
3. **Nutmeg.** A company with an army. The Banda Islands in 1621. An island swapped for Manhattan.
4. **Calico.** The English, pushed out of the spice trade, turn to Indian cloth. A tenant on the coast, and a beating in 1690.
5. **The drill.** The empire breaks up after 1707. How drilled infantry worked, and who first trained Indian soldiers in it.
6. **A mango grove.** Plassey, 1757: a battle bought beforehand with a banker's money. The victor's reward.
7. **The right to collect.** 1765: the Company becomes the tax collector of Bengal. What that did to its trade, its army and Bengal. The famine. A new English word.
8. **An office in Leadenhall Street.** A corporation as a government: the size of its army, Parliament's leash, the rest of India.
9. **The cartridge.** 1857: the soldiers turn. The crown takes over. Ending on the building.
