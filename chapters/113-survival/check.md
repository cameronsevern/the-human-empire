I treated 2025 as the cutoff. No files were edited. Findings are ranked by seriousness; confidence refers to the correction.

1. **Factual error — the nuclear-famine figures combine different scenarios.**  
   > “The most recent calculations, published in 2022, concluded that even a war between India and Pakistan, using a hundred or so weapons, would shorten the growing seasons enough to starve more than a billion people on the other side of the world.”

   The study’s hundred-weapon scenario estimates **255 million people without food at the end of year two**. Its billion-plus estimates involve larger exchanges. These are conditional model results, not a prediction that a specified number would certainly starve “on the other side of the world.” **Confidence: high.** [Xia et al., Table 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0.pdf)

2. **Doubtful central claim — extinction and catastrophic destruction are not distinguished.**  
   > “It could, and it has had the means since the 1950s.”

   Following the title, this reads as an established capacity to extinguish humanity. Nuclear weapons plainly provide the means for unprecedented destruction; whether an exchange would cause human extinction is a different, unresolved claim. The famine study cited later estimates billions of deaths, not extinction, and does not establish the 1950s threshold. Specify whether “destroy itself” means extinction or destruction of civilisation. **Confidence: high that the distinction is necessary; extinction risk itself remains uncertain.** [Xia et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43016-022-00573-0)

3. **Faulty explanation and break of the rule against absolutes.**  
   > “And no country had anything to gain by staying out.”

   Ozone protection also presented a free-rider problem: a country could benefit from others’ reductions while avoiding its own conversion costs. Montreal helped overcome that incentive through funding, obligations and trade restrictions. Subsequent prohibited CFC production demonstrates that incentives to evade controls existed. This matters because the sentence makes their absence the explanation for success. **Confidence: high.** [UNEP’s Montreal Protocol account](https://www.unep.org/ozonaction/who-we-are/about-montreal-protocol), [research identifying renewed CFC-11 production](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1193-4)

4. **Factual overstatement and unbreakable precision.**  
   > “Give the scissors a poster of one's own choosing, twenty letters long, and they will cut any DNA, in any living thing, at exactly that place.”

   CRISPR-Cas9 has targeting constraints and can cut unintended sites; getting it into the appropriate cells is another limitation. The 2012 experiment demonstrated programmable cutting in laboratory preparations, not unrestricted editing in every organism. “Any,” “any” and “exactly” conceal limitations central to the later discussion of unsafe editing. **Confidence: high.** [Original 2012 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6286148/), [NHGRI on unintended edits](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Genome-Editing/ethical-concerns)

5. **Medical overstatement, with an explanatory gap.**  
   > “Most of the patients treated so far have been freed of the disease.”  
   > “Doctors remove the patient's blood-forming cells, edit one gene, and put them back.”

   The approval evidence showed freedom from severe painful crises for a defined follow-up period—not established lifelong elimination of the disease. The treatment increases fetal haemoglobin rather than simply correcting the sickle-cell mutation. Naming sickle-cell disease and explaining that mechanism would make the example intelligible; the present account also omits the chemotherapy needed before reinfusion. **Confidence: high.** [FDA approval review](https://www.fda.gov/media/175179/download), [EMA explanation](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/casgevy)

6. **Factual conflation — influenza transmission and fatality.**  
   > “In 2011 two laboratories took a bird influenza that kills more than half of the few people who catch it, and which does not pass from person to person, and showed that five changes to its code would allow it to spread through the air between ferrets, the animals whose lungs most resemble ours.”

   This compresses distinct experiments into one result; their papers appeared in 2012 and used different approaches. More than half of **reported cases** had died, which is not the same as half of all infections. Limited probable human-to-human transmission had occurred; the important distinction is the absence of sustained transmission. Ferrets are useful influenza models, but “lungs most resemble ours” is an unsupported superlative. **Confidence: high.** [Herfst study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22723413/), [Imai study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3388103/), [CDC assessment](https://www.cdc.gov/bird-flu/php/technical-report/h5n1-06052024.html)

7. **Factual error — polio cases are understated.**  
   > “Polio, which paralysed three hundred and fifty thousand children a year in the 1980s, has been driven down to a few dozen cases.”

   “A few dozen” can describe wild-virus cases in particular years, but excludes vaccine-derived polio. WHO’s July 2025 report recorded **99 wild-virus cases and 319 vaccine-derived cases for 2024**. Specify the category and year, or describe total cases as hundreds. **Confidence: high.** [WHO emergency committee report](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-07-2025-statement-of-the-forty-second-meeting-of-the-polio-ihr-emergency-committee)

8. **Misleading description of Russia’s treaty position.**  
   > “The last treaty limiting American and Russian weapons was due to expire in February 2026, and Russia had already stopped observing it.”

   Russia suspended participation and violated verification obligations, but that is different from abandoning the numerical limits. The January 2025 US assessment could not certify full compliance, yet found no large-scale activity above those limits. Name New START and distinguish verification from weapon ceilings. **Confidence: high.** [US State Department implementation report](https://2021-2025.state.gov/2024-report-to-congress-on-implementation-of-the-new-start-treaty/)

9. **Misleading history — the discarded satellite-data story.**  
   > “American satellites had been recording the same thing for years, and their computers had been set to discard such low readings as errors.”

   Low readings triggered quality-control flags; NASA’s team noticed and investigated them before the British paper appeared. The familiar story of computers simply discarding the discovery misrepresents the sequence. **Confidence: high.** [NASA processing-team head Richard McPeters’s account, reproduced in *The IMS Bulletin*](https://www.math.uni-augsburg.de/emeriti/pukelsheim/1990c.pdf)

10. **Misleading recovery date and premature completion.**  
    > “The hole has stopped growing and is slowly closing, and the layer is expected to be back to its former state around the middle of this century.”  
    > “The sky was repaired.”

    The assessment available by 2025 projected recovery to 1980 levels around **2040 for most of the world, 2045 for the Arctic and 2066 for Antarctica**. Because this passage follows the Antarctic hole, “middle of this century” conflates different regions. Recovery is underway; “was repaired” declares it complete. **Confidence: high.** [WMO/UNEP assessment announcement](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/ozone-layer-recovery-track-helping-avoid-global-warming-05degc)

11. **Factual overstatement — Soviet biological weapons were suspected earlier.**  
    > “It was unknown in the West until a defector described it in 1989.”

    The defection revealed crucial information about the programme’s extent. Western governments already had substantial evidence and suspicions, particularly following the 1979 Sverdlovsk outbreak. A March 1980 US intelligence assessment explicitly judged an active Soviet biological-weapons programme highly probable. **Confidence: high.** [Declassified contemporary assessment](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v26/d111)

12. **Factual overstatement and prohibited absolute — inheritance is not guaranteed.**  
    > “Changes made at that stage are passed on to the children's children and to every generation after.”

    Embryo edits **can** be inherited if they enter the reproductive cells. Mosaicism can prevent that, and ordinary inheritance does not guarantee that an edited variant reaches every descendant or persists forever. The risk is heritability, not inevitable transmission through every generation. **Confidence: high.** [NHGRI on germline editing and mosaicism](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Genome-Editing/ethical-concerns)

13. **Direct contradiction with chapter 92.**  
    > “In every war since 1945, including wars that nuclear powers lost, in Korea, Vietnam and Afghanistan, the weapons have stayed in their bunkers.”

    Chapter 92 describes a nuclear torpedo aboard B-59 and says: “Bombers loaded with hydrogen bombs circled over the Arctic and the Mediterranean in shifts, waiting for a code word.” The sound claim is that nuclear weapons were not used in combat after 1945—not that they remained in storage. **Confidence: high.** [Chapter 92](chapters/92-missiles/chapter.md)

14. **Overgeneralisation about arms-control verification.**  
    > “The two great arsenals were brought down by a series of agreements, each verified by satellites and by inspectors who counted warheads on the other side's bases.”

    Verification differed by agreement. In particular, the 2002 Moscow Treaty had no verification system of its own, relying on arrangements under START. “Each” falsely makes one procedure universal. **Confidence: high.** [Arms Control Association’s treaty comparison](https://www.armscontrol.org/factsheets/us-russian-nuclear-arms-control-agreements-glance)

15. **Factual error and an absolute contradicted by the explanation that follows.**  
    > “They did not burn, were not poisonous, and reacted with nothing, and they were soon used in air conditioners and spray cans all over the world.”

    CFCs are unusually stable in the lower atmosphere, not chemically incapable of reacting. Their breakdown under ultraviolet radiation is essential to the story immediately afterwards. “Reacted with nothing” makes that mechanism appear contradictory. **Confidence: high.** [UNEP’s explanation of ozone-depleting substances](https://ozone.unep.org/20-questions-and-answers)

16. **Outdated death toll.**  
    > “In 1995 a Japanese religious sect with a large budget and trained chemists released nerve gas in the Tokyo underground and killed thirteen people.”

    By the chapter’s 2025 cutoff, the recognised toll was **fourteen**, following another victim’s death in 2020. **Confidence: high.** [AP’s thirtieth-anniversary report](https://apnews.com/article/95e9e258f347f14bc067b8b9702e051a)

17. **Reader gap — DNA turns into viral genetic material without explanation.**  
    > “The complete code of a new virus can be read in hours, which is how a vaccine came to be designed in a weekend in January 2020.”

    The preceding explanation discusses DNA alone. The coronavirus example involves an RNA genome, while chapter 112 has already introduced messenger RNA. One sentence distinguishing DNA from RNA would prevent readers from assuming all these instructions are the same molecule. This is an explanatory omission, not a contradiction with chapter 112.

18. **Reader gap — the chronology abruptly reverses.**  
    > “The line that scientists had agreed not to cross was crossed almost at once.”

    This follows approval of a treatment in **2023**, then introduces the twins in **2018**. “Almost at once” has no clear starting point: the reader must work backwards to the 2012 discovery. Establish the earlier date and explain that the boundary concerned reproductive editing, which differs from treating an existing patient.

19. **Internal contradiction in Petrov’s aftermath.**  
    > “Petrov was neither rewarded nor punished.”  
    > “He was reprimanded for failing to keep the log up to date during the alarm, retired early, and lived on a small pension in a flat outside Moscow.”

    A reprimand is a punishment in ordinary usage. If the distinction is between his false-alarm judgment and his record-keeping, state it. As written, the second sentence immediately undermines the first.

20. **Explicit style violation — signposting and an outline-derived heading.**  
    > “These cases had certain things in common, and the list explains both why they worked and why the warming of the climate has been so much harder.”

    This announces an explanation instead of giving it, contrary to “No signpost sentences.” The heading **“What the successes shared”** also reproduces a planning category rather than coming from a concrete feature of the story.

I found no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-mentions or body cross-references. Beyond the bunker claim, I found no clear direct contradictions with chapters 92, 111 or 112.
