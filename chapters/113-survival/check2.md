## Factual flags

1. **Major — not every modern warhead exceeds Hiroshima’s yield.**
   
   > “Each of those warheads is also far stronger than the first bombs.”
   
   Modern arsenals include lower-yield weapons. The deployed American W76-2 is estimated at about **8 kilotons**, below Hiroshima’s roughly 15 kilotons; some B61 settings are lower still. The existing sentence about *typical* strength does not justify “each.” [Federation of American Scientists, *United States nuclear weapons, 2025*](https://fas.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/United-States-nuclear-weapons-2025.pdf)

2. **Major — warning can be checked before impact.**
   
   > “The warning, in other words, has to be acted on before it can be checked.”
   
   This confuses checking a warning with waiting for missiles to land. American procedures cross-check satellite and ground-radar information before notifying senior leadership. Launch under attack is also an option, not an unavoidable requirement. The correct point is that assessment and decision must happen under severe time pressure. [US Department of Defense, nuclear-triad factsheet](https://media.defense.gov/2020/Nov/24/2002541293/-1/-1/1/FACTSHEET-THE-IMPORTANCE-OF-MODERNIZING-THE-NUCLEAR-TRIAD.PDF)

3. **Minor — reports were leads, not automatically cases.**
   
   > “Each report gave them a new case to surround.”
   
   Reports required investigation and confirmation; other rash illnesses could be mistaken for smallpox. A report supplied a possible case, and confirmed cases triggered containment. CDC’s contemporary photograph description explicitly distinguishes reporting, investigation, confirmation, and payment of the reward. [CDC/WHO account of the reporting scheme](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/Details.aspx?pid=7605)

4. **Minor — Montreal’s fund did not support every country.**
   
   > “Rich countries went first and paid into a fund to help poor ones change over, so that no country was asked to give up the gases without help.”
   
   The financial mechanism supports eligible developing-country parties under Article 5. Wealthier countries were contributors, not universally recipients. The correct claim concerns assistance to eligible poorer countries. [Montreal Protocol, Article 10](https://ozone.unep.org/treaties/montreal-protocol/articles/article-10-financial-mechanism)

5. **Minor — the manufacturer’s commercial-readiness chronology is misleading.**
   
   > “The largest manufacturer, which had by then developed substitutes, dropped its opposition; it now had something else to sell.”
   
   This makes ready replacements the established reason for DuPont’s change of position. When it supported international controls in 1986, it said substitutes **could become available within five years** and resumed research. Some alternatives existed for particular uses, but broad commercial replacement was still ahead. The accurate distinction is between anticipating a replacement market and already having the necessary replacements ready to sell. [Edward Parson’s research on the negotiations](https://www.belfercenter.org/sites/default/files/pantheon_files/files/publication/discussion_paper_92_02.pdf)

6. **Minor — the earlier polio-count problem remains unresolved.**
   
   > “That disease, which paralysed three hundred and fifty thousand children a year in the 1980s, has been driven down to a few dozen cases.”
   
   The revision changes the lead-in but retains the error identified in `check.md`. “A few dozen” excludes vaccine-derived disease and needs a specified year and category. WHO’s July 2025 assessment recorded **99 wild-virus cases and 319 vaccine-derived cases for 2024**. Total paralytic cases were in the hundreds. [WHO’s July 2025 assessment](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-07-2025-statement-of-the-forty-second-meeting-of-the-polio-ihr-emergency-committee)

7. **Major — the closing comparison gives smallpox eradication Montreal’s trade mechanism.**
   
   > “And a country that stayed out stood to lose its trade in the goods concerned.”
   
   The paragraph explicitly applies its explanation to both successes. Restrictions on trade with non-parties were a feature of the Montreal Protocol; smallpox eradication had no equivalent arrangement involving “the goods concerned.” Confine this explanation to ozone protection. [Montreal Protocol, Article 4](https://ozone.unep.org/treaties/montreal-protocol/articles/article-4-control-trade-non-parties)

## Clarity flags

- **Signposting:** “He thought that it did not make sense, and he had reasons, one after another.” The added clause announces the forthcoming list instead of advancing the story.

- **Signposting:** “For speed, the figure that matters is a smaller one.” This previews a statistic rather than explaining readiness. Similarly, “The ambition sounded grand, but it rested on a simple fact” announces the explanation that immediately follows.

- **Unclear referent:** “It is smaller than it was, but it runs on the same short clock.” “It” refers grammatically to warning machinery, but the next paragraph establishes that the *arsenals* are smaller.

- **Overcompressed certainty:** “Every person it could have reached next was already protected, and the outbreak ended where it stood.” “Every” and “already” make targeted containment sound like complete, instantaneous immunity. The documented Nigerian outbreak was contained over several weeks. [Foege, Millar and Henderson’s account](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1083309/)

- **Repeated conclusion:** “Because of that one lapse, a president had the launch codes open in front of him.” The paragraph has already described both the opened briefcase and the unpassed warning. This repeats the result.

- **Padding:** “That is a large reduction, and yet what is left is still enormous”; “He had caught it almost by chance”; and “None of this happened by itself.” Each adds little beyond what the surrounding facts already establish.

- **Vague wording:** “Progress could be checked by counting cases or measuring the sky, which made it hard for anyone to pretend.” Pretend what—compliance, success, or absence of danger? The added clause obscures the otherwise clear point about verification.

- **Reintroduced outline language:** “Set side by side, the two successes have a good deal in common.” This announces the comparison. The next sentence begins making it.

- **Heading mismatch:** “Nine, not twenty-five” introduces twenty-five, but the paragraph quotes a prediction of “fifteen or twenty.” Readers are left without the source of the heading’s comparison.

I found no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-mentions, or body cross-references. The new absolutes covered in the factual flags also break the style sheet.

Most earlier corrections remain intact. The main editorial need is to remove inaccurate explanatory additions and trim transitions that merely announce or repeat the point.
