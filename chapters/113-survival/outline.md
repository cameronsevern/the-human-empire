# Chapter 113 — Could humanity destroy itself?

**Line under the title:** The world, from 1958 to 2025.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. Mark clearly what has happened and what is a risk.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

It could, and it has had the means since the 1950s. There are still twelve thousand nuclear weapons, a fifth of them ready to fire within minutes, and the science that now reads and rewrites the code of life has made it possible to build a disease. Humanity has survived so far through a mixture of treaties, habits of restraint and plain luck, including the judgement of individual officers on particular nights. The record of nations working together against a shared danger is nevertheless much better than its reputation. A disease that had killed hundreds of millions was wiped out by doctors from both sides of the Cold War. A hole in the sky was noticed and is being mended. Those successes had things in common: the danger was plain, the remedy was affordable, the rich paid for the poor, and nobody could gain by cheating.

## The story

1. **Opening: five missiles.** Answer first. A bunker south of Moscow, 26 September 1983.
2. **Minutes.** The arsenals that remain; who can fire them and how quickly. What a large exchange would do to the weather. Other nights: a bomber over North Carolina, a rocket off Norway.
3. **Nine, not twenty-five.** What restraint has achieved: the test ban, the treaty against spread, the cuts, a country that built bombs and took them apart. A sentence agreed in Geneva. What is now lapsing.
4. **Reading.** The code of life: from the double helix to a genome for the price of a coat.
5. **Writing.** A bacterium's defence turned into a tool. A cure for an inherited disease. Twins in Shenzhen.
6. **A virus by post.** The other side of the tool: an old influenza rebuilt, a pox assembled from mail-order pieces, a secret Soviet programme, a treaty with four employees.
7. **The last case.** Smallpox: what it had done, how it was beaten, and who worked together.
8. **A hole in the sky.** A wonder chemical, two chemists' warning, an old instrument in Antarctica, and the treaty that every country signed.
9. **What the successes shared.** And why climate has been harder.
10. **Ending: a hospital cook.**
