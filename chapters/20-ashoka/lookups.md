# Chapter 20 fact-check lookups

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. "Fixed" means the chapter text was changed. Edict quotations and paraphrases are checked against Ven. S. Dhammika, *The Edicts of King Asoka* (Buddhist Publication Society, Wheel 386/387, 1993), text at https://www.cs.colostate.edu/~malaiya/ashoka.html (same text: https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/dhammika/wheel386.html). RE = Major Rock Edict, PE = Pillar Edict, SE = Separate (Kalinga) Edict.

## Quotations

1. "One hundred and fifty thousand were deported, one hundred thousand were killed and many more died." — correct, RE 13, Dhammika, verbatim (his bracketed gloss "(from other causes)" left off).
2. "Now Beloved-of-the-Gods feels deep remorse for having conquered the Kalingas." — correct, RE 13, verbatim.
3. "despite his remorse Beloved-of-the-Gods has the power to punish them if necessary" — correct, RE 13, verbatim.
4. "conquest by Dhamma" as the best conquest — correct, RE 13. First draft said "the only conquest worth making"; fixed to "the best conquest" (Codex).
5. "All men are my children." / "You do not understand to what extent I desire this" — correct, SE 1, verbatim.
6. "two peacocks and a deer" / "and the deer not always" — correct, RE 1, verbatim.
7. "the fault of the scribe" — correct, RE 14.
8. "Thus speaks King Piyadasi, Beloved of the Gods" (opening of the Delhi pillar text) — correct in sense; Dhammika renders PE 1 "Beloved-of-the-Gods speaks thus" and PE 2 "Beloved-of-the-Gods, King Piyadasi, speaks thus". Prinsep's 1837 reading was of the formula *Devanampiya Piyadasi laja hevam aha*. JASB vol. 6 (1837) pp. 566–609, https://archive.org/stream/journalofasiatic62asia#page/566/mode/2up. Imprecise as a single verbatim line; kept as the formula.

## Paraphrases of the edicts

9. Good people in a conquered country killed, injured or separated; a hundredth or thousandth part now pains him — correct, RE 13.
10. Hope that sons and great-grandsons make no new conquests, or use "forbearance and light punishment" — correct, RE 13.
11. Lay follower for more than two and a half years; visits to the Sangha — correct, Minor Rock Edict 1. Tour to Sambodhi ten years after coronation — RE 8.
12. Lumbini: pillar set up, village to pay only one eighth of the produce — correct, Minor Pillar Edict 1 (Rummindei).
13. List of behaviour (parents, generosity, servants, not killing, moderation in spending and saving, truth) — correct, RE 3, 9, 11; Minor Rock Edict 2 for truth.
14. All sects honoured; do not praise one's own and condemn others — correct, RE 12.
15. "The great series of rock edicts says nothing about the life of a monk" — fixed from "the rock edicts" (Codex: the Bairat minor edict addresses monks and nuns).
16. Protected creatures (parrots, bats, tortoises, porcupines, squirrels); forests not to be burnt to kill creatures — correct, PE 5.
17. Earlier kings toured to hunt; he tours to preach — correct, RE 8.
18. Banyans, mango groves, wells, rest houses, watering places; "such things … have been done by former kings" — correct, PE 7. Medical care for humans and animals, herbs imported and planted — RE 2.
19. Officers of dhamma appointed thirteen years after coronation; work among all sects, soldiers, poor, aged, prisoners, and in the households of his brothers and sisters — correct, RE 5. First draft said they "could walk into any household"; fixed (Codex).
20. Officials tour every five years — correct, RE 3. Reports at any hour (eating, women's quarters, bedchamber, chariot, park) — RE 6.
21. Five Greek kings (Antiochos, Ptolemy, Antigonos, Magas, Alexander) and envoys — correct, RE 13 ("even where Beloved-of-the-Gods' envoys have not been"). Identified as Antiochus II, Ptolemy II, Antigonus Gonatas, Magas of Cyrene, Alexander of Epirus; all alive c. 260–255 BCE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edicts_of_Ashoka
22. Unjust imprisonment and harsh treatment; anger, cruelty, laziness; inspector every five years; read aloud on Tisa days "even by a single person" — correct, SE 1.
23. Three-day stay for the condemned — correct, PE 4.
24. Regulations "of little effect", persuasion more — correct, PE 7 (dated 27 years after coronation, his last dated long edict).
25. Dhauli and Jaugada omit RE 11–13 and carry the two Separate Edicts instead — correct. Dhammika note 1; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_Rock_Edicts (Jaugada: "Major Rock Edicts 1-10, 14, Separate Edicts 1&2"). RE 13 survives at Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra, Kalsi, Girnar, Erragudi and in Greek at Kandahar.

## Decipherment

26. Delhi pillar 13 m, on top of the palace at Feroz Shah Kotla — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi-Topra_pillar
27. Brought to Delhi "in the 1350s" — imprecise. Modern summaries give 1356; the contemporary account dates the erection to 1367 (ASI Memoir on Kotla Firoz Shah, https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/22024.pdf, via Codex). Fixed to "in the fourteenth century".
28. The sultan's learned men could not read it — correct. Afif: Brahmans and Hindu devotees were invited to read it and none could. https://www.tribuneindia.com/2011/20111127/spectrum/art.htm ; https://www.notesonindianhistory.com/2018/10/the-golden-minaret-of-firoz-shah-tughlaq.html
29. Prinsep, assay master at the Calcutta mint, editor of the Journal of the Asiatic Society; 1837; Sanchi donation inscriptions ending in the same two letters, read as *danam* — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Prinsep ; Dhammika, "James Prinsep and the Discovery of King Asoka", https://smallpdfs.buddhistuniversity.net/dhammika_2012_prinsep-discovers-ashoka.pdf
30. "he had the letters d and n, and the rest followed" — imprecise. Lassen had read some signs from bilingual coins in 1836, and the signs are syllables. Fixed: "the signs for da and na", plus the coins.
31. Piyadasi identified as Ashoka through George Turnour and the Sri Lankan chronicles, 1837 — correct (same sources). Name "Asoka" itself confirmed by the Maski edict, found 1915 (not used in the text).
32. Prinsep died 1840 aged forty — correct (20 Aug 1799 – 22 Apr 1840).

## Chandragupta and the Greek sources

33. Alexander's army refused to go on "in the valley of the Indus" — wrong place. The mutiny was at the Hyphasis (Beas), in the Punjab, 326 BCE. Fixed (Codex). Died 323 BCE: correct.
34. Seleucus ceded eastern provinces and received 500 elephants — correct. Strabo 15.2.9; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid%E2%80%93Mauryan_war. c. 305–303 BCE.
35. Pataliputra 80 stadia long (about 15 km), wooden wall with loopholes, ditch, 570 towers, 64 gates — correct. Strabo 15.1.36; Arrian, Indica 10. Text reduced to "hundreds of towers" (one number per point).
36. Timber palisade found at Patna — correct. Bulandi Bagh: Spooner 1912–13; Page and Ghosh 1926–27 (137 m). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulandi_Bagh
37. City committees (foreigners nursed and buried; births and deaths; markets; tenth on sales) — correct. Strabo 15.1.51, https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/15A3*.html
38. Ants "no smaller than foxes" that dig gold — correct, Strabo 15.1.44. No slaves — Strabo 15.1.54.
39. Arthashastra found 1905 by a librarian (R. Shamasastry, Mysore), "among a heap of manuscripts" — first draft said he "was handed" it; fixed. Compiled 1st–3rd century CE (Olivelle 2013; McClish 2019). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthashastra ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Shamasastry
40. "His son pushed the frontier far down into the south" — could not verify (who conquered the south is disputed). Fixed: states only that the empire reached the south by Ashoka's accession (Codex).
41. Chandragupta ruled about 24 years — correct (Puranas). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya

## Kalinga, Kandahar

42. Kalinga conquered in the eighth year after the coronation — correct, RE 13. Ashoka c. 268–232 BCE.
43. Later Buddhist legends of the murdered brothers and the torture chamber — correct as legend (Ashokavadana). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka
44. Kandahar inscription found 1958, Greek above Aramaic, *eusebeia*; king abstains from living beings, "huntsmen and fishermen of the king have desisted from hunting" — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar_Bilingual_Rock_Inscription

## Pillars

45. Single shaft, 12–15 m, up to about 50 tons — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Ashoka
46. "No separate base … bottom left rough" — imprecise. Shafts stood on stone foundation slabs in a pit about 2.5 m deep; some buried parts are polished. Fixed to "no visible base … foot rested on a slab of stone" (Codex; Dhammika, "King Asoka's Amazing Engineers", https://smallpdfs.buddhistuniversity.net/dhammika_2010_ashokas-engineers.pdf; Falk, *Asokan Sites and Artefacts*).
47. Polished with abrasive grit and water — correct (Dhammika 2010).
48. Capital a separate block (lotus bell, abacus, animal), fixed by a metal dowel; copper bolt from Rampurva — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Ashoka ; photograph of the bolt, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Copper_bolt_in_Rampurwa_Ashok_Pillar.jpg. Length of "about sixty centimetres" could not be verified; cut.
49. "Most of the pale pillars were cut at Chunar" — imprecise; the single-quarry view is disputed (Falk assigns most shafts to Pabhosa on the Yamuna; others name Mathura stone). Fixed: Chunar "long named as the main source", others cut near the Yamuna (Codex).
50. Pillars lie close to large rivers — correct (Dhammika 2010: all within 25 km of a river except Sanchi).
51. The sultan's move: silk-cotton, reeds and raw hides, cart with 42 wheels, 200 men to each rope, large grain boats, raised by stages with ropes and windlasses, "golden pillar" (Minara-i zarrin) — correct. Afif, Tarikh-i Firoz Shahi (Elliot and Dowson vol. 3), quoted at https://www.tribuneindia.com/2011/20111127/spectrum/art.htm
52. "At least twenty" pillars — correct (twenty survive; Dhammika counts thirty with fragments).
53. "each letter about as tall as a thumbnail is wide" — could not verify; cut.
54. Brahmi: "each sign a consonant, a stroke shows the vowel" — imprecise; the bare sign carries a short a. Fixed with ka/ki/ku (Codex; Unicode core spec ch. 14).
55. Scripts of India, Sri Lanka, Tibet and south-east Asia descend from Brahmi — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script

## After Ashoka

56. Last Maurya killed by his general Pushyamitra c. 185 BCE, within fifty years of Ashoka's death (c. 232) — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brihadratha_Maurya
57. King of Kalinga (Kharavela), inscription at Udayagiri near Bhubaneswar, raid on Magadha — correct; "soon" was imprecise (2nd century BCE to 1st century CE) and "a few kilometres" loose. Fixed to "in time" and "about ten kilometres". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathigumpha_inscription
58. Pillars said to be the walking sticks of Bhima — correct for the sultan's time (Afif). First draft said "in several villages"; fixed.
59. Mahinda, Ashoka's son, to Sri Lanka — correct as the chronicles' account (Dipavamsa, Mahavamsa); stated as such.
60. Sarnath capital found 1905 (F. O. Oertel, engineer); four lions, abacus with horse, bull, elephant, lion and four wheels; just over two metres — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Capital_of_Ashoka
61. Wheel on the flag 1947; lions adopted as emblem 26 January 1950 — correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Emblem_of_India
62. India keeps "more than a million soldiers" — correct (about 1.4 million active).

## Still weak

- No Mauryan record says how the pillars were moved; the river route is an inference from where pillars and quarries lie.
- "Nothing quite like it survives from any other conqueror of the ancient world" is the standard judgement, not something a lookup can prove.
- Distances given as marching time (weeks to Kalinga, months to Kandahar) are my arithmetic from map distances (about 600 km and 2,100 km in a straight line from Patna).
