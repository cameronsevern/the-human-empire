# Chapter 21 fact-check lookups

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. "Years ago" are counted from 2026. Items marked "from Hulsewé, not confirmed online" rest on the standard translation (A.F.P. Hulsewé, *Remnants of Ch'in Law*, Leiden 1985), which could not be opened on the web during the check; the wording in the chapter was kept general for those.

## The bronze scoop

1. "In the Shanghai Museum there is a shallow bronze box ... with a short handle at one end ... it holds a fifth of a litre."
   - Verdict: correct. Shang Yang fangsheng, Shanghai Museum; 18.7 cm long overall, inside 12.5 x 7 x 2.3 cm, capacity 202.15 ml, hollow handle.
   - Source: https://live2makan.com/2025/07/16/treasures-rectangular-sheng-by-shang-yang-warring-states/ ; https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00004222
2. "made in the eighteenth year of a duke of Qin, which was about 2,370 years ago, on the order of his chief minister ... Sixteen and one fifth cubic inches ... make one measure."
   - Verdict: correct after edit (Codex: the unit is the Qin cun, about 2.31 cm, not a modern inch; a sentence now says so). 18th year of Duke Xiao = 344 BCE; inscription "爰積十六尊(寸)五分尊(寸)壹爲升".
   - Source: same; Cambridge, "Attaining accuracy and precision of measuring containers during the Qin dynasty" (dates it c. 344 BCE, 16.2 cubic cun).
3. "On the underside there is a second inscription ... cut 123 years after the first. Its forty characters announce ..."
   - Verdict: correct. Edict of the 26th year (221 BCE), 40 characters: "廿六年，皇帝盡并兼天下諸侯，黔首大安，立號爲皇帝。乃詔丞相狀、綰：法度量則不壹歉疑者，皆明壹之." 344 - 221 = 123. Paraphrased, not quoted.
   - Source: same as 1.
4. Place names Chongquan and Lin on the scoop.
   - Verdict: correct but cut for length. Chongquan = modern Pucheng, Shaanxi.

## Scene

5. "About 3,200 years ago, a line of kings called the Shang ..." oracle bones on ox shoulder blades and turtle under-shells; heat applied, cracks read; inscription cut afterwards.
   - Verdict: correct after edit. Codex pointed out that the charge was inscribed after the cracking; order of the two sentences reversed.
   - Source: Kern, Cambridge History of Chinese Literature ch. 1, https://mkern.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf2716/files/mkern/files/kern_cambridge_history_of_chinese_literature.pdf
6. "Each sign stands for a whole word, where a letter of an alphabet stands for a single sound."
   - Verdict: imprecise in the first draft ("a word, not a sound"); rewritten. Characters write words/syllables and many have a sound component; the comparison is now with a letter.
   - Source: Codex item 2; ISAC, *Exploring Written Artefacts*.
7. "A little over 3,000 years ago the Shang were overthrown by ... the Zhou." Mandate of Heaven.
   - Verdict: correct. Conquest conventionally 1046 BCE.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_of_Heaven
8. "an idea that the rulers of China went on using for three thousand years"
   - Verdict: correct after edit. Draft said it "outlasted them by three thousand years", which was wrong (Zhou ended 256 BCE; the idea was used until 1912).
9. "what had once been well over a hundred states were seven large ones"
   - Verdict: correct. About 148 states are named in the Spring and Autumn records; seven major states by the 5th-4th century BCE. Draft's "handed it to more than a hundred" changed to "dozens" (tradition gives 71 original fiefs).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period
10. Chariot nobles giving way to mass infantry with crossbows.
   - Verdict: correct (standard account, e.g. M. E. Lewis, *The Early Chinese Empires*, ch. 2).

## Shang Yang

11. "About 2,385 years ago, a new duke of Qin let it be known ..." Shang Yang, from the small state of Wey, served about twenty years.
   - Verdict: correct. Duke Xiao acceded 361 BCE; reforms from c. 359/356; Shang Yang died 338.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_Yang
12. Households registered, grouped in fives and tens, mutual liability.
   - Verdict: correct. Shiji 68; confirmed in practice by the Shuihudi "Answers" (items on the group of five, Hulsewé D 80, D 81).
   - Source: R. Eno, "4.2 The Laws of Qin", https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/items/417a4350-ac3f-413d-b1e5-df06a735051b
13. Ranks: "in time had twenty steps"; one head, one step, with land and a house plot.
   - Verdict: correct. Book of Lord Shang ch. 19; seventeen or eighteen ranks in the pre-imperial period, twenty later.
   - Source: Eno (above) speaks of "eighteen ranks"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_Yang
14. Double tax on households with two grown sons.
   - Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_Yang
15. "Land ... held by the household that worked it, which in time could buy and sell it."
   - Verdict: imprecise but safe. The tradition (Dong Zhongshu, in Hanshu 24) says Shang Yang allowed sale of land; excavated texts show the state also allotted land by rank. Worded as "in time".
16. Inn story; body torn apart by chariots.
   - Verdict: correct as a story from the sources (Shiji 68, Zhanguo ce). Chapter attributes it to "the historian who tells the story".
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_Yang
17. "The new ruler kept the laws."
   - Verdict: correct after edit (draft said "every one of his laws"; Codex item 13).

## Conquest and empire

18. "about 2,270 years ago, a boy of thirteen named Zheng became its king"
   - Verdict: correct. Born 259, king 247/246 BCE.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang
19. "The whole conquest took nine years"; Chu beat one Qin army; Qi gave up without a battle.
   - Verdict: correct. Han 230, Zhao 228, Wei 225, Chu 223, Yan 222, Qi 221 BCE. Draft's "ten years" tightened; the first line's "within ten years" stands.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin%27s_wars_of_unification
20. Two letters on wood from brothers in the Chu campaign, asking their mother for money and summer clothes.
   - Verdict: correct. Heifu and Jing to their brother Zhong and mother, c. 223 BCE, Shuihudi tomb 4, Yunmeng. "If cloth is cheap at Anlu make the clothes and send them; if dear, send cash and I will buy cloth here."
   - Sources: https://uw.manifoldapp.org/read/chinese-autobiographical-writing/section/820f214a-dc88-46be-81a6-ed533f207d0c ; https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrbwap/html/2024-11/07/nw.D110000renmrb_20241107_1-07.htm
   - Removed: the draft said one brother asks about his rank papers and the other writes "urgent" three times. The rank reading is disputed (the published translation takes the words as a personal name) and the second could not be confirmed online. Both cut.
21. Title huangdi; First, Second, Third, "ten thousand generations".
   - Verdict: correct. Shiji 6. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang
22. Advisers urged kingdoms for his sons; thirty-six districts under appointed officials.
   - Verdict: correct. Shiji 6 (Wang Wan's proposal, Li Si's reply). Codex item 12: now says districts contained counties.

## One of everything

23. Edict cut into bronze measures and weights; cast on bronze plates for wooden measures; stamped into clay measures.
   - Verdict: correct. Clay measure from Zou county, Shandong (found 1950, National Museum of China): the 40-character edict pressed with ten four-character stamps.
   - Source: https://app-api.henandaily.cn/mobile/view/news/244261900576096256429439 ; https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202508/12/WS68ecbc79498e23165e06905f/inscribed-artifact-testifies-to-qin-dynastys-standardization-of-weights.html
24. Round coin with square hole, marked with its weight; shape kept about two thousand years.
   - Verdict: correct. Ban liang ("half liang"). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Liang
25. Script: "lists of approved signs in the Qin style ... The other styles faded away over the following generations."
   - Verdict: correct after edit. Draft said the others "were dropped"; Galambos shows variation continued.
   - Source: https://shahon.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/02/Galambos-2004-The-myth-of-the-Qin-unification-of-writing-in-Han-sources.pdf
26. Speech of north and south "as far apart as Portuguese and Romanian".
   - Verdict: correct as a statement about later centuries (the usual comparison of the Chinese varieties with the Romance languages). Rewritten so that it is not claimed for 221 BCE.
27. One axle width; highways; northern walls joined.
   - Verdict: correct. Shiji 6 ("carts had the same gauge"); walls joined under Meng Tian. The rut explanation is the standard one.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty
28. Village obligations: register, group of five, tax in grain and hay, a month's labour a year, army service, travel passes.
   - Verdict: correct. Hay and straw tax in the Shuihudi field statutes; passports in "Answers" D 46 (Eno). The month a year is from Han descriptions of the Qin system (Hanshu 24).

## Shuihudi

29. Found December 1975, tomb 11, Yunmeng, Hubei; about 1,155 strips; deposited c. 217 BCE.
   - Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuihudi_Qin_bamboo_texts
30. Xi: born about 2,290 years ago (262 BCE), clerk, heard criminal cases, served in the army, died at about forty-five; district taken from Chu sixteen years before his birth (278 BCE).
   - Verdict: correct. Source: Eno (above); Shuihudi chronicle (Biannianji).
31. Written reports on rain and crops, drought, flood, locusts.
   - Verdict: correct. Field statute. Source: https://www.aisixiang.com/data/46212.html
32. Oxen measured, ten strokes per inch lost.
   - Verdict: from Hulsewé (Statutes on Stables and Parks), not confirmed online.
33. Mouse holes: three or more a fine, two or fewer a reprimand; three small holes count as one.
   - Verdict: correct. Source: https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/chinabeatarchive/134 (which says "beating" for the lesser penalty; the Chinese term sui is a reprimand).
34. Fines in shields and suits of armour.
   - Verdict: correct after edit. Codex item 4: the inference that fines equipped the army was removed; Liye records show payment in cash. Source: Eno; Korolkov, "Between Command and Market".
35. Adult or child by height.
   - Verdict: correct. "Not yet of adult stature" in "Answers" D 54 (Eno); granary statute sets 6 chi 5 cun for men.
36. Model forms: the hanged man; two soldiers and one head.
   - Verdict: from Hulsewé (Fengzhenshi, group E), not confirmed online. The existence and nature of the forms is confirmed: https://uw.manifoldapp.org/read/chinese-autobiographical-writing/section/1fd1061c-c919-4caa-9867-3f49b0de3207
37. Thief tattooed and sent to hard labour; penalties graded by value and rank.
   - Verdict: correct. "Answers" D 1, D 40 (Eno).
38. Lateness for labour service: three to five days a reprimand, six to ten a shield; rain cancels the call-up.
   - Verdict: correct. "失期三日到五日，誶；六日到旬，貲一盾；過旬，貲一甲 ... 水雨，除興."
   - Source: https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1273210
39. Governor's circular about local customs, half a century after the conquest.
   - Verdict: correct. The Yushu of Governor Teng of Nan commandery, 227 BCE (278 - 227 = 51 years).
   - Source: https://m.mokecn.com/guoxue/info_15889.html

## The tomb

40. March 1974, farmers digging a well; about eight thousand figures in three pits; mound 1.5 km to the west.
   - Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta_Army
41. Foremen's names, "more than eighty".
   - Verdict: imprecise, kept round. The figure usually given is 87 names; workshops were required to mark their work. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta_Army
42. "seven hundred thousand men"
   - Verdict: correct as Sima Qian's figure, and given as his. Source: same.
43. Labourers' graves with inscribed tiles giving name, home town, rank and debt; mostly from the east.
   - Verdict: correct. Source: https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/201207/24/P201207240636_photo_1041100.htm
44. Bronze weapons / iron tools.
   - Verdict: correct; one sentence added at Codex's request (item 11).

## Books and scholars

45. Burning: eight years after the conquest (213 BCE); histories of other states and private copies burned; medicine, divination, farming spared; court scholars kept copies; capital burned seven years later (206 BCE).
   - Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_of_books_and_burying_of_scholars
46. The 460: begins with the elixir men; the word for the killing is ambiguous; later Han writers made them students of the classics.
   - Verdict: correct after edit. Codex item 5: Sima Qian's own account already has the heir Fusu call them followers of Confucius, so the draft's "grew in the telling" was too simple. Paragraph rewritten.
   - Source: same; https://ulrichneininger.de/?p=461

## The fall and the Han

47. Death "about 2,235 years ago", aged forty-nine.
   - Verdict: correct (210 BCE, born 259).
48. Nine hundred conscripts, rain, lateness punished by beheading "according to Sima Qian"; Chen Sheng a former hired farmhand; dead in six months, killed by his driver.
   - Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Sheng_and_Wu_Guang_uprising ; https://ctext.org/shiji/chen-she-shi-jia/ens
49. Convicts from the tomb armed.
   - Verdict: correct (Shiji 6, Zhang Han's proposal). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty
50. "A little over three years after the First Emperor's death"; "fifteen years".
   - Verdict: correct. Death autumn 210; surrender of Ziying late 207 BCE; empire 221 to 207/206.
51. "After the conquest there were no more enemy heads to take."
   - Verdict: wrong (Codex item 1: campaigns continued north and south). Replaced.
52. Liu Bang: farmer's son from old Chu, minor Qin officer, emperor about 2,230 years ago (202 BCE); Han lasted four centuries.
   - Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Gaozu_of_Han
53. The official who took the maps, registers and law books.
   - Verdict: correct as a story from Shiji 53 (Xiao He); told with "the story goes". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiao_He
54. Han official buried about thirty years after Xi with law strips repeating Qin rules.
   - Verdict: correct. Zhangjiashan tomb 247, statutes of 186 BCE. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangjiashan_Han_bamboo_texts
55. Han kingdoms in the east.
   - Verdict: added at Codex's request (item 3). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Han_dynasty
56. Grain tax cut to a thirtieth.
   - Verdict: correct (one fifteenth at first, one thirtieth from the reigns of Wen and Jing).
57. "they stopped saying that they ruled because they were strong"
   - Verdict: wrong emphasis (Codex item 9: Qin inscriptions claim merit and order). Rewritten.
58. "China, most likely comes from Qin"
   - Verdict: correct as the leading explanation. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_China
59. Han measure the same size.
   - Verdict: correct; the Han sheng was also about 200 ml. Wording changed from "the same bronze scoop" to "a scoop of the same size".
