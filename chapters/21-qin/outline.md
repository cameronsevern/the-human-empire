# Chapter 21 — How did China become one country?

**Line under the title:** China, from about 2,400 years ago to about 2,200 years ago.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

China was made one country by conquest, and the conquest was won by counting. For centuries the valley of the Yellow River was divided among states that fought each other without pause. One of them, Qin, on the western edge, rebuilt itself as a machine for farming and fighting: every household registered, neighbours answerable for each other, rank earned by enemy heads, land, grain and labour measured by one set of bronze standards. That machine conquered the other six states in ten years. Its king then called himself the First Emperor and pushed the same measures, coins, script, cart gauge, roads, laws and labour duties onto everyone. The laws found in a clerk's tomb show the system from inside: exact, written, tireless, and heavy. It was the weight that broke it. Within four years of the emperor's death the conscripts revolted and the dynasty was gone. Its successor, the Han, kept nearly all the machinery and changed the story told about it.

## The story

1. **A bronze cup.** Answer first. The measure made for Shang Yang, its inscription, and the emperor's edict cut into its base 123 years later.
2. **Millet, bones and a river of mud.** The scene: millet and rice, the Yellow River, the oracle bones and the script, the Zhou kings and the Mandate of Heaven, the lords who became kings, the change from chariots to conscript infantry.
3. **A head for a rank.** Qin in the west. Shang Yang's reforms: registration, groups of five, rank for heads, land, counties, measures. Legalism in three sentences. His end.
4. **Ten years.** The six states fall. Two soldiers write home for money. The title of emperor; thirty-six districts under appointed officials.
5. **One of everything.** Measures and weights with the edict; one coin; one script, and why it mattered that the script wrote words and not sounds; the cart gauge; roads; walls in two sentences. What a farming family owed.
6. **The clerk's coffin.** Xi of Shuihudi and his bamboo strips: mouse holes, thin oxen, late labourers, a quarrel over a head. The governor's complaint about local customs.
7. **An army of clay.** The tomb at Mount Li, the pits, the stamped names, the labourers' graves.
8. **What his enemies wrote.** Sima Qian and the Han. The burning of the books and the buried scholars, read critically.
9. **Delayed by rain.** The emperor's death, the nine hundred conscripts, the collapse. What the Han kept and how it changed the justification. The name.
