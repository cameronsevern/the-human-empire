# Chapter 45 — Why did Japan close itself off?

**Line under the title:** Japan and Korea, from 1543 to about 1700.
**Length:** 4,300 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Japan did not shut itself away out of ignorance, and it never shut completely. Europeans arrived in the middle of a century of civil war, and their guns helped three commanders in turn to end it. The second of them then threw his idle armies at Korea, a peaceful kingdom governed by examined scholars, and wrecked it before a Korean admiral and a Chinese army forced him out. The family that inherited Japan, the Tokugawa, wanted above all never to be challenged again. Foreign ships brought two dangers to that aim: a religion whose followers obeyed a pope, and trading profits that could make a distant lord strong. So they banned the religion, forbade their own people to leave, and squeezed foreign trade through four guarded doors, one of which was a tiny island for the Dutch. The reward was two and a half centuries of peace, the largest city in the world, and a people among the most literate on earth. "Closed country" is a name invented later.

## The story

1. **Opening: two guns on an island.** Answer first. 1543: a storm-blown ship, a young lord, a swordsmith.
2. **The country at war.** A century of fighting lords. How quickly the gun was copied and what it did at Nagashino.
3. **Pound, knead and eat.** The three unifiers. The sword hunt and the measuring of the land.
4. **Learned in a morning.** Korea: scholar-officials, and a king's alphabet. The invasion of 1592.
5. **Thirteen ships.** The admiral, the Chinese army, and what was left of Korea.
6. **The pilot's boast.** Christianity in Japan: fast growth, sudden fear, the last rising, the picture on the floor.
7. **Hostages in the capital.** How the Tokugawa held the lords. The edicts, the fan-shaped island, the four doors.
8. **The quiet centuries.** Peace, cities and readers. A later name.
9. **Ending: the news from the Dutch ship.**
