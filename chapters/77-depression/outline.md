# Chapter 77 — What caused the Great Depression?

**Line under the title:** The United States and the world, from 1919 to 1939.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The crash of the New York stock market in 1929 did not have to become a depression. It became one because the world's banks failed by the thousand and its governments, loyal to the gold standard and to balanced budgets, did the opposite of what was needed: they let the supply of money shrink, cut their spending, and shut out one another's goods. The world's finances were also a circle of war debts that depended on American lending, and when the lending stopped the collapse crossed the Atlantic. A quarter of the workers in the richest countries on Earth had no work. Countries recovered roughly in the order in which they gave up gold. The slump discredited the belief that markets right themselves, gave governments a new job, and handed their best argument to the enemies of democracy.

## The story

1. **Opening: a rumour in the Bronx.** Answer first. December 1930: a merchant asks for his money, and a bank with four hundred thousand customers is gone in two days.
2. **A promise to pay gold.** What the gold standard was and why people believed in it. The circle of war debts. Germany's money dies in 1923.
3. **A permanently high plateau.** America in the 1920s: cars on credit, shares on credit. October 1929.
4. **Twenty-five thousand banks.** How a crash became a depression: banks with no insurance, a shrinking supply of money, falling prices that made every debt heavier. One worker in four.
5. **Liquidate.** What governments did: balance the budget, protect the currency, raise tariffs.
6. **Coffee in the locomotives.** 1931: a bank in Vienna, then Germany, then Britain leaves gold. What the slump did to the countries that sold raw materials.
7. **Fear itself.** March 1933: a new president closes every bank, and then talks on the radio. The New Deal.
8. **The paradox of thrift.** An English economist explains why the old remedies made things worse. Who recovered, and when.
9. **Ending: what it left.** A new duty for governments. An argument that has not ended. Germany's six million.
