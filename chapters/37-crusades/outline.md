# Chapter 37 — Why did the Crusades happen?

**Line under the title:** Western Europe, Constantinople and Syria, from 800 to 1204.
**Length:** 4,800 to 5,400 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

In 1095 a pope told the knights of western Europe that they could wipe out the penance owed for their sins by marching to Jerusalem and taking it by force. It cost a knight several years' income to go, most who went gained nothing and many died, and they went by the tens of thousands, which makes the First Crusade one of the clearest cases in history of a war fought for belief. Everybody else involved understood it differently. The pope wanted to lead Christendom. The emperor in Constantinople had asked for a few hundred hired cavalry against the Turks and got an army he could not control. The Jews of the Rhineland were murdered by men on their way to fight someone else. The Muslim rulers of Syria took the newcomers for one more band of raiders in a region full of them, and it was eighty years before one of them united the region and took Jerusalem back. In 1204 a crusade ended by sacking the Christian city the first one had set out to help.

## The story

1. **Opening: a castle in pawn.** Answer first. A duke mortgages his castle to a bishop to pay for the journey. What going cost.
2. **Barefoot in the snow.** Western Europe after Rome: a king crowned emperor by a pope in 800, and the long quarrel over who was master. Knights, castles and the church's problem with men who kill for a living.
3. **The emperor's request.** The Turks, the battle of 1071, and what Constantinople asked for.
4. **God wills it.** The sermon in a field in 1095. The promise. Why it worked on that audience.
5. **The enemy nearer home.** The Rhineland, spring 1096.
6. **Barbarians at dinner.** The armies reach Constantinople, seen by the emperor's daughter. The oath.
7. **Up to the bridles.** Antioch, hunger, the lance. Jerusalem, July 1099.
8. **One more invader.** Why the Muslim world was slow to answer. A Syrian gentleman's opinion of the Franks. Monks with swords; Italian merchants.
9. **Ten dinars a man.** Saladin, the battle of 1187, and the surrender of Jerusalem. A truce with an English king.
10. **Four bronze horses.** A crusade that could not pay for its ships. Constantinople, 1204.
