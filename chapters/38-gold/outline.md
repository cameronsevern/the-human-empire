# Chapter 38 — Who was the richest person in history?

**Line under the title:** West Africa and the Sahara, from about 800 to 1591.
**Length:** 4,400 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The usual answer is Mansa Musa of Mali, and what matters about him is where the wealth came from. He owned no mines. West Africa's gold was dug by villagers whom no king controlled, and it was wanted across the Sahara by people who had salt, which West Africa lacked. Three empires in turn, Ghana, Mali and Songhai, grew rich by holding the towns where the two were exchanged and taxing every load. Musa's pilgrimage to Mecca spent a fortune in gold and bought something that lasted longer: a name known in Cairo and on European maps, scholars, builders, and a place for his kingdom in the Muslim world. The last of the three empires was destroyed by a Moroccan army that crossed the desert with guns, by which time Portuguese ships on the coast had already begun to draw the gold another way.

## The story

1. **Opening: a king on a map.** Answer first. The Catalan Atlas of 1375 and the Black king holding a nugget of gold.
2. **Houses of salt.** The camel and the crossing. A traveller's account of the salt mine in the middle of the desert, and of the guide sent ahead for water. Why the south needed salt.
3. **Nuggets for the king.** Where the gold was and who dug it. The first empire: a Spanish geographer's report of the court of Ghana, the tax on each load, and the rule about nuggets.
4. **The lion's song.** The founding of Mali, as its singers still tell it. The river.
5. **Twelve years of cheap gold.** The pilgrimage of 1324: the caravan, Cairo, the price of gold. What he said about the miners. What he brought back.
6. **Books at a profit.** Timbuktu as a town of scholars. A Muslim court in a country of farmers who were not. A traveller's praise and complaints. The other cargo that went north.
7. **Cattle against guns.** Songhai on the river. The Moroccan crossing of 1591 and the battle.
8. **Ending.** Ships on the coast; a scholar marched north without his library.
