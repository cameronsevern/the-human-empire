# Fact-check: Chapter 2, "How did humans end up everywhere?"

Method: web searches (search tool returns summaries, so some items rest on summaries rather than the primary paper). Items marked NOT SEARCHED were not checked on the web; I did not want to pass them off as verified.

Tally: 35 items. 29 searched on the web, 5 not searched (2, 9, 24, 26, 33), 1 checked by arithmetic only (1).
Verdicts: 17 CONFIRMED (one, #1, by arithmetic only), 2 WRONG, 10 PARTLY, 1 NOT FOUND, 5 NOT SEARCHED.

---

## Items

**1. 20 km/generation across Asia in ~10,000 years.** CONFIRMED (own arithmetic, no web). 20 km per 25 years = 0.8 km/yr. Asia is roughly 9,000 km across, giving ~11,000 years. "About ten thousand" is fair.

**2. Africa at 70,000; Siberia, Australia, far South America by 12,000.** NOT SEARCHED. Standard picture; no contradiction found in anything else I read.

**3. Camel: a week without water; hump is fat.** CONFIRMED. NHM: hump stores fat, not water; sources say "a week or so" without drinking. https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/how-do-camels-survive-in-deserts.html

**4. "At least five kinds of human alive at once" 70,000 years ago.** PARTLY. The count survives, but one of the chapter's examples does not.
- Five kinds alive at 70,000: sapiens, Neanderthals, Denisovans, floresiensis (to ~50,000, https://australianmuseum.net.au/Homo-floresiensis), luzonensis (50,000 to 67,000, https://www.sciencenews.org/article/new-hominid-species-homo-luzonensis-philippines).
- Homo naledi (South Africa) is dated 236,000 to 335,000 years ago (https://elifesciences.org/content/6/e24231). It was long gone by 70,000. Last known Homo erectus (Ngandong) is 108,000 to 117,000 years ago (https://www.sciencenews.org/article/homo-erectus-last-known-appearance-roughly-117000-years-ago).
- The sentence "another small-brained kind had been living in southern Africa" is WRONG for 70,000 years ago.
- Replacement: "Others lived in the Philippines, and the small-brained Homo naledi had lived in southern Africa not so long before." Or cut the naledi clause entirely: "Others lived in the Philippines."

**5. Neanderthals: shorter, broader, stronger, brains as large, fire, care of injured, burial.** CONFIRMED. Brain size is roughly equal to ours (1,412 cc vs 1,487 cc in one analysis; a 2026 PNAS study found slightly larger). Shanidar 1 survived years with severe injuries, which is read as care. Burial at Shanidar is deliberate for at least some individuals. The "shorter than us" point is not contradicted. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/human-evolution-discoveries-in-iraq-103106021/

**6. Denisovans: finger bone and teeth; faces unknown.** WRONG (out of date). In June 2025 two studies (Science, proteins; Cell, mitochondrial DNA from dental calculus) tied the 146,000-year-old Harbin "Dragon Man" skull to Denisovans. Not every researcher agrees, but the molecular link is direct.
- Sources: https://www.sciencenews.org/article/skull-first-denisovan-human-cousin and https://www.sci.news/othersciences/anthropology/denisovan-harbin-cranium-14003.html
- Replacement for the paragraph's last two sentences: "They are called the Denisovans, after the cave. A massive skull from Harbin in China, found in the 1930s, has since been tied to them by its proteins and its DNA, and it gives them a face: a heavy brow and a large brain."
- (Also true but not searched by me: jaws from Tibet and Taiwan have been assigned to them. Check before adding.)

**7. Flores: ~1 m tall, chimp-sized brain, stone tools, hunted dwarf elephants; island dwarfing.** CONFIRMED. About 1 m; brain ~400-420 cc; Stegodon bones with tools, mostly young animals, itself a dwarfed form; remains to ~50,000 years ago (2016 revision). https://australianmuseum.net.au/Homo-floresiensis and https://peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Liang%20Bua.html

**8. ~100,000-year-old skulls in Israel; no descendants.** CONFIRMED on dates (Skhul and Qafzeh ~90,000 to 120,000; Misliya jaw ~180,000). The "no descendants" point is standard inference, not something a search could test directly. https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2018/january/humans-left-africa-40-000-years-earlier-than-we-thought.html

**9. Exit between 70,000 and 50,000; Sinai and Red Sea routes.** NOT SEARCHED.

**10. Sea level / twenty-storey building; Red Sea strait.** WRONG on the comparison.
- Sea level at the Last Glacial Maximum was ~120 to 125 m lower. Confirmed. A storey is ~3 to 3.5 m, so a twenty-storey building is ~60 to 70 m. 120 m is about forty storeys.
- Replacement: "by the height of a forty-storey building at its lowest."
- Strait: Bab-el-Mandeb is ~30 km wide today. Sources say sea level changes its width "from 4 to over 20 km" and that it stayed at least ~5 km wide even at the lowest stands. "Narrow channel... far shore in sight" is fine.
- Sources: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.01.12.901694v1.full.pdf, https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/80093/1/Bailey_et_al_JICA_author_pdf.pdf, https://openpolar.no/Record/ftvuamstcris:oai:research.vu.nl:publications%2F0b301c13-f4c0-4d6a-8322-7ee7ef9fc314
- Note (not searched): the lowest sea level (~20,000 years ago) came after the exit. At 70,000 to 60,000 the sea was lower than now but not at its lowest. "At its lowest" is loose; consider "by as much as the height of a forty-storey building".

**11. African genetic variety; founders "a few thousand".** CONFIRMED. Estimates: ~1,500 effective founders, census ~4,500 (Campbell and Tishkoff review); other methods give a larger effective size. Diversity falls with distance from Africa. https://typeset.io/papers/african-genetic-diversity-implications-for-human-demographic-2tjuqbznlw and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3497766

**12. Sunda/Sahul; widest crossing ~70 km; Australia at least 50,000.** PARTLY.
- Sahul needed open-water crossings (Wallace Line). "At least fifty thousand" is safe: genomic and archaeological work gives 60,000 to 65,000 (Madjedbebe 65,000 is disputed; >48,000 widely accepted). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6112744, https://jpost.com/archaeology/archaeology-around-the-world/article-876882
- The "seventy kilometres" figure: NOT FOUND in anything I retrieved. Route models differ.
- Replacement: "the widest of them dozens of kilometres across" and "Nobody swims that far."
- Related: "It was the first sea voyage that we know of" contradicts the chapter's own Flores and Luzon people, whose ancestors crossed water long before. Replacement: "It was the first great sea voyage of our kind." (Not searched, but it is an internal contradiction.)

**13. Europe by ~45,000.** CONFIRMED (and conservative). Sapiens points at Grotte Mandrin, France, are dated ~54,000; other sites ~45,000. https://www.britannica.com/story/the-earliest-modern-humans-in-europe-masteredbow-and-arrow-technology-54000-yearsago

**14. Needles; north of Arctic Circle >30,000; mammoth-bone houses.** PARTLY. Two errors: conflation, and "height of an ice age".
- Yana RHS (Arctic Siberia, ~71°N) dates to ~32,000 years ago, during a relatively warm interstadial, not the ice-age peak. It is the earliest evidence north of the Arctic Circle. Mammoth ivory was used for tools. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yana_Rhinoceros_Horn_Site and https://www.sciencenews.org/article/ancient-siberians-may-have-rarely-hunted-mammoths
- Mammoth-bone dwellings are a different place and time: Mezhyrich, Ukraine, ~18,000 years ago on the 2025 revised chronology (earlier work said ~15,000), not Arctic. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12639289/
- Replacement: "With needles, with fire, and with tools of mammoth ivory, people were living north of the Arctic Circle more than thirty thousand years ago, in the middle of an ice age. Later, on the plains of Ukraine and Russia, they built houses from the bones and hides of mammoths."
- Eyed needles: not verified for Yana specifically; the sentence as rewritten does not depend on it.

**15. Bering land bridge "a thousand kilometres wide"; dogs with first Americans.** PARTLY. Sources give ~1,000 miles (~1,600 km) north to south, so "a thousand kilometres" understates. Dogs: oldest dated dog remains in the Americas are ~10,000 years old; a 2021 genetic study says dogs likely arrived with human migration ~15,000 years ago. White Sands (23,000) predates any dog in the Americas, so "the first Americans" with dogs is shaky.
- Replacement: "The low sea had exposed a plain more than a thousand kilometres wide where the Bering Strait is now. Hunters followed herds across it without knowing that they were entering a new world, and in time their dogs trotted beside them."
- Sources: https://www.livescience.com/64786-beringia-map-during-ice-age.html, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8017920, https://kvia.com/news/us-world/2021/01/26/dogs-likely-migrated-to-the-americas-with-humans-over-15000-years-ago-study-says/

**16. White Sands >20,000, kids and teens; Monte Verde by 14,000, "far south of Chile".** PARTLY.
- White Sands: 21,000 to 23,000 years (seeds, pollen, OSL); 61 tracks, mostly teens and children. CONFIRMED. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/oldest-fossil-human-footprints-in-north-america-are-23000-years-old-study-reaffirms-180983018/
- Monte Verde II: ~14,500 years ago, accepted. But the site is at ~41.5 degrees S near Puerto Montt (south-central Chile), not the far south (tip is ~55 S). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Verde
- Replacement: "By fourteen thousand years ago there were people living in the cool, wet south of Chile."

**17. Dogs: >15,000 years, grey wolves, north of Eurasia; self-domestication; pointing; sled dogs.** CONFIRMED.
- Dog DNA is confirmed at ~36,000 years (Goyet, Belgium, 2026 aDNA); a 2025 wolf-genome study ties dogs most closely to eastern Eurasian wolves. "Cold north of Europe or Asia" holds. https://www.popsci.com/science/dna-wolves-dogs-domestication-evolution/
- Hare et al., Science 2002: 9 of 11 dogs followed the pointing cue, 2 of 11 chimps. https://courses.media.mit.edu/2003spring/mas963/hare_article.pdf
- Sled dogs: Zhokhov Island ~9,500 years. https://www.sci.news/genetics/sled-dogs-siberia-08579.html
- Self-domestication (commensal scavenger) is the leading account but is argued over; the chapter's "More likely" is OK.

**18. Bonn-Oberkassel.** CONFIRMED. ~14,300-year-old jaw buried with two humans; illness needing prolonged care. I could not retrieve the distemper paper; the chapter does not name the disease, so nothing to fix. https://www.jpost.com/science/article-891340

**19. Neanderthal genome 2010; ~2%; Denisovan DNA; EPAS1; COVID segment.** CONFIRMED.
- Non-Africans carry 1 to 4% Neanderthal DNA, refined estimate 2.5%. Papuans and Aboriginal Australians carry the most Denisovan DNA (roughly 1 to 5%). https://news.ucsc.edu/2010/05/3754.html
- Tibetans carry a Denisovan-derived EPAS1 variant. Minor nit: it is one gene, not "the gene"; suggest "a gene". https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.10.01.323113.full.pdf
- COVID: ~50 kb Neanderthal segment, ~50% of South Asians, ~16% of Europeans (Zeberg and Pääbo, Nature 2020). https://www.news-medical.net/news/20201215/Neanderthal-gene-variant-increases-risk-of-severe-COVID-19.aspx

**20. "Denny".** CONFIRMED. Died ~90,000 years ago, at least 13 years old, Neanderthal mother, Denisovan father (Slon et al. 2018). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30135579/

**21. Neanderthals ~400,000 years; gone within a few thousand years; floresiensis; inbreeding.** CONFIRMED. Neanderthal lineage in Spain ~430,000. Higham 2014: overlap with sapiens 2,600 to 5,400 years; Neanderthals gone ~40,000. Altai parents were half-siblings; El Sidrón shows long inbreeding (but variable, Vindija not). https://www.ox.ac.uk/node/22331 and https://discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/neanderthals-were-inbreeding-did-it-help-cause-their-extinction
- Not searched: "no battlefield has been found" and "some of the last Neanderthals were simply taken into the newcomers' bands" (narrative; the admixture itself is solid).

**22. Australian megafauna.** PARTLY.
- Diprotodon: ~3 m long, up to ~2 t. "Size of a small car" is fair (rhino-sized would be safer). Procoptodon: ~2 m, ~230 kg, OK. Genyornis: ~200 to 350 kg versus ostrich ~100 to 130 kg, so "twice" is fair.
- Megalania: Wroe 2002 gave an average ~3.4 m and ~100 kg (Molnar's earlier estimate up to 7 m). "Longer than a crocodile" is too strong (saltwater crocs reach 5 m and more).
- Replacement: "and a land lizard more than three metres long that hunted on land." https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/australias-giant-venomous-lizard-gets-downsized
- Timing: Saltré et al. 2016 put the Australia-wide loss ~13,500 years after human arrival, so "within a few thousand years" is WRONG. Replacement: "Within about fifteen thousand years of those landings, every one of them was gone." (This also keeps the next section's "the Americas, and faster" true.) https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v7y2016i1d10.1038_ncomms10511.html

**23. American megafauna.** CONFIRMED. Koch and Barnosky 2006: 34 megafaunal genera lost south of Alaska, ~72%; horses and proboscideans eliminated. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2556404
- Camels and horses evolving in North America: search did not return it; this is standard. NOT FOUND in search; no contradiction.
- Horses back with the Spanish: not searched.

**24. Africa lost the fewest.** NOT SEARCHED.

**25. Mammoth gestation ~2 years; first breeding in teens.** CONFIRMED by elephant analogy: ~22 months; wild females give first calf at ~12 to 14. https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/african_elephant/reproduction

**26. "A dozen" earlier warmings.** NOT SEARCHED.

**27. Wrangel Island mammoths ~4,000 years ago; no hunters.** CONFIRMED. Teeth ~4,000 years old, Egypt's pyramids were being built. Humans reached the island only after the mammoths had gone (a few hundred years later). https://www.discovermagazine.com/uncovering-the-secrets-of-mammoth-island-1161 and https://www.helsinki.fi/en/luomus/news/last-mammoths-died-remote-island

**28. New Zealand ~700 years ago; moa; extinct within ~200 years; bones in heaps.** PARTLY.
- Settlement ~1250 to 1300 AD (sources range 1200 to 1350). OK. Dinornis could browse to ~3.6 m, above an elephant's back. OK.
- Extinction: Holdaway and Jacomb 2000 say less than 100 years; the 2014 work puts extinction about a century after the earliest Wairau Bar settlement. "Two centuries" overstates. Replacement: "within about a century every moa was dead." https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3977255 and https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/humans-wiped-out-moa-the-largest-bird-that-ever-lived/ntn1wfo7p
- Bones beside ovens at early sites: NOT FOUND in search; well documented elsewhere, no contradiction.

**29. Pacific; Lapita to Fiji, Tonga, Samoa; eastern push; canoes; navigation.** CONFIRMED. The Pacific is ~165 million km2, ~32% of Earth's surface. https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific
- "About three thousand years ago": consistent with open-ocean voyaging for at least 3,000 years; Lapita specifics not retrieved.
- Easter Island ~1200 AD, Hawaii ~1000 to 1260 AD, New Zealand ~1250 to 1300: "roughly a thousand years ago" for the push east is fine.
- Navigation methods: not searched.

**30. Hawaii; Easter Island; sweet potato; Notre-Dame.** PARTLY, on the South America sentence.
- Hawaii is ~3,800 to 4,000 km from the Marquesas; "nearly four thousand" is fine. Easter Island settled ~1200 AD from Mangareva (~2,400 km). https://www.staradvertiser.com/2026/11/04/editorial/island-voices/column-new-clues-about-hawaiis-first-settlers/
- "Some of them even reached South America and came back" goes beyond the evidence. The sweet potato is the strongest sign of contact, and DNA shows Native American ancestry in eastern Polynesians around 1200 AD, but who sailed which way is argued. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/native-americans-polynesians-meet-180975269/ and https://www.downtoearth.org.in/science-technology/did-ancient-americans-settle-in-polynesia-the-evidence-doesn-t-stack-up-72262
- Replacement: "There was contact with South America too, because the sweet potato, an American plant, was growing in Polynesian gardens centuries before any European ship arrived, and islanders carry a trace of Native American ancestry."
- Notre-Dame: begun 1163, largely complete by about 1260, finished 1345. New Zealand ~1250 to 1300. "Already standing" is fine. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris

**31. Under 3,000 generations; humans more alike than two chimps from one forest.** PARTLY.
- Generations: ~69,000 years / 25 = ~2,800. CONFIRMED by arithmetic (becomes ~3,500 at 20 years per generation).
- Chimps: the support is old and narrow. A single West African group of 55 chimps has about twice the genetic variability of all humans (Gagneux, mitochondrial DNA). The "any two chimps from the same forest" pairwise claim was not directly confirmed. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/culture-speeds-up-human-evolution/
- Replacement that the source supports: "As a result, a single troop of chimpanzees in West Africa holds about twice the genetic variety of the whole human species." Otherwise cut the comparison.

**32. Skin colour; Cheddar Man; pale skin newer than bow and arrow.** CONFIRMED.
- Cheddar Man ~10,000 years old (two radiocarbon dates), dark to dark-brown skin, blue eyes (NHM). https://www.nhm.ac.uk/our-science/our-work/origins-evolution-and-futures/human-adaptation-diet-disease/cheddar-man-faq.html
- Light-skin alleles arrived with later migrations; predominantly pale skin only by the Bronze and Iron Ages. Western hunter-gatherers were dark. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7817156
- Bow and arrow: Europe ~54,000 (Mandrin, https://anthropology.uconn.edu/2023/03/06/new-study-shows-archery-appeared-in-europe-thousands-of-years-earlier-than-previously-thought/), or at least 10,000 to 12,000 (Stellmoor). Either way older than pale skin. The statement holds.

**33. Sea rose by a tall office block; bridges flooded.** NOT SEARCHED. Rise ~120 m is consistent with #10.

**34. Americas isolated more than 10,000 years; Australia isolated.** PARTLY.
- Paleo-Inuit crossed from Siberia ~5,000 to 4,000 years ago (https://alaskapublic.org/2014/08/28/new-study-sheds-light-on-peopling-of-the-arctic/); Norse reached the Americas ~1000 AD; Polynesian contact ~1200 AD. "On their own... none knew the others existed" overstates it.
- Replacement: "Apart from the Arctic fringe, the people of the Americas were now on their own, and they would remain so for more than ten thousand years." For Australia, Torres Strait contact with New Guinea is a likely exception, not searched. Suggested: "So, for the most part, were the people of Australia."

**35. World population "a few million" ~10,000 years ago.** NOT FOUND. Search surfaced only the HYDE dataset, not the figure. Commonly cited estimates are ~4 to 5 million. No contradiction.

## Other statements I believe are wrong or too strong

- "The only other animals that have managed to live everywhere are the ones we brought with us: dogs, rats and lice." (Not searched; contradicted by general knowledge.) Peregrine falcons, barn owls, orcas and red foxes live on nearly every continent without our help. Replacement: "The other animals that have spread nearly as far as we have are mostly the ones that travelled with us: dogs, rats and lice."
- "It was the first sea voyage that we know of": see #12.
- "Africa, by contrast, kept its elephants... The difference is experience." (Not searched.) This is one leading explanation, not settled; Africa also lost some large animals. Consider "The likeliest reason is experience."
- "Fire did the rest." The role of burning in the extinctions is argued over. Consider cutting to "Burning added to the pressure."
- "Neanderthals... parents were often close relatives": fine, but the evidence is variable between individuals (see #21).

## Note on Item 6 instruction
You asked for no hedges in replacements. The Harbin attribution is disputed by some researchers (e.g. Xijun Ni); the replacement states it plainly because two independent molecular lines (proteins and mtDNA) now support it.
