## Captions and placement

1. migration_map.png. "Modern humans spread out of Africa in waves, reaching Australia more than 60,000 years ago and the Americas by about 16,000 years ago (dates in thousands of years)." Section: The people already there.
2. neanderthal_skulls.png. "A Neanderthal skull (left) and a modern human skull: the Neanderthal has a longer, lower braincase, heavy brow ridges and no chin." Section: The people already there. (Check the left/right order against the image before publishing.)
3. floresiensis_skull.jpg. "The skull of Homo floresiensis, the small-bodied people found on the Indonesian island of Flores." Section: The people already there.
5. eyed_needle.jpg. "A bone sewing needle with an eye, about 59 mm long, made by Ice Age people in France." Section: To the ends of the Earth.
6. mammoth_dwelling.jpg. "A museum reconstruction of a hut built from mammoth bones, modelled on those found at Mezhyrich in Ukraine." Section: To the ends of the Earth.
7. white_sands_prints.jpg. "Human footprints pressed into mud at White Sands, New Mexico, and left there for thousands of years." Section: To the ends of the Earth.
8. oberkassel_dog.jpg. "Jaw and teeth of a dog buried alongside two people at Bonn-Oberkassel in Germany, about 14,000 years ago." Section: The wolf at the fire.
9. diprotodon.jpg. "Diprotodon, the largest marsupial that ever lived, was a wombat-like animal the size of a hippo that vanished from Australia." Section: The quiet continents.
10. megatherium.jpg. "A giant ground sloth of the kind that roamed the Americas, mounted on its hind legs in a London museum." Section: The quiet continents.
11. owen_moa.jpg. "The anatomist Richard Owen beside the skeleton of a giant moa, a flightless bird of New Zealand that humans hunted to extinction." Section: The last places.
13. hokulea.jpg. "Hokule'a, a modern double-hulled voyaging canoe built in the Polynesian tradition, sails into Honolulu after crossing from Tahiti without instruments." Section: The last places.
14. pacific_map.png. "People moved east across the Pacific in stages, reaching Samoa and Tonga about 3,000 years ago and New Zealand around AD 1200." Section: The last places.
