# Chapter 84 — Why were India and Pakistan divided?

**Line under the title:** South Asia, from 1937 to 1971.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

British India was divided because the leaders of its Muslim minority came to fear that democracy would mean permanent rule by the Hindu majority, because the leaders of the majority would not accept the loose union that might have reassured them, and because Britain, bankrupt and in a hurry, cut the country in two in ten weeks. The line was drawn in five weeks by a lawyer who had never seen India and was published two days after independence. Some fifteen million people crossed it and up to a million died. The division did not settle the question of what makes a nation: the two halves of Pakistan shared a religion and nothing else, and split in 1971. India and Pakistan have fought four wars and both have nuclear weapons.
