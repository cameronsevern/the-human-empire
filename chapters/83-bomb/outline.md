# Chapter 83 — Why did America drop the atomic bomb?

**Line under the title:** The United States and Japan, from 1938 to 1945.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

America dropped the bomb to end the war without invading Japan, and because by the summer of 1945 it would have taken a deliberate decision not to. The weapon had been built in fear that Hitler would get one first. A laboratory finding of 1938 was turned into a bomb in under seven years by an industrial effort the size of the car industry, and when it was ready the war with Germany was over and the war with Japan was not. Cities were already being burned every week. The scientists who asked for a warning or a demonstration were not heard. Two bombs killed about two hundred thousand people, the Soviet Union entered the war between them, and Japan surrendered within a week. The weapon has not been used since, and it has not gone away.

## The story

1. **Opening: a man on a business trip.** Answer first. Hiroshima at 8.15 on 6 August 1945, through an engineer from Nagasaki who was due to go home that day.
2. **A walk in the snow.** The discovery of fission at Christmas 1938. What a chain reaction is, and the idea a refugee had at a London traffic light.
3. **A letter from Long Island.** Two Hungarians go looking for Einstein. The fear of a German bomb. A squash court in Chicago.
4. **A country turned into a factory.** The project: its general and its physicist, the secret cities, the silver, the cost.
5. **The desert.** Germany turns out to have no bomb. A president dies. The test of 16 July 1945.
6. **A boy on a toboggan.** How the decision was and was not made: the burning cities, Okinawa, the planned invasion, the committee, the petition, the Soviet Union, the city struck off the list, the ultimatum.
7. **Flash and boom.** What the bomb did to Hiroshima, in the words of people who were there. The sickness that came afterwards.
8. **The second city.** The Soviet attack. A cloud over one city and a gap over another. The engineer's office.
9. **A voice on the radio.** A cabinet split three to three, an emperor's ruling, a mutiny in the palace, and a broadcast.
10. **Ending: the last one.**
