1. **Surrender sequence: a consequential gap.**  
   > “Not everyone obeyed.”

   This jumps from the emperor’s intervention early on **10 August** to the attempted coup on **14 August**, as though a final surrender order had simply been disobeyed. Japan first offered a **conditional** surrender; the Allied reply provoked further disagreement, and the emperor intervened again on 14 August. Those missing steps explain both the delay and the coup. **Confidence: high.** [U.S. Navy’s surrender history](https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-053/h-053-2.html).

2. **Wrong plutonium-bomb design.**  
   > “The plutonium bomb was a far more intricate device, in which a hollow ball of plutonium had to be crushed evenly from all sides, and it needed a test.”

   Trinity and Nagasaki used a substantially **solid plutonium core**, with a small central cavity for the neutron initiator. The hollow-shell design had been superseded. This matters because the sentence supplies the reader’s explanation of the mechanism. **Confidence: very high.** [Los Alamos researchers’ study of the Christy design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06266).

3. **Electricity figure wrong by more than an order of magnitude.**  
   > “One of those plants was the largest building in the world, and together they used a seventh of all the electricity generated in the United States.”

   Oak Ridge used roughly **0.9% of national electricity production in August 1945**, not about 14%. The one-seventh claim is a persistent historical error, examined explicitly by physicist B. Cameron Reed. **Confidence: very high.** [“Fifteen Manhattan Project Myths and Misconceptions,” myth 6, p. 15](https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/APS/fc77d112-850f-4db6-997c-e434bd4670b5/UploadedImages/Documents/Newsletters/Spring20.pdf).

4. **Ground temperature confused with the fireball.**  
   > “For an instant the ground beneath it was hotter than the surface of the Sun.”

   Hiroshima’s estimated ground-surface temperature near the hypocentre was **3,000–4,000°C**, below the Sun’s roughly 5,500°C surface temperature. The fireball was much hotter; the ground was not. **Confidence: very high.** [Hiroshima City’s account of the explosion](https://www.city.hiroshima.lg.jp/tourism-culture/history/1003068/1027532/1027533/1008360.html).

5. **The Kyoto honeymoon story is false.**  
   > “The secretary of war, who had visited it on his honeymoon, struck it off.”

   Stimson visited Kyoto in **1926 and 1929**, decades after his 1893 marriage. His opposition to bombing it is documented, but the honeymoon explanation is not. His stated concerns included its cultural importance and the consequences for relations with postwar Japan. **Confidence: high.** [Alex Wellerstein’s investigation using Stimson’s diaries and travel records](https://blog.nuclearsecrecy.com/2023/07/24/henry-stimson-didnt-go-to-kyoto-on-his-honeymoon/).

6. **The surrender opponents were not all army representatives.**  
   > “Three, the army's men, wanted to fight on unless Japan could also disarm its own soldiers, try its own war criminals and avoid being occupied.”

   The three were Army Minister Anami, Army Chief of Staff Umezu, and **Navy Chief of Staff Admiral Toyoda**. The navy itself was divided. Calling all three “the army’s men” gives the reader a misleading picture of the political split. **Confidence: very high.** [U.S. Navy’s surrender history](https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-053/h-053-2.html).

7. **A false absolute about Hiroshima’s destruction.**  
   > “Everything within a mile and a half was flattened and then burned.”

   Some substantial structures remained standing much closer to the hypocentre. The surviving shell of the Industrial Promotion Hall—the Atomic Bomb Dome—was only about **160 metres** away. Widespread destruction is accurate; universal flattening followed by burning is not. This also directly breaks the style rule against absolutes defeated by one exception. **Confidence: very high.** [Hiroshima City on the building’s survival](https://www.city.hiroshima.lg.jp/english/peace/1029869/1009930.html).

8. **Tension with chapter 80: “no precedent” needs a narrower meaning.**  
   > “The prime minister then did something that had no precedent.”

   The next sentence identifies this as asking the emperor to decide. Yet chapter 80 says: **“The mutiny failed, because the emperor was furious and ordered it crushed.”** Readers have already seen Hirohito intervene decisively in 1936. Asking him to break this particular policy deadlock may have been exceptional, but the broad wording suggests that decisive imperial intervention itself was unprecedented. **Confidence: high about the continuity problem; moderate about the narrower procedural claim.** [Chapter 80](chapters/80-pearl-harbor/chapter.md).

9. **The bomb’s operating principle remains unexplained.**  
   > “The uranium bomb was simple enough that its designers were sure of it.”

   Readers have learned how neutrons multiply, but not why the material can be stored safely and then made to explode. The missing bridge is that the uranium weapon rapidly brought separate masses together, allowing the reaction to grow explosively before the material dispersed. Likewise, “control rods” appear earlier without explaining that they absorb neutrons. This falls short of the style requirement that readers should be able to explain the device afterwards. **Confidence: high.** [National Park Service’s explanation of the two weapons](https://www.nps.gov/mapr/learn/atomic-weapons.htm).

10. **“Pure material” gives the wrong requirement.**  
    > “To make a bomb, the reaction has to run through pure material, and nature does not supply it.”

    The uranium needed **enrichment**, not complete isotopic purity. Hiroshima’s uranium was roughly 80% uranium-235. The crucial distinction is a sufficiently high concentration of suitable material, combined in the right arrangement. “Pure” also leaves readers uncertain whether the problem is chemical contamination or the mixture of uranium forms. **Confidence: very high.** [ATSDR’s technical account of the Hiroshima weapon](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp149.pdf).

11. **The German investigation conflates two stages.**  
    > “In the last months of 1944, American intelligence officers following the armies into Germany found what remained of the German effort.”

    The decisive late-1944 evidence came from **Strasbourg in occupied France**. The German installations and remaining reactor work were investigated and seized in 1945. The sentence collapses the discovery that Germany was far behind into the later capture of its facilities. **Confidence: high.** [U.S. Army account of the Alsos mission](https://www.army.mil/article/132740/Alsos_Mission_seizes_French_government_arsenal___/).

12. **The target-selection claim is too categorical.**  
    > “A list of target cities was drawn up, chosen because they had not yet been bombed, so that the effect could be measured.”

    Relative lack of damage was a selection criterion, but “had not yet been bombed” is not true of every eventual target. Nagasaki had experienced conventional raids before the atomic attack, including one on **1 August 1945**. Distinguish largely undamaged cities from entirely untouched ones. **Confidence: high.** [White Sands Missile Range Museum’s account](https://wsmrmuseum.com/2024/01/31/special-mission-16-fat-man-and-the-atomic-bombing-of-nagasaki/7/amp/).

13. **Uranium has more than two natural forms.**  
    > “Uranium comes in two forms, chemically identical.”

    Natural uranium contains **three** principal isotopes: uranium-238, uranium-235, and a trace of uranium-234. “Two main forms” preserves the useful simplification without making a false exhaustive claim. **Confidence: very high.** [Nuclear Regulatory Commission’s definition](https://www.nrc.gov/education-regulatory-research/glossary/natural-uranium).

14. **The nuclear atom is dated too early.**  
    > “Physicists had known since the start of the century that the atom is mostly empty space, with a tiny, dense nucleus at its centre, and that the nucleus holds enormous energy.”

    Rutherford proposed the nuclear atom in **1911**. Around 1900, the dense central nucleus was not established knowledge. The sentence also folds the recognition of radioactive energy into the later discovery of atomic structure. **Confidence: very high.** [American Physical Society’s account of Rutherford’s discovery](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2006/05/rutherford-discovery-atomic-nucleus).

15. **A term is promised but withheld.**  
    > “The people of Hiroshima had no word for what had happened to them, and they made one from the two things that they remembered: the flash, and the boom that followed.”

    The sentence makes the newly coined word its subject but never supplies it. Readers are left wondering what the word was and whether “flash and boom” is its translation. Either give the term or make the remembered experience the subject. **Confidence: high as a clarity finding.**

16. **An explicit signpost sentence.**  
    > “What mattered was something else.”

    This announces that the important point is coming instead of stating it. The following sentence already supplies that point: splitting uranium releases additional neutrons. It directly breaks “No signpost sentences.” **Confidence: high.**
