# Chapter 18 — Who invented democracy?

**Line under the title:** Athens and the Aegean Sea, from about 2,500 to about 2,400 years ago.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Assemblies of citizens are very old, and many cities had them. What the Athenians added was a name and a working system in which ordinary men ran a state themselves: an assembly that anyone could address, offices and juries filled by lot, pay for serving, and a yearly vote to send one man away. That system grew up together with a war fleet. The fleet beat Persia, and it was rowed by citizens too poor to own armour, which gave them a claim to power that no one could refuse. The same fleet then turned a league of free allies into an empire that paid tribute, and the tribute paid the rowers and the jurors. Democracy and empire were built by the same men with the same oars. The long war with Sparta that followed cost Athens the empire and cost Sparta its strength, and the winner was the Persian king, who paid for the last fleet. The democracy outlived the empire, and so did the machine that chose its jurors.

## The story

1. **The stone with the slots.** Answer first. A slab of marble in the museum at Athens that picked men at random.
2. **Rule by the people.** Small cities with no palace, armies of farmers, old assemblies (Uruk, Sparta's shouting). Athens after its tyrants: a nobleman hands the city to the people, and the new word.
3. **Earth and water.** Persia in three sentences. The demand for submission, the burning of a Persian city, Marathon.
4. **A hundred and seventy oars.** The silver strike and the vote to build ships. How the three-banked warship worked. Salamis.
5. **The men on the benches.** Who rowed, what they were paid, and why the city could no longer refuse them. Pay for jurors.
6. **Who wishes to speak?** The assembly, the council, the lot, the allotment machine explained step by step, the potsherd vote. Who was left out.
7. **Iron in the sea.** The alliance sworn for ever. Ships or silver. The island that tried to leave. The treasury moves to Athens. The two ships to Mytilene.
8. **Twenty-seven years.** Sparta and its helots. The walls, the plague, Sicily. The Persian governor and his arrears. The walls come down.
9. **What was left.** Sparta's ruin and the helots' freedom. The democracy restored. Whose story this is: Greek writers, and the little that Persian records add.

## What each party had at stake

- A rower: a wage, a vote, and his life every time the fleet went out.
- A helot: his freedom, which the war nearly brought and a later war did.
- A city that tried to leave: its walls, its ships and the right to decide for itself.
- A Persian governor: the tribute of the Greek cities of the coast, which the king still expected from him.
