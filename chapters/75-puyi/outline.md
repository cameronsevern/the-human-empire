# Chapter 75 — What happened to the last emperor of China?

**Line under the title:** China, from 1895 to 1935.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The last emperor gave up his throne in 1912 at the age of six, and ended his life as a gardener in Communist Beijing. His dynasty fell because, after sixty years of defeats, it lost the two groups it could not rule without: the educated families of the provinces, whose tie to the throne was cut when the examinations were abolished, and the new army it had built to save itself. Nothing took its place. The republic that followed was captured by a general and then broke into the territories of warlords. The ideas that young Chinese reached for to rebuild their country, nationalism, democracy and communism, all came from the foreign powers they wanted to resist. By 1927 two parties built on those ideas, with Soviet help, had armies, and one had turned on the other.

## The story

1. **Opening: "It will soon be over."** Answer first. A two-year-old is put on the throne in 1908 and screams; his father's words of comfort. The abdication of 1912.
2. **Fists.** After the defeat by Japan: the powers grab ports, a young emperor's reforms are crushed, and a peasant movement that believes itself bulletproof besieges the foreigners in Beijing. Eight armies. The bill.
3. **The end of the examinations.** The dynasty reforms too late: schools, a new army, assemblies, and in 1905 the abolition of the system that had bound the educated to the throne for thirteen centuries. Two deaths in two days.
4. **A doctor from the south.** Sun Yat-sen: ten failed risings, a kidnapping in London, three principles.
5. **Double Ten.** October 1911: a bomb goes off by accident. The provinces leave. A deal between the revolutionary and the general.
6. **Eighty-three days.** The general makes himself emperor and fails. The warlords.
7. **Mr Science and Mr Democracy.** 4 May 1919: students in the square. A new culture. A meeting of thirteen men that ends on a boat.
8. **The academy.** Soviet advisers, a military school, a march north. Shanghai, April 1927. The survivors go to the hills, and then walk nine thousand kilometres.
9. **Ending: the gardener.** The boy in the palace with a bicycle, the puppet of Japan, the prisoner, the citizen.
