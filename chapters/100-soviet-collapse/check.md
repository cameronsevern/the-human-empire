Read the requested files; no files edited. Findings below are ranked by seriousness. I found no hedging stacks, chapter self-mentions, explicit cross-references or clear signpost sentences.

1. **Factual error / internal contradiction — high confidence.**

   > “Once people were allowed to speak, the nations inside the empire asked to leave, and he would not shoot them to make them stay.”

   Gorbachev avoided a general war to preserve the Union, but his government did use lethal force against national movements. Soviet troops killed more than 130 people in the January 1990 intervention in Azerbaijan. The chapter itself subsequently describes killings in Georgia and Lithuania. Distinguish his restraint in eastern Europe from his much less consistent conduct inside the USSR. [Human Rights Watch investigation](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/u/ussr/ussr915.pdf).

2. **Misleading account of the breakup / missing causal bridge — high confidence.**

   > “They announced that he was ill and that a committee had taken power to save the country.”

   The reader never learns what immediately precipitated the coup: a new Union Treaty, scheduled for signature on 20 August, would transfer substantial powers to the republics. Nor does the chapter mention that roughly 76% of participating voters had supported a *renewed federation* in March, although six republics boycotted the referendum. These facts complicate the opening’s picture of nations simply asking to leave and explain why the plotters acted then. [Contemporary referendum report](https://www.deseret.com/1991/3/21/18911334/76-in-soviet-referendum-voted-to-preserve-country/); [Freedom House’s contemporary account](https://freedomhouse.org/sites/default/files/2020-02/Freedom_in_the_World_1991-1992_complete_book.pdf).

3. **Factual error — high confidence.**

   > “He was the first leader in the thousand-year history of Russia to be chosen by its voters, and Gorbachev had never been elected by anyone.”

   Gorbachev was elected Soviet president by the Congress of People’s Deputies in March 1990. The useful distinction is direct popular election versus election by the legislature. Likewise, describe Yeltsin precisely as Russia’s first popularly elected president; the thousand-year formulation needlessly invites disputes about earlier elective institutions. [Contemporary Soviet announcement of Gorbachev’s election](https://www.marxists.org/history/ussr/publications/soviet-news/1990/sovietnews_6518_0390.pdf).

4. **Factual errors / exception-breaking absolutes — high confidence.**

   > “In the Soviet Union and its satellites there was no market.”

   > “A country that had had no private property five years before now had some of the richest men in the world.”

   State planning predominated, but farmers’ markets, private household production and informal markets existed. Soviet citizens also possessed personal property; autonomous commercial cooperatives became legal in 1988, well before the 1995 auctions. Distinguish state ownership of major productive assets from the absence of either markets or private possessions. [IMF study of Soviet markets](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9789264134683/ch009.xml); [ILO account of the 1988 cooperative legislation](https://webapps.ilo.org/public/english/standards/relm/ilc/ilc89/rep-v-1.htm).

5. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.**

   > “By the 1970s growth had almost stopped.”

   Growth slowed markedly during the 1970s; it had not generally approached zero. Contemporary CIA estimates put annual growth at about 4% in 1971–75 and 2.6% in 1976–80. Estimates differ, but “growth had slowed sharply” is secure. This matters because the present wording brings the terminal economic crisis forward by a decade. [CIA economic estimates](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/USSR%20%20MEASURES%20OF%20ECONOMI%5B15681799%5D.pdf).

6. **Misleading claim / unsupported absolute — high confidence.**

   > “An empire of three hundred million people, with the largest army on Earth, had dissolved with hardly a shot.”

   > “That had not happened before.”

   The formal December settlement was comparatively peaceful; the process of disintegration included lethal repression and armed conflict, some already mentioned here. Specify the peaceful transfer at the centre rather than extending it to the entire dissolution. The assertion of historical uniqueness has no defined comparison and should go unless it can be demonstrated. [Human Rights Watch’s account of violence during the Soviet breakup](https://www.hrw.org/reports/1995/communal/).

7. **Misleading statistic — high confidence.**

   > “Men died, of drink and despair, at an average age of fifty-seven.”

   This confuses **life expectancy at birth** with the average age of men who died. Russian male life expectancy reached approximately 57–58 in 1994; that was neither a decade-wide average nor a measure assigning all deaths to drink and despair. State the measure and year. [Research on Russia’s mortality crisis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8553909/).

8. **Reader comprehension — high confidence.**

   > “In June 1991 the people of the Russian republic elected him their president.”

   Readers need a sentence explaining that Russia was itself one of the USSR’s fifteen republics, with institutions separate from the Union government. Otherwise, two presidents and two parliaments suddenly coexist in Moscow without explanation. This distinction is essential to understanding Yeltsin’s authority, the parliament defended during the coup and Russia’s subsequent withdrawal from the Union.

9. **Reader comprehension / missing mechanism — high confidence.**

   > “His half-measures had broken the old system of commands without putting a market in its place.”

   The chapter has explained planning, but the only concrete economic reform it has described is restricting alcohol. Readers cannot see how that led to factories ceasing deliveries. Give one example of enterprise reform—greater discretion over production and earnings while important prices and supplies remained controlled—and explain the resulting mismatch. [IMF account of reforms after 1985](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9789264134683/ch003.xml).

10. **Misleading causal compression — high confidence.**

    > “A little after one in the morning on 26 April 1986, during a badly run safety test, the fourth reactor of the nuclear power station at Chernobyl, in Ukraine, exploded.”

    The timing is correct, but “badly run” supplies the passage’s only technical explanation and leaves readers with operator incompetence as the cause. Dangerous reactor design, including the control rods, and failures to communicate known hazards were central. A short reference to those defects would preserve the narrative while correcting its implication. [IAEA’s revised investigation, INSAG-7](https://pub.iaea.org/MTCD/publications/PDF/Pub913e_web.pdf).

11. **Incorrect immediate cause — high confidence.**

    > “Fourteen months later the government raised the price of meat, and the workers of the great shipyard at Gdańsk, on the Baltic, laid down their tools.”

    Meat-price increases helped trigger the wider strike wave in July 1980. The Gdańsk shipyard strike began on 14 August following the dismissal of crane operator Anna Walentynowicz, initially demanding her reinstatement. The sentence merges the general background with the specific trigger of the scene that follows. [European Solidarity Centre](https://ecs.gda.pl/en/how-did-solidarnosc-solidarity-come-to-be/).

12. **Factual error / “first” broken by an exception — high confidence.**

    > “In March 1989 the Soviet Union held an election in which, for the first time since 1917, voters in many districts had a choice of candidates.”

    Experimental multicandidate **local** elections had already taken place in June 1987. Specify that 1989 brought competitive elections to the national legislature. [Contemporary UPI report](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1987/06/21/Soviets-vote-in-multi-candidate-elections-for-first-time/5196551246400/).

13. **Misleading chronology — high confidence.**

    > “In May the Hungarian government had begun to take down the barbed wire on its border with Austria, and that summer East Germans on holiday in Hungary simply walked through the gap.”

    Removing the fence did not immediately make the border freely crossable. There were escapes and the exceptional opening during the August picnic, but border enforcement continued; an East German died during an encounter with a Hungarian border guard on 21 August. Separate dismantling the fence from the later political opening. [NATO historical account](https://nato.int/en/about-us/nato-history/history-by-theme/my-country-and-nato/hungary-and-nato); [documented border incident](https://bm-tt.hu/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/AVasfuggonytortenetehudeeng.pdf).

14. **Factual error — high confidence.**

    > “Three young men died under an armoured car in an underpass.”

    They were not all crushed beneath a vehicle: two were shot and one was crushed during the confrontation. “Three men died in a clash with armoured vehicles” would avoid assigning them a shared manner of death. [Historical study of the August coup](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/slavic-review/article/abs/televorot-the-role-of-television-coverage-in-russias-august-1991-coup/BA0D539D84D423C82FF658B98D4BBA27).

15. **Contradiction with chapter 88 / unbounded “first” — high confidence.**

    > “The first crack opened in Poland, the largest of the satellites and the most restive.”

    Chapter 88 has already described the Hungarian uprising of 1956, including the army joining it and the government announcing withdrawal from the Soviet alliance. Poland in 1979 cannot be the first crack without a narrower meaning, such as the beginning of this final wave of challenges. [Chapter 88, “The secret speech”](chapters/88-cold-war/chapter.md).

16. **Doubtful anecdote presented as observed fact — medium confidence.**

    > “If nails were counted by weight, a factory made a few enormous ones.”

    > “If by number, it made tiny ones.”

    These are familiar versions of a Soviet joke and textbook illustration, rather than an identified factory episode established here. The incentive problem is real, but the illustration needs documentary support or explicit treatment as a joke. The adjacent Khrushchev chandelier complaint has contemporary reporting and could carry the point alone. [Discussion tracing the nail story’s evidence](https://skeptics.stackexchange.com/questions/22375/did-a-soviet-nail-factory-produce-useless-nails-to-improve-metrics/22386); [contemporary chandelier account](https://time.com/archive/6634626/russia-borrowing-from-the-capitalists/).

17. **Chronological error — high confidence.**

    > “By eleven the crowd at one gate had grown to twenty thousand, chanting, and the officer in charge, fearing that people would be crushed, opened the barrier on his own decision.”

    This places the unrestricted opening at approximately 11 p.m. Bornholmer Straße opened fully at about **11.30 p.m.** Earlier selective releases should not be confused with the abandonment of controls. [Chronik der Mauer’s detailed chronology](https://www.chronik-der-mauer.de/chronik/_year1989/_month11/?moc=1&month=11&opennid=181758&year=1989).

18. **Factual overreach in the ending — high confidence.**

    > “The last act of the Soviet Union was signed with it.”

    The borrowed pen is supported, but Gorbachev’s signature was not literally the Union’s last act. On 26 December the Soviet legislature formally acknowledged its dissolution. Keep the image attached to Gorbachev’s relinquishment of office rather than making an inaccurate institutional claim. [Account of the pen](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/world/europe/conor-o-clery-remembering-the-last-day-of-the-soviet-union-1.2916499); [AP chronology of formal dissolution](https://apnews.com/article/81da8064b64d87d281d20616c612d7e2).
