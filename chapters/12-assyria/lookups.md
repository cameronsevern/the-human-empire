# Chapter 12 fact-check lookups

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. Verdicts refer to the first draft; "Action" says what the final text does. ORACC and UCL pages could not be fetched directly (certificate and 403 errors), so RINAP/SAA texts were checked through other editions of the same translations.

1. "there were about three kilometres of them" (carved slabs in Sennacherib's palace). Correct. Layard's "nearly two miles" of bas-reliefs; a summary of the 1849-51 expedition gives "two miles (3km) of bas-reliefs" and 70 rooms. https://odysseyadventures.ca/articles/lachish_slides/lachish_text.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineveh
2. "A visitor reached it through three doorways in a row, each guarded by a pair of stone bulls". Correct. Approach to Room XXXVI through three pairs of human-headed winged bull colossi (Russell 1991; Ussishkin 1982). https://odysseyadventures.ca/articles/lachish_slides/lachish_text.htm
3. Relief scenes: rams on ramps, torches, soldier with ladle, three men impaled, two flayed, families and carts, king enthroned. Correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachish_reliefs ; British Museum 124904-124915.
4. Caption: "he sat on his throne while the plunder of the city of Lachish passed before him". Correct (paraphrase of RINAP 3 Sennacherib 66: "sat on a nemedu-throne and the booty of Lachish passed in review before him"). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Lachish ; Russell, Palace Without Rival, 276.
5. "set at eye level in a room where foreign visitors waited". Could not verify (the room's function is disputed). Action: cut.
6. "Iron is the commonest metal there is." Wrong: aluminium is commoner in the crust (Codex). Iron about 5 per cent of the crust; tin about 2 parts per million. Action: "one of the commonest substances in the ground". https://www.ga.gov.au/education/minerals-energy/australian-mineral-facts/iron
7. Bloomery: clay shaft, air pipe and bellows near the base, charcoal and ore charged from the top, carbon monoxide strips oxygen from solid ore, iron never melts, spongy bloom, hammering to expel slag. Correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomery ; https://www.wealdeniron.org.uk/experimental-bloomery-ironmaking/exp-bloom/
8. "the sand and clay ... melt, because they melt at a lower heat than iron does". Wrong (Codex): silica melts higher than iron; it reacts with iron oxide to form low-melting fayalite slag. Action: rewritten ("they combine with a part of the ore to make ... slag, which melts at a lower heat than iron"). https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1375923/1/1375923.pdf
9. "After most of a day" / bloom of a few kilos / several times its weight in ore and charcoal. Imprecise. Experimental smelts run 5 to 7 hours; blooms 1.5 to 10 kg; yield 10 to 20 per cent of ore; charcoal about equal to ore plus the preheat. Action: "After five or six hours". https://wealdeniron.org.uk/?p=25 ; https://warehamforge.ca/ironsmelting/smelt/smelttext.html
10. "By about 3,000 years ago, across the lands from Greece to Iran, iron was the ordinary metal". Imprecise (Codex): adoption was uneven; in Mesopotamia mainly 8th-7th centuries BCE. Action: "By Sennacherib's time it was the everyday metal". https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10814-019-09129-6
11. "ships and caravans needed a palace to organise them". Wrong, contradicts chapter 11's family firms (Codex). Action: cut.
12. "five thousand talents of iron" from Damascus. Correct. Adad-nirari III, Calah slab: 2,300 talents silver, 20 gold, 5,000 iron (the Tell al-Rimah stela gives 2,000 of iron). At about 30 kg a talent, 150 tonnes. https://livius.org/sources/content/anet/281-the-nimrud-slab
13. "crossed the river Euphrates with a hundred and twenty thousand". Correct as the king's own claim (Shalmaneser III, year 14, 845 BCE). https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/shalmaneser-iii-x00b0
14. "another king stopped sending the farmers home". Imprecise (Codex): Tiglath-pileser III built a professional core with foreign auxiliaries; levies continued. Action: rewritten; also now says only some tributaries became provinces. https://oracc2.museum.upenn.edu/saao/aebp/Essentials/Soldiers/TheAssyrianarmy/index.html
15. Sargon II took the name of Sargon of Akkad, "sixteen centuries dead". Correct (c. 2300 and 722 BCE); consistent with chapter 8.
16. "I flayed as many nobles as had rebelled against me and draped their skins over the pile." Correct. Ashurnasirpal II, Grayson RIMA 2 p. 199. https://cdli.ox.ac.uk/wiki/assurnasirpal_ii . "on the walls of his palace": could not verify for this passage. Action: now "carved in stone in a temple of his new capital" (the annals from the Ninurta temple at Kalhu).
17. Lachish "second city" of Judah. Correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Lachish . "about forty kilometres to the south-west" of Jerusalem: correct to within a few kilometres.
18. "dug by three separate teams". Wrong: at least five expeditions since 1932. Action: "several teams". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Lachish
19. Siege ramp "the oldest siege ramp that survives". Correct ("the oldest known in the world"). Same source.
20. "about three million" stones, "less than a month". Correct per Garfinkel et al. 2021 (about 3 million stones of 6.5 kg, about 25 days with round-the-clock work). "gathered from the fields": wrong for this study, which argues for quarrying (Codex). "weight of a full bucket": imprecise. Action: "six or seven kilos ... broken from the bedrock nearby". https://en.huji.ac.il/news/siege-ramps-and-breached-walls-ancient-warfare-and-assyrian-conquest-lachish
21. "more than eight hundred arrowheads", iron chain, armour scales. Correct (859 in Ussishkin's Area R, with an iron chain and iron scales). https://cojs.org/return_to_lachish-_steven_feldman-_bar_28-03-_may-jun_2002/
22. Judean counter-ramp. Correct. https://cojs.org/news-from-the-field-defensive-judean-counter-ramp-found-at-lachish-in-1983-season/ . "they tore down their own houses": could not verify. Action: cut.
23. Jars stamped "belonging to the king" sent to fortresses. Imprecise (the purpose is an interpretation). Action: cut.
24. "The sculptors ... drew the town from the south-west". Could not verify online (Ussishkin's argument). Action: cut. "all took place as shown": overstated (Codex). Action: softened to what the ground confirms.
25. "forty-six of Hezekiah's walled towns", "more than two hundred thousand people" (200,150), "like a bird in a cage", daughters sent to Nineveh. Correct. ANET 287-8 (Oppenheim). https://people.bethel.edu/~pferris/historical/hidden/HistSynopsisLinks/sennacheribinscription.htm
26. "eight kings ... kissed his feet". Correct: eight named kings in the same text. Ekron: leaders killed and hung on poles, guilty citizens made prisoners, the rest released, Padi restored. Correct. Same source.
27. Egyptians heaped severed hands. Correct; but "other rulers passed over them in a line" is wrong, since Egypt displayed them at length (Codex). Action: contrast rewritten. https://www2.cortland.edu/departments/history/docs/Candelora%20Article%202021.pdf . Hammurabi's laws prescribe impaling (153), burning (110, 157) and drowning (129, 155): correct.
28. The officer at the wall; "a land like your own land, a land of grain and wine". Correct. 2 Kings 18:26-32, NRSVue. https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Kings+18&version=NRSVUE
29. "A report survives from two of them who stood before a gate of Babylon". Correct: SAA 19 98 (ND 2632), "Peace Negotiations with the Babylonians", from Shamash-bunaya and Nabu-nammir, about 730 BCE. Catalogue entry confirmed; full text not fetched. https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/saao/saa19/shamash-bunaya
30. "about a hundred and fifty separate deportations ... well over a million people". Correct: 157 cases, 43 with figures (Oded 1979, via Radner 2018); Oded's extrapolated total is 4.4 million. https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/58863/1/Radner_Lost_tribes_of_Israel_BZAW_511_2018.pdf
31. Letter issuing deportees "food, clothing, waterskins, shoes and oil". Correct (letter to Tiglath-pileser III). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resettlement_policy_of_the_Neo-Assyrian_Empire
32. "27,290" from Samaria. Imprecise: Sargon's inscriptions give 27,290 and 27,280. Action: "more than twenty-seven thousand". Settled at Guzana (north Syria) and the cities of the Medes: correct. Same source.
33. "the Israelites ... vanish from the record"; "It worked on Israel completely". Overstated (Codex; Radner 2018 traces them for generations). Action: rewritten.
34. King's road: stations 35-40 km apart, pairs of mules, relay, about 150 officials with the royal seal, not surpassed until the telegraph (1865). Correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_communications_in_the_Neo-Assyrian_Empire
35. "seven hundred kilometres ... in less than five days". Imprecise (Codex: Radner treats five days as about the limit). Action: "about five days". https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/27226/1/Radner_Official_Epistolography_2015_Royal_Pen_Pals.pdf
36. "About three thousand of the letters". Correct to rounding: about 2,800 from Nineveh and some 300 from Kalhu; 1,200 from Sargon II alone. Same source; https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/saao/aebp/abouttheproject/index.html
37. Eunuch governors. Correct but unexplained (Codex). Action: paragraph cut for length.
38. Ashurbanipal reads "obscure Sumerian". Correct (inscription L4; British Museum translation as quoted by the Spectator and British Academy). https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/blog/what-is-assyriology/
39. "More than thirty thousand tablets and broken pieces". Correct. "the remains of some ten thousand works": doubtful unit (Codex). Action: cut. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Ashurbanipal ; https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/asbp/rlasb/Project/index.html
40. Garden relief: king on couch, queen seated, head of the Elamite king in a tree at the left. Correct. "that Ashurbanipal had just destroyed": wrong (Codex): Teumman died in battle in 653 BCE; Elam was destroyed in the 640s. Action: "whom the Assyrian army had killed in battle". https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/the-garden-party-relief-from-the-north-palace-of-ashurbanipal-room-s/QAFmvzeZOigV7g
41. Fall: Ashur sacked by the Medes in 614 BCE, alliance, Nineveh falls 612 after about three months; walls 12 km, fifteen gates. Correct. Draft had the alliance before Ashur fell: imprecise, reordered. https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-3-fall-of-nineveh-chronicle/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineveh
42. Halzi Gate: passage narrowed (7 m to 2 m), more than a dozen skeletons including children, arrowheads. Correct (Pickworth, Iraq 67, 2005). Draft said "a boy": now "children among them". https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/iraq/article/excavations-at-nineveh-the-halzi-gate1/7C8B5D5F95FD676BE3202A0AA4973BA1
43. "No subject people came to Assyria's aid". Wrong (Codex): Manneans and Egypt sent help; the last king held Harran to 609. Action: rewritten to say so.
44. "For who has ever escaped your endless cruelty?" Correct. Nahum 3:19, NRSVue.
45. Greek officer passing the ruins (Xenophon, Anabasis 3.4.10-12, "Mespila", once held by the Medes). Correct. https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0032/tlg006/perseus-eng2/3.4.4-3.4.23 . Action: cut for length.
46. Sennacherib's face hacked out of the Lachish relief. Correct; who did it is an inference (most sources: at the sack of 612 BCE; one suggests 681). Final text says "most likely". https://tyndale.cam.ac.uk/explore/articles/the-lachish-reliefs/ ; https://www.worldhistory.org/image/2801
47. Sargon II killed in battle, body not recovered; revolts followed. Correct (705 BCE; the "Sin of Sargon" text). Common knowledge in the field; not rechecked online.
48. "Plain iron is somewhat softer than good bronze." Correct (standard metallurgy: unhardened bloomery iron is softer than work-hardened tin bronze). Not rechecked online.
