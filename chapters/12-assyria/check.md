1. **Wrong; undermines the ending — high confidence.** “No subject people came to Assyria's aid, and that was the price of the method.” Also: “When the army stopped winning, there was nobody who wished it well.” The Babylonian chronicle explicitly records Manneans coming to Assyria’s aid in 616 BCE, Egyptian military assistance, and continued resistance from Harran after Nineveh fell. Egypt was a former Assyrian dependency. Assyria’s allies could not save it; that is different from having none. The claim that terror bought “no loyalty at all” also goes beyond what the evidence establishes. These are factual problems and breaches of the rule against breakable absolutes. [Source: *Fall of Nineveh Chronicle*, years 10–17](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-3-fall-of-nineveh-chronicle/).

2. **Contradiction with chapter 11, and factually wrong — high confidence.** “Bronze needed tin, tin needed ships and caravans, and ships and caravans needed a palace to organise them.” Chapter 11 explicitly says of the Ashur trade: “It was run by family firms,” then describes private investors, merchants and household production. That account is supported by the merchants’ surviving correspondence. Palaces participated in and regulated trade; they were not indispensable organisers of every caravan. [Source: Metropolitan Museum, private merchant letter](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/325842).

3. **Doubtful chronology supporting a central argument — high confidence.** “By about 3,000 years ago, across the lands from Greece to Iran, iron was the ordinary metal for tools and weapons, and bronze was kept for things that had to be cast, such as helmets and statues.” Adoption varied considerably by region and object. In Mesopotamia, iron remained associated with elites through the tenth century BCE; the substantial archaeological evidence for its replacement of bronze belongs chiefly to the eighth and seventh centuries. The sentence makes a gradual, uneven change happen across the whole region by about 1000 BCE. Bronze also continued in uses beyond the suggested residual category. [Source: Nathaniel Erb-Satullo, “The Innovation and Adoption of Iron in the Ancient Near East”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10814-019-09129-6).

4. **Wrong furnace chemistry — high confidence.** “Second, the sand and clay that were mixed in the ore do melt, because they melt at a lower heat than iron does.” Silica, the principal constituent of sand, melts at a *higher* temperature than iron. In a bloomery, silica reacts with iron oxide and other constituents to form slag with a lower melting temperature. That reaction is the missing step; the present explanation gives readers the wrong reason the process works. [Source: Charlton and colleagues, “Investigating the production provenance of iron artifacts with multivariate methods”](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1375923/1/1375923.pdf).

5. **Wrong in the geological context — high confidence.** “Iron is the commonest metal there is.” Aluminium is the most abundant metal in Earth’s crust; iron is second. Iron’s abundance in the whole Earth, including its core, does not support the following discussion of accessible rock and ore. [Source: Geoscience Australia, “Iron”](https://www.ga.gov.au/education/minerals-energy/australian-mineral-facts/iron).

6. **Unsupported historical certainty — medium-high confidence.** “Within a few generations, the Israelites who had been moved were absorbed into the peoples around them, and they vanish from the record.” This turns eventual loss of a clearly traceable population into a demonstrated timetable for complete assimilation. Assyrian documents identify Samarian deportees and descendants in military, administrative and economic settings; Yahweh-bearing names also survive. The evidence supports integration, but does not establish when every distinct identity disappeared. “It worked on Israel completely” compounds the problem. [Source: Karen Radner, “The ‘Lost Tribes of Israel’ in the Context of the Resettlement Programme of the Assyrian Empire”](https://www.academia.edu/37712389/2018_The_Lost_Tribes_of_Israel_in_the_Context_of_the_Resettlement_Programme_of_the_Assyrian_Empire_In_S_Hasegawa_C_Levin_and_K_Radner_ed_The_Last_Days_of_the_Kingdom_of_Israel_Berlin_2018_101_123).

7. **False contrast with other rulers — high confidence.** “What set Assyria apart is that its kings boasted of such things at length, in detail and with pictures, while other rulers passed over them in a line.” Egyptian rulers also displayed violence extensively in monumental pictures, including severed hands presented and counted at Medinet Habu. Chapter 11 has already introduced that temple’s elaborate war narrative. Assyria’s particular emphasis on punishment is defensible; the claim that everyone else confined such material to a line is not. [Source: Danielle Candelora’s study of Egyptian representations of prisoners and mutilation](https://www2.cortland.edu/departments/history/docs/Candelora%20Article%202021.pdf).

8. **Misleading account of military reform — high confidence.** “A century later, another king stopped sending the farmers home at the end of each summer.” This suggests that Tiglath-pileser III simply kept the seasonal agricultural levy permanently mobilised. His reforms instead made professional soldiers and foreign auxiliaries the army’s main component, supplemented by temporary levies; seasonal service continued. [Source: *Assyrian Empire Builders*, “The Assyrian army”](https://oracc2.museum.upenn.edu/saao/aebp/Essentials/Soldiers/TheAssyrianarmy/index.html).

9. **Conflated events — high confidence.** “From a branch of one tree, a little to the left, hangs the severed head of the king of Elam, a country in the south-west of Iran that Ashurbanipal had just destroyed.” The head belongs to Teumman, killed in 653 BCE. After that victory, Assyria installed replacement rulers in Elam. The devastating campaigns associated with Susa’s sack came later, in the 640s. The sentence merges the defeat commemorated by the head with the later destruction. [Sources: British Museum’s description of the garden relief](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/the-garden-party-relief-from-the-north-palace-of-ashurbanipal-room-s/QAFmvzeZOigV7g?hl=en), [Peter Dubovský, “Dynamics of the Fall: Ashurbanipal’s Conquest of Elam”](https://brill.com/previewpdf/display/book/edcoll/9789004207417/B9789004207417_027.xml).

10. **Wrong unit and excessive precision — high confidence.** “More than thirty thousand tablets and broken pieces of it are now in the British Museum, the remains of some ten thousand works.” Fragments, reconstructed tablets and distinct works are different things. A work could occupy multiple tablets, and multiple copies could survive. The museum’s research project reports estimates of roughly 2,000 to more than 10,000 original tablets, with uncertainty about which belonged to the royal library. That does not establish 10,000 works. [Source: British Museum/LMU, *Reading the Library of Ashurbanipal*](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/asbp/rlasb/Project/index.html).

11. **Conflicts with the study used for the reconstruction — high confidence.** “It was built of rough stones gathered from the fields around, each about the weight of a full bucket of water.” The recent study supplying the three-million-stone estimate argues specifically for quarrying nearby bedrock, because collecting surface stones could not sustain the proposed supply rate. It measured an average stone weight of about 6.4 kilograms. The chapter combines the older collection hypothesis with the newer quarry-based reconstruction without acknowledging the difference. [Source: Garfinkel and colleagues, “Constructing the Assyrian Siege Ramp at Lachish”](https://huji.org.ar/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Garfinkel-et-al.-2021-Constructing-the-Assyrian-Siege-Ramp-at-Lachish.pdf).

12. **Evidence overstated — high confidence.** “The ramp, the machines, the hail of arrows, the corner of the city where it happened and the fire all took place as shown.” Archaeology corroborates important elements of the siege, but cannot certify that everything happened as depicted. The machines themselves have not been recovered, and interpreting the relief’s spatial arrangement remains a scholarly question. The correct distinction is between substantial corroboration and verification of the entire picture. [Source: Garfinkel and colleagues, discussion of the iconographic evidence](https://huji.org.ar/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Garfinkel-et-al.-2021-Constructing-the-Assyrian-Siege-Ramp-at-Lachish.pdf).

13. **Apparently reverses the source’s estimate — high confidence.** “A letter could cross the seven hundred kilometres from the Mediterranean coast to the capital in less than five days, and an army on the march needed more than a month for the same journey.” Radner’s discussion of the approximately 700-kilometre route from Que to central Assyria says delivery in *much less than five days* is hard to imagine. This is an estimated lower limit, not evidence for routine delivery in under five days. “About five days” would preserve the point. [Source: Karen Radner, “Royal Pen Pals,” p. 64](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/27226/1/Radner_Official_Epistolography_2015_Royal_Pen_Pals.pdf).

14. **Reader confusion: provinces versus tributary kingdoms.** “He kept a paid army under arms all year round, with its own cavalry, engineers and supply officers, and he turned the countries that paid tribute into provinces, each under an Assyrian governor.” Readers are subsequently told that eight kings retained their thrones and that Hezekiah remained king. Explain here that *some* tributary kingdoms were annexed as provinces, while others continued under local rulers. Both arrangements existed simultaneously. [Source: Radner, “Revolts in the Assyrian Empire,” distinguishing territories inside and outside the provincial system](https://www.academia.edu/28113365/2016_Revolts_in_the_Assyrian_Empire_succession_wars_rebellions_against_a_false_king_and_independence_movements_In_J_J_Collins_and_J_G_Manning_eds_Revolt_and_Resistance_in_the_Ancient_Classical_World_and_the_Near_East_In_the_Crucible_of_Empire_Leiden_2016_41_54).

15. **Reader confusion and unexplained historical term.** “Many of the governors were eunuchs, chosen in part because a man with no sons could not hope to found a dynasty of his own.” “Eunuch” needs a brief definition at first use. Otherwise readers unfamiliar with the term cannot follow the connection between the appointment and the absence of biological heirs. This directly breaks the plain-words rule in `style.md`.

16. **Self-reference and signposting.** “The smiths who solved this found a way to make iron without melting it, and the furnace they used is simple enough to describe in a paragraph.” The final clause discusses the writing rather than the furnace and announces the explanation. End after “without melting it” and proceed to the existing “Picture a chimney…” explanation.

17. **Signpost sentence.** “The same campaign shows the method, because Sennacherib's account sets out three different treatments within a few lines.” This announces an organised demonstration instead of continuing the story. The following examples already make the distinction clear. It also draws attention to the source’s textual arrangement, which contributes little to the reader’s understanding.
