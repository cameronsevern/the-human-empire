## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Artificial intelligence is software that has learned from examples instead of following rules that somebody wrote down. Nobody programs it to recognise a face or write a sentence; it is shown millions of faces or billions of sentences and adjusts itself until it can. Since 2012, with enough data, fast enough chips and a great deal of money, such systems have gone from telling cats from dogs to writing, drawing, coding and solving problems in biology that had defeated scientists for fifty years. Like every powerful technology in this book it rests on concentrated materials: chips from one island, electricity by the power station, the written work of millions of people, and badly paid labour. It is owned by a few firms. The arguments about where it leads are fierce and unsettled. The oldest question about records still applies to it: who can read the record, who can challenge a decision made from it, and who puts it right.
