# Chapter 33 — Who ruled the Indian Ocean?

**Line under the title:** From East Africa to the South China Sea, from about 650 to about 1450.
**Length:** 4,600 to 5,200 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Nobody ruled it. The monsoon winds decided when ships could sail and forced merchants to wait for months in foreign ports, and the sea was too large and too open for any fleet to close. Power lay in the narrow places. A kingdom on Sumatra grew rich for five centuries by holding the strait that every ship between India and China had to use, with hardly any land, until a rival king sent a fleet across the Bay of Bengal and broke it. Wealth from the same trade built a temple city in Cambodia and stone towns in East Africa. Along the routes, Indian gods and writing, and later Islam, spread from Africa to Indonesia with merchants and teachers, because local rulers chose them. Hardly any of it was done by conquest, and most of the evidence is a few inscriptions, the notes of travellers, and what divers and archaeologists have found.

## The story

1. **Opening: sixty thousand bowls.** Answer first. The wreck off Belitung: an Arab ship sewn with rope, full of Chinese pottery, sunk about 830.
2. **The wind's timetable.** How the monsoon works and why it runs the ocean's calendar. Waiting for the turn; foreign quarters in every port.
3. **The kingdom that left no ruins.** The strait. A Chinese monk stops to learn grammar. Srivijaya rediscovered in 1918. How to rule water: ports, sea people, an oath over a stone.
4. **A fleet across the bay.** The Chola kings, their temple and their merchant guilds. The raid of 1025 and what it shows about power at sea.
5. **Gods by invitation.** Sanskrit, Shiva and the Buddha in lands India never conquered. Why a chief wanted a Brahmin. The mountain of stone in Java.
6. **A city of water.** Angkor: the lake that flows backwards, the reservoirs, the temple, the hospitals. What the laser survey found. How it came apart.
7. **Houses of stone.** The other shore: the Swahili towns, Kilwa and its gold, and the walls of Great Zimbabwe. The people who refused to believe who built them.
8. **Ending: a gravestone in Sumatra.** Islam arrives by the same route and the same means.
