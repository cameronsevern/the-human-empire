# Chapter 13 — How did Persia rule so many peoples?

**Line under the title:** Iran and the lands from Egypt to India, from about 2,575 years ago to about 2,450 years ago.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

In one generation the Persians took over more land and more peoples than any state before them, and they had far too few men to run it themselves. Their answer was to rule cheaply. A conquered people kept its own leaders, gods, laws and language, so long as it paid a fixed tribute and sent soldiers. Above them sat a governor, the satrap, and what tied the governors to the king was paperwork: a road lined with storehouses, sealed letters that were good for food and horses anywhere, and one office language, Aramaic, written in ink with twenty-two letters. The kings also had a creed, grown from the teaching of Zoroaster, in which the world is a contest between truth and the Lie, and they used it to turn every rebel into a liar. The arrangement cost little and lasted two centuries. Its weakness was built in: the people who had been left their soldiers, their treasuries and their followers were the people best placed to rebel.

## The story

1. **Flour for thirty-five.** Answer first. A traveller from Sardis draws flour at a storehouse in the hills of Iran on the strength of a sealed letter. The clay receipts found in the wall at Persepolis.
2. **A king from the hills.** Cyrus takes three kingdoms in a dozen years; his son adds Egypt. Larger than anything before, and the Persians are few.
3. **The god of Babylon chooses a Persian.** The Cyrus Cylinder read for what it is: a Babylonian building inscription, not a charter of rights. Local gods and local elites kept, in Babylon and in Egypt.
4. **Nineteen battles.** The empire falls apart on the death of Cyrus's son. Darius, the cliff at Behistun, the roped kings. Zoroaster, truth and the Lie, and what the word did for a king. Did Darius lie?
5. **The shopkeeper.** Satraps, fixed tribute, the silver in jars, the gift-bearers on the stairs at Persepolis, the palace built by every people.
6. **Ninety days on foot.** The royal road, its stations and its relay riders. How a sealed authorization worked, and the one that survives on leather.
7. **Twenty-two letters.** Persian is hardly written. Aramaic in ink as the office language from Egypt to Afghanistan.
8. **Water from under the hills.** The qanat: how it is dug and how it works. Five generations of free use for whoever brings water to dry land.
9. **A thousand bodyguards.** The price of ruling through others: a governor who is nearly a king, the killing of Oroites by letter, Egypt's revolts. The machine outlasts the dynasty.
