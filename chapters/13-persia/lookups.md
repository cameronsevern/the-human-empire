# Chapter 13 fact-check lookups

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. "Fixed" means the chapter text was changed. Dates are converted as BCE year + 2025.

## Opening (the ration tablet and the archive)

1. "a man named Dauma ... a party of thirty-five: twenty-three men and twelve servant boys ... from Sardis ... bound for Persepolis ... a litre and a half for each man and a litre for each boy ... sealed document from Artaphernes"
   - Verdict: correct. Tablet PF 1404 (Hallock 1969): 4.65 BAR of flour; 23 men at 1.5 QA, "boys" (puhu) at 1 QA; sealed document of Irdapirna (Artaphernes); from Sardis to Persepolis; month 9, year 27 of Darius (late 495 BCE), at Hidali. 4.65 BAR = 46.5 QA, so 12 boys. 1 QA is about 0.97 litre. Whether Dauma is one of the 23 is not stated, so the text says he drew rations "for a party of thirty-five".
   - Sources: https://achaemenica.org/articles/the-royal-road ; https://cdli.earth/artifacts/384090 (catalogue entry OIP 92, 1404) ; Hallock, Persepolis Fortification Tablets (OIP 92).
2. "Artaphernes, who was the king's brother and governed at Sardis"
   - Verdict: correct. Herodotus 5.25 (brother of Darius by the same father, governor at Sardis).
3. "The whole journey was close to three thousand kilometres."
   - Verdict: correct (rounded). Sardis to Susa about 2,500 to 2,700 km; Susa to Persepolis about 550 km. https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/royal-road/
4. "In 1933, archaeologists from the University of Chicago ... two small rooms ... tens of thousands ... sixteen years in the reign of ... Darius"
   - Verdict: correct. Found March 1933 by Herzfeld's Oriental Institute team in two rooms in the fortification wall at the north-east corner of the terrace; 20,000 to 25,000 tablets and fragments; 509 to 493 BCE (years 13 to 28).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis_Administrative_Archives ; https://www.livius.org/sources/about/persepolis-fortification-tablets/
5. "The man who ran the whole system drew two sheep a day."
   - Verdict: correct. Parnaka: 2 sheep, 90 quarts of wine, 180 quarts of flour daily (Hallock PF 654 to 669). https://iranicaonline.org/articles/persepolis-elamite-tablets ; https://www.livius.org/person/pharnaces-1/
6. "an extra ration ... twice as large for a son as for a daughter"
   - Verdict: correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis_Administrative_Archives
7. Codex: "Nobody there knew him / the storekeeper had never set eyes on him" is not in the tablet.
   - Verdict: right. Fixed: both claims cut; one sentence on what a seal is added.

## A king from the hills

8. "A little over a century before Dauma's journey ... Assyrians"
   - Verdict: first draft said "two generations", wrong (Nineveh fell 612 BCE, Dauma travelled 495 BCE). Fixed.
9. "The Median army mutinied and handed its own king over to him."
   - Verdict: correct. Nabonidus Chronicle, year 6 (550 BCE). https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-7-nabonidus-chronicle/
10. "He won one battle to the north of it, and his troops entered the city itself without a fight."
   - Verdict: correct as the Babylonian record has it (battle at Opis; army entered Babylon "without battle", October 539 BCE). Greek writers describe a siege; the contemporary chronicle is preferred. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Opis
11. "Cyrus died on campaign in Central Asia. His son Cambyses added Egypt. Within twenty-five years"
   - Verdict: correct. 550 BCE (Media) to 525 BCE (Egypt); Cyrus died 530 BCE.
12. "about four thousand kilometres, roughly the distance from Lisbon to Moscow"
   - Verdict: correct (rounded). Sardis to the Indus is about 3,800 to 4,200 km; Lisbon to Moscow about 3,900 km.

## The god of Babylon chooses a Persian

13. "In 1879 ... barrel-shaped lump of baked clay, about the size of a small loaf"
   - Verdict: correct. Found by Hormuzd Rassam's dig, March 1879; 22.5 cm long. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Cylinder
14. Summary of the text (Marduk seeks a just king, takes Cyrus by the hand, walks beside him like a friend; forced labour under the last king; gods' statues sent home).
   - Verdict: correct against the British Museum translation (I. Finkel), lines 8 to 15 and 30 to 34. The British Museum page returned 403; checked against the Wikipedia summary and the Finkel translation as recalled. "like a friend and companion, he walked at his side" is line 15.
15. "a copy of it is displayed at the United Nations in New York. It mentions no rights and frees no slaves ... ends by describing repairs to the city wall"
   - Verdict: correct. Replica given 1971; text ends with the wall and an inscription of Ashurbanipal. Same source.
16. "counted by the years of Cyrus, 'king of Babylon, king of the lands'"
   - Verdict: correct. Standard date formula of Babylonian contracts under Cyrus.
17. Egyptian official (Udjahorresnet): fleet commander, chief physician, composed the royal name, had foreigners cleared from the temple at Sais.
   - Verdict: correct. Statue in the Vatican (Museo Gregoriano Egizio). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udjahorresnet . First draft said "foreign soldiers"; fixed to "foreigners".

## Nineteen battles

18. "Cambyses died ... about 2,550 years ago ... another man had already been proclaimed king ... under the name of Bardiya ... killed in a fortress by seven Persian nobles"
   - Verdict: correct. 522 BCE. Behistun sections 11 to 13; Herodotus 3.61 to 79.
19. "a spear-bearer to Cambyses"
   - Verdict: correct. Herodotus 3.139.
20. "nineteen battles in a single year and nine kings taken prisoner"
   - Verdict: correct. Behistun section 52. https://www.livius.org/articles/place/behistun/behistun-3/
   - Codex: "nine men in nine countries" was wrong (two each in Persia, Elam and Babylon). Fixed.
21. The relief: foot on a man on his back, nine roped prisoners, winged figure, three languages.
   - Verdict: correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription
22. Median rebel: nose, ears, tongue, one eye, shown at the gate, impaled.
   - Verdict: correct. Behistun section 32 (Kent: "impaled"; Livius: "crucify").
23. "The Lie made them rebellious"
   - Verdict: correct. Behistun section 54, Kent's translation: "The Lie made them rebellious, so that these (men) deceived the people." Advice to later kings: section 55.
24. Zoroaster: date unknown, hymns memorised for more than a thousand years before being written; one creator, truth against the Lie, choice and judgment.
   - Verdict: correct. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/achaemenid-religion/
25. "He does not once mention Zoroaster, and historians argue over how closely the kings followed the prophet."
   - Verdict: correct. Sentence on the disagreement added after Codex. Same source.
26. Offerings to other gods from the royal stores.
   - Verdict: correct. Humban, Adad and others beside Ahuramazda. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis_Administrative_Archives (Henkelman, The Other Gods Who Are).
27. "Most historians today do not believe it."
   - Verdict: correct as the majority view (Briant, Dandamaev, Olmstead); not unanimous.
28. "Part of a copy has been found in Egypt, written in ink on papyrus, on an island far up the Nile."
   - Verdict: correct. Aramaic version from Elephantine; Behistun section 70 says copies were sent out.

## The shopkeeper

29. Father, master, shopkeeper; about twenty provinces; gifts before Darius, fixed tribute after.
   - Verdict: correct. Herodotus 3.89. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Herodotus/3D*.html
   - Codex: satraps existed before Darius. Right. Fixed.
30. "Babylonia owed a thousand talents of silver ... about thirty tonnes ... more silver than any other province"
   - Verdict: correct. Herodotus 3.92; Babylonian talent about 30 kg; next is Egypt at 700.
31. "The Persians themselves were excused it."
   - Verdict: correct. Herodotus 3.97. Codex: "paid nothing" was too strong, since Persians paid levies in kind. Fixed.
32. Stairway reliefs: twenty-three peoples, two-humped camel, baskets on a pole, elephant's tusk, led by an official.
   - Verdict: correct. https://www.livius.org/articles/place/persepolis/persepolis-photos/persepolis-apadana-east-stairs/

## Ninety days on foot

33. "one hundred and eleven stations ... ninety days"
   - Verdict: correct. Herodotus 5.52 to 53. Codex: stations are not "a day's walk apart". Right. Fixed.
34. Receipts audited at Persepolis.
   - Verdict: imprecise in the draft (the archive covers the Persepolis region only). Fixed: "In the country around Persepolis".
35. Travel letter on leather: steward, ten servants, white flour, coarse flour, wine or beer, nothing extra for a longer stay.
   - Verdict: correct. Arshama letter TADAE A6.9, Bodleian, late fifth century BCE; rations come from the prince's own estates (added after Codex). https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/12342
36. "a letter could cross in a week or two"
   - Verdict: correct as a modern estimate (about twelve days, Colburn 2013; older estimates seven to nine). https://achaemenica.org/articles/the-royal-road
37. Post office inscription.
   - Verdict: correct. Farley Building, New York; from Herodotus 8.98. https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/87770/neither-snow-nor-rain-isnt-usps-motto

## Twenty-two letters

38. Old Persian script used for monuments; Elamite at Persepolis; Aramaic between provinces.
   - Verdict: correct. https://iranicaonline.org/articles/persepolis-admin-archive
39. Documents from an island in the Nile and from northern Afghanistan.
   - Verdict: correct. Elephantine papyri; Khalili Aramaic documents from Bactria (353 to 324 BCE). Distance corrected from "more than four thousand" to "some three and a half thousand" kilometres. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalili_Collection_of_Aramaic_Documents
40. Hebrew square script and Arabic alphabet descend from the Aramaic script.
   - Verdict: correct.

## Water from under the hills

41. Construction: mother well, digging back from the mouth, shafts every thirty metres or so, leather buckets, slope about one in a thousand.
   - Verdict: correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qanat . Codex: the gradient is not a fixed threshold and upkeep is a continuing cost. Right. Fixed ("often by about a metre", "Most of the cost").
42. "whoever brought water to land that had never been watered could farm that land for five generations"
   - Verdict: correct. Polybius 10.28. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/10*.html
43. Qanats of the Persian period in an Egyptian oasis, with water contracts.
   - Verdict: correct. Ayn Manawir, Kharga; about twenty qanats; ostraca of the fifth and fourth centuries BCE include leases of water rights. https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-02051616
44. "The qanat was older than the empire."
   - Verdict: correct. Where it began (Iran or south-eastern Arabia) is disputed, so the draft's "in Iran and Arabia" was cut.
45. "Iran today still has tens of thousands of them."
   - Verdict: correct. About 37,000 in use in 2015. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qanat

## A thousand bodyguards

46. The story of Oroites: killed a fellow satrap and a royal messenger; a thousand spearmen; the letters read by the royal secretary.
   - Verdict: correct. Herodotus 3.126 to 128. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Herodotus/3E*.html
47. Egypt: revolt in the year Darius died, again a generation later, free for sixty years.
   - Verdict: correct. 486 BCE; about 460 BCE; 404 to 343 BCE.
48. Greek cities rose under a man the Persians had kept in power.
   - Verdict: correct. Aristagoras of Miletus, 499 BCE. Draft said "the very strongmen", which was wrong (the other tyrants were deposed). Fixed.
49. Codex: "far longer than the empire of terror that came before it".
   - Verdict: right, the comparison was wrong. Cut.
