# Chapter 93 — Why did we go to the Moon?

**Line under the title:** The United States and the Soviet Union, from 1957 to 1972.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

People went to the Moon because two rival powers were trying to prove to the rest of the world which of them owned the future. The Soviet Union won the first rounds with a satellite and a man in orbit. An American president who cared little about space needed a race that his country could still win, and chose the hardest one on offer. The rockets were the missiles of the Cold War grown large, built on the work of a German engineer who had used slave labour and a Russian one who had survived Stalin's camps. It took four hundred thousand people eight years. Twelve men walked on the Moon, the public lost interest almost at once, and nobody has been back since 1972. What lasted was what the race left in orbit and on the ground: satellites, cheap computer chips, and a photograph of the Earth.

## The story

1. **Opening: twenty-five seconds of fuel.** Answer first. The last minutes of the descent, 20 July 1969.
2. **Dreamers.** A deaf schoolteacher's equation; a professor's rocket in a cabbage field; how a rocket works and what an orbit is.
3. **Two engineers.** Von Braun and the tunnels where his rocket was built; Korolev and the gold mine.
4. **A beeping ball.** October 1957. A dog. A rocket that blew up on live television. What America did about it.
5. **"Let's go."** April 1961. The man who came down in a field. A president's choice, six weeks later, and what he said in private.
6. **Four hundred thousand.** How it was done: the giant rocket, the trick of meeting in lunar orbit, the women who wove the computer's memory and wrote its programs, the seamstresses, the mathematicians.
7. **Fire.** January 1967. The Soviet effort: a death on the operating table, a parachute that failed, four explosions.
8. **Earthrise.** Christmas 1968 and a photograph.
9. **Mule carts.** Who objected, and why.
10. **Two and a half hours.** The landing; a speech that was not needed.
11. **Afterwards.** Six landings and a cancelled programme; a handshake in orbit; what the race left behind.
12. **Ending: initials in the dust.**
