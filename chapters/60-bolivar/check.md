1. **Factual error, contradiction with chapter 47, and broken absolute — high confidence.**  
   > “Spain had also forbidden the colonies to trade with one another.”  
   > “The regions had never traded with one another or been governed together.”

   Restrictions varied by route and period; they were not a universal prohibition. Spain lifted major restrictions on intercolonial Pacific commerce in 1774 and widened permitted trade in 1778. Regions also shared viceregal governments. Chapter 47 already describes silver travelling from Potosí through Panama and commerce between Mexico and Manila. The chapter’s central explanation needs **limited integration**, not nonexistent connections. Sources: [commercial reforms](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/Places/America/United_States/_Topics/history/_Texts/BOUSIA/19%2A.html), [Cambridge history of colonial commerce](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/colombia-before-independence/commerce-and-economy-in-the-age-of-imperial-free-trade-17781796/0BDC4B11ABD4A173277280208BDF011D).

2. **Factual error affecting the main argument — high confidence.**  
   > “A legitimate monarch, accepted by every province, meant that there was no moment when authority lapsed and no opening for anyone below.”

   Pedro was not accepted by every province. At the end of 1822, several northern provinces, part of Bahia and Cisplatina remained loyal to Lisbon. Military and naval campaigns helped impose the new empire’s authority. Monarchical continuity helped Brazil retain much of its territory, but it did not eliminate contested sovereignty or popular participation. Source: [Brazilian National Library, “Guerras civis no Brasil e contra o Brasil”](https://bndigital.bn.gov.br/dossies/guerra-das-penas-os-panfletos-politicos-da-independencia-1820-1823/o-triunfo-do-imperio-brasilico/guerras-civis-no-brasil-e-contra-o-brasil/).

3. **Factual error — high confidence.**  
   > “The plainsmen's first leader was dead, and Bolívar won over his successor, a tough cattle hand who could not read, by treating him as an equal and promising his men land.”

   This makes José Antonio Páez the successor of the royalist José Tomás Boves. Páez had already fought for the patriots; he was an independent patriot commander whose forces joined Bolívar’s in 1818. Some of his men had previously served royalist commanders, but this was not a royalist army changing sides under Boves’s successor. Sources: [Páez biography](https://bibliofep.fundacionempresaspolar.org/dhv/entradas/p/paez-jose-antonio/), [Colombia’s National Museum on the 1818 alliance](https://www.museonacional.gov.co/colecciones/piezas-en-dialogo/2017/Paginas/Diciembre__2017_a_marzo_2018.aspx).

4. **Factual error and broken absolute — high confidence.**  
   > “Brazil stayed whole, and it kept slavery until 1888, longer than any other country in the hemisphere.”

   The slavery date is sound; “stayed whole” needs an exception. Brazil lost Cisplatina, which became independent Uruguay in 1828. That exception matters particularly in a chapter explaining the number of countries. Brazil retained most of its territory, rather than all of it. Source: [Brazilian Foreign Ministry’s diplomatic chronology for Uruguay](https://www.gov.br/funag/pt-br/chdd/historia-diplomatica/historia-das-relacoes-bilaterais-do-brasil/uruguai).

5. **Factual error — high confidence.**  
   > “They talked alone for several hours, and neither left a record of what was said.”

   There are no agreed minutes or verbatim transcript, but written accounts survive. Bolívar described the meeting to Santander in a letter of 29 July 1822; his secretary also prepared reports. The distinction is between incomplete, interested accounts and no accounts at all. Sources: [Bolívar’s collected writings](https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/15300/1/Doctrina_del_Libertador_Simon_Bolivar.pdf), [Leslie Bethell, “The Conference of Guayaquil”](https://histoire.ens.psl.eu/IMG/file/Coeure/The%20conference%20of%20Guayaquil.pdf).

6. **Misleading factual compression — high confidence.**  
   > “Mexico's independence came in 1821, and it came from the other direction.”  
   > “The creole colonel who had spent a decade fighting the rebels changed sides, declared Mexico independent, and briefly made himself its emperor.”

   Conservative defection was crucial, but independence came through an alliance between Iturbide and surviving insurgents, especially Vicente Guerrero. Omitting that alliance makes Hidalgo’s movement appear extinguished and independence the work of conservatives alone. One sentence about the insurgents joining Iturbide would preserve the argument while correcting the mechanism. Source: [Library of Congress, Mexico’s independence](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/mexican-revolution-and-the-united-states/independence-sp.html).

7. **Overstated factual claim — high confidence.**  
   > “He appointed four viceroys to rule its four great divisions, and under them a pyramid of governors, judges and tax collectors, nearly all of them sent out from Spain.”  
   > “They were shut out of the best posts all the same, for having been born on the wrong side of the ocean.”

   Peninsular preference became stronger under the later Bourbon reforms, but this description sweeps too broadly across offices and centuries. Creoles held substantial numbers of prestigious judicial posts, often acquired through purchase. Explain their **reduced access and renewed exclusion under the reforms**, rather than implying an uninterrupted near-monopoly extending down the bureaucracy. Source: [research on creole appointments to the royal high courts](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-latin-american-studies/article/abs/creole-appointments-and-the-sale-of-audiencia-positions-in-the-spanish-empire-under-the-early-bourbons-17011750/4B411DDD4DA1F4EAF8491E8549AB934B).

8. **Doubtful generalisation — high confidence about the missing qualification.**  
   > “They had no experience of elected assemblies of the kind the English colonies had run for a century and a half.”

   The comparison with longstanding British colonial legislatures is defensible. In this concluding account of the new republics, however, “no experience” erases the elections and representative institutions introduced during the independence era, including under the Cádiz Constitution. They lacked the same long institutional history; they did not enter independence without electoral experience. Source: [“The Spanish-American Tradition of Representation and its European Roots”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-latin-american-studies/article/abs/spanishamerican-tradition-of-representation-and-its-european-roots/6568E5BC588AA5AEAB3873762BCD7CF2).

9. **Doubtful chronology and conflation — medium-high confidence.**  
   > “They changed their minds when the rebel armies, which their leader could not control, began killing everyone who wore European clothes.”

   This compresses different phases and leaderships of the Andean rebellions into Túpac Amaru’s army acting during his lifetime. The account consulted distinguishes his initially multiethnic movement from Túpac Katari’s southern rebellion and the escalation after Amaru’s execution. “Everyone” also makes an unnecessary absolute. Identify which rebels committed the violence and when, or describe the widening racial violence without this universal claim. Source: [Open Encyclopedia of Anthropology, “Latin America”](https://www.anthroencyclopedia.com/printpdf/1271).

10. **Factual error in the opening scene — high confidence.**  
    > “That same evening a British frigate came into the same port.”

    Captain Beaver’s contemporary account says he approached La Guaira on the **morning** of 15 July and saw the French vessel coming to anchor. The two ships’ arrival on the same day is supported; the morning-versus-evening sequence is not. Source: [Beaver’s letter of 19 July 1808, reproduced in *Outline of the Revolution in Spanish America*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f3/Outline_of_the_Revolution_in_Spanish_America_%28IA_dli.granth.18407%29.pdf).

11. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.**  
    > “Chile was free within a year.”

    From the January 1817 crossing, the decisive victory at Maipú in April 1818 was more than a year away. Royalists also retained southern strongholds, with Chiloé incorporated into Chile only in 1826. Specify the liberation of central Chile or the consolidation of independence in 1818. Sources: [Chile’s National Library on Maipú](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-100629.html), [Chilean Navy on Chiloé’s surrender](https://www.armada.cl/tradicion-e-historia/principales-acciones-navales/la-guerra-de-la-independencia-1810-1826/incorporacion-de-chiloe-a-la-soberania-nacional-22-de-enero-de-1826).

12. **Inaccurate presentation of a quotation — high confidence.**  
    > “Pedro, urged on by the planters and merchants of Rio, sent a one-word answer that Brazilians still quote: ‘*Fico*’, I am staying.”

    *Fico* is the remembered keyword, not the whole recorded answer. Pedro announced his decision in response to a petition presented by Rio’s municipal representatives; the recorded statement ends “tell the people that I am staying.” Calling it a one-word answer sent to parliament invents a tidier exchange. Source: [Brazilian National Archives’ transcription of the declaration](https://historialuso.an.gov.br/index.php/historia-luso-brasileira/assuntos/bicentenario-da-independencia/6659-independencia-do-brasil).

13. **Reader loses the political sequence.**  
    > “He tore up the constitution, restored the Inquisition, and sent ten thousand veterans across the Atlantic to bring his American subjects to heel.”

    No constitution has been introduced. Neither chapter 58 nor 59 explains Spain’s Cádiz Constitution, so the reader cannot understand what Ferdinand is undoing. Briefly establish that the parliament governing during his captivity had adopted a constitution limiting royal power. “Inquisition” also needs a short explanation under the rule requiring historical terms to be explained on first appearance.

14. **Reader loses the geographical explanation promised by the title.**  
    > “He appointed four viceroys to rule its four great divisions, and under them a pyramid of governors, judges and tax collectors, nearly all of them sent out from Spain.”  
    > “The lands governed from Buenos Aires became four.”

    Neither the four divisions nor the four resulting countries are identified. The outline promises a map, but the chapter currently contains none. Without that map or a brief geographical explanation, the reader cannot connect colonial jurisdictions to the countries being explained. Here the names serve the argument: the latter four are Argentina, Bolivia, Paraguay and Uruguay.

15. **Reader loses the transition from loyalty to independence.**  
    > “The first Venezuelan republic collapsed in 1812, after an earthquake flattened its cities and the priests announced that God had passed judgement.”

    The last account of Caracas had its council swearing loyalty to Ferdinand. A republic now appears without its establishment or declaration of independence being mentioned. Add the intervening step before describing its collapse. Similarly, Bolívar’s united republic is introduced only near the end, when its dissolution is imminent; establishing it after the 1819 campaign would make the ending easier to follow.

16. **Clear signpost sentences prohibited by `style.md`.**  
    > “Two things then pushed them further.”  
    > “This was the secret of the wars of independence.”

    Both announce an explanation before giving it. The following sentences already supply the substance and can carry the transition themselves. These are structural flags, not objections to the narrator’s confidence or interpretation.

17. **Broken absolute.**  
    > “After that, the choice was between independence and the gallows.”

    As written, this leaves no room for continued loyalism, submission or accommodation—choices the chapter itself subsequently describes in Mexico and among the plainsmen. Narrow the claim to the danger facing committed insurgent leaders rather than making it the universal choice of American subjects.
