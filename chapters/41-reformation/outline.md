# Chapter 41 — Why are there Protestants and Catholics?

**Line under the title:** Germany and western Europe, from 1450 to 1648.
**Length:** 4,200 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The western church had silenced critics before. In 1517 it could not silence a German friar, for two reasons. The printing press, then sixty years old, carried his words across Germany faster than they could be burned, in cheap pamphlets that were read aloud to people who could not read. And German princes protected him, because a church with no pope above it was a church whose lands and appointments they could take for themselves. Belief and interest were tied together at every step: peasants heard a promise of freedom and were slaughtered for it, kings took the monasteries, and after a hundred and thirty years of war the settlement was that each ruler would decide the faith of his own country. The press that carried the argument carried slander and monsters just as easily.

## The story

1. **Opening: a form with a blank.** Answer first. The printed indulgence of 1454: what it promised, and what purgatory was.
2. **Letters of lead.** How Gutenberg's method worked, what a book cost before and after, how fast the presses spread, and who had got there first.
3. **Back to the sources.** The universities, and the scholars who went back to Greek and caught a forgery.
4. **The coin in the chest.** An archbishop's debt, a banker's agent, a preacher's jingle, and the friar's ninety-five points.
5. **Read aloud in the tavern.** Pamphlets and pictures, readings in taverns, monsters and slander. Why Luther did not burn: the hearing before the emperor, the castle, the Bible in German.
6. **Smite, slay and stab.** The peasants' rising of 1525 and Luther's answer.
7. **The prince's opportunity.** Church land, Henry of England, the rule that the ruler chooses. Rome's reply.
8. **Out of the window.** Thirty years of war, and a cardinal on the Protestant side.
9. **Ending: ten days apart.**
