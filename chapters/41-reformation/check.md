1. **Factual error — high confidence.** “It confirmed that each ruler was master in his own territory, in religion as in everything else, and that no outside power, neither emperor nor pope, had the right to interfere.”

   Westphalia did not give rulers unrestricted sovereignty or unrestricted control of religion. The imperial constitution continued; religious arrangements were largely fixed against a 1624 baseline, and specified rights protected religious minorities. France and Sweden participated in guaranteeing the settlement. This reverses an important part of what the peace accomplished. Sources: [treaty text and introduction, German Historical Institute](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/peace-treaties-of-westphalia-october-14-24-1648), [Oxford University Press discussion](https://blog.oup.com/2014/10/non-westphalian-peace/).

2. **Factual error; break of the rule against absolutes — high confidence.** “For the first time, a German farmer or shoemaker who could read, or who knew someone who could, was able to find out what the book actually said.”

   German translations circulated before Luther, including printed Bibles beginning with Mentelin’s edition of 1466. Luther greatly enlarged their audience and produced an exceptionally influential translation; he did not first make their contents accessible in German. Source: [Princeton University Library’s pre-Luther German Bible](https://dpul.princeton.edu/gutenberg/catalog/44558g85p).

3. **Misleading chronology of Luther’s beliefs — high confidence.** “If this was true, an indulgence was worse than useless, because it taught people to put their faith in a receipt.”

   Placed before the ninety-five theses, this gives Luther’s initial position the character of a completed rejection. In 1517 he attacked false assurances and abuses while explicitly retaining a legitimate role for papal indulgences: theses 38 and 71 are particularly clear. His developing break with Rome needs to remain a development. Source: [Oxford’s edition of the ninety-five theses](https://editions.mml.ox.ac.uk/editions/luther-95-theses/).

4. **Factual error; absolute — high confidence.** “It had its own courts and its own taxes, it paid none to the king, and it sent a stream of money to Rome.”

   Ecclesiastical exemptions varied, and churches and clergy did pay royal taxation. English clergy, including religious orders, paid subsidies to the Crown centuries before the Reformation. The correct point is that the church enjoyed substantial fiscal privileges, not complete exemption. Source: [The National Archives, religious houses and taxation](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/religious-houses-lands-1000-1530/).

5. **Factual error — high confidence.** “In Germany the emperor fought the Protestant princes for years and could not beat them.”

   Charles V decisively defeated the Schmalkaldic League at Mühlberg in 1547. He failed to turn military victory into a lasting religious settlement. That distinction matters to the explanation of why Protestantism survived. Source: [German Historical Institute, Battle of Mühlberg](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/battle-of-muehlberg-on-april-24-1547-1547).

6. **Misleading absolute — high confidence.** “Western Christians had been one church for more than a thousand years, and they were now permanently several.”

   Western Christianity had a dominant church, but it had not enjoyed uninterrupted institutional unity. Most relevant here, Hus’s execution did not extinguish his movement: Hussite religious practice received formal recognition in 1436. This also complicates the opening contrast between earlier critics successfully silenced and Luther escaping suppression through print. Source: [University of Vienna, research on the Hussite crusades and settlement](https://ucrisportal.univie.ac.at/de/publications/the-crusades-against-the-hussites-in-bohemia-14191436/).

7. **Misleading simplification and reader gap — high confidence.** “From this point on there were two organised camps, each sure that the other was leading souls to hell.”

   Protestantism was not a single organised church or consistently united camp. The missing distinction between Lutheran and Reformed Protestantism becomes consequential when the story reaches France, the Netherlands and Westphalia. The outline reasonably reserves Calvin’s Geneva for later, but readers still need a sentence establishing that reformers also disagreed with one another. Westphalia recognised the Reformed confession alongside Catholicism and Lutheranism. Source: [German Historical Institute’s treaty introduction](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/peace-treaties-of-westphalia-october-14-24-1648).

8. **Direct contradiction with an earlier chapter; absolute — high confidence.** “A book before Gutenberg was a manuscript, written out by a copyist with a pen.”

   Chapter 31 has already described the *Diamond Sutra*, commercial Chinese printing and inexpensive printed examination books. Its sentence “Under the Song, government offices, monasteries and commercial workshops all printed” directly contradicts this unrestricted claim. Specify that most **European** books were manuscripts. Source: [chapter 31](chapters/31-exams/chapter.md).

9. **Doubtful account of transmission — medium-high confidence.** “What happened was that somebody translated the ninety-five points into German and took them to a printer.”

   The documented early printed editions were Latin. This sentence makes a German translation the established mechanism of the initial printing breakthrough, when that particular sequence is not securely demonstrated. The safer story distinguishes the theses’ rapid circulation from Luther’s successful German pamphlet, the *Sermon on Indulgences and Grace*, published in March 1518. Sources: [Library of Congress’s 1517 edition](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021667736/), [Oxford’s edition and introduction to the sermon](https://editions.mml.ox.ac.uk/editions/ablassgnade6/).

10. **Factual error — high confidence.** “In France, Catholics and Protestants fought eight civil wars in a generation, and on a single night in 1572 a Paris mob murdered about three thousand Protestants in their houses.”

    The Paris massacre continued for several days. Its opening involved a decision by the royal council to kill Protestant leaders, followed by wider killing involving troops, militia and crowds. The sentence compresses both the duration and responsibility into a spontaneous-looking single-night mob attack. Source: [Musée protestant, Saint Bartholomew’s Day](https://museeprotestant.org/en/notice/st-bartholomews-day-24th-august-1572/).

11. **Reader gap — high confidence.** “In 1521 he was summoned before the emperor, Charles V, a young man of twenty-one who ruled Spain, the Netherlands, Austria and much of Italy as well as Germany.”

    Readers need the name **Holy Roman Empire** and a brief distinction between Charles’s hereditary kingdoms and his authority over the German princes. Chapter 34 established the western imperial title, but this wording makes Charles sound like the direct ruler of a single consolidated country. Frederick’s ability to protect Luther and the emperor’s difficulty enforcing religious policy then become puzzling. The continuing division of authority is visible in the [imperial settlement of 1648](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/peace-treaties-of-westphalia-october-14-24-1648).

12. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.** “The oldest piece of European printing that carries a date is not a book.”

    The intended distinction is printing **from movable type**. European woodcuts predate the indulgence, including the Saint Christopher impression carrying the date 1423. Its paper is now dated approximately 1428–1435, still earlier than 1454. Sources: [John Rylands catalogue](https://www.digitalcollections.manchester.ac.uk/view/PR-XYLO-17249-00002/1), [British Library study of the dated indulgence](https://bl.iro.bl.uk/entities/digitalcollection/efd33f07-9923-49cd-8012-1ea1aa89a258?f.contributors=Ing%2C+Janet%2Cequals&f.types=Article%2Cequals&spc.page=1).

13. **Misleading description of earlier technology — high confidence.** “The Chinese had been printing for seven hundred years by carving a whole page into a block of wood, and both they and the Koreans had experimented with separate characters that could be rearranged.”

    “Experimented” understates established Korean metal-type printing. The surviving *Jikji* was printed with cast movable metal characters in 1377. A short acknowledgement would prevent the following account from implying that Gutenberg first advanced movable type beyond experiments into metal book production. Source: [Bibliothèque nationale de France on the *Jikji*](https://www.bnf.fr/en/jikji-treasure-world-printing).

14. **Factual error; absolute — high confidence.** “He stopped writing for scholars.”

    Luther continued producing scholarly Latin works, including *On the Bondage of the Will*, his 1525 response to Erasmus. He added an enormous vernacular audience; he did not abandon scholarly readers. Source: [publisher’s account of *The Bondage of the Will*](https://boydellandbrewer.com/book/the-bondage-of-the-will-hb/?v=7885444af42e).

15. **Absolute contradicted by events — high confidence.** “Elsewhere there was none.”

    Here “none” means no peace outside Germany during the period following 1555. France’s Edict of Nantes brought the religious wars to an end in 1598, within that period. The intended point—that serious religious wars continued elsewhere—does not require claiming uninterrupted war everywhere else. Source: [Musée protestant, the eight wars of religion](https://museeprotestant.org/en/notice/the-eight-wars-of-religion-1562-1598/?parc=36101).

16. **Factual overstatement; absolute — high confidence.** “Catholic countries adopted the change at once.”

    Adoption was staggered: several Catholic countries changed in 1582, Catholic German territories began changing in 1583, and Hungary followed in 1587. The neighbouring-town image can survive, but it should not depend on universal immediate Catholic adoption. Source: [calendar adoption chronology](https://www.timeanddate.com/calendar/julian-gregorian-switch.html).

17. **Reader gap, especially after chapter 26.** “If Luther was right, however, there was no need for monks, since prayers could not buy anyone out of purgatory, and no need for a pope.”

    Chapter 26 established monasteries as communities of worship, work, learning, hospitality and care. Removing one claimed spiritual function does not explain why those communities should cease to exist. Readers need the missing theological connection: Luther challenged monastic vows and the idea that the monastic life provided a superior route to salvation. Likewise, rejection of papal authority needs its own brief explanation rather than appearing to follow automatically from rejecting indulgences.
