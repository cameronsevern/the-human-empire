# Flow pass, chapters 12 to 22

One read-through of chapters 12 to 22 after reading style.md and chapters 8 to 11. Images, image lines and captions were not touched. No section heading was removed; one was renamed because two chapters shared it. Word counts below are body words (headings, image lines and caption lines left out), before and after.

## By chapter

### 12. Assyria (4,480 to 4,491)
- Aramaic alphabet: added that the twenty-two letters are a cousin of the Lebanese traders' script from the end of chapter 11, so that 13 and 14 can lean on it and need not explain it again.
- Nothing else. Opening, iron section and ending already follow on from chapter 11 without repeating it.

### 13. Persia (4,555 to 4,578)
- The king's mail was explained from scratch although chapter 12 had just described the Assyrian relay as the oldest known. Added one sentence saying the Persians took the relay over from the Assyrians and stretched it.
- "wrote one" had no subject. Herodotus is now named at his first appearance (the shopkeeper saying) and again at the couriers. He comes back in 15 and 18.
- Section "Twenty-two letters" renamed "A letter in three languages", because chapter 14 has a section with the same heading.
- Cut the re-explanation of wedge-shaped signs against the alphabet (told in 11 and 12) down to a passing reference to the Assyrian scribes with scrolls and pens.

### 14. One God (4,234 to 4,217)
- Opening: tied the Babylonian oil tablets to the Persepolis ration slips of the chapter before, since two neighbouring chapters open on a storekeeper's tablet.
- Fall of Israel: chapter 12 already told the deportation of Samaria and the fading of the deportees. Cut to a shorter passage that refers to the Assyrians' "usual way". Kept the 27,000 figure, which matches 12.
- Added that Judah lost Lachish to Sennacherib (from 12), and that the Lachish of the potsherd letters had been rebuilt since that siege. Without this the reader meets a burned Lachish twice with no link.
- Alphabet: cut the Babylon clerk comparison to a clause. Kept the consonants-only detail, which is new here.
- "Clay lasts for ever" changed to "Clay lasts".
- Cyrus and the clay cylinder: introduced in full in 13. Cut the description of the object and its finding, kept what the cylinder says about returning peoples (new here) and the comparison with the prophet.
- The garrison on the Nile island: linked to the Aramaic papers from the same island in 13.

### 15. Money (4,453 to 4,444)
- Opening reordered to start with Ephesus and its temple, so that it does not open "In 19xx a team dug" straight after chapter 14 does.
- Sardis identified as the city at the western end of the Persian royal road.
- Seals: explained in 13, cut here to a passing reference.
- The Persians were introduced again as "a people from Iran who were then building the largest empire". Now "Cyrus and his Persians, fresh from their victory over the Medes".
- Herodotus no longer introduced a second time.

### 16. Caste (4,132, unchanged)
- No changes. It already leans on earlier chapters in passing (wagon herders, the quay of Akkad, Hammurabi's stone) and ends by pointing at the wandering teachers of 17.

### 17. Thinkers (4,178 to 4,109)
- Shang Yang: 17 and 21 told the same story with the same details (groups of five and ten, the prince's tutors, the chariots, "the new ruler kept his laws"). The full story belongs in 21. Here he keeps his idea, the heads-for-rank rule the section heading needs, and one sentence on his end.
- India: "hereditary priests" who taught sacrifice and rank were described as if new. Now "the Brahmins, with their sacrifices and their four ranks". Rebirth referred to as "the new belief" and "the wheel", as in 16.
- Greek cities: "lived in about a thousand cities" against "several hundred" in 15, 18 and 19. Now "came to live in about a thousand cities, most of them tiny", which is the count over the whole period and all coasts.

### 18. Democracy (4,473 to 4,409)
- The recap of the Bronze Age collapse and the borrowed alphabet (third telling after 11 and 17) cut to two sentences that carry the point needed here: no palaces, so no standing armies.
- "a king of Sumer" changed to "the king": Sumer is not a name the earlier chapters use.
- Persia was introduced from scratch (extent, governors, taxes). Cut. The Greek cities now pay "the satrap at Sardis, and through him Darius"; the rebels burn Sardis; Darius is named where the text said "the Great King" and "the old king". Later "governors" changed to "satraps" to match 13 and 19.
- The rival island is named as Aegina, "the island of the turtle coins" (15). "Ten silver coins" changed to "ten drachmas", the unit 15 explained.
- Herodotus named. The Persian "office records" are now "the ration tablets of Persepolis".

### 19. Alexander (4,150 to 4,120)
- Greece and its foot soldiers: marked as continuing from 18 ("still", "as the men of Marathon had").
- Persia introduced again, with satrap defined a second time and the royal road carrying orders "in days" (13 says a week or two). Replaced by three sentences of passing reference; the "in days" claim is gone.
- Darius: the reader knows Darius from 13 and 18, and this is a different man. Now "the third to bear the name of Darius".
- "the city's god" in Babylon is now Marduk; "Persia's old religion" is now "Zoroaster's religion".
- Cut the fourth comparison of an alphabet with "hundreds of signs on a clay tablet", and the second explanation of papyrus.

### 20. Ashoka (4,545 to 4,519)
- Alexander was introduced as "a young king from the north of Greece who had conquered every land from Egypt to Afghanistan". Cut to the mutiny at the river. Seleucus likewise.
- The Buddha was introduced in full a second time. Cut to a passing reference; kept the sentence that names his teaching dhamma.
- Added one sentence tying dhamma to the dharma of chapter 16, which is the same word.
- "Brahmans" changed to "Brahmins" to match 16 and 17.
- Aramaic at Kandahar tied to the Persian office language of 13.

### 21. Qin (4,536 to 4,514)
- Zhou lords and the change from chariots to massed farmers: told in 17. Cut to passing references. Kept the oracle bones, the Mandate of Heaven and the crossbow, which are new here.
- Shang Yang is brought in as a man the reader has met; the Legalists are set against Confucius by name. The scoop in Shanghai is now said to carry his name (it does: the first inscription names him).
- Bamboo strips: described in full in 17, cut to a reference.
- Coins: knife and spade money and the round coin were explained in 15; cut to a reference.
- The old walls "against the horsemen of the grasslands" contradicted the argument of 22, where the walls hold pasture taken from the herders. Now "along their borders with the herders".
- Signpost removed: "The reason lies in what Shang Yang had built."
- "What the Han changed for good was the weight and the story" was compressed. Opened out with "First… Second…".
- The Han's "old books" are tied to Confucius.

### 22. Wall (4,504 to 4,480)
- Qin, the First Emperor, Liu Bang and the Han were all introduced without names, as if 21 had not happened. Now named, and cut.
- Date contradiction: the Han founder's march north was "about 2,200 years ago", while 21 has him become emperor "about 2,230 years ago". The battle was in 200 BCE, two years after he took the throne. Now "about 2,225 years ago, two years after he took the throne".
- Sima Qian introduced a second time; now a reference back to the stories about the First Emperor.
- Wooden strips: third description of the strip book cut to a clause.
- Signpost removed: "That choice makes sense once it is clear what the grassland lacked."
- Greeks in Bactria tied to Alexander.

## Cross-chapter issues and how they were settled

1. Persia introduced four times (13, 15, 18, 19). Full account stays in 13; the others now refer to Cyrus, Darius, satraps, Sardis and the royal road as known things.
2. Alphabet against wedge-shaped signs explained five times (11, 12, 13, 14, 19) and the Greek borrowing twice (17, 18). 12 states the link to chapter 11 once; 13 and 14 refer back; 17 keeps the Greek vowels; 18 and 19 no longer explain it.
3. Shang Yang told twice (17, 21). Idea in 17, story in 21.
4. Zhou lords and the Warring States told twice (17, 21). Full in 17, reference in 21.
5. The Buddha told twice (17, 20), Alexander twice (19, 20), the Cyrus cylinder twice (13, 14), the fall of Samaria twice (12, 14), bamboo or wooden strip books three times (17, 21, 22), knife and spade money twice (15, 21), the Qin to Han story twice (21, 22). In each case the later telling is now a passing reference.
6. Dates: one real clash, the Han founder's defeat (22 against 21), fixed. Other dates checked against each other and found to agree: fall of Nineveh (12, 14), Cyrus at Babylon and Sardis (13, 14, 15), the Greek revolt and Dauma's journey (13, 18), Alexander's death and the Punjab (19, 20), Seleucus and the five hundred elephants (19, 20), Shang Yang in Qin (17, 21), the unification (17, 21, 22).
7. Numbers: Greek cities "about a thousand" against "several hundred" reconciled in 17. Royal mail "in days" (19) against "a week or two" (13): the claim in 19 is removed. Pay of a drachma a day agrees in 15 and 18.
8. Names: Brahmins or Brahmans (now Brahmins); governors or satraps (satraps after 13); dhamma and dharma (linked in 20); two kings called Darius (marked in 19); Herodotus named once in 13 and used by name after that.
9. Shared heading "Twenty-two letters" in 13 and 14: 13 renamed.
10. First lines: Because / Cheaply / It came from / Nobody in particular did / It came in two steps / Because / The Athenians did / Because / Yes / By conquest, and by counting / Less to keep raiders out. They vary enough. 12 and 17 share "Because" with 10 and 11, which is the sequential writer's own habit.

## Specific worries rechecked

- 13, PF 1404: the chapter's own lookups give the Hallock text and the arithmetic works (23 men at 1.5 and 12 boys at 1 make the 46.5 measures issued). A web search this pass did not turn up the tablet text itself. Left as is.
- 13, "king of Babylon, king of the lands": the standard date formula of contracts under Cyrus. Left.
- 13, "walked beside him like a friend": confirmed in Finkel's British Museum translation, line 15 ("like a friend and companion, he walked at his side"). It is a paraphrase in the chapter, not a quotation. Left.
- 15, a sheep for a twenty-fourth of a stater, and the king's gain of about a sixth: both confirmed in Kroll's essay on the coins of Sardis (15 to 20 per cent). The profit is one scholar's reconstruction and is disputed (see check.md item 6); the chapter already gives it as what numismatists reckon. Left.
- 18, walls pulled down to pipe-girls: confirmed, Xenophon, Hellenica 2.2.23 (Warner and Brownson translations). Paraphrase, left.
- 19, 180,000 talents: Diodorus 17.80 gives the figure for the treasure gathered at Ecbatana (seen through secondary citations, not the text). Arithmetic is right at an Attic talent. Left.
- 19, Rosetta Stone "tax favours": the decree does record remission of taxes to temples by the thirteen-year-old Ptolemy V. Left.
- 21, Shuihudi details: mouse holes, lateness rule and height as the test of adulthood are confirmed in the chapter's lookups. The ox beaten ten strokes an inch, the hanged-man form and the two soldiers with one head rest on Hulsewé's translation (Statutes on Stables and Parks; Models for Sealing and Investigating) and could not be confirmed online, by the chapter's checker or in this pass. They are given as paraphrase, not quotation. Left.
- 22, envoys as merchants (Hanshu 96A, Du Qin on Jibin) and "ploughing with wood, weeding by hand" (Yantie lun ch. 36): both match the Chinese text quoted in the chapter's lookups. Paraphrase, left.

## Not resolved

- The three Shuihudi details above have not been seen in a published translation by anyone in the process. They are well known in the literature and I believe them, but if the rule is strict they should be checked against Hulsewé, Remnants of Ch'in Law (A7 and group E), or cut.
- PF 1404 and the 180,000 talents likewise rest on secondary reports of Hallock and Diodorus.
- 13 and 14 both open on a ration tablet and both close by going back to the storekeeper. I linked them instead of rewriting either opening. If it still reads as a repeat, 14 is the one to change, since the tablet is 13's whole subject.
- 13's last sentence (a storekeeper looks at a new seal and measures out the flour) is an imagined scene, not a recorded one. It reads as an image, and chapters 8 to 11 do not end on invented scenes. Left for the author to decide.
- 17 and 21 use the same Warring States map with captions dated "about 2,300 years ago" and "about 2,285 years ago". Both are near enough true; captions were left alone.
- Length: the brief asks for under 4,700 words. That holds for body words (highest is 13 at 4,578). Counted with headings and captions, six chapters are between 4,900 and 5,150, as they were before the pass.
