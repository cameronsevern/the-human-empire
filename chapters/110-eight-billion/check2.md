## Factual flags

1. **Major — successive billions did not invariably arrive faster.**

   > “Each new billion, in other words, arrived faster than the one before, after an almost endless wait for the first.”

   The chapter’s own chart gives 1987, 1999 and 2011: two consecutive twelve-year intervals. The correct claim is that the intervals shortened dramatically over the long run, not at every milestone. [Population Reference Bureau’s milestone table](https://www.prb.org/app/uploads/2011/07/2011-world-population-data-sheet-presentation.pdf).

2. **Major — the new transition imposes a misleading historical sequence.**

   > “For Malthus to be proved wrong, two things had to change. Fewer people had to die, and more food had to be grown. Death gave way first.”

   Increased agricultural output did not wait for the nineteenth-century sanitation improvements described next. English agricultural output and productivity had already risen substantially before Malthus wrote. Moreover, falling mortality alone increases population pressure; escaping the Malthusian constraint concerns sustained improvements in resources and living standards relative to population. The two developments overlapped rather than following this simple sequence. [Robert Allen, *Tracking the Agricultural Revolution in England*](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3Af0df3e20-026b-4086-9f24-eac653219aba).

3. **Major — the opening still compares different age boundaries. This earlier flag remains unresolved.**

   > “Over the last two hundred years, and above all since 1950, clean water, vaccines and cheap medicines cut the share of children who die from nearly half to about one in twenty-five.”

   Later, “nearly half” means deaths before **fifteen**, while the modern figure means deaths before **five**. Changing “deaths” to “share” does not make these comparable. Use matching age boundaries or state both ages. [Historical mortality synthesis](https://ourworldindata.org/child-mortality-in-the-past); [UNICEF’s under-five estimates](https://www.unicef.org/esa/press-releases/global-child-deaths-reach-historic-low-2022-un-report).

4. **Minor — nitrogen was not the limiting factor in every field.**

   > “However hard a farmer worked, the yield of every field in the world was limited by it.”

   Nitrogen was a major agricultural constraint, but a field’s limiting factor could instead be phosphorus, water, or another requirement. The new wording strengthens an existing false universal. The correct statement concerns nitrogen’s widespread importance, not every field. [Minnesota Pollution Control Agency’s explanation of soil nutrient limitations](https://stormwater.pca.state.mn.us/soil_chemical_properties_and_processes).

5. **Minor — contraception improved before the pill project.**

   > “Decades passed before the means improved.”

   Positioned between 1916 and Sanger’s 1951 meeting, this implies that contraceptive methods did not improve during the intervening decades. Latex condoms introduced after 1920 were stronger, thinner and longer-lasting than earlier rubber versions. The long wait was for the pill, not for any improvement in contraception. [Dittrick Medical History Center](https://artsci.case.edu/dittrick/online-exhibits/history-of-birth-control/contraception-in-america-1900-1950/condoms-and-sponges/).

6. **Minor — the original pill contained synthetic hormones.**

   > “The pill uses the body's own hormones to prevent the release of an egg, so that there is nothing to fertilise.”

   Enovid contained norethynodrel and mestranol, synthetic compounds that act through the body’s hormonal system. “The body’s own hormones” incorrectly describes its ingredients. The explanation of ovulation suppression is sound. [FDA’s history of Enovid](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-history-exhibits/drug-therapeutics-regulation-us).

7. **Minor — replacement fertility also accounts for the sex ratio at birth.**

   > “Two children replace their two parents, and the small extra allows for those who die young.”

   The extra above two accounts both for mortality before and during reproductive ages and for slightly more boys than girls being born. Even with no premature deaths, two births per woman would not quite replace the mothers’ generation under the usual sex ratio. [UN, *World Social Report 2023*](https://www.un.org/development/desa/dspd/wp-content/uploads/sites/22/2023/01/2023wsr-fullreport.pdf).

8. **Minor — oral treatment was not confined to “everyone else.”**

   > “For everyone else, he had his staff mix salt and sugar in drums of water, and told the families to keep giving it by the cupful.”

   The revised distinction suggests separate drip-only and oral-only groups. The original report says severe cases received initial intravenous treatment **and adjunct oral solution**, conserving scarce intravenous supplies. This is a new narrowing of the account, rather than a repeat of the earlier, corrected “ran out of drip bottles” problem. [Mahalanabis and colleagues’ original report](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/oral-fluid-therapy-of-cholera-among-bangladesh-refugees-5/).

## Clarity flags

1. **Prohibited signposting in the Malthus passage.**

   > “His reasoning ran in a few plain steps.”  
   > “From that, Malthus drew his conclusion.”  
   > “The gloomiest part followed from the rest.”

   These announce reasoning or a conclusion immediately before supplying it. The surrounding explanation already establishes the connections.

2. **Further signposts that add little orientation.**

   > “The discovery concerned a single ingredient.”  
   > “The scale of that change is hard to take in.”  
   > “What a shrinking country looks like can already be seen.”  
   > “The plainest measure of all comes from a census of living things.”

   Each advertises the next fact instead of stating it. “Census” also suggests an enumeration, whereas the following sentence correctly describes estimates.

3. **Repetition around the food problem.**

   > “All those surviving children raised the problem that Malthus had foreseen.”  
   > “More living children meant more mouths, and Malthus had said that the land could not feed them.”

   The added first sentence repeats the second without supplying a new connection.

4. **Padding immediately explained by the next sentence.**

   > “The money came from a private source.”  
   > “Women took it up quickly.”  
   > “Across the world, the change in the size of families was enormous.”

   The widow’s funding, six million users, and fall from five children to slightly over two each make these introductory statements redundant.

5. **An absolute contradicted by the next explanation.**

   > “Everything depended on how long the second fall lagged behind the first.”

   The chapter subsequently says the speed of mortality decline mattered too. The size of the birth–death gap also matters, not just its duration. “Everything” breaks the style sheet’s rule.

6. **Certainty and a single cause where the chapter itself supplies several.**

   > “In a world where nearly half of all children died, a large family was the way to be sure of raising a few.”  
   > “Once that danger passed, the reason for the large family went with it.”

   More births improved the chances; they did not make survival certain. “The reason” also conflicts with the next paragraph’s explanation that children supplied useful labour. This is an internal inconsistency, not an objection to the chapter’s interpretation.

7. **An ambiguous absence.**

   > “For most of the diseases that killed children, however, there was still nothing.”

   “Nothing” can mean no vaccine, no treatment, or no preventive measure. The intended claim appears to concern vaccines.

8. **A weakened reference after paragraph splitting.**

   > “None has found a way of bringing the birth rate back up to that level.”

   “That level” now sits well away from the replacement threshold, after several intervening figures and examples. Naming replacement level would remove the ambiguity.

9. **Unnecessary repetition and an unclear transition in the final section.**

   > “The change shows first in the land.”  
   > “Water tells the same story.”

   These announce categories rather than advance the explanation. The water paragraph then moves immediately into fossil-fuel use, so its opening also promises a more focused subject than it delivers.

10. **Production notes remain in reader-facing captions.**

   > “the Commons file is 758 px wide”  
   > “the Commons file is 697 px wide”

   These details do not serve the historical narrative or explain the images.
