# Chapter 110 — How did there get to be eight billion of us?

**Line under the title:** The world, from 1798 to 2025.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

There are eight billion of us because, over the last two centuries and above all since 1950, people stopped dying young. Clean water, vaccines, cheap medicines and a packet of sugar and salt cut the deaths of children from nearly half to fewer than one in twenty-five. Births stayed high for a generation or two after deaths fell, and the population multiplied in the gap. It was fed, against every prediction, by fertiliser made from the air and by new strains of wheat and rice. Then births fell too, wherever girls went to school and couples could choose, and they have now fallen below the level of replacement in the countries where two thirds of humanity live. The human total will stop growing within the lifetime of a child born today. The price of feeding it has been paid by the rest of the living world.

## The story

1. **Opening: a baby in Manila.** Answer first. 15 November 2022, and the long count before it.
2. **Half the children.** What life was for most of history: births and deaths in balance, and how.
3. **A parson's arithmetic.** Malthus in 1798, and why he looked right.
4. **A pinch of salt.** Why death retreated: drains and chlorine, the needle, the pill bottle, and a remedy mixed in a refugee camp in 1971. The numbers for children.
5. **Bread from air.** Nitrogen: the factory that feeds half the world.
6. **A short stalk.** The Green Revolution: a plant breeder in Mexico, a shipment of seed to India, a book that predicted mass starvation. What it cost.
7. **The gap.** Why population exploded: the years between falling deaths and falling births.
8. **Choosing.** Why births fell: surviving children, schooling for girls, and a pill paid for by an heiress. Persuasion and force: Bangladesh's health workers, India's emergency.
9. **Below two.** The world now: shrinking countries, a young Africa, and the forecast peak.
10. **A plate.** What it takes to feed eight billion: land, water, energy.
11. **Ending: four per cent.**
