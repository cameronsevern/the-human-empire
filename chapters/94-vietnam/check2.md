## Factual flags

1. **Major — American assistance had not ended.**

   > “Without American help, the southern army could not hold.”

   The new sentence undermines the earlier correction from aid being cut off to aid being reduced. Congress appropriated **$700 million in military assistance for fiscal 1975**. South Vietnam lacked renewed American combat intervention; it was not without American help. Specify the absence of American air support or combat intervention. [Ford’s supplemental assistance request](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/special-message-the-congress-requesting-supplemental-assistance-for-the-republic-vietnam).

2. **Major — escalation becomes the beginning of the war.**

   > “The war itself came the next year.”

   In context, this means 1965. The war was already underway, as the chapter itself establishes through the insurgency, military advisers and earlier fighting. What began in 1965 was the sustained bombing campaign against North Vietnam and deployment of large American ground combat formations. Name that escalation. [State Department account of American escalation](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/gulf-of-tonkin/1000).

3. **Major — Ali’s punishment is attributed to speech rather than refusal of induction.**

   > “The price of saying it was high.”

   The following sentence describes loss of his title, exclusion from boxing and a prison sentence. These followed his **refusal to submit to military induction**, not simply his antiwar remark. The transition changes the causal meaning of an otherwise substantially accurate passage. [National Archives account](https://www.archives.gov/research/african-americans/individuals/muhammad-ali), [contemporary judicial account of his refusal and licensing dispute](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/308/11/1568823/).

4. **Major — Thieu’s acceptance had another documented cause.**

   > “The southern president agreed only because Nixon promised in writing to send the bombers back if the north attacked again.”

   This unchanged sentence’s **“only because”** is contradicted by Thieu’s own acceptance letter. He expressly acknowledged American threats to cut off aid if he refused to join the agreement. His acceptance followed both assurances of continued support and threats of abandonment. [Thieu’s letter to Nixon, 21 January 1973](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v09/d320).

5. **Minor — the new television transition reintroduces a corrected historical error.**

   > “Cruelty in war was nothing new. What was new was that the people at home could see it.”

   Domestic audiences had already seen graphic wartime images. *The Battle of the Somme* reached millions of cinema viewers in 1916 and showed casualties and dead bodies. The distinction is Vietnam’s regular television coverage in people’s homes, not the first opportunity to see wartime suffering. The chapter’s next sentence about earlier newsreels also contradicts the addition. [National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-somme), [Imperial War Museums film record](https://film.iwmcollections.org.uk/record/1310).

6. **Minor — Ali’s quotation is placed a year late.**

   > “In 1967 the heavyweight boxing champion of the world was called up, and refused to go. I ain't got no quarrel with them Viet Cong, he said.”

   The refusal occurred in April 1967, but the famous remark dates from **February 1966**, following his draft reclassification. The juxtaposition makes it sound like a statement first made at his refusal. Identify it as something he had already said. [Historical study documenting the February 1966 interview](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1F836EC149FD95174C482F6520F3DECA/S2057563723000172a.pdf/icons_and_ontological_insecurity.pdf).

7. **Minor — not all Americans had left Saigon.**

   > “The Americans had already gone.”

   The embassy evacuation had finished, but American journalists remained and witnessed the city’s capture. Specify the departing diplomats and military personnel. AP’s eyewitness account describes American correspondents remaining after the surrender. [Associated Press retrospective](https://apnews.com/article/1996f738e1e4f8186897df54cb918491).

## Clarity flags

- **Prohibited signposts.**

  > “The election was not held, and the reason is not hard to find.”  
  > “His method had two halves.”  
  > “The second half had consequences far beyond Vietnam.”  
  > “The cost of the whole war can be put in two numbers.”

  These announce an explanation, structure or figures instead of delivering them. They fall under the style sheet’s explicit prohibition on signpost sentences. The last also promises the whole cost immediately before describing suffering that those death totals do not capture.

- **An unclear chronological reset.**

  > “By then the question was no longer whether to leave, but how. Richard Nixon was elected in 1968 on a promise to end the war with honour.”

  The preceding section has reached 1971. “By then” points to 1971, but the next sentence returns to 1968 without acknowledging the move.

- **Repetition at the opening of Ho’s story.**

  > “The other side could afford to lose battles. The leader of the other side had been at this for fifty years.”

  “The other side” repeats immediately. The first sentence also needs reconciliation with the earlier statement that the southern guerrilla movement “never recovered”: enduring losses and suffering no decisive consequences are different ideas.

- **An accidental location.**

  > “The unfairness found its most famous voice in a boxing ring.”

  This suggests Ali made the protest in the ring. His occupation supplies the connection, but the sentence reads as a scene-setting claim.

- **A misleading singular.**

  > “One American tried to stop it.”

  This can sound like only one American intervened. Thompson acted with his crew, and the following account itself describes other Americans helping evacuate civilians. Introduce the pilot without suggesting solitary resistance. [U.S. Army account recognising Thompson, Andreotta and Colburn](https://usacac.army.mil/Article-Library/View-Content?ArtMID=1586&ArticleID=1355&PageID=5&PgrID=575).

- **A broad generalisation contradicts the chapter’s own account.**

  > “The communist countries did not stand together; they fought one another.”

  Earlier, the Soviet Union and China supply North Vietnam’s war effort. The intended point concerns later conflict between Vietnam, Cambodia and China; the sentence needs that temporal and geographical scope.

- **Several additions repeat the sentence beside them.**

  > “The French, however, had other plans. France wanted its colony back.”  
  > “The other side, meanwhile, kept its army supplied. The north sent men and weapons south…”  
  > “Single images could carry more weight than any report. Two photographs did as much as any battle.”

  Each pair makes essentially the same introductory move twice. These are stronger candidates for trimming than transitions that explain an otherwise difficult connection.

- **“One belief” overcompresses the explanation.**

  > “The plan rested on one belief.”

  The preceding sentence already identifies an intention to exploit French artillery and aircraft; the next adds assumptions about Vietnamese transport. “One” unnecessarily makes these calculations exclusive.

No hedging stacks, chapter self-reference or explicit chapter cross-references appeared.

## Overall

The revision improves spacing and connections, but it is not entirely fact-neutral: several new bridges change chronology, causation or scope. Correct those additions, remove the clearest signposts, and retain the chapter’s confident interpretation. No files were edited.
