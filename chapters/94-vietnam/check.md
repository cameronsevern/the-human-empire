Read the requested files; no files changed. Findings below are ordered by seriousness. I found no direct contradictions with chapters 87, 89 or 90, and no hedging stacks, chapter self-reference, explicit cross-references or clear prohibited signpost sentences.

1. **Wrong fact — aid had been reduced, not cut off.**

   > “Congress had cut off the money, and Nixon had been driven from office by a scandal at home.”

   Congress appropriated **$700 million in military assistance for South Vietnam for fiscal 1975**. It reduced aid and declined additional requests; it had also barred funding for renewed American combat operations. Those are different from cutting off the money altogether. This matters because the sentence explains the South’s collapse. **Confidence: high.** [Ford’s request for supplemental assistance](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/special-message-the-congress-requesting-supplemental-assistance-for-the-republic-vietnam).

2. **Misleading generalisation — the southern army did not uniformly lack conviction or resistance.**

   > “The northern army attacked, and the southern one, which had a million men and no faith in its cause, dissolved in fifty-five days.”

   The rapid collapse is real; “no faith in its cause” assigns one state of mind to an entire army. At Xuan Loc, the southern 18th Division and supporting units resisted repeated attacks for almost two weeks. The explanation needs to accommodate determined resistance alongside collapsing formations, disastrous withdrawals and leadership failures. **Confidence: high that the blanket claim is untenable.** [U.S. Army official history, concluding discussion](https://history.army.mil/portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/91-7.pdf).

3. **Misleading fact — Geneva did not represent agreement by every great power.**

   > “The great powers agreed that Vietnam would be divided for the time being at the seventeenth parallel, with Ho's government in the north and a French-backed one in the south, and that an election would be held within two years to reunite the country.”

   The military settlement established a temporary regrouping line; the conference’s final declaration envisaged elections in July 1956. **The United States did not join that declaration, and the southern State of Vietnam rejected the settlement.** Distinguish the ceasefire arrangements from the political declaration, particularly because the next paragraph concerns the election’s cancellation. **Confidence: high.** [Dulles’s contemporary statement on the conference results](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v16/d1053), [Geneva conference account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Geneva_Conference).

4. **Misleading attribution — Eisenhower’s estimate has lost its date and opponent.**

   > “The American president of the day wrote in his memoirs that everyone he had talked to agreed that Ho would have won four votes in five.”

   Eisenhower described knowledgeable contacts’ assessment of a hypothetical election **during the fighting against France**, with Ho competing against **Bao Dai**. He also made the percentage an estimate. Positioned immediately after the unheld reunification election, this reads as a prediction about the proposed 1956 contest under Diem. **Confidence: high.** [*Mandate for Change*, p. 372, reproduced excerpt](https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~furrg/ike1.html).

5. **Wrong comparison — “all sides” changes the bombing denominator.**

   > “Aircraft dropped more than seven million tons of bombs on Vietnam and its neighbours, three times the weight dropped by all sides in the Second World War.”

   The familiar roughly threefold comparison uses **American** Second World War bombing, not bombing by every belligerent. British and American aircraft alone dropped about 2.7 million tons in Europe; that excludes the Pacific and other countries’ bombing. Retain the Indochina total only with a consistently defined comparison. **Confidence: high that “all sides” is wrong; totals vary with the definition of ordnance.** [U.S. Air Force account of European bombing](https://www.mildenhall.af.mil/Portals/9/documents/welcome/AFD-130117-006.pdf%3Fver%3D2016-04-19-142646-083), [bombing-data overview](https://storymaps.esri.com/archives/stories/2017/vietnam-bombing_orig/index.html).

6. **Doubtful medical claim — established cancer risks and birth-defect claims need separating.**

   > “They sprayed seventy-five million litres of weed-killer over the forests and rice fields of the south, to strip the cover from the guerrillas and the food from their supporters, and it contained a poison that has caused cancers and birth defects ever since.”

   The total covers several herbicides; dioxin contamination particularly concerns Agent Orange and related formulations. Evidence supports associations with several cancers, but the National Academies found insufficient evidence to establish an association with birth defects in exposed people’s offspring. That does **not** establish absence of harm; it means the sentence presents a contested causal conclusion as settled. **Confidence: high about the distinction; moderate about the full extent of reproductive harm in exposed Vietnamese populations.** [National Academies, *Veterans and Agent Orange: Update 11*, summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25137/chapter/2).

7. **Wrong absolute — the Tonkin resolution was not the sole asserted legal basis throughout.**

   > “That resolution was the only legal basis for the war that followed.”

   It was the principal explicit congressional authorisation for escalation. Administrations also claimed presidential constitutional authority and relied on congressional funding; Congress repealed the resolution in 1971 while fighting continued. Their arguments’ validity was disputed, but “only” is still inaccurate. **Confidence: high.** [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/tonkin-gulf-resolution), [contemporary Justice Department opinion](https://www.justice.gov/olc/opinion/effect-repeal-tonkin-gulf-resolution).

8. **Misleading military explanation — territory mattered.**

   > “Since territory meant nothing, success was measured by the number of enemy dead, and every unit reported its count.”

   Attrition strategy elevated body counts, but securing villages, roads, bases and population centres remained central to the war. Pacification programmes explicitly sought to extend southern government control. Explain why enemy losses became a principal measure without declaring territory meaningless. **Confidence: high.** [National Archives account of CORDS and pacification](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/vietnam-war/civil-operations.html).

9. **Wrong fact and prohibited absolute — tours were not universally one year.**

   > “Each served for exactly one year and counted the days.”

   A standard Army tour in Vietnam was twelve months; Marine tours were generally thirteen. Extensions, repeat tours and early departures also occurred. Moreover, a deployment tour was not the entirety of a conscript’s military service. **Confidence: high.** [Marine Corps official history, discussion of tour lengths](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/US%20Marines%20in%20Vietnam%20The%20Defining%20Year%201968%20%20PCN%2019000313800_9.pdf).

10. **Wrong chronology — the French military return began in 1945.**

    > “Its army returned in 1946, and the war that followed lasted eight years.”

    French forces re-established themselves in southern Vietnam in **September 1945**, with British assistance. Their agreed return to the north occurred in 1946; full-scale Franco–Viet Minh war followed late that year. The sentence conflates those stages. **Confidence: high.** [Pentagon Papers account, reproduced by the University of Houston](https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/disp_textbook_print.cfm?psid=4091&smtid=3).

11. **Wrong chronology — Dien Bien Phu was occupied in 1953.**

    > “In 1954 the French commander set a trap.”

    The decision and airborne occupation belong to **November 1953**; the siege took place in 1954. The following sentence makes the placement of the garrison part of the incorrectly dated action. **Confidence: high.** [French Ministry of Defence chronology](https://www.cheminsdememoire.gouv.fr/fr/revue/indochine-1954).

12. **Wrong date — the coup and murders occurred on successive days.**

    > “On 1 November the generals seized power, and Diem and his brother were shot in the back of an armoured car.”

    The coup began on **1 November 1963**; Diem and Nhu were killed on **2 November**. **Confidence: high.** [National Archives chronology](https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/remembering-vietnam-online-exhibit-episodes-1-4).

13. **Misleading physical description — most helicopter evacuees did not leave from rooftops.**

    > “Through the previous night, helicopters had lifted seven thousand people from the roofs of the city to ships offshore, and thousands more had been left behind at the embassy walls, holding up their papers.”

    Approximately seven thousand people were evacuated during 29–30 April, but roughly **4,870 left from the Defense Attaché Office compound**, using ground landing zones. About 2,100 left from the embassy, which used its grounds as well as its roof. The sentence turns the iconic rooftop scene into the mechanism of the whole evacuation. **Confidence: high.** [Marine Corps official evacuation account](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/U.S.%20Marines%20in%20Vietnam_The%20Bitter%20End%201973-1975%20%20PCN%201900310900_9.pdf).

14. **Misleading contrast — earlier wars were also heard and seen.**

    > “Earlier wars had reached the public through censored newspaper reports.”

    Earlier wars also reached audiences through radio, photographs and cinema newsreels. The important distinction is television’s regular presence in people’s homes and Vietnam’s different censorship arrangements, not an earlier monopoly of newspapers. **Confidence: high.** [Australian Department of Veterans’ Affairs account of changing war coverage](https://anzacportal.dva.gov.au/resources/australia-and-vietnam-war).

15. **Reader comprehension and unsupported absolute — the Paris agreement’s consequences are left unstated.**

    > “Everyone concerned understood what that meant.”

    A general reader may not understand whether this means imminent conquest, continued civil war or the abandonment of the South. Explain the military consequence of northern troops remaining. “Everyone” also erases differing expectations: Nixon promised renewed American action against serious violations, while Thieu objected strongly to the settlement. **Confidence: high about the documented disagreement.** [State Department account of the negotiations and assurances](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v09/preface).

16. **Reader comprehension — “Viet Cong” appears without identification.**

    > “I ain't got no quarrel with them Viet Cong, he said.”

    This is the first appearance of the term. Identify it as the name commonly used for the southern communist-led insurgents already described. Chapter 89 explains Chinese Communists and chapter 90 distinguishes Korea’s armies; neither supplies this missing Vietnamese identification. This directly breaches the requirement to explain historical terms at first use.

17. **Reader comprehension — the central atrocity is never named.**

    > “On the morning of 16 March 1968, a company of American infantry was landed by helicopter beside a village on the central coast, in an area that had cost it several men to mines and booby traps.”

    The entire section describes **My Lai** without giving the name. Readers cannot connect this substantial episode to the event they will encounter elsewhere. The “few names” rule permits names needed to follow the story; this one earns its place.

18. **Style — an unlimited willingness to die is not a supportable description of a population.**

    > “To the Vietnamese on the other side, it was the last stage of a long fight to rid their country of foreign rule, and they were prepared to go on dying for that without limit.”

    The problem is specifically “without limit,” not the interpretation that national liberation motivated the communist side. It turns demonstrated endurance into an absolute claim about every participant’s willingness. Describe the leadership’s willingness to sustain enormous losses or the movement’s ability to outlast American commitment.

19. **Style — universal recruitment claim.**

    > “Every village that was burned made recruits for the other side.”

    “Every” requires evidence for each village and excludes other possible responses, including flight, withdrawal from politics or hostility to both armies. The defensible point is that destruction alienated civilians and helped insurgent recruitment. It can remain forceful without the universal quantifier.

20. **Style and reader comprehension — an unexplained heading.**

    > “The Americans who fought were young, and they were not a cross-section of their country.”

    This opens the section headed **“Nineteen,”** but neither this sentence nor the rest of the section explains the number. Readers may assume it asserts an average soldier’s age, although no such statistic is actually supplied. Give the heading a concrete connection to the story or remove the unexplained numerical implication.
