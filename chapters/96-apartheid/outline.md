# Chapter 96 — How did apartheid end?

**Line under the title:** South Africa, from 1948 to 1994.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Apartheid ended because the people it was built to control made the country impossible to govern, because the outside world stopped lending it money and playing sport with it, and because, when the Cold War ended, a prisoner and a president chose to bargain. It had been the most complete system of racial rule ever written into law: every person classified at birth, and the majority stripped of their land, their vote and finally their citizenship. Peaceful protest was tried for fifty years and answered with bullets, and its leaders then turned to sabotage. What finally worked was a mixture: strikes, boycotts and risings at home, sanctions from abroad, and four years of negotiation in which the country came close to civil war. In April 1994 everyone voted. The law is gone and the inequality it built is still there.

## The story

1. **Opening: a walk to a gate.** Answer first. 11 February 1990, and a face that nobody had been allowed to see for twenty-six years.
2. **A corner of Africa.** Who the white South Africans were; gold; the land law of 1913.
3. **Apartness.** 1948 and what followed: a register of races, a pencil in the hair, the pass book, the schools, the "homelands", the bulldozers.
4. **Fifty years of petitions.** The African National Congress; the method that Gandhi had left behind; a charter in a field. Sharpeville.
5. **The spear.** Mandela: a lawyer chooses sabotage. The trial and the speech from the dock. The island.
6. **Children.** Soweto, 1976. A death in a police van.
7. **Ungovernable.** The 1980s: the townships, the unions, the emergency. A speech that sank the currency. Banks, sport, and a concert.
8. **Tea with the president.** Secret talks from a prison. A wall falls in Berlin. Nine days in February.
9. **The worst years.** 1990 to 1994: the killing, a murder at Easter and a broadcast, the bargain.
10. **The queue.** April 1994. A rugby jersey.
11. **Truth.** The commission: what it offered and what it could not.
12. **Afterwards.** One term. What changed and what did not.
13. **Ending: a report at a grave.**
