1. **Wrong chronology that changes the causal story — high confidence.**  
   > “Within days a large American bank declined to renew its loans, and others followed.”

   Chase Manhattan announced its withdrawal on **31 July 1985**, before Botha’s speech on **15 August**. The speech deepened an existing financial crisis; it did not trigger Chase’s decision. [Peterson Institute chronology](https://www.piie.com/commentary/speeches-papers/case-62-2-and-85-1)

2. **Factual error and breakable absolute — high confidence.**  
   > “In April 1994 every adult South African voted for the first time, and the prisoner became the president.”

   All adult citizens became eligible to vote in a common national election; not everyone voted, and many had voted previously under restricted franchises. Nearly twenty million participated. “For the first time, adult South Africans of every race could vote in the same national election” states the achievement accurately. [South African government’s account](https://www.gcis.gov.za/sites/default/files/docs/resourcecentre/multimedia/25%20Years%20of%20Freedom%20booklet.pdf), [AP election retrospective](https://apnews.com/article/18393fe2f2eeb6f0217c3ca347c40453)

3. **Misleading account of land dispossession — high confidence.**  
   > “A law of 1913 forbade Africans, who were two thirds of the people, to own or rent land outside reserves that made up seven per cent of the country.”  
   > “The rest belonged to the whites.”

   The act restricted land purchases and tenancy outside scheduled areas, but did not instantly extinguish all existing African ownership. The Cape was exempt from its purchase prohibition because property ownership underpinned the qualified franchise. The remaining land was not simply all privately owned by whites. Preserve the seven-per-cent point while distinguishing restrictions on acquiring land from actual ownership. [Colin Bundy’s account of the act](https://sahistory.org.za/archive/centenary-1913-land-act-colin-bundy-18-april-2013)

4. **Wrong timing of Soviet withdrawal — high confidence.**  
   > “That argument was now dead, and so was the Soviet support on which the exiled Congress had lived.”

   In context, “now” means the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989. Soviet support did not end then: ANC–Soviet dealings continued through 1990, and the termination of military training became an issue in 1991. The changing Soviet position weakened the ANC’s backing; the sentence prematurely makes that backing disappear. [History of Soviet support for the anti-apartheid struggle](https://sahistory.org.za/article/union-soviet-socialist-republics-ussr-and-anti-apartheid-struggle)

5. **Misstates the amnesty mechanism — high confidence.**  
   > “To receive it, he had to tell the whole truth in public.”

   Full disclosure of relevant facts was required, together with the statutory political-objective criteria. **Public testimony was not required in every case**: applications involving no gross human-rights violation could be granted without a hearing. This matters because the paragraph makes public confession the universal price of amnesty. [TRC final report, processing applications](https://www.justice.gov.za/trc/report/finalreport/vol6_s1.pdf)

6. **Wrong date and ambiguous claim about office — high confidence.**  
   > “That night it was Mandela, who held no office, who went on state television to speak to the nation.”

   Hani was murdered on **10 April 1993**; the television address containing the white-woman passage quoted here was delivered on **13 April**. Mandela held no *government* office, but was ANC president. [Text and date of the address, ANC archive](https://anc.co.za/themes/Famous-Speeches/television-address-to-the-nation-by-anc-president-nelson-mandela-on-the-assassin.html)

7. **False “first” — high confidence.**  
   > “He came from a royal family of the Thembu people in the rural east, had run away to Johannesburg to escape an arranged marriage, and had opened the first Black law practice in the country.”

   Black legal practices existed decades before Mandela and Oliver Tambo opened theirs in 1952. Pixley ka Isaka Seme, for example, had a Johannesburg practice in 1911. Naming the Mandela–Tambo partnership and dropping “first” avoids both the error and the erasure of Tambo. [Richard Rive and Tim Couzens on Seme](https://sahistory.org.za/archive/seme-richard-rive-and-tim-couzens)

8. **False generalisation about Black South Africans — high confidence.**  
   > “In 1995 South Africa hosted the world championship of rugby, the Afrikaners' game, which Black South Africans had always detested as the sport of the oppressor.”

   Black rugby had a substantial history reaching back into the nineteenth century. Hostility towards the white Springbok establishment cannot be equated with universal hatred of rugby. “Always” also directly breaks the style rule on absolutes. [National Museum’s history of Black rugby](https://nationalmuseumpublications.co.za/south-africas-black-rugby-tradition-from-the-rhodes-trophy-to-the-south-africa-cup-1898-1971/)

9. **Misleading omission — high confidence.**  
   > “On 21 March 1960 several thousand people gathered outside the police station in the township of Sharpeville, without their pass books, and offered themselves for arrest.”

   Following an uninterrupted account of ANC campaigning, this reads as another ANC action. The demonstration was organised by the **Pan Africanist Congress**, an ANC breakaway; both organisations were subsequently banned. One short identification would prevent readers from assigning this pivotal event to the wrong organisation. [Sharpeville history](https://sahistory.org.za/article/sharpeville-massacre-21-march-1960?page=0)

10. **Misleading chronology of renewed resistance — high confidence.**  
    > “The quiet was broken by schoolchildren.”

    Soweto was a major escalation, but mass resistance had already revived with the **1973 Durban strikes**, involving tens of thousands of workers. The present transition from a quiet decade straight to 1976 obscures the resurgence of organised labour, which the chapter later makes central to apartheid’s defeat. [History of the Durban strikes](https://sahistory.org.za/article/1973-durban-strikes?page=1)

11. **Chronology slips backwards without warning — high confidence.**  
    > “That president had a stroke and was replaced by a cautious lawyer from the conservative wing of the party, F. W. de Klerk.”

    Placed after the July 1989 tea meeting, this suggests the stroke followed it. Botha suffered his stroke in **January**, relinquished the party leadership in February, met Mandela in July, and left the presidency in August. “Had already suffered a stroke” would restore the sequence. [South African History Online’s account](https://sahistory.org.za/article/apartheid-early-1980s)

12. **Doubtful attribution, rather than a demonstrably invented sentiment — medium confidence.**  
    > “He wrote later that as he walked towards freedom, he knew that if he did not leave his bitterness behind, he would still be in prison.”

    I could verify this sentiment in **Bill Clinton’s recollection of a conversation with Mandela**, but not as something Mandela himself wrote. Unless the lookups contain a specific written passage, change the attribution or replace the quotation. This also engages the style requirement that quotations be checked against a reference. [Clinton’s recorded remarks, 2000](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PPP-2000-book2/pdf/PPP-2000-book2-doc-pg1724-2.pdf)

13. **Unsupported absolute in the District Six explanation — high confidence about the exception; medium about the causal claim.**  
    > “Most of the ground stayed empty for decades, because nobody would build on it.”

    Some of the cleared district was redeveloped, notably for Cape Technikon. “Nobody would build” is therefore false, and cannot by itself explain the vacant land. The statement about prolonged emptiness can stand separately if supported; its universal explanation cannot. [Cape Peninsula University of Technology’s acknowledgement of building on District Six](https://www.cput.ac.za/component/rsblog/tag/district-six-campus?Itemid=101)

14. **Small factual error and internal inconsistency — high confidence.**  
    > “On the first morning of the election in 1994, Mandela cast his own vote at a school in the green hills of Natal.”

    Mandela voted on **27 April**. Voting began on **26 April**, initially for special categories of voters—the extra day included in the chapter’s earlier “four days.” Use the date or “the first day of general voting.” [Mandela Foundation timeline](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/uploads/files/DNL_Supplementary_Information_Timeline.pdf), [AP election chronology](https://apnews.com/article/18393fe2f2eeb6f0217c3ca347c40453)

15. **A general reader needs the legal and practical meanings separated.**  
    > “The races would be separated completely, in law, for ever.”

    The following pages describe Black people working in white mines, kitchens and urban areas. Explain that apartheid denied equal residence and political rights while retaining dependence on Black labour. Otherwise readers must reconcile “separated completely” with the economy themselves.

16. **The central financial mechanism needs one explanatory step.**  
    > “The currency lost a third of its value, and the stock exchange closed.”

    Readers are given consequences without being shown why nonrenewal of loans produced them. Explain that substantial debts were short-term: when banks stopped replacing maturing loans, South African borrowers suddenly needed money to repay them. That makes the financial pressure in the opening intelligible. [Account of the debt crisis](https://www.piie.com/commentary/speeches-papers/case-62-2-and-85-1)

17. **Style: a breakable absolute.**  
    > “Every funeral became a rally, and at many of them the police opened fire, which produced more funerals.”

    “Every funeral” includes ordinary private funerals and claims universality unnecessarily. Specify funerals of people killed in the struggle and use “many” or “often.” The point about repression feeding mobilisation remains forceful.

18. **Style: an unnecessary signpost.**  
    > “It ended with the words that made him famous.”

    This announces the significance of the quotation immediately before giving it. Under the explicit ban on signpost sentences, move directly to the speech’s ending.
