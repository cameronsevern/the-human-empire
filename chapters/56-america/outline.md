# Chapter 56 — Why did the American colonies rebel?

**Line under the title:** British North America, from 1754 to 1791.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The colonies rebelled over who had the right to tax them. Britain had doubled its debt winning a world war and asked the colonists, who paid almost nothing, to contribute. The colonists answered with the principle the English themselves had won in 1689: no tax without the consent of your own representatives. Ten years of argument about small sums turned into an argument about whether Parliament could rule them at all. They won the war because Britain was three thousand miles away and France joined in. What they built afterwards was new: a republic made on purpose, from a written plan, in which power came from the people and was divided so that no part could take it all. The plan's language of equal rights was written by slave owners, and it protected slavery. It could also be claimed by the people it left out.

## The story

1. **Opening: a broken padlock.** Answer first. Boston harbour, December 1773: tea that had just been made cheaper goes into the water.
2. **A volley in the backwoods.** The Seven Years' War starts in the American forest and ends with Britain master of North America and deep in debt.
3. **The lightest taxes in the empire.** Who the colonists were. The stamp tax of 1765 and why it enraged the people it touched. The rights of Englishmen.
4. **Cheaper tea.** Ten years of duties, boycotts and soldiers in Boston. The Company's tea. The port closed. Shots at dawn in 1775.
5. **Common sense.** A pamphlet in plain words turns a tax dispute into a war for independence. The Declaration and the ideas behind it.
6. **The loudest yelps.** A fifth of the people were enslaved. Who offered them freedom. The Native nations and the loyalists.
7. **Three thousand miles.** Why Britain could not win. The general who kept losing and kept his army. France.
8. **A machine on paper.** The constitution of 1787: what it was for, how it divides power, what it says about slavery without using the word. Who could vote.
9. **Ending: the key.** A general goes home. A key arrives from Paris.

## Left for later

The French Revolution. The westward growth of the United States and the removal of the Native nations. The civil war over slavery.
