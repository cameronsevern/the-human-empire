# Chapter 3 fact-check lookups

Method: web searches and page fetches this session. Search-tool summaries were often thin, so where I relied on a summary rather than a page I say so. Items marked "not verified" were searched but no source answered the specific point.

## Verdicts

### 1. Sungir: PARTLY
- Confirmed: about 34,000 years ago; a man (about 40) and two boys (about 10 and 12); red ochre; 16 spears of mammoth ivory; more than 20 armbands; about 300 pierced fox teeth. https://www.archaeology.org/news/6380-180214-russia-sunghir-burials , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungir
- Beads: Wikipedia and Discover say more than 13,000. The 2018 study reported "over 10,000". "About thirteen thousand" is fine.
- Labour is where sources conflict. Wikipedia says about 10,000 hours (about 0.77 h per bead). Discover says 2,500 hours. The "one hour" figure is one hobbyist page. https://www.discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/how-we-know-ancient-humans-believed-in-the-afterlife
- Ten thousand hours is about five working years, so "several years" holds on the Wikipedia estimate only.
- Replacement: "A single bead took most of an hour to make. Thirteen thousand of them is about five years of one person's working life, and all of it went into a hole in the ground with the dead."
- Also: the spears are ivory, so "carved from mammoth tusk" is right.

### 2. Elephants and chimpanzees: CONFIRMED
- Elephants stand over dead companions, sometimes for days. Chimpanzee mothers carry infant corpses for days, on average about three, and sometimes over 100. https://www.livescience.com/do-animals-grieve , https://www.discovermagazine.com/are-animals-capable-of-grief-41888
- Interpretation is debated, but the behaviour itself is documented. "Grief is clearly not ours alone" is slightly strong. Safer: "so mourning is clearly not ours alone"? That is not safer. Leave it.

### 3. Qafzeh: PARTLY
- Confirmed: about 13 years old. The remains are dated about 95,000 to 100,000 years ago. Red ochre lumps and Glycymeris shells (naturally perforated, some ochre-stained, strung, carried about 35 km) come from Qafzeh. Pierced Nassarius shells come from Skhul. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skhul_and_Qafzeh_hominins , https://www.discovermagazine.com/a-90-000-year-old-skeleton-may-be-one-of-the-earliest-known-victims-of-a-sharp-object-attack-49426
- Wrong or unsupported: "hands turned up beside his neck, and across them somebody placed the antlers". Sources say the hands were on either side of the neck, and the red deer antlers were clasped in the hands against the chest. "Turned up" is not in any source.
- "In the same caves" is fine if it means Qafzeh and Skhul, which are different caves.
- Replacement: "In a cave in Israel, a boy of about thirteen was laid on his back with his hands beside his neck, and in his hands somebody placed the antlers of a red deer. In the same region, at Qafzeh and nearby Skhul, there are lumps of red ochre, a soft red stone that can be ground into powder, and seashells with holes in them for stringing."
- "A hundred thousand years ago": "nearly a hundred thousand" is more accurate.

### 4. Red ochre from Africa to Australia: CONFIRMED
- Mungo Man (Lake Mungo, about 40,000 years old) was sprinkled with red ochre. The nearest source is about 200 km away. https://www.thecollector.com/mungo-man-discovery/ (secondary; fine for this claim).
- The Qafzeh ochre covers the Middle East and Africa.

### 5. La Chapelle-aux-Saints: WRONG as written
- Reassessment: he had lost most but not all of his teeth. Several incisors, canines and premolars remained, and he could probably chew, perhaps with difficulty. Wikipedia on the skeleton: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Chapelle-aux-Saints_1 ; also a scilit abstract (left incisors, canines and premolars intact), not opened in full.
- He had advanced arthritis and hip and back problems. He was in his 60s or 70s.
- Pit: National Geographic says a natural depression that was at least partly modified. Rendu et al. (PNAS 2014) say the pit is not natural. https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/131216-la-chapelle-neanderthal-burials-graves , https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/new-evidence-suggest-neandertals-buried-their-dead/
- "Toothless" and "could not have chewed his own food for years" are the old Boule-era reading and are wrong.
- Replacement: "In a cave in France, an old Neanderthal man, who had lost most of his teeth and was crippled with arthritis, was laid in a hollow that had been cut deeper for him. His hips and back were so bad that he could hardly have moved without help. Somebody had looked after him, and when he died, somebody made sure the hyenas did not get him."
- Hyena-gnawing evidence: the animal bones on site were gnawed and the Neanderthal's were not, so the hyena line holds.

### 6. "Probably the only animal that knows it will die": NOT FOUND as a fact (consistent with the text as hedged)
- Scholars say we alone "fully anticipate the inevitability of death". Researchers like Monsó argue animals have a minimal concept of death. There is no evidence either way on their own mortality. https://lithub.com/what-does-animal-grief-tell-us-about-how-they-understand-death , https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/do-animals-understand-death
- "Probably" is the right word. Keep as is.

### 7. Chauvet: CONFIRMED (details noted)
- Found 18 December 1994 by Jean-Marie Chauvet, Eliette Brunel and Christian Hillaire (amateur cavers). A draught of air from the rock led them to it. https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/chauvet/en/discovery-3 , https://artsandculture.google.com/story/uAUh3dHUU30TLw , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvet_Cave
- Sealed by a rockfall about 29,000 years ago (2012 study) and untouched until 1994, so "more than twenty thousand years" is safe, and "more than twenty-five thousand" would also be.
- Oldest painting about 36,500 years (2020 recalibration). Scraping walls clean is documented. Lions, rhinos and many predators are catalogued.
- Not verified: "four horses' heads, each with a different expression" (the Panel of Horses exists; I did not find a page counting four). Charcoal smudging with fingers was not mentioned in the page I read. Both are widely stated in Chauvet literature but I could not confirm them here.
- "Cleared the stones away, squeezed through": fine.

### 8. "More than three hundred painted caves in France and Spain": CONFIRMED
- Wikipedia: "nearly 350" caves with prehistoric art in the two countries. Other counts run 300 to 350 depending on definitions. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_painting
- Note: "painted" is loose, since many have only engravings. "Decorated caves" is more exact. Optional: "More than three hundred decorated caves are known in France and Spain alone."

### 9. Sulawesi pig: CONFIRMED
- Nature, July 2024: a red painting at Leang Karampuang, at least 51,200 years old (laser-ablation U-series on the calcite crust). https://www.nbcnews.com/news/rcna160304 , https://www.sciencealert.com/cave-painting-of-a-pig-hunt-could-be-the-oldest-story-ever-recorded
- Minor: some reporting describes the three figures as part-human therianthropes. "Three small human-like figures" is safer. Optional edit.

### 10. Deep caves and fat lamps: PARTLY
- Lamps confirmed. Over 100 small stone lamps were found at Lascaux. They burned rendered animal fat. A single lamp lights about 3 m around it. https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/lighting , https://www.history.com/articles/lascaux-paintings-stone-age
- Not verified: "hundreds of metres from the entrance" and "only by crawling". True of some caves (Niaux, Chauvet is about 400 m long), but I found no page stating it. Lascaux itself has many paintings near the entrance hall.
- Replacement: "The paintings, by contrast, are often deep inside, far from the entrance, in chambers that can only be reached by passages where people had to crawl."

### 11. Lascaux reindeer: PARTLY
- Sources conflict. Wikipedia says "no images of reindeer" yet notes one in the Apse. A paleolithic study states one reindeer. Another says "one or possibly a few". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lascaux , https://www.ancient-origins.net/node/13095
- The "reindeer diet" claim: reindeer dominate the bones (one source gives 88%), but the same source notes that the refuse bones and the art have not been shown to be the same age.
- Animals depicted (Wikipedia): horses 364, stags 90, aurochs and bison each 4 to 5%. So "horses, wild bulls and stags" is right.
- Replacement: "The people who lived near Lascaux ate mainly reindeer, as the bones in their rubbish show. However, among the hundreds of animals on its walls there is at most one reindeer."

### 12. Humans rare, hand stencils: CONFIRMED
- Wikipedia: human figures are "rare and usually schematic". Cueva de las Manos: over 2,000 hand stencils, more than 9,000 years old, mostly blown through a tube, made by men, women and children. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_painting , https://britannica.com/place/Cueva-de-las-Manos
- "Children who must have been lifted up to reach" is invention. Replacement: "Some of the hands belong to children."
- France and Indonesia stencils: stated in the general literature (not looked up separately here). Spelling for the Argentine site is "Cueva de las Manos".

### 13. Flutes: PARTLY
- The Hohle Fels flute is made from a griffon vulture wing bone, 34 cm long, with five finger holes. The finders (Conard) put it "very close to 40,000" years old; the published dating is "at least 35,000". Fragments from Geissenkloesterle redated to about 42,000 (2012). https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/bone-flute-is-oldest-instrument--study-says , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohle_Fels
- "Spaced to give a scale of notes": only a flute-maker's blog (kassaflutes.com) says a replica plays a pentatonic scale. Not reliable.
- Replacement: "In caves in southern Germany, between thirty-five and forty-two thousand years ago, people made flutes from the wing bones of vultures and from mammoth ivory, with finger holes cut with care. So there was music, and no doubt there was singing and dancing long before anyone thought to carve a flute."

### 14. Lion-man: PARTLY
- Confirmed: mammoth ivory; 31.1 cm tall; about 40,000 years old (layer dates 35,000 to 41,000). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion-man_of_Hohlenstein-Stadel
- Hours: replication took over 370 hours (Hein and Wehrberger). Ulm Museum says 360, Cook 320. "Something like four hundred" is a bit high.
- Found in a chamber almost 30 m from the entrance. "Hide at the back of a cave" is not supported. "Polished by handling" is not in the Wikipedia article. One source says polished with hematite powder, another with leather and saliva.
- Replacement: "No such creature has ever existed. Somebody imagined it, and then cared enough about it to spend some three hundred and fifty hours making it real, and others cared enough to keep it and carry it into the cave. Language had given people the power to talk about things that are not there. The lion-man is one of those things, and after forty thousand years we can still hold it." (Also change "something like four hundred hours" in the paragraph above to "something like three hundred and fifty hours", and "two months" to "some two months".)

### 15. Forager religion (Peoples, Duda, Marlowe 2016): PARTLY
- Paper confirmed: Human Nature 27, 261-282. Animism is reconstructed as the oldest trait. High gods do not follow the pattern of the other traits. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4958132
- I could not confirm "near-universal" for animism, "common" for shamanism, or "rare" for moralizing high gods from the pages retrieved. The chapter's "every such people has someone whose job is to deal with the unseen" is too absolute. Shamanism or healers are very common but not found in every society.
- Replacement: "Because of this, almost every such people has someone whose job is to deal with the unseen." And for the high god: "Above all, there is almost never a great god in the sky who made the rules and watches to see whether you keep them."

### 16. Inuit seal and the "souls" quote: CONFIRMED for seal; PARTLY for the quote
- Seal drink: confirmed as a widespread Inuit practice. https://www.kitikmeotheritage.ca/the-art-of-seal , https://eloka.nsidc.org/communities/elders/maps/all-seals-subsistence.html
- Quote: Rasmussen, Intellectual Culture of the Iglulik Eskimos (1929), p. 56: "The greatest peril of life lies in the fact that human food consists entirely of souls." One scholarly article attributes it to a brother of a shaman from the Igloolik region. I did not confirm from a page whether the speaker was Ivaluardjuk or Aua. https://erudit.org/fr/revue/etudinuit/2017/v41/n1-2/1061435ar.html
- The chapter text paraphrases without naming the speaker, so it is safe. "A Danish visitor" is fine (Rasmussen was Danish-Greenlandic). "A century ago" is right (1921-24).
- Not verified: "come back in a new body". It is the standard belief but I did not find a page for it.

### 17. San healing dance: PARTLY
- Sources: women sit and clap and sing; men are the main dancers and healers; trance deepens as "n/um" heats up; most men healed at some point. https://collections.peabody.harvard.edu/objects/details/742441 , https://films.der.org/film/n-um-tchai/e6712c7e-4d40-47bc-a76a-1f5926f62759
- Not verified: "laying hands on everyone", "argue with the dead for the life of a sick child", "travel to the spirit world". They match the Katz and Biesele literature but I found no page that states them.
- "The San of the Kalahari": the chapter's name for the people is Ju/'hoansi elsewhere. Use one name.

### 18. Shamanic reading of caves: WRONG (too strong)
- David Lewis-Williams and Jean Clottes, The Shamans of Prehistory (1998). The model is widely criticised as vague and hard to test (Helvenston and Bahn 2003, Kehoe, Bahn). No source supports "many who study the caves think so". https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/1FF28AD11720F100A0776738CF53B354 , https://www.bradshawfoundation.com/books/shamans_of_prehistory.php
- Replacement (an attribution, not a hedge): "The archaeologist David Lewis-Williams read the caves this way: the dark chambers were where a healer went to meet the spirits, and the animals on the walls are the spirits he met on the journey." Alternative: cut and end the paragraph at "Something like this is found among foragers on every continent."
- Note: "Something like this is found among foragers on every continent" is general and fine.

### 19. Synchrony (Wiltermuth and Heath 2009): CONFIRMED
- Psychological Science 20(1): 1-5. Three experiments. Walking in step, singing and moving in time raised cooperation in later economic exercises, including ones requiring personal sacrifice. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19152536/
- Note: "for a few minutes" and "share or cheat" are loose but fair. The games were a numbers/public-goods style game, not strictly "share or cheat".

### 20. Nineteenth-century communes: PARTLY
- Sosis 2000 (Cross-Cultural Research 34: 70-87): religious communes lasted longer. Sosis and Bressler 2003 (37: 211-239): 83 communes (30 religious, 53 secular). Among religious communes, more costly demands meant longer life. Among secular ones there was no such effect. https://www.ovid.com/journals/ccres/pdf/10.1177/1069397103037002003~cooperation-and-commune-longevity-a-test-of-the-costly , https://www.patheos.com/blogs/epiphenom/2009/09/why-religious-communes-succeed-and.html
- "Hundreds of small communities were founded": the 2000 paper drew on about 200 communes (secondary source). The 2003 sample was 83. "Hundreds" is stretching it.
- Replacement: "In the United States in the nineteenth century, dozens of small communities, in the studies of them, were founded by people who wanted to live together and share everything." Better and plain: "In the United States in the nineteenth century, more than eighty small communities that have been studied closely were founded by people who wanted to live together and share everything. Thirty were religious and the rest were not. The religious ones lasted far longer, and those that demanded the most of their members, in fasting, plain dress and giving up property, lasted longest of all. Among the secular ones, strict rules made no difference."
- "Plain dress, fasting, giving up property" are plausible examples of the costly requirements; I did not confirm the specific list.

### 21. Hxaro: CONFIRMED
- Wiessner: nearly all families had at least one partner 150 to 200 km away; ostrich-eggshell beads are the most valued gifts; 93% of extended visits were to hxaro partners; it works as risk buffering. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7104358 , https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/ostrich-beads-lesotho-social-networks-180974368/
- "Some of them in camps two hundred kilometres away" is right. Arrows: not found in the pages I read but standard in the literature.

### 22. Shells and stone far inland: CONFIRMED
- Rochereil (Dordogne) shells include Mediterranean species; the Mediterranean was about 350 km away, the Atlantic about 200 km. Cantabrian Magdalenian sites hold flint from the western Pyrenees and Sud-Aquitaine. https://archaeo.peercommunityin.org/articles/rec?id=237 , https://peercommunityjournal.org/articles/10.24072/pcjournal.185/
- "Moved from hand to hand along chains of friends": inference. Wiessner herself warns that a distant bead does not mean face-to-face contact, which supports a chain of exchange, not a single walk. The sentence "exactly as beads still move in the Kalahari" is an analogy; fine.

### 23. Seasonal aggregation: NOT FOUND (as a documented fact)
- Sources only offer a hypothesis that Dolni Vestonice and Pavlov were gathering places, by analogy with the Ju/'hoansi. https://www.theposthole.org/node/507 (not peer-reviewed). No source for "the salmon ran" or "hundreds of people could eat for weeks".
- Replacement: "Among the Ju/'hoansi, bands still gather in the dry season at the big waterholes, and the great ice-age sites of central Europe, such as Dolni Vestonice, are the kind of place where such gatherings happened. For most of the year a person lived among a few dozen familiar faces. For those few weeks they belonged to something far larger." If that is too strong for the author, cut the paragraph and go straight to the Venus figures.

### 24. Venus figurines: PARTLY
- Counts: "over 200" figurines; the range is the Pyrenees to Lake Baikal (Mal'ta, Buret'). The 7,000 km span is plausible; I did not find it stated. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_figurine
- Faces and feet: most lack both, but not all. The Brassempouy head has a face with a drawn brow and nose. Some have tapered legs ending without feet. So "no feet and no faces" is true of the famous ones (Willendorf, Lespugue).
- Replacement: "Across ice-age Europe, people carved small figures of women with heavy breasts and hips, most with no feet and no faces. More than two hundred have been found, from southwestern France to Lake Baikal in Siberia, some seven thousand kilometres apart."

### 25. Chimpanzees and strangers: CONFIRMED
- Wilson and Wrangham 2003; Wilson et al. 2014 (Nature, 152 lethal attacks). Chimpanzees kill or attack isolated strangers from other communities. https://www.doi.org/10.1038/nature13727 , https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/09/140917131816.htm
- "Flee or attack" is right. "Because it has no way of knowing whether the stranger is safe" is interpretation and could be cut: "A chimpanzee that meets a strange chimpanzee from another troop will flee or attack."

### 26. Göbekli Tepe core facts: PARTLY
- Confirmed: name means "Potbelly Hill"; T-shaped pillars; carved foxes, boars, snakes, vultures, scorpions, cranes (Wikipedia lists foxes, boars, gazelle, snakes, vultures, lions); arms and hands carved on pillars; earliest layers about 9600 BCE (about 11,600 years ago); wild fauna only, gazelle dominant, plus aurochs; no domestic animals or plants; bedrock quarries with flint tools; evidence of feasting. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6bekli_Tepe , https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/tag/feasting/
- 1994: Schmidt visited in 1994, found the 1963 survey notes, and began excavating in 1995. "Walked over it ... and began to dig" is compressed. Optional: "the next year began to dig."
- Weight: DAI says the central pillars of Enclosure D are 5.5 m and about 8 to 10 tons each. "More than ten tons" overstates. https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/?p=347
- Bones: sources say well over 100,000 animal bones, so "tens of thousands" understates. Fine either way.
- Transport: DAI says in the worst case about 500 m, in the best less than 100 m. Wikipedia says quarries about 100 m from the hilltop. "Several hundred metres" is too far.
- Workforce: Schmidt estimated hundreds of people. Other researchers argue 7 to 14 people could move one pillar and 12 to 24 could build the structures in under four months (Wikipedia). "That takes hundreds of people" is too strong.
- Stonehenge: about 5,000 years old, so "more than twice as old" is true by arithmetic (no Göbekli page mentions it). Pyramids: Giza about 4,500 years old, so the gap is about 7,000 years. True by arithmetic.
- Replacement: "The largest are five and a half metres high and weigh about ten tons, as much as two elephants."
- Replacement: "Every pillar was cut out of the bedrock with tools of flint, levered loose, and hauled a hundred metres or more by ropes and muscle."
- Replacement: "That takes a large team working together. They had to come together from camps scattered over a wide country. They had to be fed for weeks, which meant great hunts and great feasts, and the site is full of the remains of both."
- The next sentences ("Hundreds of people who mostly did not know one another quarried, dragged and carved...") need the same fix: "Dozens of people who mostly did not know one another quarried, dragged and carved because they believed in the same thing strongly enough to give months of their lives to it." Dozens is the safe floor and still makes the point.
- Later "Hundreds of people cannot gather on one ridge season after season, and stay for weeks, without stripping the country of game." Change to: "A crowd cannot gather on one ridge season after season, and stay for weeks, without stripping the country of game."

### 27. "No farms" / domestic use: PARTLY
- DAI: no animal husbandry and no domesticated plants at Göbekli Tepe. So "no farms" is true. https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/tag/feasting/
- But: 7,268 grinding tools for wild cereals (Dietrich et al. 2019, PLoS ONE) and rock-cut cisterns (Wikipedia: at least 150 cubic metres). Lee Clare reports middens, hearths and domestic debris from beginning to end. Dietrich and Notroff say no typical domestic architecture is demonstrated yet. https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Gobekli_Tepe
- The chapter says "not a storehouse or a fort. It was a place for whatever the foxes, the vultures and the stone giants meant." "Not a storehouse" is safe because no storage buildings are documented; "not a fort" fine. No "not a house" sentence appears in the chapter text I read. There is also the line "the people who built them were hunters": true.
- Replacement: "The people who built them were hunters. They had no farms, though they ground wild wheat and barley on stones." (Adds a true detail that also sets up the einkorn paragraph.)
- Replacement for the shrine sentence: "What they built, at such cost, was a place for whatever the foxes, the vultures and the stone giants meant."

### 28. Einkorn and Karacadag: PARTLY
- Heun et al., Science 278: 1312-1314 (1997): DNA fingerprinting of 1,362 wild lines and domesticated einkorn, closest match on the Karacadag slopes. https://www.sciencenorway.no/agriculture--fisheries-archaeology-forskningno/on-the-track-of-the-worlds-first-farmer/1448265
- Distance: Science Norway and several tour pages say about 30 km. That does not match the map. My own rough check from the coordinates I remember (Göbekli about 37.22 N, 38.92 E; Karacadag summit about 37.67 N, 39.83 E) gives roughly 90 km. I did not check this with a mapping tool, so treat it as a flag and have the number checked. "Less than a hundred kilometres" is safe either way.
- Scope: Heun tested einkorn only, not "farmed wheat from all over the world". Emmer later pointed to the same region.
- Replacement: "And it stands in a telling place. A little under a hundred kilometres away is a range of hills where wild wheat still grows. When scientists compared the DNA of farmed einkorn wheat with wild einkorn from across the Middle East, the closest match was the wild einkorn of those hills."

### 29. "As much as two elephants": CONFIRMED
- African bulls are about 5 to 7 tons; Asian bulls about 3 to 5. Ten tons is about two African bulls or two to three Asian ones. https://www.discoverwildlife.com/news/animal-facts/mammals/facts-about-elephants (secondary pages; quality mixed).
- Note: if the pillar weight is changed to "about ten tons", "as much as two elephants" still works.

## Other issues found while reading

- Para 1: "a band of mammoth hunters" and "winters colder than any in Europe today": reasonable. Sungir was the Last Glacial, with temperatures several degrees below today's, but "colder than any in Europe today" is plausible for Russia's plain. Not searched.
- "Seals live in salt water and are always thirsty": seals mostly do not need to drink (they get water from food). The chapter presents this as the Inuit explanation, which is accurate to the belief. Keep as belief, not fact. The wording is already "because seals live in salt water and are always thirsty". Consider "because, the Inuit said, seals live in salt water and are always thirsty."
- "Among peoples who had been out of touch with one another for tens of thousands of years": reasonable; not searched.
- "Neanderthals also buried their dead, at least sometimes": fine; the claim is still debated, but "at least sometimes" is the standard form.
- Cave-mouth habitation and "nobody could have stood back to admire": inference; leave.
- "Language had given people the power to talk about things that are not there": opinion; fine.
