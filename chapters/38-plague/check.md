1. **Doubtful central claim — high confidence that the evidence is disputed.**  
   > “A century and a half after the plague, the land tax of Egypt brought in a fraction of what it had before, and the wages of an ordinary worker in Cairo bought less than they had in 1340.”

   The wage conclusion depends on a disputed reconstruction. Pamuk and Shatzmiller find substantial post-plague wage gains followed by some decline; their account does not support presenting universal impoverishment as settled. Falling aggregate tax revenue also does not establish falling income per survivor. Distinguish agricultural contraction, government revenue and individual living standards. Sources: [Pamuk and Shatzmiller](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022050714000072); [Christ’s reassessment of Egyptian decline](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/282940395/Christ_Economic_Decline.pdf).

2. **Unsupported geographical extrapolation — high confidence.**  
   > “It killed close to half the people between Central Asia and the Atlantic in about five years, and that made the survivors scarce.”

   Estimates approaching half are commonly proposed for Europe; they are not an established aggregate for this entire Eurasian territory. Central Asian evidence establishes an outbreak in 1338–39, not continent-wide mortality, and that date also falls outside the implied five-year European chronology. Regional mortality varied greatly. Confine the estimate to a defensible region and period. Sources: [Spyrou and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04800-3); [regional mortality research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8913360/).

3. **Doubtful premise underlying the comparison — high confidence.**  
   > “The share of the dead was much the same.”

   Comparable English and Egyptian mortality is an assumption, not a secure control for this comparison. Pamuk and Shatzmiller report estimates of roughly one-quarter to one-third for Egypt’s initial outbreak, with further losses from recurrences; the chapter assigns England one-half immediately. Separate first-wave mortality from cumulative depopulation. Source: [Pamuk and Shatzmiller](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022050714000072).

4. **Internal inconsistency and missing explanation.**  
   > “Where working people were free to walk away to a better offer, as in England, scarcity made them richer and freer.”  
   > “Where they were not, as in Egypt, it made the whole country poorer.”

   Later, English serfs cannot legally leave, while Egyptian villagers flee to Cairo. The intended distinction seems to concern the ability to obtain better terms from competing landlords, rather than the ability to move. Explain why Egyptian flight did not produce comparable bargaining power. As written, the opening makes freedom both the cause and the consequence of the English outcome.

5. **Wrong tax description — high confidence.**  
   > “In 1380, to pay for a war with France, the government demanded a shilling from every adult in the country, the same from a ploughman as from a lord.”

   The assessment targeted an average of twelve pence per liable person, with richer taxpayers supposed to subsidise poorer ones. Individual payments could range from four pence to twenty shillings; paupers were exempt, and liability began at fifteen. It was deeply burdensome without being the identical individual charge described here. Source: [research on the surviving 1381 returns](https://repository.canterbury.ac.uk/download/a38343468ec9bcf25ea9f65955ee90ba27b923ac80c2fc235ff55e3912df0ebb/401175/PollTax_ArchCant131.pdf).

6. **Wrong construction timeline — high confidence.**  
   > “The work stopped that summer, with one great wall and a few arches standing, and it was not taken up again.”

   Siena’s cathedral authority dates partial construction of the extension through 1357. The plague disrupted the project, but structural defects and subsequent decisions also mattered: this was not simply a building permanently frozen in summer 1348. That distinction matters because the unfinished cathedral supplies the chapter’s closing image. Source: [Opera del Duomo’s construction history](https://operaduomo.siena.it/en/panorama-and-new-cathedral/).

7. **Disputed transmission mechanism presented as established — high confidence about the uncertainty.**  
   > “The disease moved slowly while it travelled by land, at the pace of rodents and carts of grain.”

   This turns one possible transmission account into a known itinerary and speed. The relative contributions of rat fleas, human ectoparasites and pneumonic transmission remain disputed. Keep the established connection with transport and trade without claiming that overland movement followed rodents’ pace. Sources: [Dean and colleagues’ modelling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5819418/), and [the published critique](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6112721/).

8. **Overstatement of legislative failure — high confidence that qualification is needed.**  
   > “The law failed, and the reason is that the people breaking it included the lords themselves.”

   It failed to preserve pre-plague conditions permanently, but that is different from having no effect. Research describes substantial enforcement and possible restraint on wage increases. The chapter already mentions thousands of fines, then leaves their consequences unexplained. Specify the law’s eventual failure rather than implying immediate impotence. Sources: [research on implementation, 1349–1400](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ehr.13355); [economic-history overview](https://eh.net/encyclopedia/the-economic-impact-of-the-black-death/).

9. **False absolute about the revolt’s consequences — high confidence; also a style breach.**  
   > “The rising had failed completely, and it made no difference.”

   Its emancipation demands failed, but it had consequences, including the abandonment of the medieval government’s poll-tax experiment. Even if demographic pressure explains most of serfdom’s decline, “made no difference” is indefensible. Source: [*The Transformation of Medieval England*](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781317872603_A23914038/preview-9781317872603_A23914038.pdf).

10. **Misleading recurrence period — high confidence.**  
    > “It came back in 1361, when it killed so many children that people called it the children's plague, and it then returned to some part of Europe and the Middle East every ten or fifteen years for three centuries.”

    The second pandemic continued into the nineteenth century, including in Egypt and Ottoman territories. The ten-to-fifteen-year rhythm also needs a specified locality: outbreaks across this enormous area did not share one timetable. Source: [research on the Cairo outbreak of 1801 and regional recurrence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5493801/).

11. **False absolute about pre-plague mobility — high confidence; also a style breach.**  
    > “A serf who ran away had nowhere to go, since every village had more labourers than it needed.”

    Serfs migrated before the Black Death, including between rural settlements and into towns. Restrictions and enforcement varied. Labour abundance weakened bargaining power; it did not eliminate destinations. Source: [research on servile migration before and after the plague](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/continuity-and-change/article/servile-migration-and-seigniorial-reaction-in-england-the-serfs-of-great-waltham-and-high-easter-essex-c-13361361/FEE7266397005ACF06410A59D9CB33AD).

12. **Misleading medical wording — high confidence.**  
    > “If the infection reached the lungs, the victim coughed blood and could pass it on with a breath, and in that form almost nobody survived.”

    Pneumonic plague spreads through infectious respiratory droplets, typically during close contact with a coughing patient. “With a breath” suggests effortless transmission through ordinary breathing; bloody sputum is also possible rather than universal. The untreated lethality is defensible. Source: [CDC explanation of transmission](https://www.cdc.gov/plague/causes/index.html).

13. **Unsupported superlative — factual doubt and style breach.**  
    > “It was the most productive farming in the world, and it needed constant work.”

    “Most productive” needs a measure: output per acre, per worker or per year can produce different rankings. Nothing here establishes a worldwide comparison. The argument needs only the fertility of Nile agriculture and its maintenance requirements. **High confidence that the superlative is unsupported as written; no confidence in a single replacement world ranking.** [Borsch’s irrigation study](https://www.scholarworks.wmich.edu/tmg/vol1/iss1/7/) supports the maintenance argument.

14. **A general reader lacks the missing step.**  
    > “A basin could not be kept up by half a village.”

    This asserts the mechanism that the England–Egypt comparison needs to explain. Why could survivors not maintain fewer basins, pool labour or concentrate cultivation? Explain which shared canals and embankments still required maintenance even when fewer fields were planted. Otherwise “a single machine” remains a metaphor standing in for the causal explanation.

15. **Signpost sentence prohibited by `style.md`.**  
    > “The second difference was the landlords.”

    This announces the next analytical category rather than stating the difference. The following sentence can introduce the Mamluk rulers directly. This is a specific signpost issue, not an objection to the narrator taking a position.

16. **Unexplained architectural term at the ending.**  
    > “It rises on one side of an open square where the nave was meant to be, with tall empty windows that frame the sky, and tourists climb it for the view.”

    “Nave” is unexplained. Readers also need to know that the project would have incorporated the existing cathedral into a much larger church; otherwise they may picture Siena as having only an unfinished cathedral. Briefly establish that arrangement when the building first appears.
