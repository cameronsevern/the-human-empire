# Chapter 37 — How did the Mongols conquer so much?

**Line under the title:** Mongolia, China, Persia and Europe, from 1162 to 1294.
**Length:** 4,400 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The Mongols were few, perhaps a million people, and they conquered the largest land empire there has ever been. They could do it because every man was a mounted archer who fed himself from his herd, so the whole army moved at the speed of a horse and needed no supply wagons. One leader broke up the clans, sorted the men into tens, hundreds and thousands, and promoted on merit. What horsemen could not do was take a walled city, so the Mongols collected engineers from each people they beat and used them against the next. The same conquests that emptied cities tied Asia and Europe together more closely than ever before: one authority ran the roads from Korea to the Black Sea, and goldsmiths, gunpowder, cobalt and monks travelled along them.

## The story

1. **Opening: a goldsmith from Paris.** Answer first. A friar from Flanders reaches the Mongol capital in 1254 and finds a Parisian who has built the khan a silver tree that pours four drinks.
2. **A country of grass.** What life on the steppe trained people for: the horse, the bow, the herd as a walking larder. Why the steppe was usually divided.
3. **The boy who was left behind.** Temüjin's childhood, his rise, and what he changed: tens and thousands, clans broken up, the man who shot his horse.
4. **Fifteen wet years.** The tree rings. The walls of north China, the flood that went the wrong way, and the hiring of engineers.
5. **The caravan at Otrar.** The murdered merchants, the desert crossing, Bukhara. What a city knew when it chose to open its gates or not. Craftsmen marched away.
6. **Where the grass ran out.** The sons and grandsons: Hungary, Baghdad, the defeat in Palestine, and the Persian catapults that broke south China.
7. **Safe by day and by night.** The post stations and the tablet. Merchants, a monk from Beijing in Bordeaux, blue paint from Persia on Chinese jars.
8. **Ending: the road stays open.**
