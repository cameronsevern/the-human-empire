## Factual flags

1. **Major — the added nuclear-disarmament explanation misrepresents the bargain.**

   > “Since the weapons could not be used, giving them away seemed to cost little.”  
   > “What it received in return was a promise on paper.”

   Ukraine’s negotiations show that it attached substantial security, economic and bargaining value to the weapons despite lacking immediate launch control. It demanded—and received—compensation for their highly enriched uranium and American dismantlement assistance, alongside security assurances. The new wording makes a difficult, materially compensated negotiation sound like surrendering a nearly worthless asset for signatures alone. [Account by US negotiator Steven Pifer](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-trilateral-process-the-united-states-ukraine-russia-and-nuclear-weapons/)

2. **Minor — the new Maidan transition starts the bloodshed too late.**

   > “Then it turned to bloodshed.”

   Immediately before the third-week-of-February account, this implies that the preceding winter protest had not already involved killings. Protesters were killed in January; February brought the largest escalation. This is a new chronological implication, separate from the earlier check’s corrected sniper-death total. [UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission](https://ukraine.ohchr.org/en/six-years-after-killings-ukraine-justice-remains-elusive)

3. **Minor — occupation abuses were known before the withdrawal.**

   > “Only when they had gone could anyone see what the occupation had meant.”

   Residents witnessed abuses during the occupation, and accounts reached outsiders before Russian forces withdrew. Human Rights Watch published a detailed Bucha account on **30 March 2022**, including civilian deaths and residents’ experiences. Withdrawal enabled wider access and fuller documentation; it did not make the abuses visible for the first time. [Human Rights Watch’s contemporary report](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/30/devastation-and-loss-bucha-ukraine)

4. **Major — countries outside the West did not collectively remain distant.**

   > “The rest of the world kept its distance.”

   By the chapter’s end date, North Korea had supplied troops fighting alongside Russia, as well as weapons and ammunition; Iran had supplied drones. These are direct counterexamples to the new generalisation. The following, more specific sentence about declining sanctions does not establish military or political distance. [NATO’s October 2024 confirmation of North Korean deployments](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2024/10/28/doorstep-statement?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block), [2025 NATO Parliamentary Assembly report](https://www.nato-pa.int/document/2025-crink-report-azubalis-020-pcnp)
