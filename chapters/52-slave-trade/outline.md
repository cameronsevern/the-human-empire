# Chapter 52 — Why were Africans enslaved in the Americas?

**Line under the title:** The Atlantic coast of Africa, from 1482 to about 1800.
**Length:** 3,600 to 4,400 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Twelve and a half million Africans were shipped across the Atlantic because the plantations of the Americas ate labour, the native peoples of the Americas were dying of imported disease, Europeans would not go and died when they did, and Africans could be bought. They could be bought because slavery and the sale of war captives were old in Africa, and because African kingdoms were strong. Europeans could not conquer the coast. Disease killed them, and the kings set the terms, so for three centuries the trade was a partnership between European shippers and African rulers and merchants. Some rulers refused, some grew rich, and all of them faced the same trap: a neighbour who sold captives could buy guns. The trade made war and kidnapping pay over a third of a continent.

## The story

1. **Opening: a king's letter.** Answer first. Afonso of Kongo writes to the king of Portugal in 1526 that traders are carrying off his people.
2. **Who would cut the cane.** The labour problem of the Americas: the dying islanders, the servants who would not come, and the disease that Africans survived.
3. **The white man's grave.** Why Europeans stayed on the beach for three hundred years: fever, and kings who charged rent. The castle at Elmina.
4. **Wealth in people.** Slavery inside Africa and how a person became a captive. What the ships paid: Indian cloth, iron, shells from the Indian Ocean, guns.
5. **The kings' choice.** Benin, which closed the trade in men; Dahomey, which lived by it; the queen who fought the Portuguese for forty years. The trap.
6. **A boy in a field.** One captive's journey from the interior to the castle to the ship. The plan of a slave ship.
7. **The count.** What the records of thirty-six thousand voyages show: how many, from where, to where.
8. **Ending: what it did.** A continent that stopped growing. Kongo's civil wars, and its soldiers in the cane fields.
