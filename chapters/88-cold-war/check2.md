## Changed facts

No existing number, name, date, or quotation was removed or replaced. However, several additions introduce factual or causal claims. For wholly new sentences, there is no old counterpart to quote.

1. **Exclusive dependence on aircraft**
   - **Old:** “They chose to supply a whole city by air, which nobody had done before.”
   - **New:** “Instead, they chose to supply a whole city by air, which nobody had done before.” Followed by: “A city needs food every day, and coal to cook with and to keep warm, and all of it now had to come in by aircraft.”
   - The addition turns the airlift’s importance into a claim that there were no other supplies.

2. **Winter operations**
   - **Old:** No counterpart.
   - **New:** “The flights went on through the winter.”
   - A new, accurate chronological statement.

3. **Timing of containment**
   - **Old:** “That advice became American policy for the next forty years.”
   - **New:** “That advice became American policy for the next forty years, and it was put into practice within about a year.”
   - Adds an interval between the telegram and implementation, consistent with the following March 1947 example.

4. **Reciprocal escalation**
   - **Old:** “Both were acting from fear, and each took the other's precautions as proof of its hostile intentions.”
   - **New:** “Both were acting from fear.” Followed by: “As a result, each took the other's precautions as proof of its hostile intentions, and answered them with precautions of its own, which the other read in the same way.”
   - Adds a causal feedback loop. This is a historical interpretation, not an error merely because it is confidently stated.

5. **NATO’s collective-defence provision**
   - **Old:** No counterpart.
   - **New:** “In plain terms, an attack on any one of them would be treated as an attack on them all.”
   - Adds an accurate general summary of Article 5. [North Atlantic Treaty](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/1949/04/04/the-north-atlantic-treaty)

6. **The Soviet bomb and domestic repression**
   - **Old:** “In the United States, the discovery that Soviet spies had stolen the secret of the bomb set off a hunt for traitors.”
   - **New:** The same sentence now follows “The Soviet bomb had a second consequence.”
   - **Old:** No counterpart.
   - **New:** “The hunt spread far beyond the people he named.”
   - Together these additions strengthen the claim that the wider investigations followed the Soviet bomb and McCarthy’s accusations.

7. **Khrushchev’s explanation of Stalinism**
   - **Old:** “Khrushchev wanted to save the system by cleansing it.”
   - **New:** The same sentence, followed by: “The fault, in his telling, lay with one man, and once the man was condemned the party could carry on.”
   - Adds an interpretation of the speech’s political purpose. I would not flag that interpretation merely for taking a position.

8. **Hungary’s precedence**
   - **Old:** “In October 1956 students in Budapest marched to demand free elections and the withdrawal of Soviet troops.”
   - **New:** The same sentence now follows “Hungary was the first to act on it.”
   - Introduces a false claim of precedence.

9. **Hungary’s apparent victory and the reason for intervention**
   - **Old:** “A reforming communist became prime minister, and announced that Hungary would hold free elections and leave the Soviet alliance.”
   - **New:** The same sentence, followed by: “For a few days it looked as though the rising had won. However, that last promise went further than Moscow would allow.”
   - **Old:** “On 4 November a thousand Soviet tanks entered the city.”
   - **New:** The same sentence now follows “Moscow's answer was overwhelming.”
   - Adds a defensible description of temporary optimism, but also a misleading causal sequence.

10. **The exhibition’s intended message**
    - **Old:** “In July 1959 the American vice-president opened an exhibition in Moscow, which included a full-sized model of an ordinary American house.”
    - **New:** “In July 1959 the argument was carried into a kitchen. The American vice-president opened an exhibition in Moscow, which included a full-sized model of an ordinary American house. The choice made a point: the Americans were showing off not rockets but the comforts of an ordinary home.”
    - Adds an interpretation of the display’s purpose.

11. **Checkpoint Charlie intentions**
    - **Old:** No counterpart.
    - **New:** “Neither side wanted to be the first to give way, and neither wanted to be the first to fire.”
    - Adds a claim about the participants’ intentions, rather than merely connecting the existing events.

Other additions principally restate existing claims or supply imagery. For example, Schumann’s photograph making him famous was already stated in its caption; the inward-facing Wall defences were already described in the body.

## Factual flags

1. **Major — newly introduced false precedence.**
   > “Hungary was the first to act on it.”

   Poland’s unrest preceded Hungary’s: the Poznań uprising occurred in June 1956, followed by the Polish October. The Budapest demonstration began partly in solidarity with Poland. Hungary therefore was not the first eastern European country to challenge the existing order after Khrushchev’s denunciation. [Institute of National Remembrance](https://eng.ipn.gov.pl/en/news/2514%2CThe-63rd-Anniversary-of-the-Hungarian-Uprising-1956-Poznan-23-October-2019.pdf)

2. **Major — newly introduced causal sequence reverses the relevant chronology.**
   > “However, that last promise went further than Moscow would allow.”
   >
   > “Moscow's answer was overwhelming.”

   Following the announcement of withdrawal from the Soviet alliance, these sentences make the invasion appear to be Moscow’s response to that announcement. The Soviet leadership decided on renewed intervention on **31 October**; Nagy announced withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact on **1 November**, after fresh Soviet troop movements had become apparent. Withdrawal cannot explain the earlier decision to invade. [Soviet Presidium working notes](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB14/doc9.htm), [Wilson Center historical study](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/publication/ACFB4E.pdf)

3. **Minor — newly introduced absolute about supplies.**
   > “A city needs food every day, and coal to cook with and to keep warm, and all of it now had to come in by aircraft.”

   “All” is incorrect. West Berliners also obtained food and heating coal from the eastern sector, and businesses received supplies through eastern trade. The airlift sustained the western sectors without making every other source disappear. [US Army history, *The City Becomes a Symbol*](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo88399/pdf/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo88399.pdf)

4. **Major — existing chronology made more misleading by the new transitions.**
   > “The Soviet bomb had a second consequence.”
   >
   > “The hunt spread far beyond the people he named.”

   These frame the wider purge as an outgrowth of the August 1949 Soviet test and McCarthy’s 1950 accusations. Federal loyalty investigations were already institutionalised under Truman’s **March 1947** executive order. The bomb and McCarthy intensified an existing campaign; they did not initiate its spread into government employment. [Truman Library, loyalty-program history](https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/education/presidential-inquiries/trumans-loyalty-program)

5. **Major — existing false symmetry in nuclear operations.**
   > “By the 1960s the two countries had more than thirty thousand nuclear warheads between them, and bombers kept in the air around the clock so that they could not be caught on the ground.”

   The shared grammatical subject attributes continuous airborne bomber alert to both countries. That was an American practice; Soviet Long Range Aviation did not adopt the equivalent posture. The combined warhead total and the American airborne-alert practice need separate subjects. [*Assessing Nuclear Competitions in the 21st Century*, hosted by the US Department of Defense](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/Litigation_Release/Litigation%20Release%20-%20Assessing%20Nuclear%20Competitions%20in%20the%2021st%20Century%202010.pdf)

6. **Minor — existing refugee total assigned to the wrong destination.**
   > “About two and a half thousand Hungarians were killed, and two hundred thousand fled across the border to Austria.”

   Approximately **200,000 fled Hungary altogether**: about **180,000 to Austria and 20,000 to Yugoslavia**. The sentence assigns the entire exodus to Austria. [UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/asia/node/11077)

7. **Minor — existing misstatement of citizenship.**
   > “From West Berlin a person could fly to West Germany and be given citizenship on arrival.”

   East Germans were generally already recognised by the Federal Republic as German citizens. Arrival did not create their citizenship. Recognition and documentation of existing citizenship should be distinguished from granting it. [German Federal Agency for Civic Education](https://www.bpb.de/themen/migration-integration/regionalprofile/deutschland/299944/vom-mauerbau-zum-mauerfall-28-jahre-deutsch-deutscher-migrationsgeschichte/)

These do not repeat the earlier check’s corrected points.

## Clarity flags

- **Unnecessary announcement:** “Its length mattered less than its argument.” This announces the argument immediately before giving it. It is close to the style sheet’s prohibited signposting.

- **Repeated conclusion:** “In other words, there was no need for war and no hope of friendship. What was needed was patience.” The preceding sentence has already prescribed patient containment until internal change. The final sentence repeats “patiently” without advancing the explanation.

- **Repeated reasoning:** “The logic ran step by step: whoever struck first would still be destroyed by the answer, and so striking first gained nothing.” This largely repeats the immediately preceding sentence about surviving an initial strike and destroying the attacker. “The logic ran step by step” is also an unnecessary explanatory label.

- **Padding:** “People learned to live with this, as people learn to live with most things.” The added clause supplies neither orientation nor a concrete explanation.

- **Repeated framing:** “Underneath lay a simple question. Which system gave ordinary people a better life?” is followed shortly by “Underneath the jokes about washing machines lay the same argument as before: which way of life people would choose, if they were free to choose.” The repeated “Underneath …” construction and repeated question make the second passage feel like a recap.

- **Chronological ambiguity:** “Then, in 1957, it astonished the world by putting the first satellite into orbit. Khrushchev told a room of Western ambassadors that history was on his side. We will bury you, he said.” This sequence suggests the boast followed Sputnik. The remark was reported in **November 1956**, before Sputnik. The quotation itself is unchanged, but the new “Then” strengthens the misleading chronological reading. [Contemporary TIME report](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,867329,00.html)

- **Exception-breaking absolute:** “The test came in the one place where people could vote on the question with their feet.” Berlin was a crucial escape route, but “the one place” unnecessarily claims worldwide exclusivity.

- **Repeated emphasis:** “The loss was heavy, and it fell where it hurt most.” Later in the same paragraph: “At that rate, the state was bleeding to death.” The skilled-worker losses and the leaders’ warning of collapse already convey both points. The additions repeat the paragraph’s conclusion.

- **Repeated transition:** “A divided city, however cruel, could be lived with, and a nuclear war could not. The danger, however, had not passed.” Two adjacent uses of “however” are conspicuous.

- **Unclear backward time jump:** “On the third day a nineteen-year-old East German border guard…” follows a paragraph covering the Wall’s rebuilding “over the years.” “The third day” makes readers reconstruct that the narrative has returned to August 1961.

I found no hedging stacks, chapter self-mentions, or explicit cross-references.

## Overall

The revision improves paragraphing and orientation, but it is not entirely fact-neutral. The most consequential additions falsely give Hungary precedence and imply the wrong sequence behind Soviet intervention. Several other additions repeat explanations already made. No files were edited.
