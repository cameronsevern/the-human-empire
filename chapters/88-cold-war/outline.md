# Chapter 88 — What was the Cold War?

**Line under the title:** Europe, the United States and the Soviet Union, from 1945 to 1962.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The Cold War was a contest that lasted forty-five years between the two countries left standing in 1945, each of which believed that its own way of life was the future of mankind and that the other meant to destroy it. It began over the fate of the lands that the Soviet army had taken from Hitler. It stayed cold because both sides had weapons that made a direct war suicidal, so it was fought by every other means: an arms race, rival alliances, spies, propaganda, the policing of opinion at home, and wars in other people's countries. Its front line ran through the middle of one city. In 1961 one side had to build a wall there to stop its own people from leaving.

## The story

1. **Opening: a plane every ninety seconds.** Answer first. Berlin, 1948: a city fed from the air by the men who had bombed it.
2. **Two kinds of safety.** How the allies fell out: what Stalin wanted in eastern Europe and why; what Washington saw. Slices of salami.
3. **A long telegram.** The iron curtain speech; a diplomat's advice; the doctrine of 1947. Each side's picture of the other.
4. **Two Germanies.** The blockade ends; two states; two alliances.
5. **Duck and cover.** The Soviet bomb, the hydrogen bomb, and what it meant to live under them. Why neither could attack.
6. **A list of names.** Fear at home: a senator in America; trials and informers in the east.
7. **The secret speech.** Stalin's death, Khrushchev's speech, and Budapest in 1956. The unwritten rule.
8. **A kitchen.** The contest over who lived better.
9. **The wall.** Three million leave through Berlin. One night in August 1961. Tanks at a checkpoint.
10. **Ending: a bricklayer.**

## Left for later

China, Korea and Vietnam. The Cuban missile crisis. The space race. Latin America. Détente and the end of the Soviet Union.
