1. **Fact; high confidence.** “It was called cold because the two never fought each other directly.” Soviet pilots fought American pilots in Korea. The distinction is that the superpowers avoided an openly declared, full-scale war against each other. “Never” also breaks the rule against exception-breaking absolutes. [US Air Force Museum](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/196389/USAFmuseum/soviet-pilots-over-mig-alley/)

2. **Fact and chronology; high confidence.** “Their weapons had made that a form of suicide, and so they fought by every other means.” This makes mutual nuclear destruction explain the Cold War from its beginning. America alone possessed atomic weapons until 1949; secure retaliatory forces developed subsequently. The later paragraph explains second-strike deterrence well, but the opening projects it backwards. Chapter 83 already establishes the four-year American monopoly. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/articles/endingthearmsrace-start.htm)

3. **Fact; high confidence.** “To the Americans and the British, who had gone to war over the independence of Poland, this looked like one tyranny replacing another.” Britain went to war over Poland; America entered after Pearl Harbor and Germany’s declaration of war in December 1941. The relative clause incorrectly includes both countries. [FDR Presidential Library](https://www.fdrlibrary.org/wwii-facts)

4. **Fact; high confidence.** “In the last year of the war the Soviet army had driven the Germans out of Poland, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria and Czechoslovakia, and half of Germany itself, and it was still there.” The army did not remain throughout this list: Soviet and American troops withdrew from Czechoslovakia in 1945. This matters because the following account makes continuing military occupation sound like the uniform mechanism of communist takeover. [State Department records](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v01/d583)

5. **Fact; high confidence.** “Germany was where the two pressed hardest against each other, and it split along the line where the armies had met.” Germany’s division followed negotiated occupation-zone boundaries, not simply the final battlefield positions. Western forces withdrew from territory allocated to the Soviet zone, while western contingents entered Berlin. [US government account of Berlin’s status](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/public/2021-07/40-096-212551262-007-010-2021.pdf)

6. **Fact; high confidence.** “Over the twenty-eight years that it stood, at least a hundred and forty people died trying to cross it.” The documented minimum of 140 includes people who were not escaping and eight border guards. The corresponding research identifies 101 refugees killed or otherwise dying during escape attempts. Either retain 140 and describe deaths connected with the Wall, or use the escape-specific figure. [Berlin Wall Foundation’s breakdown](https://www.stiftung-berliner-mauer.de/sites/default/files/media/standorte/sbm/historischer%20ort/themen/2017_08_08_hertle_nooke_berliner_mauer_todesopfer.pdf)

7. **Fact; high confidence.** “In eleven months their aircraft made more than a quarter of a million flights and delivered over two million tons.” These are totals for the roughly fifteen-month airlift, which continued until September 1949. The blockade lasted approximately eleven months, ending in May. The sentence conflates the two periods. [US Air Force Museum](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Portals/7/documents/education/Global%20Reach%20Gallery%20Teacher%20Resource%20Guide2.pdf?ver=2017-01-11-081222-963)

8. **Fact; high confidence.** “Russia had been invaded from the west twice in thirty years, across the Polish plain, and the second invasion had killed twenty-seven million of its people.” Twenty-seven million is the estimated **Soviet Union-wide** death toll, including Ukrainians, Belarusians and other peoples, not Russia’s alone. The switch between Russia and the USSR obscures whose losses are being counted. [Mark Harrison, *Counting the Soviet Union’s War Dead*](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/98747/7/WRAP-Counting-Soviet-Union%27s-War-Harrison-2018.pdf)

9. **Doubtful attribution; high confidence that qualification is needed.** “A married couple from New York were sent to the electric chair for passing atomic secrets.” Julius Rosenberg’s espionage is established; Ethel’s personal participation remains disputed, and the evidence does not justify presenting both as demonstrated transmitters of atomic secrets. Both were convicted of conspiracy to commit espionage. Distinguish the conviction from what subsequent evidence establishes. [Federal Judicial Center’s trial history](https://www.fjc.gov/sites/default/files/trials/Rosenberg_Trial_1.pdf), [AP on the declassified evidence and historians’ disagreement](https://www.ap.org/news-highlights/spotlights/2024/declassified-memo-from-us-codebreaker-sheds-light-on-ethel-rosenbergs-cold-war-spy-case/)

10. **Fact and overgeneralisation; high confidence.** “American leaders had assumed that their monopoly of the atomic bomb would last for ten or fifteen years.” Estimates varied substantially. Some American intelligence estimates anticipated a Soviet bomb in 1949–50; others favoured 1953. A blanket ten-to-fifteen-year expectation exaggerates the consensus and therefore the surprise. [Historical study hosted by the Smithsonian](https://www.si.edu/media/NASM/NASM-Doc2-strategiccommandandcontrol---evolutionofocr.pdf)

11. **Fact and style; high confidence.** “In the Soviet Union and its satellites there was no press to expose an accuser and no court that could acquit.” The point about political show trials is defensible; the assertion about every court is not. Soviet courts could and did acquit defendants, although acquittal rates were low. Confine the claim to the predetermined political trials being described. This is precisely an absolute that exceptions break. [Cambridge University Press, “Accusatorial Bias in Russian Criminal Justice”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/sociology-of-justice-in-russia/accusatorial-bias-in-russian-criminal-justice/2E2BEE81BB8F000D173E49B3E9D7F534)

12. **Fact and style; high confidence.** “By 1948 every country behind the Soviet lines had a one-party government that took its orders from Moscow.” Soviet occupation and communist government were not interchangeable: Soviet troops occupied part of Austria, which retained a democratic government. “One-party” also collapses the distinction between communist monopoly of power and nominal coalition or satellite parties. Specify the eastern European states intended, rather than making Soviet military lines a universal political boundary. [State Department history of Austria](https://history.state.gov/countries/austria)

13. **Chronology; high confidence.** “The aid to rebuild western Europe followed three months later.” June 1947 brought Marshall’s proposal; the Marshall Plan became law in April 1948. Chapter 86 correctly describes the June event as a speech inviting a European plan. This is a chronological tension with that chapter, rather than a contradiction if “aid” is intended to mean its announcement. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/marshall-plan)

14. **Fact; high confidence.** “The border between the two Germanies had been fenced and mined since 1952.” The strengthened border regime dates from 1952, but systematic mine-laying began in 1961. The sentence gives both measures the earlier date. [German Federal Agency for Civic Education](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutschlandarchiv/295022/todesopfer-des-ddr-grenzregimes-eine-recherche/)

15. **Fact; high confidence.** “By 1961 about three million East Germans had gone that way, one in six of the population.” Roughly 2.7–3 million is the total exodus from East Germany between 1949 and 1961, not a count of people who all crossed Berlin and then flew out. Many used Berlin, especially after the inner-German border tightened; the total needs to be separated from that particular route. [German Federal Agency for Civic Education](https://www.bpb.de/kurz-knapp/hintergrund-aktuell/337560/1961-bau-der-berliner-mauer/)

16. **Doubtful claim; medium-high confidence.** “The weapons did have one effect that their makers had not planned.” Nuclear deterrence was not an unforeseen discovery. The scientists’ June 1945 Franck Report explicitly considered preventing attack through fear of overwhelming retaliation, although it doubted that an arms race would provide lasting security. Chapter 83 has already introduced these scientists’ anticipation of nuclear rivalry. Distinguish an outcome they did not desire from one they did not foresee. [Franck Report](https://sgp.fas.org/eprint/franck.html)

17. **Reader orientation and numerical precision; high confidence.** “The East German one grew until it had ninety thousand officers and nearly twice as many informers, in a country of sixteen million.” These are approximately **1989** figures, inserted between events in 1952 and 1953 without a date. A reader can easily assign them to the early Cold War. The usual estimates are about 91,000 full-time employees and 189,000 unofficial collaborators—slightly **more**, not “nearly,” twice as many. The three-number stack also conflicts with “one number per point.” [German Federal Agency for Civic Education](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutschlandarchiv/557173/das-ende-der-stasi-1989-1990/)

18. **Reader orientation.** “The Soviet Union later formed an alliance of its own from the states that it controlled.” Name and briefly identify the Warsaw Pact here, alongside NATO in the preceding sentence. Otherwise, when Hungary announces that it will “leave the Soviet alliance,” the reader has only an unnamed organisation introduced several sections earlier. Chapter 86 explains the western treaty but supplies neither alliance’s name.

19. **Explanation missing under style.md.** “The hydrogen bomb uses an atomic bomb merely as its trigger, to set off the same reaction that powers the Sun.” “The same reaction” does not explain the mechanism. Chapter 83 has taught the reader nuclear splitting; one short sentence explaining that the trigger creates conditions in which light nuclei fuse would give the reader the essential contrast.

20. **Reader comprehension.** “Their weapons had made that a form of suicide, and so they fought by every other means.” Those other means are never clearly defined at the outset, and proxy wars largely disappear from the account. A brief explanation that the powers armed and supported opposing sides in wars elsewhere would prevent “cold” from sounding like “largely bloodless.” This can be done without taking over the later chapters on Korea and Vietnam.
