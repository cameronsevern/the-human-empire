# Chapter 73 — Why was there a revolution in Russia?

**Line under the title:** Russia, from 1905 to 1924.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

There were two revolutions in 1917. The first, in February, was a collapse: three years of a losing war had broken the army, emptied the bread shops and used up the tsar's authority, and when soldiers refused to fire on hungry crowds the monarchy was gone in a week. The government that replaced it made two decisions that destroyed it, to go on fighting and to put off giving the peasants the land. The second revolution, in October, was a seizure of power by a small, disciplined party under Lenin that promised peace, land and bread, and that believed a revolution in backward Russia would set off the rest of Europe. It did not. The Bolsheviks kept power through a civil war, a terror and a famine, and built a state in which one party ruled in the name of the workers. For the next seventy years, every government on Earth had to decide what it thought of that.

## The story

1. **Opening: a queue for bread.** Answer first. Petrograd, 23 February 1917: women textile workers walk out.
2. **Father Tsar.** The empire in 1914: peasants and land, giant factories, a tsar who believed God had appointed him. A Sunday in 1905.
3. **One rifle among three.** The war: defeats, the tsar at the front, the empress and the holy man, the railways failing.
4. **Eight days.** February: the crowds grow, a regiment mutinies, the tsar signs in a railway carriage.
5. **Two governments.** The liberals and the council of workers and soldiers. Two fatal decisions.
6. **The sealed train.** Lenin: who he was, what his party was, how Germany sent him home, and three words.
7. **A quiet night.** October: the bridges, the telephone exchange, the palace. The decrees. The assembly that sat for one day.
8. **Reds and Whites.** Three years of civil war. Why the Reds won. The cellar at Yekaterinburg. Terror and famine.
9. **Ending: the glass case.** What had been built by 1924: one party, a secret police, a promise to the world's workers. Lenin's body.
