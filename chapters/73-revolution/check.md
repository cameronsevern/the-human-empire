1. **Factual error; high confidence.** “Trotsky sent the Red Army across the ice and shot the survivors.”

   Thousands of Kronstadt’s defenders escaped to Finland; others were imprisoned or sent to labour camps. Many were executed, but “the survivors” falsely implies all of them. Tukhachevsky commanded the assault; Trotsky bore political responsibility rather than personally directing the executions. [Library of Congress account](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gmd/gmd7/g7001/g7001s/2017588308.pdf).

2. **Misleading political account and reader gap; high confidence.** “They were liberals: lawyers, professors and landowners who wanted Russia to become a country like France or Britain.”

   This broadly describes the first cabinet, apart from Kerensky, but the narrative carries that description through to October. Socialist ministers joined in May, and the socialist Kerensky became prime minister in July. The Soviet’s moderate socialist leaders initially supported the government. Without this development, readers cannot understand why workers and soldiers turned against their existing socialist representatives. [Saint Petersburg Encyclopaedia, cabinet membership](https://encspb.ru/object/2855700547?dv=1000000000&lc=en).

3. **False exclusivity; high confidence.** “In all of Russia, one party was prepared to tell them to go ahead.”

   The Bolsheviks were not alone in supporting radical action on land and opposing the war. Left Socialist Revolutionaries were important allies, and the Bolsheviks adopted much of the Socialist Revolutionary land programme. This also breaks the rule against absolutes that an exception defeats. [Research on the Bolshevik–Left SR coalition](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-2281.12170).

4. **Contradiction in the reader’s account of events.** “It did not, and instead Russia fell into civil war.”

   “It” refers to Europe catching fire. Chapter 72 has already described German sailors raising red flags, revolt spreading through the cities and the emperor falling. The distinction needed is between **revolution occurring** and **a lasting allied communist government emerging**. The present wording appears to deny the revolution the reader has just encountered. “Instead” also misleadingly makes Russia’s civil war follow the failure of European revolution.

5. **Factual overgeneralisation; high confidence.** “They had been serfs until 1861, the property of their landlords.”

   “They” refers to the entire peasantry. Privately owned serfs constituted roughly half the peasants; millions were state or crown peasants with different legal positions. The 1861 emancipation concerned privately owned serfs, with separate reforms for other categories afterwards. [Cambridge history of emancipation](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805210/89197/excerpt/9780521089197_excerpt.pdf).

6. **Factual error in the stated period; high confidence.** “The workers lived in barracks and cellars, laboured eleven hours a day, and had no legal way to complain, since unions and strikes were forbidden.”

   The passage is set in 1914, but unions had acquired a restricted legal existence in 1906. Repression and wartime closures severely limited them; that is different from an uninterrupted prohibition and “no legal way to complain.” [Victoria Bonnell, *Roots of Rebellion*, chapters on legal labour organisation](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.5233051).

7. **Doubtful anecdote presented as established fact; high confidence that qualification or removal is needed.** “In December 1916 a group of noblemen, hoping to save the monarchy from itself, poisoned him, shot him and pushed him under the ice of the river.”

   The poisoning comes from the conspirators’ disputed accounts. Forensic evidence supports death by gunshot and does not establish poisoning. The secure account is that they shot Rasputin and disposed of his body in the river. [Forensic evaluation of Rasputin’s death](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11953134/).

8. **Factual error; high confidence.** “Its prime minister had slipped away in a car borrowed from the American embassy to look for loyal troops, and found none.”

   Kerensky did obtain troops under General Krasnov and attempted to retake Petrograd. They were insufficient and were defeated; he did not find none. A contemporary Military Revolutionary Committee report records fighting against the Kerensky–Krasnov forces on 30 October. [Russian historical document archive](https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/143432-soobschenie-shtaba-voenno-revolyutsionnogo-komiteta-o-boyah-protiv-kontrrevolyutsionnyh-voysk-kerenskogo-locale-nil-krasnova-30-oktyabrya-u-stantsii-aleksandrovskoy-31-oktyabrya-1917-g).

9. **Overgeneralisation affecting the explanation of victory; high confidence.** “They also won because the Whites made no secret of meaning to bring back the landlords.”

   White forces’ association with landlords certainly damaged their support, but their programmes varied. Wrangel explicitly introduced land reform intended to transfer agricultural land to cultivators through purchase. Distinguish peasants’ justified fears and actual landlord restorations from a uniform, openly declared White policy. [Wrangel’s programme in US diplomatic records](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1920v03/d714).

10. **Misleading paraphrase; high confidence.** “A few weeks earlier he had told an audience of students that people of his generation would probably not live to see the revolution.”

    Lenin said his generation **might** not see the **decisive battles** of the coming revolution, in a passage discussing European socialist revolution. “Probably,” the removal of “decisive battles,” and its placement before February make this a substantially stronger prediction about Russia than the source supports. [Lenin’s January 1917 lecture](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1917/jan/09.htm).

11. **Misconstructed historical scene; high confidence.** “In a siding at a provincial station, his own generals came to his carriage and told him that the army would not fight for him.”

    At Pskov, General Ruzsky pressed Nicholas to abdicate, while other senior commanders’ recommendations arrived by telegram. The account turns this into a collective visit and substitutes a categorical statement about the army for their advice. Nicholas’s own diary distinguishes Ruzsky’s visit, the commanders’ replies and the later arrival of two parliamentary delegates. [Published diary extract](https://www.fontanka.co.uk/twentysixthfebruarytofourthmarchbd3f19b8).

12. **Chronological error; high confidence.** “On the fourth day the tsar, from his headquarters five hundred kilometres away, telegraphed the order to end the disorders by force.”

    Counting 23 February as the first day, the fourth is 26 February. Nicholas’s telegram was dated **25 February**, ordering the disturbances stopped the following day. The subsequent shooting belongs on the 26th. [Dated telegram transcription](https://www.his2rie.dk/kildetekster/den-russiske-revolution/tekst-8/).

13. **Misleading explanation and reader gap; high confidence.** “In 1903 the Russian Marxists split over this, and Lenin's faction took the name Bolsheviks, which means ‘those of the majority’.”

    The immediate disputes concerned membership rules and control of party institutions, including the newspaper’s editorial board—not simply whether workers needed revolutionary leadership. Explain that “majority” referred to a particular congress alignment. The next sentence, “They were in fact a few thousand people,” otherwise compares a voting majority with the population of Russia. [Lenin’s account of the congress disputes](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1905/feb/00.htm).

14. **Overliteral treatment of the sealed train; high confidence.** “It put him and thirty companions into a railway carriage, sealed the doors so that they should have no contact with Germans on the way, and sent them across Germany to the Baltic coast.”

    The arrangement was negotiated through a Swiss intermediary and gave the travellers nominal extraterritorial status. German escorts occupied a separate portion, divided by a chalk line. “Sealed” should not become a picture of all doors physically sealed by Germany. [Swiss National Museum](https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2017/04/by-train-to-the-revolution/).

15. **Calendar error and break of the absolute rule; high confidence.** “Russia still used an old calendar that ran thirteen days behind the one used in the rest of Europe, so that 23 February in Petrograd was 8 March in Paris.”

    The conversion is correct; “the rest of Europe” is not. Greece, for example, retained the Julian civil calendar until 1923. The following sentence’s “everyone else’s reckoning” repeats the error. [Calendar adoption dates](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0200307180).

16. **Misleading completeness; high confidence.** “The grave was found sixty years later.”

    The main grave was located in 1979, but it contained nine victims. The separate remains of Alexei and one sister were found in 2007. Following a description of all eleven victims, “the grave” implies that everyone was recovered together. [Forensic historical review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32072440/).

17. **Reader gap.** “The Bolsheviks won a quarter of the votes.”

    Readers need to know who won instead: the Socialist Revolutionaries were the largest party, drawing heavily on peasant support. Without this, the assembly’s suppression loses its political meaning, and the earlier assertion that the land decree “made the villages his” seems inconsistent with the result. [Assembly election results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Russian_Constituent_Assembly_election).

18. **Reader gap.** “Lenin came out of hiding to announce to a congress of soviets that power had passed to them.”

    Nothing has explained why Lenin went into hiding. Nor has the narrative explained the change from one Petrograd Soviet to a national congress. Briefly establish the July crackdown and that councils elsewhere sent delegates to a national meeting.

19. **Reader gap and unexplained historical term.** “The crowds filled the main avenue, and the mounted police who were sent to clear it found that the Cossack horsemen who were supposed to help them were letting the marchers duck under their horses' bellies.”

    “Cossack” is unexplained, and the sentence introduces two mounted forces without clearly distinguishing police from soldiers. A few words identifying the Cossacks as cavalry troops would make the failure of repression understandable.

20. **Explicit signpost violation.** “One oddity needs explaining.”

    This announces an explanation rather than giving it. The calendar explanation can begin directly.

21. **Chapter self-reference.** “The dates here are the Russian ones, which is why the revolution of that October took place, by everyone else's reckoning, in November.”

    “The dates here” discusses the chapter’s presentation rather than the historical story. It also leaves readers uncertain about dates after Russia changed calendars in February 1918. Explain the historical naming convention directly.
