# Chapter 111 — How do we know the climate is changing?

**Line under the title:** The world, from 1856 to 2025.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. State what is measured as measured and what is forecast as forecast.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

We know because the physics was worked out more than a century and a half ago, because the gas in question has been measured every day since 1958 and has risen every year, because its chemical fingerprint shows that it comes from burning coal, oil and gas, and because the warming that the physics predicted has arrived on schedule. The carbon that plants laid down over hundreds of millions of years is being returned to the air in two centuries. The companies that sold it understood this in the 1970s and paid to cloud the question. Those who have burned the most are the rich; those who are hurt first are the poor. Thirty years of negotiation have not stopped emissions from rising, but they have bent the forecast, and the machines that could replace fossil fuel have become cheaper than anyone predicted.

6. **On schedule.** What has been measured: temperature, ice, sea, fire and flood.
7. **Whose carbon.** Who has emitted, in total and per head; who is hurt first. An island nine feet high.

9. **Paris.** The negotiations: Rio, Kyoto, Copenhagen, Paris. A schoolgirl with a sign.
10. **The price of sunlight.** What has changed: the forecast, and the cost of the alternatives. What has not.
11. **Ending: the line.**
