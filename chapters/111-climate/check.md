I checked against the chapter’s stated endpoint of **2025**. I found no direct contradictions with chapters 61, 97 or 110, and no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-mentions or numbered cross-references. The findings below are ordered by seriousness. No files were edited.

1. **Wrong displacement figure — high confidence.**  
   > “In 2022 a summer of heat killed more than sixty thousand people in Europe, and a monsoon of unheard-of violence drove thirty-three million Pakistanis from their homes.”

   Thirty-three million people were **affected**; about **eight million were displaced**. The sentence substantially exaggerates displacement. [UN account of the floods](https://un-dco.org/stories/pakistan-case-study-coordinated-and-comprehensive-response-2022-floods).

2. **Missing distinction between zero and net zero — high confidence.**  
   > “The carbon dioxide already in the air will stay there for hundreds of years, and the warming will not stop until emissions fall to nothing.”

   Stabilising CO₂-driven warming requires **net zero CO₂ emissions**: remaining emissions balanced by removals. It does not require every emission to cease. Other greenhouse gases also affect whether total warming stabilises. Additionally, some emitted CO₂ is absorbed relatively quickly; a fraction persists for millennia. [IPCC physical-science summary](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/).

3. **The central evidential argument skips a necessary step — high confidence.**  
   > “There are two ways of knowing that it did not.”

   The second “way”—the ice-core record—shows that the present concentration is exceptional, but does not by itself identify the source of the increase. The attribution rests on several observations together: emissions accounting, carbon isotopes, declining oxygen, and evidence that oceans are absorbing carbon overall. Calling the historical record an independent proof against volcanic or oceanic origins gives the reader an invalid inference. [NOAA explanation of attribution](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/climate-qa/how-do-we-know-build-carbon-dioxide-atmosphere-caused-humans).

4. **The isotope evidence is presented as too simple — high confidence.**  
   > “As fossil fuel is burned, the carbon dioxide in the air is being diluted with carbon of that dead kind, in just the proportion that the tonnage burned would lead one to expect.”

   Fossil carbon does dilute atmospheric carbon-14, but there is no simple correspondence with fuel tonnage alone: nuclear weapons testing greatly increased carbon-14, and exchanges with oceans and vegetation affect the record. The conclusion is sound; the claimed direct proportionality needs replacing with an accurate account of the fingerprint. [MIT explanation](https://climate.mit.edu/ask-mit/ive-heard-carbon-14-atmosphere-proves-fossil-fuels-are-cause-climate-change-how).

5. **Emissions categories silently change — high confidence.**  
   > “On that measure the United States comes first, with about a quarter of everything since 1850.”

   The quarter refers to cumulative **fossil-fuel and industrial CO₂**, excluding land-use change—not “everything.” The same scope problem affects the surrounding national shares. State what is counted before assigning responsibility. [Global Carbon Budget 2024](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/965/2025/).

   > “More than half of everything emitted since the Industrial Revolution began has been emitted since 1990.”

   This likewise needs restriction to fossil-fuel and industrial CO₂. Including land-use emissions changes the proportion substantially: the IPCC estimated 42% of cumulative anthropogenic CO₂ through 2019 had been emitted since 1990. That older figure should not replace a 2025 figure, but it demonstrates why the category matters. [IPCC emissions assessment](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-2/).

6. **A geological comparison lacks a defensible basis — medium confidence.**  
   > “Humanity now burns fossil fuel that took roughly a million years to form in each single year.”

   This conflates the age or maturation time of deposits with the rate at which fuel accumulated globally. I could not substantiate the million-to-one ratio. A relevant published calculation estimated that fossil fuels consumed in **1997** embodied ancient biomass equivalent to **over 400 years of present global plant productivity**—a different comparison entirely. Use a defined, sourced comparison or remove the number. [Dukes, *Burning Buried Sunshine*](https://agris.fao.org/search/es/records/65dfc1c24c5aef494fe5149b).

7. **The measurement record is not uninterrupted — high confidence.**  
   > “We know because the physics was worked out more than a century and a half ago, because the gas in question has been measured every day since 1958 and has risen every year, because its chemistry shows that the extra comes from burning coal, oil and gas, and because the warming that the physics predicted has arrived.”

   “Every day since 1958” is false as a description of the Mauna Loa record. There have been interruptions, including a funding interruption in 1964 and the 2022 volcanic eruption. Describe a sustained measurement programme, not an unbroken daily series. Also, fossil fuels account for most of the human-added CO₂, not all; land-use change contributes. [Scripps history](https://www.scrippsco2.ucsd.edu/history_legacy/keeling_curve_lessons.html), [Scripps account of the eruption](https://scripps.ucsd.edu/news/historic-keeling-curve-observation-interrupted-volcanic-eruption).

8. **An absolute contradicts the chapter’s own description — high confidence.**  
   > “The line has never once turned down.”

   It turns down seasonally, as the preceding paragraph explains. The **annual average** has continued rising. This is exactly the kind of exception-breaking absolute prohibited by `style.md`. [Scripps history of the curve](https://keelingcurve.ucsd.edu/2013/04/03/the-history-of-the-keeling-curve/).

9. **The second American withdrawal falls outside the stated period — high confidence.**  
   > “The United States has since left it, rejoined, and left again.”

   During 2025 the United States **notified its second withdrawal**. That withdrawal took effect on **27 January 2026**. For a chapter ending in 2025, the final action must be described as initiated rather than completed. [UN depositary notification](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/CN/2025/CN.71.2025-Eng.pdf).

10. **Paris’s obligations are understated — high confidence.**  
    > “It imposed no targets.”  
    > “Its force lay in embarrassment.”

    Paris did not impose internationally allocated, binding national emissions quotas. It did establish collective temperature goals and legally binding procedural duties, including preparing, communicating and maintaining national contributions and reporting information. Reputational pressure is important, but these sentences erase the distinction between nonbinding achievement of national targets and binding treaty obligations. [UN legal explanation of the agreement](https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/pa/pa.html).

11. **Environmental awareness did not begin in 1962 — high confidence.**  
    > “The idea that industrial civilisation might be damaging the world that it depends on entered public life in 1962, with a book by an American marine biologist.”

    Carson helped transform modern environmental politics; she did not introduce the underlying idea into public life. George Perkins Marsh’s influential *Man and Nature* appeared in **1864**, describing human destruction of environmental systems. This is also an implied “first” that one earlier example breaks. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/tmp93000823/).

12. **Foote’s exclusion is asserted without support — high confidence.**  
    > “Her paper was read out to a scientific meeting by a man, since women did not present, and was forgotten.”

    Joseph Henry read the paper, but the AAAS **did not prohibit women from presenting**. The American Physical Society reports that Foote presented research herself the following year. Why Henry presented this particular paper should not be settled by an invented categorical rule. [APS history](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2023/07/carbon-dioxide-atmosphere-eunice-foote).

13. **Two different experiments are collapsed into one — high confidence.**  
    > “Three years later an Irish physicist in London, John Tyndall, measured the effect precisely.”

    Foote measured heating in sunlit cylinders; her apparatus did not isolate absorption of the Earth’s outgoing longwave infrared radiation. Tyndall investigated gases’ absorption of radiant heat. His contribution was more than a more precise measurement of the same experiment. [Historical analysis of Foote’s apparatus](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsnr.2020.0031).

14. **The comparison with government science overstates the finding — medium-high confidence.**  
    > “The match was closer than that of most government scientists of the period.”

    The 2023 analysis found Exxon’s projections broadly comparable to independent academic and government models, and sometimes better. It does not establish superiority over **most government scientists**. The strong, supported point is that the company’s projections were skilful and broadly accurate. [Harvard’s account of the research](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2023/01/harvard-led-analysis-finds-exxonmobil-internal-research-accurately-predicted-climate-change/).

15. **Different technologies did not follow the same cost curve — high confidence.**  
    > “Wind turbines and batteries followed the same curve.”

    Their costs fell, but by different amounts and with different learning rates. IRENA reports a **2010–2020** fall in electricity costs of **85% for solar PV**, **56% for onshore wind**, and **48% for offshore wind**. “The same curve” is misleading immediately after a specific solar percentage and production-doubling rule. [IRENA cost report](https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2021/Jun/IRENA_Power_Generation_Costs_2020.pdf).

16. **Thunberg’s initial strike was daily — high confidence.**  
    > “In August 2018 a fifteen-year-old Swedish girl stopped going to school on Fridays and sat instead on the pavement outside her country's parliament, alone, with a hand-painted sign that read ‘School strike for climate’.”

    She initially protested **every school day for three weeks**. The Friday schedule followed. [Fridays for Future’s account through the UN](https://sdgs.un.org/partnerships/fridaysforfuture).

17. **Tuvalu’s islands are misclassified — high confidence.**  
    > “The Pacific nation of Tuvalu consists of nine coral atolls whose highest point is under five metres above the sea.”

    Tuvalu comprises **six atolls and three reef islands**. “Nine islands” would avoid an unnecessary geographical error. [UNESCO’s description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6707/).

18. **Annual temperature and long-term warming need separating.**  
    > “The ten hottest years in the record are the last ten, and 2024 was the first in which the average passed one and a half degrees above the old level.”

    A general reader will reasonably wonder how this fits the preceding “one and a third degrees,” and whether the Paris limit has already been conclusively crossed. Explain that a single year fluctuates around the longer-term warming trend, while the Paris threshold concerns sustained warming. The figures themselves need not conflict. [WMO’s explicit distinction](https://wmo.int/publication-series/state-of-global-climate/state-of-global-climate-2024).

19. **The drought explanation omits its connecting mechanism.**  
    > “Warm air holds more water, and so droughts are drier and downpours heavier.”

    More atmospheric moisture helps explain heavier downpours, but does not explain drought to a newcomer. Supply the missing step: warming increases evaporative demand and can dry soils and vegetation more rapidly. Drought changes also vary by region and type, so the universal wording is too broad. [NOAA’s water-cycle explanation](https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/freshwater/water-cycle).

20. **The ending leaves the crucial stock-versus-flow distinction implicit.**  
    > “On the mountain in Hawaii it made no visible difference.”

    After the reported pandemic emissions reduction, this can sound as though reducing emissions achieves nothing. Explain that humanity was still adding substantial CO₂: turning down a running tap does not lower the water already accumulating in a bath. The expected effect was a slightly slower increase, difficult to distinguish from natural variability. [Met Office explanation](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/research/news/2021/how-did-covid-19-lockdowns-affect-the-climate).

21. **The principal company remains unnecessarily anonymous.**  
    > “In 1977 a senior scientist at the largest oil company in the world told its management committee that there was general scientific agreement that mankind was influencing the global climate through the release of carbon dioxide from the burning of fossil fuels.”

    Name **Exxon**. The company recurs throughout the section and bears the central allegation; withholding its name makes the story harder to follow and assess. The “few names” rule expressly permits names needed to follow the story. [Research identifying the company](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2023/01/harvard-led-analysis-finds-exxonmobil-internal-research-accurately-predicted-climate-change/).

22. **The insecticide passage needs a bridge to what readers already know.**  
    > “She was dying of cancer as she defended it, and the most notorious of the chemicals was banned in the United States ten years later.”

    Name **DDT**, and distinguish the environmental consequences of widespread spraying from the disease-control benefits described in chapter 110. Those accounts are compatible, but an uninformed reader is left to reconcile lifesaving insecticides with a ban on an unnamed chemical. This is a continuity gap, not a contradiction.

23. **An explicit signpost sentence breaks `style.md`.**  
    > “There are two ways of knowing that it did not.”

    This announces the structure of the coming answer instead of giving the evidence. It also makes the section read like a numbered case—the approach the style guide warns against. The isotope evidence can begin directly.

24. **A numerical list breaks “one number per point.”**  
    > “The average American is responsible for about fourteen tons of carbon dioxide a year, the average Chinese for about nine, the average Indian for two, and the average Nigerian for less than one.”

    Four figures make the reader retain a miniature table. One vivid comparison would establish the disparity more clearly. The preceding national-share paragraph and the catalogue of impacts under “On schedule” have the same tendency.

25. **The final absolute fails because of the seasonal cycle — high confidence.**  
    > “Every person now alive was born under a lower number than the one it shows today.”

    At a seasonal trough, someone born during the preceding seasonal peak was born under a **higher** reading. Even at a record high, “every person” includes newborns for whom the comparison is meaningless at the measurement’s resolution. Tie the ending to annual averages or generations if it needs to remain literally true. [Scripps explanation of seasonal variation](https://keelingcurve.ucsd.edu/2013/04/03/the-history-of-the-keeling-curve/).
