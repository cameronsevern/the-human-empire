## Factual flags

Related sentences are grouped where they share the same problem. “Major” means the issue affects the chapter’s argument; “minor” means it affects a detail or a narrower claim.

1. **“Yes. However, for most of our history a war was over before breakfast.”**  
   Whether humans have *always* fought wars remains contested: neither archaeological evidence nor modern hunter-gatherer observations establish warfare throughout human existence. A short raid also does not establish a short war; the chapter itself describes feuds lasting generations. [Review of warfare’s origins](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajpa.23751).  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

2. **“One of the women was within a few weeks of giving birth.”**  
   The reported estimate for the Nataruk foetus is six to nine months. That supports late pregnancy, but not this precise proximity to childbirth. [Excavators’ account](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/evidence-of-a-prehistoric-massacre-extends-the-history-of-warfare).  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

3. **“Another group of people came to the lagoon and killed them all.” / “We know how, because the bodies were never buried.”**  
   A massacre is the excavators’ interpretation, but other researchers have challenged both the absence of burial and some diagnoses of traumatic injury. The remains also cannot establish that nobody escaped or was spared; the subsequent descriptions of exactly how bodies fell and were covered inherit this excessive certainty. [Published challenge](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27882979/), [excavators’ reply](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3Acffd2b10-5ae9-4a5a-a11f-e9f493bdfab3).  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

4. **“So the killers did not live nearby. They had walked in from somewhere else, carrying weapons made for use on people, and they spared nobody.”**  
   Rare obsidian suggests a possible difference in home ranges; it does not establish where its users lived, since material can move through exchange. Nor does a weapon’s use against a person establish that it was manufactured specifically for that purpose, and neither the attackers’ journey nor their treatment of survivors is known. [Excavators’ account](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/evidence-of-a-prehistoric-massacre-extends-the-history-of-warfare).  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

5. **“For a long time, there were two pictures of life before history, and people chose between them according to taste.” / “Neither man had ever met a hunter.”**  
   This presents Hobbes and Rousseau’s philosophical arguments as competing field reports about prehistoric people; Rousseau explicitly described his reasoning as hypothetical rather than historical. The claim that neither had met “a hunter” is unsupported and, literally, concerns a much broader category than a member of a hunter-gatherer society. [Rousseau’s text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46333/old/46333-h/46333-h.htm).  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

6. **“Wherever chimpanzees have been watched for long enough, the same thing has been seen.”**  
   Lethal aggression is widespread but not universal across studied communities, and systematic extermination followed by territorial annexation is a still narrower phenomenon. The major comparative study found evidence of killings in 15 of 18 chimpanzee communities, with substantial variation between populations. [Wilson and colleagues](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13727).  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

7. **“It is very likely older than the human line itself.”**  
   Similar behaviour in chimpanzees and humans does not establish its presence in their last common ancestor: it could also have evolved independently. An ancestral propensity for coalitionary killing is an evolutionary hypothesis, not a conclusion demonstrated by the Gombe observations. [Review of the competing explanations](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajpa.23751).  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

8. **“No bonobo has ever been seen to kill another. Two apes, equally close to us, live in opposite ways.”**  
   The absolute contrast needs updating: research published in 2026 describes an intergroup attack followed by the acquisition of an infant that subsequently died, although the immediate cause of death and intention remain uncertain. A 2025 report also documents severe coalitionary aggression, so bonobos cannot straightforwardly represent the opposite of violent chimpanzees. [2026 study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40297-w), [2025 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41056910/).  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

9. **“Few would get out alive.”**  
   The aeroplane scenario is an imagined illustration, not an experiment with a known mortality outcome. Its numerical implication cannot be presented as an established fact about chimpanzees.  
   **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

10. **“However, set the two side by side, and a chimpanzee is in a fight hundreds of times more often than a human is.”**  
    This resembles Wrangham’s comparison of *reactive aggression*, but the wording expands it into a general measurement of “fights.” The chapter supplies neither the human sample nor comparable definitions and observation periods, making the apparent precision misleading. [Wrangham’s explanation](https://theecologist.org/2018/sep/21/evolution-human-aggression-curtis-abraham-interviews-richard-wrangham).  
    **Confidence: medium. Severity: minor.**

11. **“Among chimpanzees, the biggest male with the longest teeth gets his way, because nobody can stand up to him alone.”**  
    Chimpanzee dominance depends on alliances and social support as well as individual fighting ability. Size and teeth do not automatically determine which male holds power. [Research on coalition formation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/7990237/).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

12. **“Chimpanzees do sometimes gang up on a bully, but they cannot plan it.”**  
    Chimpanzees show evidence of anticipating future situations and preparing aggressive actions. There is a defensible distinction between that capacity and human language-mediated conspiracy, but categorical inability to plan is stronger than the evidence supports. [Experimental and observational research](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0036782).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

13. **“They had no chief, and they meant to keep it that way.” / “Slowly, as a result, the temper itself was bred out.” / “These are the marks of a domesticated animal.” / “We did it to ourselves, one dead bully at a time.”**  
    These passages turn the execution hypothesis into established evolutionary history, including uniform prehistoric political arrangements and a demonstrated reproductive mechanism. Neither the ethnographic example nor changes in human anatomy prove that executing domineering men caused self-domestication; the later assertion that our ancestors “lost most” reactive violence inherits the same uncertainty. [Wrangham’s hypothesis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10427284/), [critical discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7250791/).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

14. **“The same thing happened to wolves at the edge of the camp, where the bold and calm ones were fed and the savage ones were driven off.”**  
    The process by which wolves became dogs remains disputed. This particular sequence of feeding and exclusion is a reconstruction, not an observed or archaeologically established explanation. [Review of competing domestication models](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8083978/).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

15. **“However, their bodies had changed too, although nobody had selected for that. Their faces grew shorter, their teeth smaller, and their ears floppy.”**  
    The fox experiment’s behavioural results do not establish this entire package of physical changes as newly produced by selection for tameness. Researchers have challenged that account because the founding animals already came from fur farms and several supposedly novel traits predated the experiment; its use as decisive proof of human self-domestication is therefore unsound. [Historical reassessment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31810775/).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

16. **“That is exactly how the chimpanzees of Gombe killed.”**  
    The preceding description includes discussion, agreement by everyone and a decision to execute a particular offender. Those are precisely the features that the execution hypothesis distinguishes from chimpanzee coalitionary attacks; numerical superiority does not make the processes identical. [Wrangham’s distinction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10427284/).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

17. **“In the late 1920s an American scholar spent three years in Arnhem Land … among people who had never farmed and had hardly met a European.”**  
    Warner conducted his research while based at the Methodist mission at Milingimbi. “Had hardly met a European” needs evidence about the particular informants and risks presenting a community already experiencing colonial contact as an untouched prehistoric analogue. [Account of Warner’s fieldwork](https://www.academia.edu/39949838/Arnhem_Land_Bark_Painting_The_Western_Reception).  
    **Confidence: medium. Severity: minor.**

18. **“The total was about two hundred men killed.”**  
    Warner recorded approximately 100 deaths and doubled that figure to estimate unrecorded cases. The chapter presents an extrapolation as the total supplied by informants, giving its subsequent comparison unwarranted solidity. [Source audit, citing Warner’s calculation](https://whatweowethefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/How-violent-was-the-pre-agricultural-world.pdf).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

19. **“Measured against their numbers, it is a heavier toll than Europe suffered in the century that held both world wars.”**  
    This comparison does not specify annual mortality, cumulative population share or proportion of all deaths, and it compares twenty years with a century. It also leaves “Europe” and the included causes of death undefined, so the claimed ranking cannot be checked from the information given.  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

20. **“These looked dramatic and were almost harmless, because a man who is watching can dodge a spear.” / “In twenty years, the arranged battles killed only a handful of men.”**  
    Warner distinguished relatively restrained formal encounters from deadly pitched battles; accounts of his data report 29 deaths in two *gaingar* battles. Treating arranged fighting as uniformly almost harmless erases that distinction and materially distorts the evidence supporting the raids-versus-battles argument. [Discussion of Warner’s categories and deaths](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/298908384_The_origins_of_war).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

21. **“From the Arctic to the Amazon, wherever people who lived by hunting were asked, the answers were much the same. Even the gentlest of them were not gentle by our standards.”**  
    Hunter-gatherer societies show substantial variation in warfare, homicide and conflict resolution. A systematic study of 21 mobile forager societies found much lethal violence to be interpersonal rather than warfare, so the chapter cannot generalize the Arnhem Land pattern—or its earlier “typical act of war” claim—to most human history. [Fry and Söderberg](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23869015/).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

22. **“People who believe that the world is full of wills do not believe in accidents.”**  
    Belief in sorcery does not logically entail denying every accident or natural cause. The statement needs to concern the particular community’s explanations of particular deaths, rather than assigning a universal belief to everyone with an animistic worldview.  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

23. **“Yet when a scholar counted the killings that they remembered, he found that they had murdered one another more often than people do in the United States.”**  
    The comparison needs the study period, population denominator and a dated US homicide rate. As written, a historical estimate based on remembered killings becomes a timeless comparison with a changing modern statistic, without establishing that the categories match.  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

24. **“A band of hunters lost a man to violence every few years. It was almost never wiped out, and what happened at the lagoon in Kenya was rare.”**  
    No population size, period or representative evidence supports this universal loss rate or the claimed frequency of extermination. Sparse archaeological examples cannot by themselves establish rarity, while ethnographic accounts include attacks intended to destroy entire camps. [Review of forager cooperation and warfare](https://gwern.net/doc/sociology/2022-boyd.pdf).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

25. **“To begin with, there was nothing to steal.” / “Killing was dangerous work even when it was done at dawn, and it brought the winners almost nothing they could eat.”**  
    Mobile foragers could compete over access to water, productive territory and other resources without possessing herds or permanent storehouses. The chapter also already identifies women as targets of capture, so the absence of stored wealth does not establish an absence of material incentives. [Nataruk researchers’ proposed motives](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/evidence-of-a-prehistoric-massacre-extends-the-history-of-warfare).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

26. **“A band that felt threatened could pack up in an hour and walk for a week, and its enemies had little reason to follow.” / “For three hundred thousand years, most humans could answer a threat by walking away from it.”**  
    Mobility is a possible escape strategy, not evidence that safe, productive and socially accessible land was always available nearby. The precise packing time, journey duration and extension across 300,000 years are unsupported, while territorial rights and neighbouring populations could constrain movement even among mobile foragers. [Review of variation in early warfare](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajpa.23751).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

27. **“A man who thought the quarrel foolish stayed at home, and nobody could punish him for it.” / “Every one of them was also a hunter whose family would go hungry if he died.”**  
    Absence of a chief does not establish absence of social sanctions or obligations to support relatives. Nor did household food necessarily depend on one man’s hunting: gathering and food-sharing make the asserted inevitability of family hunger untenable. [Review of cooperation in foraging societies](https://gwern.net/doc/sociology/2022-boyd.pdf).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

28. **“A chimpanzee knows the members of his own group and nobody else, and so every stranger is a target.” / “They could make peace.”**  
    Chimpanzees can encounter former groupmates after community division, and females can move between communities, so outsiders need not be unknown or invariably attacked. Chimpanzees also reconcile after conflicts; the defensible human distinction here is negotiated peace *between groups*, not an exclusive capacity to make peace in any sense. [Intergroup-relations review](https://blog.michael-lawrence-wilson.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/07.-Wilson-Wrangham-2003.pdf), [research on chimpanzee reconciliation](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781118922545.ch4).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

29. **“About two thirds of them had been wounded by weapons.” / “The wounded include men, women and children in roughly equal measure.”**  
    The two-thirds figure includes lesions of uncertain origin; the reassessment identifies clear healed or unhealed trauma in 62.3% of the examined individuals. “Equal measure” also confuses counts with proportions: children and adults were not equally represented among the injured, although the study found no statistically significant age or sex difference. [Jebel Sahaba reassessment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89386-y).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

30. **“They were attacked, recovered, and were attacked again, year after year, until an arrow finally killed them.”**  
    Healed and unhealed injuries support recurrent violence, but do not establish annual attacks or eventual death by arrow for each individual. Many skeletons lack evidence of injury around death, and even an unhealed injury does not automatically identify the cause of death. [Jebel Sahaba reassessment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89386-y).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

31. **“Why there? The answer is in the land.” / “The river was the only place where anyone could live, so they stayed, and fought for it, and buried their dead on the hill.”**  
    Climatic instability and pressure on habitable land are proposed explanations, not demonstrated motives for these attacks. The cemetery cannot establish that its users had no possible refuge, fought specifically for the river or experienced the narrated sequence of floods, contraction and conflict. [Jebel Sahaba reassessment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89386-y).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

32. **“The people there never farmed, because they did not need to.”**  
    Northwest Coast peoples cultivated and managed plants, including root foods and tobacco. Distinguishing their economies from cereal agriculture is reasonable, but “never farmed” erases documented cultivation, and abundance alone does not establish the reason for their subsistence choices. [Research on Northwest Coast cultivation](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780295801100_A26577050/preview-9780295801100_A26577050.pdf).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

33. **“The same pattern appears wherever hunters stopped moving.” / “It grows from a pile of food that cannot be moved, and a crowd of neighbours who know where it is.”**  
    Sedentism, stored resources and population pressure can contribute to warfare, but they are neither universal causes nor sufficient conditions. The chapter’s own accounts of revenge and sorcery contradict this single-cause conclusion, and its claim that all four restraints disappear together overlooks kinship ties and political arrangements that can persist after settlement. [Review of competing explanations](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajpa.23751).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: major.**

34. **“People who make pots are people who have something to keep.”**  
    Pottery can serve cooking and processing as well as storage. Sherds alone do not demonstrate substantial stored food, permanent residence or a motive for the Nataruk attack.  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

35. **“In the hills of the Middle East, people were beginning to plant the wild grain they had once only gathered.”**  
    Following the return to Nataruk around 10,000 years ago, this makes cultivation sound like the next development. Evidence places wild cereal cultivation in parts of southwest Asia around 11,500 years ago, so the closing chronology is late unless the narrative explicitly moves backwards. [Archaeobotanical chronology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5150421/).  
    **Confidence: high. Severity: minor.**

## Clarity flags

1. **“It is a small war” / “It was a murder committed by a committee.”**  
   The chapter needs a consistent distinction between war, feud, homicide and execution. Otherwise readers cannot tell why the Nataruk attack counts as war, the Kalahari execution does not, and remembered murders support conclusions about warfare. **Severity: major.**

2. **“Africa, Australia and the Nile, from before our species to about 10,000 years ago.”**  
   The subtitle promises a prehistoric time span, but much of the evidence comes from twentieth-century observations, and the geographic scope includes North America. Readers need a clearer indication of when they are encountering direct prehistoric evidence and when a modern comparison. **Severity: minor.**

3. **“People who lived by hunting” / “bands” / “groups.”**  
   These terms slide between mobile camps, wider marriage networks and settled fishing societies. A newcomer may assume they describe the same social unit and subsistence pattern throughout, making the later change to permanent settlements harder to follow. **Severity: minor.**

4. **“Between groups, we can be worse than a chimpanzee, and for the same reason.”**  
   “The same reason” has several possible referents: self-domestication, planning, cooperation or numerical superiority. The causal connection is central but currently ambiguous. **Severity: minor.**

5. **“When the anger had been thrown off, the killers stood still and let themselves be speared through the thigh.”**  
   The reader is left to infer why accepting this wound would settle the obligation to avenge a death. The missing explanation is the participants’ shared recognition of the ceremony as an accepted settlement, rather than simply an emotional release. **Severity: minor.**

## Overall

There are **40 flags: 21 major and 19 minor**—35 factual flags and five clarity flags, with related statements grouped together. The chapter is readable and its examples are memorable, but its central argument repeatedly converts hypotheses, selected ethnographic cases and archaeological interpretations into universal history. That conflicts directly with the style sheet’s requirements to acknowledge consequential disagreement and avoid brittle absolutes; the most substantial problems concern the origins of warfare, the execution account of self-domestication, the Arnhem Land evidence and the claim that stored food explains war.
