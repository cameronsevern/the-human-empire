# Chapter 67 — How did we learn that germs cause disease?

**Line under the title:** Europe, from 1847 to 1900.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

For all of history, people did not know what made them ill. Doctors blamed bad air. Two men in the middle of the 1800s, a Hungarian in a maternity ward and an English doctor with a map, proved by counting that disease was carried on hands and in water, and were not believed, because they could not say what was being carried. A French chemist and a German country doctor then found it: living things too small to see, each kind causing its own disease. They could do so because of better microscopes and the new dyes of the chemical industry, and their work was among the first products of a new arrangement, the research laboratory, in which discovery was organised and paid for. The result, through sewers, clean water, antiseptic surgery and vaccines, was the largest change in the length of human life there has ever been.

## The story

1. **Opening: the first clinic.** Answer first. Vienna, 1847: two maternity wards, one of which kills three times as many mothers.
2. **Bad air.** What doctors believed, and why it was a reasonable belief in a city that stank. Cholera arrives from India.
3. **The pump.** London, 1854: a doctor maps the dead around a water pump, and then compares two water companies.
4. **Swan necks.** Pasteur: a chemist asked to help a distiller finds that fermentation is alive. The flask that kept broth fresh. Heating wine.
5. **Carbolic.** A surgeon in Glasgow reads Pasteur and tries a sewage disinfectant on a boy's broken leg.
6. **A country doctor's microscope.** Koch: anthrax, then the methods (dyes, jelly, the dish) and the bacillus of tuberculosis in 1882. Twenty years of hunting.
7. **Mauve.** Where the dyes came from: a teenager's failed experiment, a German industry, and the laboratory as a place where discovery is a job. An American "invention factory".
8. **Fourteen bites.** Vaccines made on purpose: fifty sheep in a field, and a boy bitten by a mad dog.
9. **Ending: the handle.** What changed: sewers, water, surgery, the death rate of children. The pump today.
