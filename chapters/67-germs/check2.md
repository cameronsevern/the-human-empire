## Factual flags

1. **Major — the revision makes Meister Pasteur’s first human patient again.**

   > “A human patient was another, and the first one arrived before Pasteur was ready.”

   Pasteur’s records document human rabies-treatment attempts involving Girard in May 1885 and Julie-Antoinette Poughon in June, before Meister in July. Meister was the celebrated successful case, not the first human treatment attempt. This is a **new regression**, not a repetition of the earlier objection to the now-qualified “tested it thoroughly only on dogs.” [Historical study discussing the laboratory notebooks](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9027159/).

2. **Major — the new bridge reintroduces the Petri-dish chronology error.**

   > “With these tools Koch went after a far bigger target than anthrax.”

   “These tools” includes the Petri dish in the preceding paragraph, but the next sentence dates the tuberculosis announcement to March 1882. Petri published his dish modification in **1887**. The existing “A few years later” does not repair the chronology once the new bridge explicitly makes those tools precede the tuberculosis work. [CDC, “Etymologia: Petri Dish”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7774570/).

3. **Major — earlier anthrax research is reduced to observation alone.**

   > “Nobody had shown, however, whether the rods caused the disease or merely turned up alongside it.”

   Davaine had already supplied experimental evidence for a causal connection in **1863–64**; Koch explicitly built on it. Koch greatly strengthened the demonstration and explained the organism’s life cycle, but he did not begin with nothing beyond an observed association. This concerns the newly added sentence, not the previously corrected claim about Bassi and the first microbial proof. [K. Codell Carter’s analysis of Koch’s papers and predecessors](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0025727300044689).

4. **Major — clean drinking water was not an entirely unintended result.**

   > “The smell went away, and so, without anyone having intended it, did the sewage in the drinking water.”

   Keeping polluted water out of London’s supply was already an explicit policy objective. The **1852 Metropolis Water Act** required changes to intake locations and filtration, before the Great Stink and the main sewer construction. The correct distinction is between misunderstanding disease transmission and deliberately improving water quality. The former does not imply the latter was accidental. [Original 1852 Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/15-16/84/pdfs/ukpga_18520084_en.pdf).

5. **Minor — Semmelweis did not reject every alternative because conditions were identical.**

   > “He checked each of the accepted explanations and found that none fitted, because whatever was supposed to be killing the mothers in one clinic was present in the other as well.”

   Some relevant practices differed. Semmelweis describes women delivering on their backs in the first clinic and on their sides in the second; he changed the first clinic’s practice to test that explanation. Some hypotheses failed because changing the suspected factor did not solve the problem, rather than because that factor was already identical. The related “one difference only” also overstates the comparison. [Semmelweis’s own account](https://www.academia.dk/MedHist/Biblioteket/Print/semmelweis_1861.html).

6. **Minor — the present-tense absolute about rabies is false.**

   > “It takes weeks to develop after a bite, and once its symptoms appear it is always fatal.”

   Symptomatic rabies is **almost always fatal**, but documented survivors exist. Incubation commonly lasts weeks to months and varies more widely. The earlier distinction between exposure and established illness has been fixed; this is a separate objection to the surviving absolute in a general medical statement. [CDC Yellow Book: rabies](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-associated-infections-diseases/rabies.html).

## Clarity flags

1. **An ambiguous claim that vaccination could not be repeated.**

   > “It was a lucky find that could not be repeated.”

   A general reader may understand this to mean cowpox vaccination could not be performed successfully again. The intended point appears to be that its discovery supplied no general method for developing vaccines against other diseases.

2. **The tuberculosis sentence confuses inheritance with transmission within families.**

   > “If a germ caused it, then it was caught and not handed down in families.”

   “Handed down in families” can mean either inherited genetically or transmitted between relatives. The intended contrast needs the precise word **inherited**.

3. **“The engineer” has no antecedent.**

   > “Over the next few years the engineer built eighty miles of great brick tunnels to carry the city's filth downstream.”

   No engineer has been introduced. “An engineer” would satisfy the preference for few names without making the reader search backwards.

4. **The new surgery bridge asserts a causal step that the following figures do not explain.**

   > “That ought to have made the operating table a safer place. In one way it did the opposite.”

   The following sentence reports more operations and high mortality, but does not explain how anaesthesia increased danger. More operations can increase the number of deaths without increasing the risk of each operation.

5. **Broken absolutes obscure otherwise useful comparisons.**

   > “Only the water differed.”

   This reads as an exact claim about individual households, whereas the point is that the intermingled populations provided a strong comparison.

   > “They did not die under the knife.”

   The intended subject is evidently patients dying of postoperative infection. As written, “they” refers broadly to the amputees, making an unnecessary categorical claim.

   > “The second difficulty was telling them apart, since a sample from a sick body holds many kinds mixed together.”

   This makes every sample sound mixed. The useful point is that mixed samples made isolation difficult.

6. **Several additions announce the explanation instead of giving it.**

   > “The answer came from outside medicine altogether.”

   > “That answer raised a further question.”

   > “The results showed in the figures.”

   > “Although mauve was found in London, that industry grew up in Germany, for a reason.”

   These fit the style sheet’s prohibition on signpost sentences. The last can lose “for a reason”; the others are immediately followed by the information they announce.

7. **Some additions repeat adjacent sentences without supplying a connecting step.**

   > “A woman could not choose between them. Which clinic she entered depended on the day she happened to arrive.”

   These substantially overlap with the preceding alternate-day admissions sentence.

   > “That was still a general claim. It had still to be proved…”

   The repeated “still” is conspicuous.

   > “This was a new way of finding things out.”

   The surrounding sentences already explain precisely what was new about company laboratories.

   > “Childhood had been a dangerous time for as long as anyone had kept count.”

   The next sentence immediately supplies that point with the mortality figures.
