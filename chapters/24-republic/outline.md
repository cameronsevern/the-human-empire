# Chapter 24 — Why did the Roman Republic fall?

**Line under the title:** Italy and the Mediterranean, from about 2,160 to about 2,010 years ago.
**Length:** 4,800 to 5,400 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The Republic was destroyed by its own success. Conquest filled Italy with slaves and made a few families enormously rich, while the farmers who did the fighting came home to nothing. When the state would not give its soldiers land, generals did, and an army that looks to its general for its future belongs to him. From the day one consul marched his legions on Rome, the city belonged to whoever commanded the most loyal soldiers. In the same years, Roman citizenship became something worth a war: the Italian allies fought Rome to be let in, and won by losing. Augustus ended the civil wars by solving the problem that started them. He put the army on a fixed wage and a pension paid from taxes, in his own name, and left the old offices standing as scenery.

## The story

1. **Opening: air and light.** Answer first. A young noble tells a crowd that the wild animals of Italy have dens and the men who conquered the world have nothing. What conquest did to the Italian countryside: long service, public land, slaves.
2. **Benches for clubs.** The tribune and his land law. He is beaten to death by senators. His brother, ten years later, the cheap grain and the same end. The boundary stones.
3. **The men's baths.** What citizenship was worth: the vote, land, no direct tax, and a body the magistrate could not flog. The allies have none of it. The flogging at Teanum. The crucified citizen in Sicily.
4. **A bull and a wolf.** The allies' champion is murdered and they revolt, with their own senate, consuls and coins. Rome wins by granting what they asked. The citizen roll more than doubles.
5. **The general's men.** Marius enlists men with no land. Sulla's command is taken away and he marches on Rome; his officers refuse, his soldiers follow. The lists of names. A hundred thousand veterans on confiscated farms.
6. **Six thousand crosses.** The slaves' side of the conquest: Spartacus.
7. **The river.** Caesar in Gaul, the order to disband, the Rubicon, dictator for life, twenty-three wounds. Why killing him settled nothing.
8. **First citizen.** A boy of eighteen buys an army. Cicero's head. Egypt. The Republic "restored". The fixed term, the pension and the two taxes. The list on the temple wall. The last words.
