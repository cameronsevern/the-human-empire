# Chapter 87 — Why did the European empires give up their colonies?

**Line under the title:** Africa and Asia, from 1945 to 1975.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The empires ended because the war had bankrupted the countries that owned them and broken the belief that Europeans could not be beaten, because the colonised had built mass movements that turned the rulers' own words about freedom against them, and because both of the new great powers wanted the old empires gone. Where there were few European settlers the rulers negotiated and left. Where there were many, as in Algeria and Kenya, they fought, and lost anyway. In thirty years a third of humanity changed governments. The new states inherited borders drawn by strangers and economies built to ship one crop or one metal to Europe, and the powers that had let go of the flag often kept hold of the rest.

6. **Part of France.** Algeria: a million settlers, a massacre on the day of victory in Europe, eight years of war, torture, a republic brought down, and a million people with suitcases.
7. **The canal.** Suez, 1956: who now gave the orders.
8. **Neither side.** Bandung and the invention of the "third world".
9. **Sixteen graduates.** The Congo: a king's speech, a prime minister's answer, and seven months.
10. **What was handed over.** Borders, one-crop economies, coups, and what Nkrumah called the new colonialism. The gains.
11. **Ending: a tooth.**
