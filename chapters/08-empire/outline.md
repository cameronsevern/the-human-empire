# Chapter 8 — What was the first empire?

**Line under the title:** Iraq and Syria, from about 4,350 years ago to about 4,150 years ago.
**Length:** 4,500 to 5,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room: set up each idea, walk through the reasoning, say why a fact matters.

The movements are the story, not section headings. Facts get a lookup pass after drafting.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

An empire is one ruler holding many peoples who would sooner rule themselves. The first was Akkad, built on the plain of Iraq by a usurper who called himself True King. Beating cities was old. Keeping them was new, and he did it with a handful of tools that every later empire reused: his own men as governors, a paid army that never went home, walls pulled down, one set of measures and one language of record, and a daughter placed at the head of a conquered city's religion. It worked only while it was held together by force and by a strong king. Every new reign opened with a general revolt. After a little under two centuries the empire came apart, under rebellion, invaders and a long drought together. The kingdom was gone within a few generations of its founder. The ambition to be him lasted two thousand years.

## The story

1. **True King.** Answer first. Then the name: a man born a king does not need to call himself the true one.

2. **The cupbearer.** What is known and what was later told about his rise. The king who had just beaten all the cities of the plain, and how the cupbearer beat him and led him to the god's gate in a neck-stock. Thirty-four battles and weapons washed in the sea.

3. **Keeping it.** Why beating a city and holding it are different problems. The tools: governors from his own city, 5,400 men who ate before him every day, walls torn down, a new capital, one system of measures, records in his own language, ships from distant lands at his quay.

4. **The priestess.** Force is not enough. His daughter made high priestess in a conquered city, to join the gods of the two peoples. She is the first author whose name we know. She was driven out in a revolt and came back.

5. **Everyone rebels.** Each king's death is a signal. The son who put down the cities with slaughter. The grandson who faced all of them at once, won nine battles in a year, and was declared a god. The carved stone of him climbing a mountain.

6. **Who was king? Who was not king?** The collapse: four kings in three years, mountain raiders, and the rains failing. The ancient explanation (a king offended a god) set beside the modern one (drought). Why an empire is brittle.

7. **The name that lasted.** The city has never been found. The stories were copied for two thousand years, and later kings took his name.
