## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Hunting bands killed any man who tried to rule them. Within a few thousand years of the first storehouses, people were dying in rows to accompany a ruler into his grave. Three things made the difference. A war leader, chosen for an emergency, kept command because the emergencies never stopped and because he controlled the grain that fed the fighting men. A city of strangers could no longer gang up on a bully the way a camp of thirty could. And people believed: the king was the god's steward in Iraq, and a god himself in Egypt, and he was thought to keep the world in order. Force, service and belief worked together, and no king lasted long on only one of them.

## The story

1. **The death pit.** Answer first. Then Ur, about 4,500 years ago: seventy-four attendants laid in rows in a ruler's grave. Nobody at Çatalhöyük had been worth dying for.

2. **The big man.** Sumer's cities had temples and councils before they had kings. War between walled cities called for one commander. The word for king meant "big man". The poem in which a king asks the elders and then the young men. Why the war leader never stood down.

3. **The first army.** The carved stone from Lagash: a block of helmeted spearmen behind a wall of shields. What it takes to field that, and why only a storehouse can. The hunters' rule that nobody can be made to fight is gone.

4. **Why not kill the bully?** The band's old remedy and why it fails in a city. The guard fed from the storehouse. The story that kingship came down from heaven. The king as builder, shepherd and judge.

5. **One river.** Egypt: a valley a thousand kilometres long with desert on both sides, a current that carries boats north and a wind that carries them south. One king takes all of it. The carved palette with the raised mace. The king as a god who keeps the world in order.

6. **A hill of stone.** The Great Pyramid: what it took, who built it, what they ate, the gang names, the boatman's logbook. Why they did it, and what building it did to Egypt.

7. **The price.** Servants buried with the first kings of Egypt too. The practice ends in both lands. What the kings had learned.

8. **More than one city.** Sumer's kings fight each other and cannot hold what they win. End in Kish, with a cupbearer.
