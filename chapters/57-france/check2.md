## Factual flags

1. **Major — the Committee’s chronology remains wrong, and the revision strengthens it.**

   > “A large assembly could not direct a struggle on so many fronts by debate. It therefore gave emergency powers to a committee of its members, which soon numbered twelve, the Committee of Public Safety.”

   This follows the June purge, southern rebellions, and Toulon’s surrender, making the Committee’s creation their consequence. It was established on **6 April 1793**, before those events. The earlier check’s membership objection has been fixed; its chronology objection has not. The correct sequence is creation in April, followed by the widening crises and expansion of the Committee’s powers. [French National Archives: Committee of Public Safety](https://rdf.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/garance/entities/agent/051694/).

2. **Minor — destroying records did not automatically defeat a lord’s claim.**

   > “Without the records, a lord could not show what he was owed.”

   Burning documents could seriously obstruct collection, but the sentence makes documentary destruction conclusive. Rights could benefit from legal presumptions: the March 1790 settlement presumed specified dues redeemable unless contrary proof was supplied, placing a substantial burden on the peasant. The correct point is that destroying records made claims harder to establish or enforce, not necessarily impossible. [Decree of 15 March 1790, Title III](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/D%C3%A9cret_du_15_mars_1790_relatif_aux_droits_f%C3%A9odaux), [legal history of the decree](https://www.revuegeneraledudroit.eu/blog/2015/03/15/le-decret-des-15-28-mars-1790-consecration-dune-revolution-juridique-controversee/).

3. **Major — trials were severely curtailed, but not universally predetermined.**

   > “A trial under those rules was a formality.”

   The law of 22 Prairial drastically weakened defendants’ protections and accelerated executions. Nevertheless, acquittals continued, and research identifies successful defence efforts by defendants, friends, and families even after its passage. Calling every trial a formality erases that consequential distinction. The correct fact is that the law made convictions and executions much easier, while acquittal remained a real outcome. [Research discussion in *La Révolution française*](https://journals.openedition.org/lrf/pdf/1591), [text of the law](https://www.revolution.chnm.org/d/439/).

4. **Minor — the palace invasion and royal departure occurred on the same day.**

   > “They broke into the palace, and the next day they escorted the royal family back to Paris, where it would be under the eyes of the people.”

   The march began on **5 October 1789**. The crowd broke into the royal apartments early on **6 October**, and the royal family left for Paris that afternoon—not the following day. This error predates the flow revision. [Palace of Versailles: departure of the king](https://en.chateauversailles.fr/discover/history/key-dates/departure-king-1789).

5. **Minor — the tithe was not uniformly one tenth.**

   > “A French peasant therefore paid the king's tax, paid a tenth of his crop to the church, and paid a tangle of dues to the local lord, for the use of the lord's mill and oven and for the right to sell his own land.”

   “A tenth” presents the tithe’s nominal definition as a uniform actual rate. Rates varied by locality and crop; commonly cited rates were between one eleventh and one thirteenth. The correct general statement is that peasants paid a share of their harvest as a church tithe. This also predates the revision. [Académie française: *dîme*](https://www.dictionnaire-academie.fr/article/A9D2526), [research on peasant obligations in 1789](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ahrf_0003-4436_1980_num_242_1_4377?pageId=T1_541).
