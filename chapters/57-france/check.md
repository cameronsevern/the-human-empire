I read the requested files and checked the disputed claims against sources. No files were edited. References below are to [chapter 57](chapters/57-france/chapter.md). Ranked by seriousness:

1. **Misleading account of who drove religious persecution — line 107.**  
   > “They closed the churches, renamed the cathedral of Paris the Temple of Reason, and started a new calendar with twelve months named after the weather and a week of ten days.”

   “They” makes Robespierre and the Committee responsible for a single coherent programme. The church closures and Cult of Reason were driven substantially by other revolutionary authorities and activists; Robespierre opposed compulsory dechristianisation and advocated protecting worship. This distinction matters to the chapter’s explanation of his politics. Also, the months refer to agricultural activities and natural phenomena as well as weather. **Confidence: high.** [Robespierre’s December 1793 intervention](https://www.persee.fr/doc/arcpa_0000-0000_1912_num_80_1_40092_t1_0712_0000_3).

2. **The war narrative obscures French initiative — lines 81 and 87.**  
   > “In April 1792 France went to war with Austria, and Prussia joined in.”  
   > “Britain, Spain and Holland had joined the war, so that France faced enemies on every frontier.”

   These sentences, following “The foreigners … were alarmed,” make the wars sound primarily imposed on France. France declared war on Austria and subsequently on Britain and the Dutch Republic. Foreign threats were real, but revolutionary leaders’ decision to seek war belongs in the causal explanation of the Terror. **Confidence: high.** [William Doyle, *The Oxford History of the French Revolution*, “War against Europe”](https://erenow.org/modern/the-oxford-history-of-the-french-revolution/9.php).

3. **Contradiction with chapter 56 — line 113.**  
   > “Americans and French had made revolutions within a few years of each other, in the name of the same ideas, and only one had devoured its own people.”

   Chapter 56 explicitly says: “The war was fought between neighbours as well as between armies,” and describes sixty thousand loyalists leaving permanently. It also describes armed rebellion against an American state government in 1786. The defensible distinction is the absence of a comparable nationwide revolutionary Terror, rather than the absence of internal violence or persecution. **Confidence: high.** Source: [chapter 56](chapters/56-america/chapter.md).

4. **Feudal dues did not disappear overnight — line 53.**  
   > “Dues, tithes, tax exemptions, the hunting rights and the pigeons were all given up.”

   Some obligations were abolished outright, but many seigneurial dues were declared redeemable: peasants remained liable until they bought them out. Uncompensated abolition came in 1793. The current wording removes four years of a conflict central to the countryside’s experience. **Confidence: high.** [August 1789 decree, especially Article 1](https://revolution.chnm.org/items/show/331).

5. **Wrong destination for the royal flight — line 75.**  
   > “On a night in June 1791 he and his family slipped out of Paris in disguise, in a large coach, heading for the eastern frontier and the armies of the queen's brother, the emperor of Austria.”

   The intended destination was Montmédy, inside France, where loyal French troops under Bouillé would protect the king. Austrian territory provided a possible refuge, but Austrian armies were not the immediate destination. **Confidence: high.** [University of South Florida’s historical map description](https://etc.usf.edu/maps/pages/3600/3676/3676.htm); [Paris Musées’ contemporary print](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/louis-xvi-stop-d-at-varennes-in-his-way-from-paris-to-montmedi).

6. **The execution total is mislabelled — line 103.**  
   > “In about a year the courts sent some seventeen thousand people to the guillotine.”

   The familiar figure of approximately 16,600–17,000 is for recorded judicial executions or death sentences across France, not exclusively guillotinings. Use “sentenced to death” or “executed after trial.” **Confidence: high.** [University of Chicago Press, Laure Murat excerpt](https://press.uchicago.edu/books/excerpt/2015/Murat_Man_Thought_Napoleon.html); [discussion of the court records citing historian Hugh Gough](https://www.livescience.com/reign-of-terror.html).

7. **False claim of unprecedented consultation — line 37.**  
   > “Meanwhile the government did something that no European state had done before.”

   Soliciting grievance lists accompanied earlier Estates-General, including that of 1614. The scale and political consequences in 1789 were remarkable; the practice itself was longstanding. This also violates the prohibition on absolutes defeated by one exception. **Confidence: high.** [Gilbert Shapiro and John Markoff, “Officially Solicited Petitions”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/petitions-in-social-history/officially-solicited-petitions-the-cahiers-de-doleances-as-a-historical-source/7DDB50C0620BE170073EE75660D18FDD).

8. **Two days collapsed into one — line 109.**  
   > “On 27 July 1794 he rose to speak and hinted that there were more traitors to be unmasked among the members.”

   The speech threatening unnamed enemies was on **26 July**. His opponents shouted him down and secured his arrest on **27 July**; execution followed on **28 July**. **Confidence: high.** [National Assembly: speech of 26 July 1794](https://www2.assemblee-nationale.fr/decouvrir-l-assemblee/histoire/grands-discours-parlementaires/robespierre-26-juillet-1794).

9. **Lafayette’s authorship is overstated — line 55.**  
   > “Its chief drafter was the nobleman who would send Washington the key to the Bastille, and the American ambassador in Paris, Thomas Jefferson, looked over his drafts.”

   Lafayette prepared an influential early proposal with Jefferson’s advice. The adopted Declaration emerged from multiple proposals, committee work and article-by-article debate; calling him its “chief drafter” conflates his proposal with the final text. **Confidence: high.** [National Assembly’s account of its drafting](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/revolution-francaise/la-declaration-des-droits-de-l-homme-et-du-citoyen).

10. **“All men” is materially inaccurate — line 83.**  
    > “A new assembly, elected by all men, abolished the monarchy that same month and put the king on trial for treason.”

    The election substantially broadened male suffrage, but excluded domestic servants and indigent men and imposed age and residence requirements. Voting was indirect, with very low participation. “Elected under a much broader male franchise” would convey the change accurately. **Confidence: high.** [National Assembly’s explanation of the 1792 electorate](https://www2.assemblee-nationale.fr/decouvrir-l-assemblee/histoire/le-suffrage-universel/la-republique-et-le-suffrage-universel/1788-1848-les-premieres-reflexions-autour-du-suffrage/1792-le-suffrage-universel-est-introduit-pour-l-election-de-la-convention-nationale).

11. **The Committee’s formation is misplaced and compressed — line 91.**  
    > “The assembly responded by giving emergency powers to a committee of twelve of its members, the Committee of Public Safety.”

    It was established on **6 April 1793 with nine members**, subsequently expanding to twelve. Because this follows the southern rebellions and Toulon’s surrender, the reader also receives the wrong sequence: the Committee already existed before those events. **Confidence: high.** [National Assembly history](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/revolution-francaise/la-terreur).

12. **Earlier monarchies also conscripted — line 95.**  
    > “Kings had fought their wars with hired professionals.”

    The contrast excludes compulsory recruitment and militia service under monarchies. Prussia’s canton system, introduced in 1733, is a clear counterexample. France’s distinctive development here was the scale and proclaimed mobilisation of the whole society. **Confidence: high.** [German Historical Institute: the 1733 recruitment order](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/the-holy-roman-empire-1648-1815/introduction-of-the-brandenburg-prussian-canton-system-of-military-recruitment-kantonreglement-issued-by-frederick-william-i-quot-the-soldier-king-quot-as-an-order-to-general-field-marshal-albrecht-konrad-finck-von-finckenstein-may-1-1733).

13. **The end of the Terror is too abrupt — line 109.**  
    > “The Terror stopped almost at once.”

    Robespierre’s fall conventionally marks its end, and executions declined sharply. Nevertheless, dismantling emergency institutions took time, and political repression and killing continued. The sentence encourages the mistaken inference that removing one man immediately ended the machinery and violence. **Confidence: high on the sequence; moderate on how much qualification the narrative needs.** [Doyle, “Thermidor”](https://erenow.org/modern/the-oxford-history-of-the-french-revolution/12.php); [*The Oxford Handbook of the French Revolution*, “Thermidor and the Myth of Rupture”](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34337/chapter-abstract/291386447).

14. **The Declaration’s protection is presented without its express limit — line 55.**  
    > “It goes on to say that all power comes from the nation, that the law must be the same for everyone, and that no one may be punished for his opinions.”

    Article 10 protects opinions subject to their expression not disturbing legally established public order; Article 11 likewise allows legal punishment for abuses of expression. The qualification matters in a chapter about how proclaimed rights coexisted with repression. **Confidence: high.** [Declaration, Articles 10–11](https://www.elysee.fr/la-presidence/la-declaration-des-droits-de-l-homme-et-du-citoyen).

15. **An absolute undermines the opening explanation — line 5.**  
    > “They had swept away every old rule for settling a quarrel, and they had no new rule that their opponents accepted.”

    “Every old rule” is untenable: ordinary legal processes and substantial bodies of existing law survived, even as institutions were reorganised. The actual argument concerns the lack of an accepted way to settle disputes over **sovereignty and revolutionary legitimacy**. Narrowing it would strengthen the explanation and satisfy `style.md` without weakening the narrator’s confidence.

16. **A decisive political transition is missing — line 89.**  
    > “The great cities of the south rose as well, and the naval base at Toulon handed itself over to the British fleet.”

    A general reader will assume these were extensions of the religious and conscription revolt just described. Many urban rebellions instead opposed Parisian domination and the purge of moderate republican deputies; they were not uniformly royalist. A short explanation of that split is needed to make the government’s widening definition of “traitor” intelligible. **Confidence: high.** [Larousse’s account of the Revolution](https://www.larousse.fr/encyclopedie/divers/R%C3%A9volution_fran%C3%A7aise/140733).

17. **The central machine is never explained — line 101.**  
    > “In 1789 a doctor in the Assembly had proposed that executions should be the same for everyone and as painless as possible, by means of a simple machine.”

    “Guillotined” has already appeared, but the text never describes a raised blade falling between upright posts onto the prisoner’s neck. Readers unfamiliar with the device cannot picture why it was considered quick, standardised or humane. This directly misses `style.md`’s instruction to explain a device in the order it operates.

18. **A major person arrives only as a superlative — line 105.**  
    > “In the spring of 1794 the Committee turned on its own allies and executed the most popular revolutionary in France for suggesting that the killing should slow down.”

    This is Danton, but the reader has never encountered him and cannot understand what losing this ally meant. “Most popular” also needs a defined basis. One brief identification of his earlier leadership and his association with calls for clemency would make the event understandable; attributing the execution solely to that suggestion oversimplifies the factional struggle.

19. **Explicit signposting — line 107.**  
    > “The men who did this were not cynics, and that is the unsettling part.”

    “That is the unsettling part” announces the reader’s intended reaction instead of giving the explanation. It falls directly under `style.md`’s ban on signpost sentences. The following sentence already supplies the substantive point.
