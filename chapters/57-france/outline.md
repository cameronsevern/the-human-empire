# Chapter 57 — Why did the French Revolution turn to terror?

**Line under the title:** France, from 1788 to 1794.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The revolution began as a bankruptcy. A king who could not pay his debts had to ask his subjects for help, and they took the chance to remake the country: they abolished the privileges of birth, declared equal rights and tried to rebuild everything from the calendar to the church. It turned to terror because of what happened next. The king tried to flee, the rest of Europe went to war with France, the devout west of the country rose in revolt, and Paris was hungry. The revolutionaries had destroyed every old rule for settling a disagreement and had no new one that both sides accepted, so opponents became traitors. A government of twelve men saved the country by conscripting the whole nation, and killed tens of thousands of its own citizens to do it. What survived was the end of legal privilege, a nation of citizens, the mass army and the language of rights.

## The story

1. **Opening: "Nothing."** Answer first. The king's diary for 14 July 1789.
2. **Twenty-eight million.** The old order: three estates, privilege, who paid tax and who did not. The debt, and why the king had to call an assembly that had not met for 175 years.
3. **The price of bread.** The hungry spring of 1789. A country asked to write down its complaints. The commoners declare themselves the nation.
4. **Seven prisoners.** The fall of the Bastille, the burning of the manor records, the night the nobles gave up their rights, the Declaration, and the market women who fetched the king.
5. **Left and right.** Two years of rebuilding: departments, the metre, church land, paper money. The oath that split the country. Where "left" and "right" come from.
6. **The king's profile.** The flight of June 1791. War with Europe. August and September 1792. The republic and the king's trial.
7. **The whole nation.** 1793: enemies on every border and revolt at home. Twelve men and the first mass conscription.
8. **A humane machine.** The guillotine and the Terror: how many died and who they were. The new calendar. The logic of virtue, and the day it ended.
9. **Ending: what was left.** What France kept. Why America's revolution was gentler. Which men?

## Left for later

Napoleon. The slave revolution in Saint-Domingue. Nationalism in the rest of Europe. Socialism.
