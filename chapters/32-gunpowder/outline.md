# Chapter 32 — Who invented gunpowder?

**Line under the title:** China under the Song dynasty, from about 850 to 1232.
**Length:** 4,400 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Gunpowder was found by Chinese alchemists looking for a drug against death, and it was turned into weapons by the richest and most inventive society of its age. Song China fed a hundred million people on new rice, burned coal, cast more iron than Europe would for six centuries, paid its bills in printed paper and steered its ships by a magnetic needle. It was also a state that had deliberately put scholars above soldiers, that had no horse pastures, and that bought peace from its northern neighbours with silk and silver. In 1127 horsemen from the forests of Manchuria took its capital and carried off its emperor all the same. Wealth and invention did not make it safe, because an invention does not stay with its inventor: the besiegers of one decade are the bomb-makers of the next.

## The story

1. **Opening: the fire drug.** Answer first. The alchemists' warning about a mixture that burned down the house. What saltpetre is and why the mixture burns without air.
2. **Night markets.** The Song capital, with no curfew. New rice and a doubled population. Coal in the kitchens and the iron output of 1078. The scroll of the river festival.
3. **Twenty-five kilograms of small change.** Iron coins in Sichuan. Merchants' receipts. The government takes over in 1024 and prints the first state paper money; the temptation that came with it.
4. **The south-pointing needle.** From fortune-telling to navigation. The official who noticed that the needle does not point quite south. Ships with walls inside them.
5. **Arrows by the hundred thousand.** The first recipes, in a military handbook of 1044. Fire arrows, bombs, and a bamboo tube that spits flame. A state arsenal.
6. **Elder brother, younger brother.** The lost passes in the north, the lack of horses, and the treaty of 1005. An army of a million foot soldiers. Paying for peace as the Han had.
7. **The emperor who painted birds.** An alliance with the Jurchen to destroy the old enemy. The siege of 1126. The court marched north. The water clock that nobody could rebuild. The dynasty survives in the south and executes its best general.
8. **Ending: the thunder-crash bomb.** A century later the Jurchen defend the same city with iron bombs against a new enemy who is taking notes.
