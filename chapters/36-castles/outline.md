# Chapter 36 — Why are there so many castles in Europe?

**Line under the title:** Western Europe, from about 900 to 1300.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Europe is full of castles because for about three hundred years it had almost no government larger than a district. After Charlemagne's empire broke up and the raiders came, kings could not protect anyone, and power fell to whoever had a fortified house and a band of armoured horsemen. A castle was a private fortress, and from it a lord ruled, judged and taxed everything within a morning's ride. Knights were paid in land, peasants were tied to it, and the whole arrangement ran on personal promises. Underneath it, a farming revolution of heavy ploughs, horses, mills and a new rotation doubled the population and paid for stone castles, cathedrals, towns and universities. Kings never quite got the lords back under control, and so they had to bargain with them. Magna Carta and the first parliaments came out of that bargaining.

## The story

1. **Opening: a heap of earth at Hastings.** Answer first. The Normans land in 1066 and build a castle before they fight. Five hundred more in a generation.
2. **Nobody is coming.** The broken empire and the raiders from three directions. Why defence became local. What a mound and a fence could do.
3. **A man on a horse.** What a knight cost and how he was paid. The oath between two hands. Government as a chain of promises.
4. **Three days a week.** The village under the castle. What a serf owed and what he got. The king's great survey of 1086.
5. **The eight-ox plough.** The heavy plough, the furlong and the acre; the horse collar; three fields; mills. Twice as many people.
6. **Town air.** Charters and the year and a day. Guilds, and the guilds of teachers and students that became universities.
7. **Walls of glass.** Stone castles and the cathedrals: the pointed arch and the flying buttress. A vault that fell down.
8. **The meadow by the river.** Kings against barons. Magna Carta, what it said and whom it left out. The king has to ask: parliaments.
9. **Ending.** Twenty-five thousand castles in the German lands alone, and what became of them.
