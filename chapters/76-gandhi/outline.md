# Chapter 76 — How did Gandhi fight an empire without weapons?

**Line under the title:** India and South Africa, from 1893 to 1947.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Gandhi saw that a few thousand British officials could rule three hundred million Indians only because Indians cooperated: as clerks, policemen, soldiers and taxpayers. His method was to withdraw that cooperation in public. People would break an unjust law openly, accept the beating or the prison sentence without hitting back, and so force the rulers to show their violence in front of the world. It asked for people willing to suffer and die for a belief and unwilling to kill for it. It turned a debating society of lawyers into a movement of millions and made British rule impossible to justify. It did not work on everyone: it needed an opponent who cared how he looked, and it could not hold Hindus and Muslims together. Britain left in 1947, exhausted by another world war, and the country was divided as it went.

## The story

1. **Opening: a handful of salt.** Answer first. April 1930: a man of sixty walks to the sea and picks up a lump of salt.
2. **A thousand officials.** How Britain ruled India, and on whose labour. The polite nationalism of the lawyers.
3. **The train at Pietermaritzburg.** The making of Gandhi: a shy law student in London, a night on a station platform in South Africa, twenty years of organising, and the books and beliefs the method came from.
4. **Ten minutes at Amritsar.** April 1919: a walled garden, fifty rifles.
5. **The spinning wheel.** The first great campaign: boycott, homespun cloth, the peasants join. A police station burns and Gandhi calls it all off. A trial at which the accused asks for the heaviest sentence.
6. **Like ninepins.** The salt campaign: the march, the raid on the salt works, an American reporter. A visit to the king.
7. **Who is the nation?** The people the movement did not carry: the leader of the "untouchables", and the leader of the Muslims. A letter to Hitler.
8. **Quit India.** The second world war, the prisons, the famine in Bengal. Why Britain left.
9. **Ending: barefoot in Bengal.** What the method needs. August 1947, and where Gandhi was.
