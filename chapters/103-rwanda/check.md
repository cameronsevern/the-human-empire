1. **Factual error in the central explanation — high confidence.** “Nobody stopped it because the countries that could have done so had no interests in Rwanda and had just lost soldiers in another African war.” France had substantial interests in Rwanda and supported its government militarily; the Somalia explanation applies particularly to American reluctance, not uniformly to every potential intervening country. The chapter itself later describes France’s interests. Distinguish American disengagement from French commitment to its ally. [Human Rights Watch, “Choosing War”](https://www.hrw.org/legacy/reports/1999/rwanda/Geno1-3-11.htm).

2. **Factual error that distorts the intervention argument — high confidence.** “The killers were not an army.” “They were gangs with knives, who ran when anyone stood up to them.” Soldiers and gendarmes played a substantial role, using firearms, grenades and sometimes mortars. Some militia groups retreated before determined resistance, but that does not establish universal flight or an uncontested military solution. These sentences also violate the rule against absolutes that an exception breaks. [Human Rights Watch, “The Genocide”](https://www.hrw.org/reports/1999/rwanda/Geno1-3-02.htm).

3. **Incorrect comparison — high confidence.** “The dead were being added to at a rate of eight thousand a day, which is faster than the gas chambers had managed.” This compares Rwanda’s average with an unspecified Holocaust rate. Reconstruction of August–October 1942 gives approximately 1.32 million murders in 92 days, including about 292,000 shootings: the remaining camp killings alone exceeded 8,000 daily. Remove the comparison or specify genuinely comparable periods and methods. This is an external factual error, rather than a direct contradiction of chapter 82’s figures. [Lewi Stone, *Science Advances*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6314819/).

4. **Factual error — high confidence.** “The new government abolished the identity cards and made it an offence to speak of Hutu and Tutsi at all.” Rwanda removed **ethnic classifications from identity cards**, not identity cards themselves. Laws against discrimination, divisionism and genocide ideology restrict speech, but mentioning Hutu or Tutsi is not categorically illegal; official commemoration explicitly names the genocide against the Tutsi. [US State Department, Rwanda human-rights report](https://2021-2025.state.gov/reports/2022-country-reports-on-human-rights-practices/rwanda/).

5. **Factual error and direct contradiction with chapter 87 — high confidence.** “With allies from Uganda and Angola, it marched across a country the size of western Europe and overthrew the old dictator who had ruled Zaire since the murder of its first prime minister in 1961.” Mobutu’s continuous presidential dictatorship began in **1965**. Chapter 87 explicitly says: “Mobutu made himself president by a second coup in 1965 and ruled the country for thirty-two years.” Naming Mobutu here would also help readers reconnect this account with the earlier story. [Chapter 87, line 99](chapters/87-independence/chapter.md:99).

6. **Factual error — high confidence.** “The alliance of the Cold War, which had never fired a shot in anger, bombed the Bosnian Serb army for two weeks, and within three months the war was over.” NATO’s first combat engagement was on **28 February 1994**, when its aircraft shot down four aircraft violating the Bosnian no-fly zone. It also conducted air strikes before the 1995 campaign. Name NATO rather than requiring readers to identify “the alliance.” [NATO’s operational history](https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/operations-and-missions/peace-support-operations-in-bosnia-and-herzegovina-1995-2004).

7. **Misleading causal compression — high confidence.** “Srebrenica finally shamed the Western governments into acting.” Srebrenica helped change policy, but the immediate trigger for the major bombing campaign was the **28 August 1995 Sarajevo marketplace shelling**. Without that intervening event, readers will assume the bombing followed directly after the July massacre. [NATO, “Crossing the Rubicon”](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2005/07/01/crossing-the-rubicon).

8. **Factual error and consequential omission — high confidence.** “Kagame's troops advanced steadily from the north, took the capital on 4 July, and held the whole country two weeks later.” The French-led Operation Turquoise controlled a protected zone covering roughly one fifth of Rwanda in the southwest. It remained until August. The RPF’s victory ended the genocide, but “held the whole country” is wrong, and omitting Turquoise leaves France’s actual intervention out of a chapter about intervention. [UNAMIR’s official history](https://peacekeeping.un.org/sites/default/files/past/unamirFT.htm).

9. **Incorrect refugee total, destination and timescale — high confidence.** “The defeated government ordered the population to leave, and in a few days two million Hutu walked across the border into Zaire, the huge country to the west, carrying their mattresses and cooking pots.” More than two million was the regional refugee total by late August, including approximately **1.2 million in Zaire**, 580,000 in Tanzania and 270,000 in Burundi. The extraordinary flight into Goma occurred over several July days, but it was not the entire two-million-person exodus. The following paragraph also merges several camps into one settlement. [UNHCR, *The State of the World’s Refugees*, chapter 10](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/legacy-pdf/3ebf9bb60.pdf).

10. **Incorrect equivalence of mandates — high confidence.** “The United Nations sent peacekeepers with the same orders as in Rwanda.” Bosnia’s mandate was not identical. Security Council Resolution 836 authorized force in response to bombardment or armed incursions into safe areas, and authorized supporting air power. The distinction matters: failure to exercise an existing authority is different from lacking that authority. [Security Council Resolution 836](https://mandates.un.org/mandate/S/RES/836%281993%29?subject=Self-Defence+%28International+Law%29).

11. **Overstatement contradicted by the chapter’s own account — high confidence.** “Its commander had warned his superiors three months before, and was told to do nothing, and within two weeks most of his soldiers had been taken away from him.” Headquarters prohibited Dallaire’s proposed weapons raid, but instructed him to raise the information with the president and brief relevant ambassadors. The later fax section itself describes an instruction to act diplomatically. “Was forbidden to raid the arms caches” preserves the criticism accurately. [The UN’s reply of 11 January](https://cgi.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/evil/warning/unresponse.html).

12. **Misleading chronology — high confidence.** “A circle of powerful men around the president and his wife saw in that agreement the end of their own position, and they began to prepare a different solution.” The ensuing newspaper and militia examples read as developments after the August 1993 agreement. However, the Hutu Ten Commandments appeared in **December 1990**, and militia training was underway by early 1992. Explain that the peace agreement threatened a movement already cultivating hatred and violence. [ICTR’s media-case account](https://unictr.irmct.org/en/news/three-media-leaders-convicted-genocide); [Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/reports/1999/rwanda/Geno1-3-02.htm).

13. **Incorrect commander at the stated moment — high confidence.** “Its commander was a thin, austere officer in his thirties named Paul Kagame.” The October 1990 invasion was initially commanded by **Fred Rwigyema**. Kagame returned from studying abroad and took command after Rwigyema’s death. There is no need to add a lasting new character: simply make clear that Kagame subsequently became commander. [Clinton Presidential Library, RPF background](https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/research/archives/finding-aids/paul-kagame-and-rwanda-patriotic-front-rpf).

14. **Doubtful statistical certainty — high confidence that qualification is needed.** “Other researchers think the figure too high by half.” “Even at the lower number, it was the deadliest war anywhere since 1945, and it has never fully stopped.” The Congo mortality dispute cannot safely be reduced to two settled totals. The Human Security Report rejected the early surveys and estimated approximately 860,000 excess deaths for 2001–07, compared with the IRC’s 2.83 million for that period. A universal ranking does not survive merely halving the headline estimate. Identify the five-million figure as an estimate of **excess mortality**, explain that its baseline is disputed, and drop the unsupported ranking. [Human Rights Watch’s account of the competing estimates](https://www.hrw.org/reports/drc0910webwcover.pdf).

15. **Incorrect period and overly exact inference — high confidence.** “In 1993 Rwanda imported more than half a million machetes from China, which was enough for one in every three adult men.” The frequently cited evidence covers **January 1993–March 1994** and records approximately **581,000 kilograms** of machetes. The numerical conversion assumes an average weight of one kilogram; the associated comparison is with adult **Hutu** men. The sentence turns a weight-based estimate covering fifteen months into a firm count for one year. [Human Rights Watch, *Leave None to Tell the Story*](https://justice-survivors.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/leave-none-to-tell-the-story-genocide-in-rwanda-11.pdf).

16. **Incorrect withdrawal timing — high confidence.** “Belgium pulled out its whole contingent within a week and urged other countries to do the same.” Belgium announced withdrawal within a week of the 7 April murders, but the contingent’s final departure was **19 April**. Distinguish the decision from its completion. [UN *Juridical Yearbook*, account of the withdrawal](https://legal.un.org/unjuridicalyearbook/pdfs/english/by_chapter/chpVIII/2018/chpVIII.pdf).

17. **Reader-orientation problem.** “The man whom Rwanda had installed soon quarrelled with his sponsors, and in 1998 a second war began.” The previous paragraph never introduces this man or describes his installation. Readers must infer an entire transfer of power. Introduce Laurent-Désiré Kabila—or “the rebel leader installed as president”—before referring back to him, and briefly identify the opposing sides in the second war. “Nine African countries sent armies” otherwise supplies scale without explaining the conflict.

18. **Unexplained historical term and unclear allegation.** “They put it about that the Tutsi inside the country were all agents of the invaders, a fifth column that planned to enslave the Hutu once more.” “Fifth column” is not explained on first appearance, contrary to *style.md*. The “agents” wording already does its work, so the term can go. Also ensure that “enslave … once more” is unmistakably the extremists’ claim: the preceding account of cattle ownership and rank has not established a history of literal Hutu enslavement.

19. **Style: an absolute that erases exceptions.** “Then it spread to the hills, and it became the task of the whole population.” This collapses perpetrators, victims, rescuers, resisters and nonparticipants into “the whole population.” The accurate point is that officials mobilized civilians across the country. The chapter’s own rescue section supplies counterexamples; this is not an objection to its moral position.

20. **Style: unsupported absolute at the ending.** “There is nowhere else for either to go.” The preceding observation—that survivors and released perpetrators often live as neighbours—is strong enough. This sentence asserts that neither group has any possibility of moving, which the narrative has not established. Describe the conditions that keep these particular neighbours together rather than making a universal claim.
