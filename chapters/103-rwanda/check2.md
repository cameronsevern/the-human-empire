## Factual flags

1. **Major — the new transition puts the Yugoslavia tribunal after the war.**

   > “Once the killing was over, the response took a different form.”

   Followed by “Two international tribunals were set up,” this gives the wrong chronology. The Yugoslavia tribunal was established on **25 May 1993**, while the Bosnian war was continuing and more than two years before Srebrenica. The Rwanda tribunal was established on **8 November 1994**, after that genocide. Their establishment cannot collectively be placed after the killing ended. Sources: [ICTY establishment history](https://www.icty.org/en/about/tribunal/establishment), [ICTR founding documents](https://unictr.irmct.org/en/documents).

2. **Minor — the Yugoslavia tribunal was not confined to one war.**

   > “Both courts had been invented for a single war.”

   The ICTY’s jurisdiction covered serious crimes throughout the former Yugoslavia from 1991 onward. Its work encompassed separate conflicts in Croatia, Bosnia, Kosovo, and Macedonia. The accurate distinction is between **tribunals with geographically and temporally limited mandates** and a permanent court—not between single-war courts and a permanent one. Sources: [ICTY mandate](https://www.icty.org/en/about/tribunal/mandate-and-crimes-under-icty-jurisdiction), [prosecutor’s history](https://www.icty.org/en/about/office-of-the-prosecutor/history).

3. **Minor — Srebrenica’s protection was tested over several days.**

   > “The promise of safety was tested on a single day.”

   The final assault began on **6 July 1995** and continued until the town fell on **11 July**. Dutch observation posts were attacked during that advance. The fall occurred on the stated day; the test of the protection did not begin and end then. Source: [ICTY, *Blagojević and Jokić* judgment, account of 6–11 July](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/blagojevic_53/tjug/en/bla-050117e.pdf).

4. **Minor — incomplete records are not an absence of anyone recording deaths.**

   > “Counting such deaths is hard, since no one records them.”

   The agency invoked in the next sentence actually recorded reported deaths through household surveys. Its latest survey covered **14,000 households**. The difficulty was obtaining comprehensive, representative mortality information and estimating excess deaths, not that nobody recorded deaths at all. Source: [International Rescue Committee, *Mortality in the Democratic Republic of Congo: An Ongoing Crisis*, methods and executive summary](https://www.rescue.org/sites/default/files/document/661/2006-7congomortalitysurvey.pdf).

- **Announcement-style signposts**
  
  > “To understand how neighbours could do this to one another, it helps to see first how little divided them.”

  > “One school shows what that departure meant to the people left behind.”

  These announce what the coming material will demonstrate. The style sheet specifically prohibits that form of signposting; the examples themselves provide the explanation.

  The immediately preceding plan was reported in January; its alleged execution follows in April. “Almost at once” needs an anchor to the plane’s destruction, rather than appearing to refer to the informant’s warning.
