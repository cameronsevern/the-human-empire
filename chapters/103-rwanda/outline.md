# Chapter 103 — Why did nobody stop the genocide in Rwanda?

**Line under the title:** Rwanda, Bosnia and the Congo, from 1990 to 2003.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. Plain and restrained throughout.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Nobody stopped it because the countries that could have done so had no interest in Rwanda and had just lost soldiers in another African war. A United Nations force was there, its commander warned his superiors three months ahead, and he was told to do nothing; when the killing began most of his troops were taken away. Governments avoided the word genocide because a treaty they had signed in 1948 would have obliged them to act. Eight hundred thousand people were killed in a hundred days, mostly by their neighbours, at the urging of a radio station. The same failure was repeated in Bosnia a year later, in a town the United Nations had promised to protect, and the aftermath of Rwanda spilled into the Congo, where it killed several times as many. After the Cold War, the promise of "never again" turned out to depend on whether anyone powerful had a reason to keep it.

5. **Ten soldiers.** The first days: a prime minister, ten Belgians, and the lesson the killers had drawn from Somalia. The evacuation. A school.
6. **Work.** How it was done and by whom: roadblocks, churches, neighbours. The speed.
7. **Acts of genocide.** The word that governments would not use, and why. What five thousand soldiers could have done.
8. **A captain with a car.** Those who did act.
9. **Two million.** How it ended; the camps across the border; what the world then paid for.
10. **Africa's world war.** The Congo, 1996 to 2003.
11. **A safe area.** Bosnia: the break-up of Yugoslavia, a siege, and Srebrenica in July 1995. What finally brought intervention.
12. **Courts.** The tribunals; the grass courts; an apology that took three and a half hours; a general on a park bench.
13. **Ending: the same pump.**
