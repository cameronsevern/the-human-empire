I read the requested files and checked the main doubtful claims. Findings are ordered by seriousness. No files were edited.

1. **Unverified quotation presented as congressional testimony — high confidence.**  
   > “He now discovered that it caused insanity and murder, and told Congress that its users were mostly Negroes, Mexicans and entertainers, whose satanic music was driven by it.”

   The “satanic music” passage comes from an alleged Anslinger quotation whose provenance cannot be established. Penn State, which holds his papers, explicitly says the cited folder does not contain it. His documented racism does not authenticate these particular words or their attribution to Congress. Replace this with verified testimony. [Penn State Special Collections](https://psu.libanswers.com/faq/391108)

2. **Chronology reverses cause and consequence — high confidence.**  
   > “In 2009 the British government's chief adviser on drugs, a professor of pharmacology, published a ranking of twenty substances by the harm that they do to the user and to others.”

   The ranking placing alcohol above heroin and crack appeared in **2010**, after David Nutt’s dismissal in October 2009. The present sequence wrongly makes that publication the cause of his dismissal. [Original ranking](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21036393/). Nutt was dismissed following earlier public criticism of government drug policy. [Contemporary dismissal report](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2009/oct/30/drugs-adviser-david-nutt-sacked)

3. **Treaty described as a blanket prohibition — high confidence.**  
   > “In 1961 a United Nations convention bound nearly every government on Earth to treat the production and sale of opium, coca and cannabis as crimes.”

   The convention preserves controlled **medical and scientific** production and supply; its penal provisions concern activities contrary to the convention. Also, adoption in 1961 and eventual near-universal participation are different events. This distinction is central to the chapter’s question, especially when legal opioids appear later. [Single Convention, articles 4 and 36](https://www.euda.europa.eu/drugs-library/single-convention-narcotic-drugs-1961_en)

4. **Overconfident attribution of political murders — high confidence that revision is necessary.**  
   > “To stop the government from sending him to the United States for trial, he had three presidential candidates murdered and a passenger aircraft blown up in the air with a hundred and seven people on board.”

   The three-candidate claim treats distinct assassinations as settled Escobar operations. In Carlos Pizarro’s case, judicial findings instead implicate paramilitaries and security-service personnel; investigators have examined efforts to divert blame towards Escobar. Retain a securely documented individual case rather than this collective attribution. [2023 judgment](https://files.lafm.com.co/assets/public/2023-09/186SENTENCIA2022-00144%20%286%29.pdf); [report on the investigation](https://elpais.com/america-colombia/2023-09-01/la-fiscalia-abre-una-investigacion-contra-miguel-maza-marquez-por-el-magnicidio-de-carlos-pizarro-mas-de-30-anos-despues.html?comments_container=true)

5. **The opioid death total changes categories without warning — high confidence.**  
   > “Over a million have died since the pill was launched.”

   In this context, readers will understand a million deaths caused by OxyContin or by opioids. The familiar million-plus figure covers **all drug overdoses**, including deaths involving stimulants and other drugs. The preceding 17,000 and 108,000 figures also cover all drugs. State the category explicitly and distinguish Purdue’s contribution from the entire epidemic. [CDC overdose data](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db522.htm); [CDC account of the opioid epidemic](https://www.cdc.gov/overdose-prevention/about/understanding-the-opioid-overdose-epidemic.html)

6. **Oregon comparison omits its treatment programme and misdates implementation — high confidence.**  
   > “The American state of Oregon removed the penalties for possessing small amounts of any drug in 2020, without Portugal's clinics, saw people using in the streets and deaths rise, and restored the penalties four years later.”

   Voters approved the measure in 2020; decriminalisation began in **February 2021**. Civil penalties remained. The measure also funded treatment and recovery services, although implementation was badly delayed. “Without Portugal’s clinics” makes this sound like an experiment deliberately conducted without treatment provision. Describe the delayed rollout instead. [Oregon Judicial Department](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/about/Pages/reports-measures.aspx); [report on implementation and reversal](https://apnews.com/article/96e36ed60e999572bbf47c160b412a73)

7. **Different cannabis regimes collapsed into “fully legal” — high confidence.**  
   > “Uruguay in 2013 became the first country to make cannabis fully legal, and Canada, Germany and about half the American states have followed.”

   Germany legalised limited adult possession, home cultivation and noncommercial cultivation associations, not ordinary commercial retail supply. “Fully legal” is also too absolute for regulated systems with age, quantity and supply restrictions. Separate legal adult use from legal commercial sales. [German Health Ministry explanation](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/en/themen/cannabis/faq-cannabis-act)

8. **Portugal’s outcomes stated too absolutely — high confidence.**  
   > “Use did not rise.”

   Some measures did rise: reported lifetime use increased between 2001 and 2007, and changes differed by age and measure. The defensible conclusion is that decriminalisation did not produce the feared major surge, while several measures of harmful use improved. That is different from saying no use measure increased. [Hughes and Stevens’ assessment](https://undrugcontrol.info/images/stories/documents/BJC-portugal-decrim.pdf)

9. **Dependence confused with any subsequent use — high confidence.**  
   > “Back at home, away from the war and the boredom and the cheap supply, more than nine in ten of them simply stopped.”

   The striking finding concerns **remaining dependent or becoming dependent again**, not universal abstinence. Some veterans used opiates after returning without resuming dependence. Give the follow-up period and say that most were no longer dependent. [Original follow-up study](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article-abstract/99/4/235/138453)

10. **Imprisonment ranking is outdated — high confidence.**  
    > “No country locks up a larger share of its people.”

    This is false for the chapter’s 2025 endpoint: El Salvador and other countries exceeded the US rate. Either attach the claim to a historical year when it was true or say the US has one of the highest rates. This also violates the rule against exception-breaking absolutes. [World Prison Brief ranking](https://www.prisonstudies.org/world-prison-data/highest-lowest/highest-lowest-prison-population-rate?language_content_entity=en-gb)

11. **Contradiction with chapter 65 — high confidence.**  
    > “In the nineteenth century hardly any of this was illegal anywhere.”

    Chapter 65 explicitly describes Chinese opium bans, smugglers, arrests and the destruction of prohibited imports. It also describes Taiping prohibitions on opium, alcohol and tobacco. China is too substantial an exception for this worldwide formulation. Restrict the statement to the particular Western markets being described. [Chapter 65]( chapters/65-opium/chapter.md)

12. **Prescription counts confused with quantities sold — high confidence.**  
    > “Prescriptions of opiate painkillers in the United States quadrupled in ten years.”

    The commonly cited fourfold increase is in opioid analgesic **sales measured in kilograms per population**, between 1999 and 2010, not the number of prescriptions. More prescriptions, stronger doses and longer prescriptions are different quantities. [CDC’s explicit definition](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db189.htm)

13. **Wrong account of the cannabis hearings — high confidence.**  
    > “A federal law against it passed in 1937 after a hearing of little more than an hour.”

    House hearings ran on **five separate days**, followed by a Senate hearing. A short debate or individual session should not be substituted for the legislative hearing process. [Hearing transcripts and dates](https://www.druglibrary.net/schaffer/hemp/taxact/taxact.htm)

14. **Trafficking and possession penalties blurred — high confidence.**  
    > “Five grams of crack, the weight of two sugar lumps, brought five years.”

    The 1986 five-gram threshold concerned federal **trafficking offences**, including possession with intent to distribute. The mandatory minimum for simple crack possession arrived in 1988. Specify the offence; otherwise the following powder comparison teaches the wrong legal rule. [US Sentencing Commission history](https://www.ussc.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/research-and-publications/research-publications/2017/20170711_Mand-Min.pdf)

15. **A single brain mechanism is made universal — high confidence.**  
    > “Every drug that people take for pleasure works on this signal, by one route or another.”

    Classic psychedelics are a substantial exception to this account of pleasure-seeking drugs as dopamine-reward stimulants: their principal action is through serotonin receptors, and they do not typically produce addiction. Limit the explanation to the addictive substances it actually describes. [NIH explanation](https://health.nih.gov/health-topics-a-z/psychedelic-drugs)

16. **The rat example promises an inevitable outcome — medium-high confidence.**  
    > “In an experiment that has been repeated many times, a rat given a lever that delivers cocaine will press it until it starves.”

    Cocaine self-administration depends on dose, access schedule and experimental conditions; mortality is not an inevitable result, and death from cocaine toxicity is not proof of starvation. Research explicitly examining food intake and lethality found differing outcomes under different administration conditions. Use a precisely sourced experiment rather than this universal version. [Experimental study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7770601/)

17. **Addiction and physical dependence need separating — high confidence.**  
    > “When the drug is withdrawn, the circuit that was being stimulated falls silent, and they feel wretched until they take it again.”

    Withdrawal is not simply a reward circuit switching off. More importantly for readers, tolerance and withdrawal can occur during appropriate medical treatment without addiction; addiction involves compulsive use despite harm. The paragraph currently makes these interchangeable. [NIDA treatment principles](https://nida.nih.gov/sites/default/files/podat-3rdEd-508.pdf)

18. **The ending exaggerates its own supporting study — high confidence.**  
    > “It was cited more than six hundred times over the next thirty years, almost always as proof that such drugs were rarely addictive.”

    The 2017 analysis found **608 citations over roughly 37 years**, of which **72.2%** used it as evidence that addiction was rare. “Almost always” materially overstates that proportion. [Original citation analysis](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMc1700150)

19. **The two Purdue guilty pleas are inaccurately merged — high confidence.**  
    > “The company twice admitted to criminal charges of deceiving doctors.”

    The 2007 case concerned misleading marketing and misbranding. The 2020 pleas concerned a conspiracy to defraud the United States and violate drug law, plus kickback conspiracies. “Twice admitted … deceiving doctors” is not an accurate description of both cases. [Justice Department account of the 2020 pleas](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/opioid-manufacturer-purdue-pharma-pleads-guilty-fraud-and-kickback-conspiracies)

20. **“First” contradicts the chapter’s own treatment of alcohol — high confidence.**  
    > “The first American law against a drug was passed in San Francisco in 1875.”

    Alcohol is explicitly a drug here, and prohibitory alcohol laws came earlier: Maine prohibited manufacture and sale in 1851. Qualify the San Francisco claim as an early prohibition targeting **non-alcoholic recreational drugs**, or simply describe the ordinance without claiming priority. [Maine’s official history](https://www.maine.gov/sos/kids/about/history)

21. **Women’s political history is compressed into a false priority claim — high confidence.**  
    > “The campaign against the saloon was therefore the first great political movement of American women.”

    Women had already organised extensively against slavery and for women’s rights. Temperance was one of several overlapping movements, not an uncontested first. [National Park Service history](https://www.nps.gov/wori/learn/historyculture/abolition-womens-rights-and-temperance-movements.htm)

22. **The central opioid story withholds the names readers need.**  
    > “In 1996 that company brought out a pill containing a large dose of a synthetic relative of morphine, designed to release it slowly over twelve hours.”

    The chapter never identifies **Purdue Pharma, OxyContin or the Sackler family**. These are recurring subjects, exactly the names that style.md permits because readers need them to follow the story. Their omission makes the account feel like a riddle.

23. **Fentanyl is unnamed, and “replaced” obscures what happened — high confidence on the factual distinction.**  
    > “After 2013 the heroin was replaced by a laboratory-made substance fifty times stronger, a few grains of which can kill.”

    Name fentanyl. Its growing presence involved adulteration and substitution, with overlapping heroin and synthetic-opioid markets—not a clean replacement beginning in 2013. Explain that dealers increasingly mixed it into or sold it instead of heroin. [CDC’s account of the overlapping waves](https://www.cdc.gov/overdose-prevention/about/understanding-the-opioid-overdose-epidemic.html)

24. **A missing explanation makes Portugal’s alternative hard to understand.**  
    > “The money saved on police and prisons was spent on clinics.”

    “Clinics” does not explain what changed for someone dependent on heroin. A brief account of medication such as methadone, medical support and outreach would connect policy to outcomes. The reader otherwise jumps from ending criminal penalties to falling infections without seeing the mechanism. [OECD description of Portugal’s treatment system](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/addressing-problematic-opioid-use-in-oecd-countries_a18286f0-en/full-report/component-7.html)

25. **Explicit cross-reference breaks style.md.**  
    > “Wine, the coca leaf of the Andes, tobacco, opium, tea and coffee have all appeared in this story, usually as something that a government was taxing or fighting over.”

    “Have all appeared in this story” refers to the book’s presentation rather than the historical events. Chapters 53 and 65 already supply the background; simply describe governments taxing and fighting over these substances.

I found no additional clear contradictions with chapters 53 or 95, no hedging stacks, and no explicit chapter-self-reference. I would not flag ordinary transitions or confident judgments merely for their tone.
