# Chapter 106 — Why are some drugs illegal?

**Line under the title:** The United States and the world, from 1874 to 2025.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Which drugs are legal has depended far more on who used them, who sold them and when the law was written than on how much harm they do. People in every society in this book have taken something to change how they feel, and states have taxed it, traded it and banned it, sometimes all at once. The United States banned alcohol in 1920, found that it had created a murderous black market, and gave up after thirteen years. It then applied the same policy to opium, cocaine and cannabis, exported it to the world by treaty, and has kept it up for a century, at the cost of crowded prisons at home and hundreds of thousands of dead in the countries that grow and ship the drugs. The deadliest drug epidemic in American history was started by a legal painkiller sold by a respectable company. Bans do reduce use. Every arrangement tried so far has had a heavy price.
