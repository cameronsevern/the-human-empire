# Chapter 58 — How did the slaves of Haiti win their freedom?

**Line under the title:** Saint-Domingue, the French colony that became Haiti, from 1789 to 1825.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The enslaved people of France's richest colony freed themselves by war. They rose in 1791, when the French Revolution had set their masters quarrelling, and over thirteen years they outfought the planters, a Spanish army, a British army and the largest expedition Napoleon ever sent overseas. Their leaders were former slaves, many of their soldiers had learned war in Africa, and yellow fever fought on their side. France abolished slavery in 1794 because the insurgents had already ended it on the ground. Haiti became the first country founded by former slaves, and the rest of the slave-owning world punished it for the example: it was shunned for decades, and in 1825 France made it pay for the property the planters had lost, which included the Haitians themselves.

## The story

1. **Opening: the plain on fire.** Answer first. The night of 22 August 1791.
2. **The pearl.** The colony in 1789: its wealth, its three kinds of people, and why most of its slaves had been born in Africa.
3. **Which men?** The news from Paris. The planters want self-rule, the free people of colour want equality, and nobody mentions the half million.
4. **The opening.** The rising takes shape. Who the rebels were. A coachman of nearly fifty who became their general.
5. **A decree in the north.** 1793: France at war with everyone. A French commissioner frees the slaves to win an army, and Paris confirms it.
6. **Fever.** A British army comes to take the colony and dies. Toussaint as ruler, and his problem: freedom or sugar.
7. **The trunk of the tree.** Napoleon's expedition of 1802. A dinner invitation, a ship, a cell in the mountains.
8. **Roots.** The news that slavery is coming back. Dessalines, the fever again, the last battle. The first of January 1804.
9. **The bill.** What Haiti changed: Louisiana, the fear among slave owners, the end of the British slave trade. Warships in 1825, and a debt paid until 1947.
