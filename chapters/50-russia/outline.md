# Chapter 50 — How did Russia get so big?

**Line under the title:** Siberia, China and Central Asia, from 1552 to 1771.
**Length:** 4,300 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Russia crossed Asia in about sixty years because it was chasing fur along a network of rivers through country where nobody had guns or a state to stop it. The trappers and Cossacks collected sable skins as tribute, held hostages to make sure of them, and built a wooden fort at every river crossing. They were stopped on the Amur by the other great expanding power of the age, the Manchus, a people from the north-east who had just conquered China. The two empires settled their border by treaty, with Jesuit priests translating into Latin, and then each was free to deal with the horse peoples caught between them. For two thousand years mounted archers from the grasslands had been the terror of every farming state. Now forts, firearms, wagonloads of grain and agreed frontiers left them nowhere to retreat. The last free steppe empire, the Zunghars, was wiped out by a Qing army and smallpox in the 1750s. The lines drawn then are still, more or less, the borders of Russia and China.

## The story

1. **Opening: soft gold.** Answer first. What a sable skin was worth and who wanted it.
2. **Eight hundred men.** A merchant family, a Cossack captain, and the mountains nobody had bothered to cross. Rivers, portages and wooden forts, all the way to the Pacific.
3. **A skin from every man.** The fur tribute and the hostage hut. The hunter's side and the Cossack's.
4. **Keep your hair or keep your head.** The Manchus: banners, the fall of the Ming, the gate in the wall, and how a million ruled a hundred and fifty million.
5. **A treaty in Latin.** The fort on the Amur and the meeting at Nerchinsk.
6. **Grain across the desert.** The Zunghars and their lama-prince. An emperor marches across the Gobi. Why supply beat speed, and the Russian wall of forts against the slave raiders.
7. **The new frontier.** The destruction of the Zunghars, and the word genocide. Tibet, Mongolia and the far west.
8. **Ending: the people who came back.**
