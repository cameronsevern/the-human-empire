# Chapter 42 — How did a few hundred Spaniards conquer the Aztecs?

**Line under the title:** Central Mexico, from 1428 to about 1560.
**Length:** 4,200 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

They did not do it by themselves. The Mexica, whom we call Aztecs, ruled an empire of resentful tribute-payers and had one stubborn enemy, Tlaxcala, which they had kept surrounded for generations as a source of captives for sacrifice. When a small Spanish force arrived with steel, horses and a woman who could translate, the Tlaxcalans and then many others decided to use the strangers to destroy their overlord. The army that took the Mexica capital in 1521 was overwhelmingly native. An epidemic of smallpox, brought by the Spanish, had already killed a great part of the defenders. The allies thought they were winning their own war. Their later petitions to the king of Spain show what they believed they had been promised, and how little of it they kept.

2. **The city on the lake.** Tenochtitlan: its size, causeways, market and tribute lists.
3. **Feeding the sun.** Why the Mexica sacrificed people, what has been dug up, how the need for captives shaped their wars, and Tlaxcala without salt.
4. **A woman who spoke three languages.** Cortés lands against orders. What he had. Malintzin. Tlaxcala fights him, then weighs him up.
5. **Guests with a hostage.** The entry into the city, what Moctezuma was thinking, the massacre at the festival, and the flight by night.
6. **The great rash.** Smallpox in the valley.
7. **Thirteen boats over the mountains.** The siege: the lake, the water pipe, the second city that changed sides, and who did the fighting.
8. **Letters to the king.** What the allies expected and what they got. The dying that followed.
9. **Ending: the smallest state.**
