# Chapter 23 — How did Rome become so powerful?

**Line under the title:** Italy, Sicily, Spain and North Africa, from about 2,360 to about 2,170 years ago.
**Length:** 4,500 to 5,200 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Rome did not tax the Italians it defeated. It asked them for soldiers, and it let some of them, and even its own freed slaves, become Romans. An empire that takes silver makes its subjects poorer and gives them a reason to wait for its fall. An empire that takes soldiers and shares the loot gives them a reason to turn up. The result was a reserve of men that no rival could match, and it is the reason Rome could lose a fleet to a storm or fifty thousand men in an afternoon and still be at war the next spring. Carthage paid its soldiers and Hannibal never lost a battle in Italy; Rome was beaten again and again and won the war. The arrangement had one flaw that it carried from the start: it produced nothing for Rome unless there was a war, and half of every army had no vote.

## The story

1. **Opening: the afternoon at Cannae.** Answer first. Hannibal destroys the largest army Rome had ever fielded and waits for envoys. None come. The Senate refuses to ransom its prisoners and buys slaves to arm. A Greek hostage, Polybius, later asks how this city did it, and writes down the count of men.
2. **Soldiers instead of tribute.** A small republic on the Tiber. The settlement after the Latin war: separate treaties, grades of citizenship, allies who owe men and pay no tax. Freed slaves become citizens; the letter of the king of Macedon. Colonies and roads. Why the system needed a war every year. Who fought and for what: nobles for glory, farmers for loot and land.
3. **A king who won.** Pyrrhus beats Rome twice and goes home. The pattern is set.
4. **The raven.** Carthage: a trading city with a hired army. The war for Sicily. Rome copies a wrecked warship, trains rowers on dry land, and fits a boarding bridge. Fleets lost to storms and rebuilt. The rams on the seabed. Sicily taxed in grain: the first subjects who pay instead of serve. Carthage cannot pay its soldiers.
5. **Thirty-seven elephants.** Hannibal's plan is aimed at the allies, not the city. The Alps. He sends allied prisoners home free. How Cannae worked. Capua and the south go over.
6. **Eighteen colonies.** Why the centre held. The Delayer. The cost: twelve colonies say they have nothing left. Capua's punishment. Scipio in Spain and Africa; Zama.
7. **The historian at the fire.** Fifty years on, Carthage is destroyed with Polybius watching. No Carthaginian account survives. Slaves and silver pour into Italy. The allies who carried half the load still have no vote.

## Left for later

The Gracchi, Marius, the war with the allies, Caesar and Augustus. Roman law and the citizen as a holder of rights. Roman rule in the Greek east and Judea.
