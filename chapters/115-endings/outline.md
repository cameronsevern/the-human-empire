# Chapter 115 — How do empires end?

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Empires end as governments. The people, the language, the fields, the roads and the skills mostly carry on, usually under a successor that keeps what worked. What fails is one or more of four things: the money to pay the soldiers, the belief that the rulers have a right to rule, the land and health the whole thing stands on, or the ability to withstand a blow from outside. The quarrel that runs through this story, between those who explain history by materials and those who explain it by ideas, has an answer of sorts: materials set what is possible, and belief decides whether people will fight, obey or refuse, which is what actually ends a government. The present world is different from every earlier empire in two ways. It has no outside: there is no neighbour to carry on if it fails. And it can see itself, which none of the others could.

## The story

1. **Opening: a boy sent to the country.** Answer first. Italy, 476: an ending that nobody noticed.
2. **Four ways to fall.** Money, belief, the ground underfoot, the blow from outside, each with endings already told. Why it is usually more than one.
3. **What comes through.** Roads, laws, languages, crops, borders. The same farmer under seven empires. The successors who kept the machinery.
4. **When it was the people who ended.** The exceptions: conquest that was also extermination.
5. **Iron and belief.** The long argument between materials and ideas, weighed on the evidence: what materials decide, what belief decides; armies that lost with the better weapons; crowds that won with none. The cost of belief.
6. **No outside.** What is new: one economy, one air, one network. What used to happen when an empire fell, and why it cannot happen that way again.
7. **A species that can count.** The other new thing: measurement and memory. What has already been done with them.
8. **What a fall would look like.** Guesswork, marked as such: the fast ways and the slow way. What the slow way looked like the last time, in the rubbish pits of Britain.
9. **Ending: an ape that keeps records.**
