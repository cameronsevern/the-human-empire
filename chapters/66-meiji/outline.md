# Chapter 66 — How did Japan catch up so fast?

**Line under the title:** Japan, from 1853 to 1905.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Japan was forced open by American warships in 1853 and had watched what the same kind of ships had done to China. Fifteen years later a group of young samurai from two outlying domains overthrew the shogun in the emperor's name, and then did the opposite of what their slogans had promised: they set out to learn everything the West knew. They sent half the government abroad to look, hired thousands of foreign experts, and in a decade abolished the lords, the samurai class, and the old taxes, and created a conscript army, schools for every child, railways and factories. They could do it because Japan was already literate, orderly and commercial, and because they wrapped a revolution in the oldest authority the country had. Fifty-two years after the warships came, Japan sank the Russian fleet. It had also learned empire.

## The story

1. **Opening: a train on a beach.** Answer first. March 1854: American sailors set up a miniature railway, and a samurai official rides on the roof.
2. **Black ships.** The squadron of 1853. What the shogun's government already knew about China. The treaties.
3. **Expel the barbarians.** A slogan, a wave of killings, and two domains that fire on foreign ships and are shelled in return. Five young men smuggled to London.
4. **Restoration.** January 1868: the coup in the emperor's name. A boy of fifteen. Edo becomes Tokyo. "Knowledge shall be sought throughout the world."
5. **Half the government.** The embassy of 1871 to 1873: what it went to see and what it concluded. A girl of six sent to America. Foreign experts by the thousand.
6. **Topknots.** Ten years of decrees: no more domains, no more classes, a tax in money, an army of conscripts, schools, a railway, a new calendar.
7. **The last charge.** 1877: the samurai rebel under the hero of the restoration, and are beaten by farmers' sons with rifles.
8. **Rich country, strong army.** Silk and the mill girls. Factories built by the state and sold to friends. A constitution from Prussia and a cult of the emperor. Why Japan could do what China could not.
9. **Ending: the strait.** War with China, then with Russia. May 1905. What Asia made of it, and what Japan did next to Korea.

## Left for later

Japan's empire in Korea and China. The Pacific war. Japan's recovery after 1945.

## To verify

- Perry: dates of both visits, number of ships and how many were steamers, the president's letter, the gifts including the model locomotive (scale, track, speed) and the telegraph, and the description of an official riding on the carriage roof. The treaty of March 1854 and its ports. The treaty of 1858 and its terms.
- The slogan. The assassination of 1860. The firing on ships in 1863 and the bombardments of 1863 and 1864. The five students of 1863 and who they became.
- January 1868: who acted, the emperor's age, the reign name, the fate of the shogun, the renaming of Edo. The oath's fifth article.
- The embassy: dates, numbers, countries, its failure to revise the treaties, its conclusions about how recent Western wealth was, the meeting with Bismarck. The girl sent to America: name, age, later life.
- Foreign experts: numbers. Which country served as the model for the navy, the army, the constitution.
- Reforms: 1871 domains abolished; class distinctions ended; 1872 schooling and the first railway; 1873 conscription, the land tax and its rate, the calendar; 1876 the sword ban and the end of stipends.
- 1877: the leader, numbers, the last stand and date.
- The state silk mill of 1872; the sale of state factories; silk's share of exports. The constitution of 1889 and the share of people who could vote. The education decree of 1890.
- The war of 1894 to 1895: cause, the treaty, Taiwan, the indemnity. The end of consular courts (1899). The alliance of 1902.
- The war of 1904 to 1905: the Russian fleet's voyage (distance and months), the battle of May 1905 (ships lost on each side), the reaction in Asia. Korea's annexation (1910).
