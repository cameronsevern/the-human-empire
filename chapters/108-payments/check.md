I checked the chapter against the requested readings and treated 2025 as its endpoint. No files were edited. Findings are ordered by seriousness.

1. **Misleading central explanation — high confidence.** “Nothing goes anywhere.” And: “When a card is tapped, a message is sent, and numbers are changed in the books of two banks.” Physical cash does not travel, but money does transfer: bank deposits and interbank settlement balances are money. The tap normally authorises a payment; settlement follows later, as the chapter itself subsequently explains. Distinguish authorisation, movement of deposits and settlement rather than equating “electronic” with “nothing moves”. [Bank of England explanation](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/explainers/how-is-money-created?sf129973328=1).

2. **False historical premise; prohibited absolute — high confidence.** “China never had the card.” China had approximately **4.21 billion bank cards in circulation in 2013**, before the red-envelope campaign described here. Mobile payments overtook an existing banking and card infrastructure; China did not simply skip cards. This also makes the later reference to users linking their bank accounts unnecessarily puzzling. [People’s Bank of China statistics](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/eportal/fileDir/image_public/UserFiles/goutongjiaoliu/upload/File/2013%E5%B9%B4%E7%AC%AC%E5%9B%9B%E5%AD%A3%E5%BA%A6%E6%94%AF%E4%BB%98%E4%BD%93%E7%B3%BB%E8%BF%90%E8%A1%8C%E6%80%BB%E4%BD%93%E6%83%85%E5%86%B5.pdf).

3. **Anachronistic foundation for India’s story — high confidence.** “The government had issued more than a billion Indians with an identity number tied to their fingerprints and the pattern of their eyes, and it had opened basic bank accounts for half a billion people who had never had one.” The account total belongs years after the 2016 launch. Even by December 2016, Jan Dhan had opened about **260 million accounts**, not 500 million. Moreover, accounts opened are not automatically a count of distinct, previously unbanked people. [Indian government figures](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1479788&lang=2&reg=48).

4. **Badly understated transaction value — high confidence.** “Today nearly every adult does, and a sum equal to about half of everything the country produces passes through it each year.” Safaricom reported roughly **KSh38.3 trillion** in annual M-Pesa transactions for FY2025; Kenya’s 2024 GDP was roughly **KSh16.2 trillion**. That is more than twice GDP, not half. Transaction turnover counts money repeatedly, so this comparison also needs a brief explanation. [Safaricom financial statements](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/annualreport_2025/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/safaricom-financial-statements.pdf), [Kenyan Treasury GDP statement](https://www.treasury.go.ke/official-launch-economic-survey-2025).

5. **Wrong sanctions chronology and misleading equivalence — high confidence.** “The same month, the Western powers cut the largest Russian banks off from the messaging system through which the world's banks send one another payment instructions.” The initial seven disconnections happened on **12 March 2022**, following February announcements; the largest bank, Sberbank, was disconnected in June. “One was done to protesters in a democracy and the other to a state that had invaded its neighbour, and the tool was the same.” The mechanisms differed: freezing accounts prevents access to funds; disconnecting SWIFT removes a messaging channel. Neither distinction undermines the political comparison, but both matter to its accuracy. [SWIFT’s account](https://www.swift.com/news-events/news/message-swift-community), [EU announcement](https://enlargement.ec.europa.eu/news/ukraine-eu-agrees-exclude-key-russian-banks-swift-2022-03-02_en).

6. **Absolutes that defeat the privacy argument — high confidence.** “Cash leaves no trace.” And: “A person who pays in notes cannot be profiled, sold to advertisers, or switched off.” A cash purchaser can still identify themselves through a loyalty account, a delivery address or a named booking. Cash avoids an automatic bank-payment trail; it does not guarantee anonymity or immunity from exclusion. These directly break the rule against absolutes that one exception disproves.

7. **Disputed origin story presented as established fact — high confidence that it is doubtful.** “In 1949 a New York businessman finished dinner at a restaurant and found that he had left his wallet in another suit.” Diners Club’s forgotten-wallet story is contested, including by its early publicist Matty Simmons. The secure historical event is the establishment of Diners Club in 1950; the dinner should not carry the narrative as verified history. This is precisely where `style.md` says to replace a weak example rather than discuss its reliability in the body. [Historical research discussing the conflicting accounts](https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/d68bb2c4-c08d-4499-b312-6db1883c9d4a/content).

8. **The ending reverses its own meaning — certain.** “On the list, in the least cashless country on Earth, was a supply of banknotes and coins sufficient for a week.” “Least cashless” means most dependent on cash, contradicting the preceding paragraph. “One of the most cashless” would preserve the intended point without adding an unsupported world ranking.

9. **Unsupported inference about frozen accounts — high confidence.** “About two hundred and eighty accounts were frozen.” And: “Those people could not buy food or fuel or pay their rent.” The official figure was approximately **280 financial products**, including accounts, credit cards and credit lines—not 280 people. The evidence does not establish that every affected person had no other means of purchasing necessities. State the restriction on access to frozen funds rather than asserting universal consequences. [Canadian parliamentary testimony](https://www.parl.ca/Content/Committee/441/DEDC/Evidence/EV11880029/DEDCEV10-E.PDF).

10. **Creditworthiness equated with wealth — high confidence.** “The issuing banks use much of their share to pay for the air miles and cash rewards that persuade people to use their cards, and the most generous rewards go to the customers with the best credit, who are the richest.” High credit scores and high incomes are different attributes. Federal Reserve research finds redistribution between lower- and higher-score cardholders across income groups. The cash-to-rewards subsidy argument can stand without identifying the best-credit customers as necessarily the richest. [Federal Reserve research](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/who-pays-for-your-rewards-redistribution-in-the-credit-card-market.htm).

11. **Uncertain ownership presented as certain; “never” has counterexamples — high confidence.** “Nobody has established who he, she or they were, and the million or so coins that he created in the first months have never been touched.” The large holding attributed to Satoshi is an estimate, not an established inventory. Some coins demonstrably associated with Satoshi were transferred, including the early payment to Hal Finney. Distinguish the estimated dormant holding from all coins Satoshi mined. [Research on early mining and transactions](https://aidenlab.org/bitcoin.pdf).

12. **Demonetisation’s immediate UPI effect overstated — medium-high confidence.** “It did push tens of millions of people onto the new system.” The immediate growth was in digital payments broadly, including wallets and cards. RBI figures put UPI at roughly **two million transactions in December 2016** and four million monthly in early 2017—transactions, not distinct users. Tens of millions adopting UPI specifically because of demonetisation needs separate evidence and a stated timescale. [RBI assessment](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/PublicationsView.aspx?id=17447).

13. **M-Pesa’s early growth is accelerated by a year — high confidence.** “Within two years nine million Kenyans were using it.” Safaricom reported about **6.2 million registered users in March 2009**, two years after launch. The nine-million milestone belongs closer to its third anniversary. “Registered users” also differs from active users. [Safaricom’s 2009 annual report](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/images/2009_annual_report.pdf).

14. **Outdated global inclusion figure — high confidence.** “About one and a half billion adults in the world still have no account of any kind.” The World Bank’s 2025 report, using 2024 data, gives **1.3 billion adults** without financial accounts. This fits the chapter’s stated endpoint and should replace the undated figure. [World Bank release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/07/16/mobile-phone-technology-powers-saving-surge-in-developing-economies).

15. **Outdated present-tense robot count — high confidence.** “The company employs a million and a half people and three quarters of a million robots.” Amazon announced its **millionth deployed robot in June 2025**. Either date the earlier figure or use the 2025 figure consistently with the chapter’s endpoint. [Amazon announcement](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/operations/amazon-million-robots-ai-foundation-model).

16. **Potential confusion with chapter 86 — high confidence as a reader problem, not a strict contradiction.** “A banknote is a promise printed by a government.” Chapter 86 has already explained: “A dollar was now a promise of nothing except another dollar.” The new sentence leaves “a promise of what?” unanswered and risks reviving the idea of convertibility into gold. Explain acceptance as money or the issuing institution’s liability. I found no unequivocal contradiction with chapters 86, 102 or 107 beyond this unresolved wording.

17. **The M-Pesa explanation omits the backing — high confidence.** “What the Kenyan telephone company had built was a bank ledger, held in a computer and operated from a keypad.” A reader can reasonably infer that Safaricom simply became a bank or created spendable money by typing numbers. M-Pesa is an electronic-money transfer service, with customer funds safeguarded through bank arrangements. One sentence explaining where the deposited cash is held would complete the mechanism and distinguish it from bank lending. [World Bank discussion of safeguarding electronic money](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/365901468337465944).

18. **The main Indian institution is never identified — high confidence.** “In 2016 a body set up by the central bank and owned by the country's banks switched on a payments system that worked like a public road.” Neither **UPI** nor its operator, **NPCI**, is subsequently named. Unlike an incidental inventor, this system is the subject of the whole section and the reader needs a name to attach to it. Explain UPI once in plain language; otherwise repeated references to “it” and “the new system” become difficult to retain.

19. **Bitcoin’s mechanism starts halfway through — high confidence.** “The idea was to give the ledger to everyone.” The following explanation covers copying the record and choosing who adds a block, but never explains how someone authorises a payment or why another person cannot spend their coins. A short explanation of the owner’s secret key and verifiable signature would connect this section to the earlier two-key explanation. Also explain that the network rejects attempts to spend the same coins twice. [Bitcoin’s original paper, sections 2–3](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf).

20. **Unclear party count — high confidence.** “In that second a message makes a round trip among four parties.” The ensuing route contains the shop, its bank, the network and the issuing bank; the cardholder is also present. Meanwhile, the conventional “four-party” card model counts the cardholder, merchant, issuer and acquirer, with the network connecting them. Explicitly identify the four intended participants or omit the count: the reader should not have to reconcile two different ways of counting.

21. **An invention does not support the sentence introducing it — high confidence.** “Two inventions made the system fast.” The magnetic stripe helps explain card processing; the cash machine explains access to cash. Nothing subsequently shows how the ATM accelerated the card-payment system. Either supply that connection or let the cash machine enter as a separate development. This is a broken explanatory transition, rather than an objection to a confident narrative voice.

I found no clear hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-mentions, prohibited chapter cross-references or standalone announcement-style signposts. The substantial style violations are the exception-prone absolutes identified above.
