## Factual flags

1. **Major — China’s card history is again overstated.**

   > “China largely skipped the card, and went straight from cash to the telephone.”

   The new “went straight” clause reintroduces the historical problem identified in the earlier check. This is not merely a question of cards being issued but unused: the People’s Bank of China recorded approximately **3.92 billion card purchase transactions in the fourth quarter of 2013**, before WeChat’s red-envelope campaign. Mobile payments expanded over an established card and banking infrastructure. [PBOC’s fourth-quarter 2013 payment statistics](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/eportal/fileDir/image_public/UserFiles/goutongjiaoliu/upload/File/2013%E5%B9%B4%E7%AC%AC%E5%9B%9B%E5%AD%A3%E5%BA%A6%E6%94%AF%E4%BB%98%E4%BD%93%E7%B3%BB%E8%BF%90%E8%A1%8C%E6%80%BB%E4%BD%93%E6%83%85%E5%86%B5.pdf).

2. **Major — UPI’s supporting programmes become necessary conditions.**

   > “It rested on two earlier programmes, each of which solved a problem that would otherwise have stopped it.”

   Aadhaar and expanded bank ownership helped UPI reach more people; UPI did not require every customer to obtain an Aadhaar identity or a newly opened basic account. Customers could use existing accounts and debit-card credentials. NPCI’s documentation describes Aadhaar-based onboarding as an alternative to debit-card onboarding. The correct distinction is between enabling mass adoption and making the payment system possible. [NPCI’s onboarding circular](https://www.npci.org.in/PDF/npci/upi/circular/2022/OC-137-Revised-timeline-for-enablement-of-customer-onboarding-on-UPI-through-Aadhaar-OTP-Authentication.pdf).

3. **Minor — UPI participation is not unrestricted.**

   > “Any bank and any telephone application may connect to it.”

   > “A road is open to every vehicle, and the system is open to every bank.”

   UPI is interoperable, but banks and application providers require admission and compliance with NPCI’s rules. Third-party applications participate through payment-service-provider banks. The correct fact is that **approved participants share interoperable infrastructure**, not that any application can simply connect. [NPCI’s third-party application rules](https://www.npci.org.in/PDF/npci/upi/circular/2017/UPI%20Circular%2015B_single%20PSP%20Model_SDK%20approach%2015%20Sept.pdf).

4. **Major — Bitcoin does not guarantee that cheating costs more than it yields.**

   > “The cost of cheating is thus made far greater than the reward.”

   Proof of work makes rewriting confirmed history computationally expensive. It does not fix the value an attacker could obtain or guarantee that every attack is uneconomic. Bitcoin’s original paper makes security conditional on honest participants controlling the majority of computing power and discusses incentives separately. The correct claim concerns the difficulty of rewriting history, not a guaranteed cost-to-reward inequality. [Bitcoin white paper, sections 4–6 and 11](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf).

5. **Minor — The pizza offer has the completion date.**

   > “On 22 May 2010 a programmer in Florida offered ten thousand bitcoins to anyone who would have two pizzas delivered to his house.”

   The offer was posted on **18 May**. On **22 May**, Laszlo Hanyecz reported that the exchange had succeeded. This is unchanged by the flow revision but was not flagged in the earlier check. [Original offer and completion posts](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=137.0).

6. **Minor — The illustration explains insertion, not tapping.**

   > “The terminal reads the account number from the chip through these contacts.”

   This caption sits inside the explanation of tapping a card. Exposed contacts are used when a card is inserted; contactless payment communicates through the card’s antenna. The caption needs to distinguish the two interfaces so it does not misdescribe the chapter’s central action. [EMVCo’s contactless explanation](https://www.emvco.com/emv-technologies/emv-contactless-chip/).

7. **Major — Cash’s supposed immunity contradicts the demonetisation passage.**

   > “Cash leaves no record. A payment in notes cannot be profiled, sold to advertisers, or blocked.”

   > “That makes it money that nobody in the middle can watch or switch off.”

   The earlier privacy flag remains unresolved, and the new sentence strengthens it. Cash avoids an automatic bank-payment trail; a recorded sale or identified customer can still connect it to a person. More directly, the chapter itself describes India removing banknotes’ legal-tender status. Cash cannot be remotely frozen like a bank balance, but authorities can restrict its use or withdraw its legal status. [RBI’s November 2016 withdrawal notice](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=10684).

8. **Minor — Sweden’s world ranking is not supported.**

   > “Sweden has gone further than any other country towards doing without cash.”

   > “On the list, in the country that uses cash least, was a supply of banknotes and coins sufficient for a week.”

   The earlier reversed wording has been fixed, but the replacement still asserts an unjustified world ranking. Norway reported cash accounting for **2% of point-of-sale payments** in spring 2024; Sweden’s 2025 report put its latest surveyed cash share at about **10%**. Survey differences counsel against a precise ranking, but certainly do not establish Sweden as the minimum. “Among the countries using cash least” preserves the point. [Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/publications/Norges-Bank-Papers/2024/memo-1-24-payment-services/web-report-papers-1-24-payment-services/), [Riksbank](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/payments--cash/payments-in-sweden/payments-report-2025/trends-on-the-payments-market/the-swedish-payments-market-is-almost-entirely-digital/cash-use-continues-to-decline/).

## Clarity flags

- **Announcement-style signposts:** “The scheme has several parts.” and “It had another effect, which lasted.” announce explanations rather than deliver them. “What made the habit spread was a tradition.” similarly withholds the concrete red-envelope tradition until the next sentence.

- **Padding:** “The sums add up.” contributes nothing between the fee explanation and its annual total. “Others soon saw what that made possible.” supplies neither a person nor a distinct idea.

- **Repeated wording:** “That bookseller grew into something much larger than a bookshop. An order placed with that bookseller today…” repeats “that bookseller” in consecutive sentences. “A road is open to every vehicle…” repeats the public-road analogy after the paragraph has already explained interoperability.

- **Exception-prone absolutes:** “If the envelope never arrived, there was nobody to complain to.” confuses the absence of dependable formal recourse with the impossibility of complaining. “Every movement is timed” extends the preceding item-picking measurement to all movement.

- **Internal contradiction through an absolute:** “Nobody wants to spend a coin…” follows an example of someone doing precisely that. The later descriptions of ransom payments and savers also show willingness to use volatile cryptocurrency. The practical argument against volatility does not require “nobody” or “no seller.”

- **Unclear scope:** “The fees, however, are not the main business. The larger profit is in the debt.” Whose business? The preceding paragraphs discuss issuing banks, acquiring banks and card networks. Identify card issuers here; networks do not generally earn the cardholder’s borrowing interest.

- **Unclear comparison:** “A small sum for a single disc, it showed…” makes “it” awkwardly refer to a purchase while describing it as a sum, and leaves “small” without a useful comparison.

- **Too many anonymous organisations:** “the country's largest online marketplace,” “the messaging company,” “both,” and “the larger of the two” become difficult to track. Alipay and WeChat Pay recur enough to justify naming them under the style sheet’s own exception.

I found no explicit chapter self-mentions, prohibited chapter cross-references, or hedging stacks. Useful explanatory transitions should remain; the style sheet expressly welcomes them.
