1. **Factual framing and contradiction with chapter 30 — high confidence.**  
   > “It began when arguments about nature stopped being settled by what the great books said, and started being settled by measurements that a stranger could repeat.”

   This makes repeatable observation the European invention that distinguishes science from its predecessors. Yet chapter 30 already says Ibn al-Haytham “began with observations that anyone can repeat” and describes al-Ma’mun’s surveyors checking Greek measurements. Even Ptolemy conducted quantitative optical experiments. The defensible change is the expansion and institutionalisation of these practices, not their beginning. [Museo Galileo’s reconstruction of Ptolemy’s experiments](https://brunelleschi.imss.fi.it/vitrum/evtr.asp?c=8250).

2. **Overstated proof of heliocentrism — high confidence.**  
   > “What turned Copernicus's idea from a clever alternative into something that had to be true was a mass of numbers, collected by a man who did not believe it.”

   Also:
   > “Now a mathematician was telling theologians that when scripture said the Sun moved, they would have to read it as a figure of speech, because he had seen otherwise.”

   Neither Tycho’s observations nor Galileo’s discoveries uniquely demonstrated Earth’s motion. Tycho’s alternative put the planets around the Sun while the Sun circled a stationary Earth; it accommodated Venus’s phases. Kepler’s work greatly strengthened heliocentric astronomy, but “had to be true” and “seen otherwise” erase the remaining scientific dispute. Readers need one sentence explaining Tycho’s alternative. [Museo Galileo, “Phases of Venus”](https://catalogue.museogalileo.it/indepth/PhasesVenus.html).

3. **Unsupported torture scene — high confidence.**  
   > “Shown the instruments of torture, as the procedure required, he knelt and read out a statement that he cursed and detested his errors.”

   The documented event is a verbal threat of torture during interrogation on 21 June 1633; the abjuration followed the next day. Being shown instruments is not established, and was not an inevitable procedural requirement. The sentence combines separate events into an unsupported scene. [Thomas F. Mayer’s study of the proceedings](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/church-history/article/galileos-nontrial-1616-pretrial-16321633-and-trial-may-10-1633-a-review-of-procedure-featuring-routine-violations-of-the-forum-of-conscience/F5BB9632FA3F4C34B33C119A401EB3BC); [Maurice Finocchiaro, *Retrying Galileo*, p. 252](https://pyrkov-professor.ru/Portals/0/Mediateka/XVII%20vek/finocchiaro_m_a_retrying_galileo_16331992.pdf).

4. **False account of the knowledge available before Galileo — high confidence.**  
   > “This was a reasonable way to proceed in a world where the ancient writers really had known more than anyone living.”

   This contradicts chapter 30’s account of advances beyond Greek mathematics and optics, and chapter 47’s own subsequent account of Islamic astronomy and Tycho’s superior measurements. Ancient authorities remained prestigious; they did not possess universally superior knowledge. [Stanford’s account of pre-Copernican corrections to Ptolemy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/copernicus/). It also breaks the rule against absolutes that one exception defeats.

5. **Kepler’s chronology and discovery process are distorted — high confidence.**  
   > “After four years of calculation, all done by hand, he had a combination of circles that matched Tycho's observations almost exactly.”

   > “He started again and spent two more years on it.”

   Following the paragraph’s 1601 starting point, this puts the ellipse around 1607. Kepler began working on Mars in 1600 and reached the ellipse in 1605. Moreover, the successful “vicarious hypothesis” fitted observed directions closely; the famous eight-minute discrepancy arose when he required a physically consistent model. He did not simply discard one nearly successful calculation and start from nothing. [Research reconstructing the 1600–1602 manuscripts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0315086026000145); [Stanford, “Johannes Kepler”](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kepler/).

6. **Leeuwenhoek’s discoveries are placed after his election incorrectly — high confidence.**  
   > “Leeuwenhoek was elected to the society, and he went on to discover bacteria and the cells of the blood.”

   His election was in 1680. He described red blood cells in 1674, and his 1676 observations are generally credited with the discovery of bacteria. These belong before the election, not as its sequel. [His 1674 letter](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/leeu027alle01_01/leeu027alle01_01_0010.php); [1676 letter and observations](https://www.lensonleeuwenhoek.net/content/wrote-letter-40-of-1676-10-09-henry-oldenburg); [study documenting his election](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10482-023-01859-4).

7. **The Royal Society’s publication practice is made into an absolute rule — high confidence.**  
   > “Their second rule was to publish everything, in enough detail that a person who had not been there could repeat it.”

   Publishing reports that enabled scrutiny was an important aspiration, but “publish everything” misrepresents practice. The Society left many of Leeuwenhoek’s communications unpublished; he published substantial material himself. Describe the practice without presenting universal publication as a formal numbered rule. [Research on Leeuwenhoek’s letters and their publication](https://academic.oup.com/femsle/article/369/1/fnac007/6534901). This also breaks the style sheet’s prohibition on brittle absolutes.

8. **The Greek-versus-experimental contrast is misleading — high confidence.**  
   > “Greek writers had argued the question from first principles and disagreed.”

   In isolation this is true, but its juxtaposition with Ibn al-Haytham’s experiments implies that Greek optics lacked experimental investigation. Ptolemy described apparatus and measured refraction through water and glass. Ibn al-Haytham substantially revised that tradition; he did not introduce experimentation into a purely speculative subject. [Ptolemy’s experimental apparatus and results](https://brunelleschi.imss.fi.it/vitrum/evtr.asp?c=8250).

9. **Newton’s theological work is reduced to an unsupported time comparison — high confidence that revision is needed.**  
   > “He spent more years on alchemy and on calculating the date of the end of the world from the Bible than he did on physics.”

   These activities overlapped, and the sentence supplies no defensible measure of “more years.” His extensive theology also cannot be reduced to calculating an apocalypse date: he opposed confident date-setting and rarely wrote down terminal dates. The secure fact is that he devoted substantial work to alchemy and biblical interpretation alongside mathematics and natural philosophy. [Newton scholar Stephen Snobelen’s explanation](https://isaac-newton.org/statement-on-the-date-2060/).

10. **Leeuwenhoek was not an entirely unknown outsider — high confidence.**  
    > “The power of the method was shown by a man who had no standing at all.”

    He lacked university credentials, but by 1676 he had already been introduced to the Society by the physician Reinier de Graaf and had been sending observations since 1673. He also held civic appointments in Delft. “No standing at all” exaggerates the obstacle and conceals the relationships that helped his reports receive attention. [Royal Society biographical record](https://catalogues.royalsociety.org/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=NA8438&src=CalmView.Persons); [research on his introduction and correspondence](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsnr.2024.0045).

11. **An absolute about comets erases earlier natural explanations — high confidence.**  
    > “Comets had always been taken as signs of God's anger, appearing without warning.”

    Comets were often interpreted as portents, but that was never the only interpretation. Seneca argued that they were enduring celestial bodies with long orbits; Aristotle offered a natural atmospheric explanation. Halley’s achievement was identifying and predicting a particular returning comet, not first treating comets as natural phenomena. [Seneca’s *Natural Questions*, with scholarly commentary](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/seneca-natural-questions/selection9-7-28-30).

12. **The Tusi mechanism omits the condition that makes it work — high confidence.**  
    > “One of these, a small circle rolling inside a larger one so that a point on it moves back and forth in a straight line, appears in Copernicus's book with the same construction and nearly the same lettering on the diagram.”

    The smaller circle must be **half the larger circle’s diameter**, and the tracing point must lie on its circumference. Without that relationship, a reader picturing two arbitrary circles cannot understand the claimed straight-line motion. This is essential explanatory detail, not a refinement. [Mathematical Association of America, *Hands on History*, p. 93](https://maa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/NTE72.pdf).

13. **The longitude explanation leaves out the crucial comparison — high confidence.**  
    > “An observer who noted the moment at which a moon vanished behind the planet, and compared it with the time predicted for Paris, could work out how far east or west of Paris he stood.”

    Chapter 40 has already explained the need for home time and local time. Here, explicitly say the observer records the event in **local time** and compares that with its predicted **Paris time**. Otherwise “noted the moment” does not explain how the observation yields longitude. One hour’s difference corresponds to fifteen degrees.

14. **The central physical law is left in mathematical shorthand — high confidence.**  
    > “Halley wanted to know what path a planet would follow if it were pulled towards the Sun by a force that weakened with the square of the distance.”

    A general reader may not know what “with the square” means. Give the operational meaning: at twice the distance, the pull is one quarter as strong. The following passage also says gravity “holds” the Moon in orbit without explaining why it does not fall straight down; its continuing sideways motion is the missing step.

15. **Explicit self-reference and an overbroad claim — high confidence.**  
    > “The result, three years later, was a book that joined together everything described so far.”

    “Described so far” refers to the chapter’s own contents, directly breaking the self-reference rule. “Everything” also includes optics, microorganisms and experimental institutions, which the *Principia* did not unify. Specify terrestrial and celestial motion.

16. **Signposting rather than stating the point — high confidence.**  
    > “Others refused, and their refusal is easy to mock and worth understanding.”

    “Worth understanding” announces the explanation instead of beginning it. Similarly:
    > “The power of the method was shown by a man who had no standing at all.”

    This announces what the Leeuwenhoek story will demonstrate. Both conflict with “No signpost sentences”; the second also has the factual problem noted above.
