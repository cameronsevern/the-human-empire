# Chapter 102 — What caused the financial crisis of 2008?

**Line under the title:** The United States, Britain and the world, from 1973 to 2012.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The crisis was caused by banks that lent enormous sums to people who could not repay, sold the loans on to investors all over the world as if they were safe, and borrowed thirty times their own capital to do it. It was made possible by a change of mind thirty years in the making. When the post-war economic order broke down in the inflation of the 1970s, a school of economists who had always distrusted the state won the argument, and governments from London to Santiago to Moscow sold what they owned, broke the unions, cut taxes at the top and set finance free. That brought down inflation and helped a billion people in Asia out of poverty. It also widened the gap inside rich countries and left the banks to police themselves. In 2008 the lesson of the 1930s was remembered just well enough to prevent a second depression, at public expense, and the anger at how it was done has not gone away.
