1. **The explanation is missing the step that makes banks fail.**  
   > “The investment bank gathered several thousand such loans into a pool, and sold investors the right to the monthly payments flowing out of it.”

   A general reader will reasonably ask: if banks sold the loans, why did the banks collapse? Explain that banks also retained mortgage securities and financed holdings with short-term borrowing that lenders could refuse to renew. Falling asset values and disappearing funding then reinforced each other. This is a central missing link, not optional technical detail. **Confidence: high.** [FDIC history of the crisis](https://www.fdic.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/crisis-response.pdf).

2. **Lehman’s failure is assigned a disputed motive as settled fact.**  
   > “The American government had decided over the weekend to let it fail, as an example.”

   Officials sought a private rescue; after that failed, they maintained that they lacked the authority and sufficient collateral for an effective government rescue. Whether those constraints really prevented intervention remains disputed. “As an example” turns one interpretation into the sole established explanation. **Confidence: high that the sentence overstates the evidence; lower on the ultimate counterfactual.** [Treasury testimony explaining the decision](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/tg645).

3. **The IMF’s purpose is wrong—and contradicts chapter 86.**  
   > “In 1976 it had to ask for an emergency loan from the international fund that had been set up to help poor countries in difficulty.”

   The IMF was established to support international monetary stability and help member countries with balance-of-payments problems, irrespective of whether they were poor. Chapter 86 correctly explains that it would lend to a country whose dollar reserves were running out, and separately describes the World Bank’s development role. Name the IMF here so readers can recognise it. **Confidence: very high.** [IMF Articles of Agreement, Article I](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/aa/pdf/aa.pdf).

4. **India’s growth acceleration is put a decade too late.**  
   > “Its finance minister used the crisis to abolish most of the permits and open the country to trade, and growth doubled.”

   India’s major growth acceleration began around 1980. Growth averaged roughly 6% during the 1980s and remained around that level after the 1991 reforms; it did not simply double because of those reforms. Their contribution to making growth sustainable is a defensible claim. **Confidence: high.** [IMF account of India’s growth and reforms](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2015/09/28/04/53/sp060404).

5. **The elephant chart is made to prove more than it measures.**  
   > “It drops into a trough, where the working people of America and Europe gained almost nothing in a generation.”

   The original chart compares global income percentiles in **1988 and 2008**, not the same people over thirty years after 1980. Changes in the countries and populations occupying those percentiles affect its shape. It cannot establish that American and European working people generally gained almost nothing. Distinguish particular groups’ stagnation from this blanket claim. **Confidence: high.** [Resolution Foundation’s analysis of the chart](https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/comment/blind-men-and-the-elephant-what-does-the-elephant-curve-really-tell-us-about-rich-countries/).

6. **The poverty statistic has the wrong endpoint.**  
   > “The share of humanity living in extreme poverty fell from more than two in five to about one in ten.”

   The preceding sentence places this within the thirty years after 1980. The familiar World Bank estimate of **10% belongs to 2015**, using its then-standard $1.90-a-day threshold, not approximately 2010. Specify the dates and give a brief explanation of “extreme poverty”; otherwise readers cannot interpret the measure. **Confidence: high.** [World Bank, *Poverty and Shared Prosperity 2018*](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/poverty-and-shared-prosperity-2018).

7. **The imprisonment count is false without a much narrower scope.**  
   > “In the United States one senior banker went to prison.”

   Several senior banking and mortgage executives received prison sentences for crisis-related fraud. Colonial Bank senior vice-president Catherine Kissick received eight years; Taylor, Bean & Whitaker’s former CEO Paul Allen received forty months. If the intended claim concerns senior executives at the largest Wall Street banks, specify that category. **Confidence: very high.** [DOJ: Kissick](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/former-colonial-bank-senior-vice-president-sentenced-8-years-prison-fraud-scheme), [DOJ: Allen](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/former-tbw-ceo-sentenced-40-months-prison-fraud-scheme).

8. **The description of deregulation implies that banking rules disappeared.**  
   > “They were able to because, over the previous thirty years, governments had come to believe that markets could be trusted to look after themselves, and had taken away the rules.”

   Substantial regulation remained. The defensible account is that important restrictions were removed, supervision failed, and dangerous activities developed outside effective oversight. Later, “In 1999 that law was repealed” repeats the problem: the legislation repealed Glass–Steagall’s affiliation restrictions, not every provision separating banking activities. **Confidence: very high.** [Congressional Research Service’s legal account](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R41181.html).

9. **The subprime statistic is attached to the wrong category.**  
   > “By 2006 a fifth of all new American mortgages were of this kind.”

   “This kind” follows loans with no income checks, steep payment resets, and a spectacularly unaffordable strawberry-picker mortgage. The roughly one-fifth figure refers to **subprime lending**, broadly lending to borrowers with weaker credit histories. It does not mean one-fifth combined all those features. Introduce and explain “subprime” before giving its share. **Confidence: high.** [Federal Reserve testimony](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/testimony/braunstein20070521a.htm), [Federal Reserve research distinguishing documented and undocumented subprime loans](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2008/200859/index.html).

10. **The banking freeze is an exception-breaking absolute.**  
    > “Nobody knew which other banks were owed money by Lehman, or which were in the same condition, and so banks stopped lending to one another altogether.”

    Exposure and solvency were uncertain, not universally unknown, and interbank lending did not stop altogether. Research on the overnight federal funds market finds continued lending, with terms increasingly sensitive to borrower risk. “Lending contracted sharply” preserves the point accurately. **Confidence: very high.** [New York Fed, *Stressed, Not Frozen*](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr437.html). This also breaks style.md’s rule on absolutes.

11. **The Depression comparison contradicts the earlier account unless its period is narrowed.**  
    > “This time the governments did the opposite of what their predecessors had done.”

    Chapter 77 describes Hoover’s emergency lending agency, Roosevelt’s deposit insurance, public works, abandonment of gold, and subsequent recovery. The contrast works against the initial response of **1929–32**, not against governments throughout the 1930s. The heading “Not 1931” helps, but the surrounding prose explicitly compares whole decades. **Confidence: high; internal contradiction in scope.**

12. **The AIG passage omits what made its guarantees immediately dangerous.**  
    > “The last step was insurance.”

    The following explanation covers eventual losses but not the immediate demands for cash. AIG’s contracts could require it to post collateral as securities deteriorated or its own credit rating fell. Those demands helped precipitate its rescue before all the underlying mortgages had finished failing. A sentence explaining this would connect the insurance mechanism to the collapse. **Confidence: high.** [GAO review of AIG’s rescue](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a585561.html).

13. **The housing downturn is falsely simultaneous everywhere.**  
    > “In 2007 they began to fall, everywhere at once.”

    National measures fell, but local markets peaked and declined at different times; some still recorded increases. Say that prices fell nationally or across many markets together. **Confidence: very high.** [OFHEO’s fourth-quarter 2007 house-price report](https://www.fhfa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-03/2007Q4_hpi.pdf). “Everywhere at once” also violates the rule against brittle absolutes.

14. **The inflation narrative starts too late.**  
    > “Prices of everything else began to climb, and unions demanded pay rises to keep up, which pushed prices higher still.”

    Following “The price of oil quadrupled,” this implies that general inflation began with the 1973 oil shock. Inflation was already accelerating in the late 1960s; the oil shock intensified it. Chapter 97 supplies the oil-shock story, so this chapter can add that prior inflationary background rather than repeat a misleading starting point. **Confidence: high.** [Federal Reserve history of the Great Inflation, 1965–82](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/great-inflation).

15. **The Greek revelation is dated incorrectly.**  
    > “In 2010 it emerged that Greece had borrowed far more than it had admitted.”

    The decisive disclosure came in **October 2009**, when the newly elected government revealed that fiscal deficits had been substantially understated. The first rescue programme followed in 2010. Distinguish the annual deficit from accumulated debt. **Confidence: very high.** [European Stability Mechanism’s chronology](https://www.esm.europa.eu/publications/safeguarding-euro/runaway-train-greece-sounds-alarm).

16. **Repeated anonymous central bankers become hard to distinguish.**  
    > “The head of the central bank, meanwhile, had raised interest rates to twenty per cent, and put one worker in ten out of a job.”

    Readers have already met an unnamed central banker in September 2008. This is Paul Volcker; the later advocate of banks managing their own risks is Alan Greenspan; the Depression scholar is Ben Bernanke. Give these three names and their roles when introduced. Style.md allows names needed to follow the story, and here anonymity obscures changes in policy and responsibility.

17. **An unexplained financial category interrupts the causal chain.**  
    > “The following year Congress passed another law declaring that a new and fast-growing class of financial contracts would not be regulated at all.”

    The reader is never told what these contracts do or how they connect to the later insurance guarantees. Identify over-the-counter derivatives and explain the relevant example: a contract paying out when specified debt defaults. “Not regulated at all” also needs narrowing to the regulatory exclusions actually enacted. **Confidence: high.** [Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission’s discussion of OTC derivatives and AIG](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/historical/fct/fcic_report_final_20110127.pdf).

18. **This is a signpost sentence prohibited by style.md.**  
    > “To see how the richest country in the world arrived at that evening, it is necessary to go back to the last time the rules had been rewritten.”

    It announces the narrative’s next move instead of making it. The following sentence already begins the historical explanation. This is the clearest structural style breach; I found no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-mentions, or numbered chapter cross-references.

19. **The strike dates need the second year.**  
    > “In the winter of 1978 the lorry drivers, the hospital porters and the dustmen all went on strike.”

    The major strikes described occurred in **January and February 1979**, during the winter of **1978–79**. Use that span or “early 1979.” **Confidence: very high.** [London Museum’s account](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/winter-discontent/).

20. **The monarch’s title is wrong.**  
    > “In November 2008 the Queen of England went to open a new building at the London School of Economics.”

    Elizabeth II was Queen of the **United Kingdom**, not Queen of England. “The Queen” or “Queen Elizabeth II” is sufficient. **Confidence: very high.** [National Portrait Gallery’s biographical account](https://www.npg.org.uk/collections/queen-elizabeth-ii).
