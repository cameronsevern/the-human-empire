# Chapter 81 — How was the Second World War won?

**Line under the title:** The world, from 1939 to 1945.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,800 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The war was won by factories and by Soviet soldiers. Germany and Japan began with better armies and needed a short war, and for two years they got one. Once the Soviet Union had survived the first blow and the United States had been brought in, the war became a contest of production, and it ran on oil and engines. The Allies had most of the world's oil and made several times as many trucks, tanks, aircraft and ships. The Soviet Union did most of the killing and dying on land: three of every four German soldiers who fell were killed on the eastern front, and twenty-seven million Soviet citizens died. When it ended, two countries were left standing over the ruins of all the others.

## The story

1. **Opening: a toast.** Answer first. Tehran, November 1943: Stalin raises his glass to American machines.
2. **Lightning.** The pact of August 1939. Poland. A new way of using tanks, radios and aircraft. France falls in six weeks. Britain alone, with radar.
3. **Six hundred thousand horses.** June 1941: the invasion of the Soviet Union, what it was for and how it was fought. The gates of Moscow. Factories on railway wagons.
4. **Tanya's notebook.** Leningrad: food as a weapon. Hunger elsewhere: Bengal.
5. **The Volga.** The drive for oil, Stalingrad, and the turn of the war.
6. **Fire in a cylinder.** How an internal combustion engine works and why it changed war. Whose oil it was. The trucks the Red Army rode in.
7. **The bridge of ships.** The Atlantic: submarines, a broken code, and ships built faster than they could be sunk.
8. **A bomber an hour.** American production; who did the work. Simple machines in great numbers. Mould from a melon: penicillin.
9. **Firestorm.** The bombing of cities and what it did and did not achieve.
10. **The other half of the world.** China's long war. Midway and the Pacific. Soldiers of the empires.
11. **The beach and the marsh.** June 1944 in France and in Belarus. Berlin.
12. **Ending: a handshake on a broken bridge.**
