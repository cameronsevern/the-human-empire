# Chapter 107 — Who invented the internet?

**Line under the title:** The United States, Britain, Switzerland and Taiwan, from 1943 to 2025.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. Explain each machine so that a reader could explain it to a friend.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Nobody invented the internet. It was built in layers over fifty years, each resting on the one below: a machine that calculates with switches, a switch with no moving parts, a way of printing millions of those switches on a sliver of sand, a method of sending messages in small pieces that find their own way, and a scheme for linking documents that a physicist gave away for nothing. Nearly every layer was paid for by governments, chiefly the American one in the course of the Cold War, and then turned into products by private firms. The result joined most of the human race in one network that no state built alone and none controls. It also came to depend on a handful of companies and, for its most advanced parts, on a few factories on one island.

## The story

1. **Opening: two letters.** Answer first. Los Angeles, 29 October 1969.
2. **Switches.** What a computer is: arithmetic done by switches, and everything turned into numbers. A secret machine in England and a thirty-ton one in Philadelphia; six women with cables.
3. **A grain of sand.** The transistor of 1947. Eight engineers walk out. Many switches on one chip, and who bought the first ones.
4. **Doubling.** Moore's prediction and what fifty years of it did.
5. **Packets.** The agency founded after Sputnik. A way of sending messages in pieces, thought of twice. Four computers in 1969. A sign borrowed for the post.
6. **A network of networks.** The common language of 1974 and 1983: what it left out, and why that mattered later. Rough consensus.
7. **On every desk.** The personal computer: a garage, a laboratory that gave its ideas away, a software contract.
8. **Vague but exciting.** A physicist in Geneva, a proposal of 1989, and a decision in 1993.
9. **Under the sea.** What the network is made of: cables, warehouses, electricity.
10. **One island.** How an advanced chip is made, and how few can make it: a Dutch machine, tin droplets, Taiwan.
11. **Who is in charge.** Standards bodies, five companies, and governments that came late.
12. **Ending: lo.**
