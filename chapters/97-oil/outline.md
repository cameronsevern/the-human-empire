# Chapter 97 — Why does the world fight over oil?

**Line under the title:** The Persian Gulf and the world, from 1908 to 1991.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The world fights over oil because in the twentieth century it rebuilt its whole way of life around one liquid, and the largest and cheapest pools of that liquid lie under a handful of countries around the Persian Gulf. For fifty years a few Western companies pumped it on terms set in the age of empire and sold it for almost nothing. When a government tried to take its oil back, as Iran's did in 1951, it was overthrown. In the 1970s the producing states acted together, took ownership, and in a single winter quadrupled the price. Ever since, keeping the oil flowing has been a first concern of every great power, and the countries that own it have found that sudden wealth from the ground is a mixed blessing.

2. **Rock oil.** Lamp fuel from Pennsylvania; a monopoly; then the engine.
3. **Five dollars a day.** The car for everyman: the moving line, the wage, the suburb. A world that switched from coal.
4. **Sixteen per cent.** Persia, 1901 and 1908: a concession and a strike. A navy changes its fuel.
5. **Thirty-five thousand gold coins.** Arabia: a desert king, a Californian company, a well in 1938, and a meeting on a warship.
6. **Sisters.** How seven companies ran the trade, and what the owners of the ground received.
7. **An old man in pyjamas.** Iran nationalises its oil; a boycott; the coup of 1953.
8. **Baghdad, 1960.** A club of producers that nobody feared. Why the balance shifted by 1970: American wells at full stretch, and a colonel in Libya.
9. **The weapon.** October 1973: a war, an airlift, a cut and a price. What it did to the rich countries and to the poor ones.
10. **Money from the ground.** Where the money went: ownership, banks, weapons, mosques, the dollar. The curse of easy wealth, and one country that escaped it.
11. **A doctrine.** 1979 and 1980; the war of 1991.
12. **Ending: the stone age.**
