1. **Factual error — high confidence.** “In 1968 Mao called in the army.”

   The army’s major intervention began in **January 1967**, under orders to support revolutionary forces. Military units then backed competing factions and helped escalate the fighting. In 1968 military control was consolidated and independent mass movements suppressed. The present sequence makes the army arrive after violence it had already helped shape. Source: [Dong Guoqiang and Andrew Walder, “Forces of Disorder”](https://sociology.stanford.edu/sites/sociology/files/dong_and_walder_2018.pdf).

2. **Misleading comparison of death estimates — high confidence.** “Some historians, working from provincial archives, put the figure at forty-five million.”

   Following “In three years about thirty million people died,” this presents forty-five million as another estimate for the same period. Dikötter’s estimate is **at least forty-five million premature deaths during 1958–1962**, including deaths from violence and overwork. Specify the different period and scope. This correction does not establish that his estimate is the best one. Source: [Dikötter’s account of his findings](https://www.frankdikotter.com/books/maos-great-famine/key-arguments.html).

3. **Doubtful factual claim — high confidence that the wording overstates matters.** “It was the worst famine in history, and it happened in peacetime, in years of ordinary weather, under a government that controlled every part of the country.”

   “Years of ordinary weather” is too categorical. Research using rainfall and temperature records identifies adverse weather, including drought, while finding its contribution substantially smaller than the effects of policy. The defensible point is that weather cannot explain the catastrophe’s scale. Source: [Chris Bramall, “Agency and Famine in China’s Sichuan Province”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/abs/agency-and-famine-in-chinas-sichuan-province-19581962/F39E361A42764CBB9F94071C2CB822E0).

4. **Factual error and contradiction with chapter 90 — high confidence.** “China had been admitted to the United Nations four months before.”

   China was a founding UN member. In October 1971 the PRC’s representatives **replaced the Republic of China’s representatives in China’s existing seat**. Chapter 90 already explains the Soviet boycott over the refusal to give “China’s place” to the Communist government. “Admitted” contradicts that explanation. Source: [UN membership history](https://treaties-prod.un.org/Pages/HistoricalInfo.aspx?clang=_en).

5. **Anachronism — high confidence.** “The party had to decide what to say about the man whose face was on the banknotes.”

   Mao’s portrait was not on the circulating national banknotes when the party prepared its 1981 verdict. The hundred-yuan note depicting Mao with three other leaders entered circulation on **10 May 1988**, despite bearing the date 1980. His portrait above Tiananmen would provide a contemporary image. Source: [People’s Bank of China catalogue of withdrawn fourth-series notes](https://huhehaote.pbc.gov.cn/hangzhou/125335/5420839/5331129/2024041710275048920.pdf).

6. **Factual overstatement and prohibited absolute — high confidence.** “The country was united, armed with nuclear weapons, and owed nothing to anyone.”

   China had outstanding foreign commercial obligations from machinery purchases financed through deferred payments. Low external indebtedness is defensible; owing nothing is not. Sources: [Wen Tiejun, *Ten Crises*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-16-0455-3_3), and [Nigel Harris’s contemporary discussion of foreign borrowing](https://www.marxists.de/china/harris/13-natind.htm).

7. **Geographical overstatement — high confidence.** “Over the next twelve years seventeen million city teenagers were sent to villages and state farms in the remotest parts of the country, with no date of return.”

   The destinations were not uniformly remote frontier regions. Roughly three quarters went to rural areas relatively near their home cities; millions did go much farther away. Retain the displacement and indefinite duration, but distinguish ordinary rural placements from remote ones. Source: [University of Vienna’s account of sent-down youth](https://beijing-spring.univie.ac.at/en/the-democracy-movement-1978-1981/educated-youth-sent-to-the-countryside/).

8. **Chronological ambiguity — high confidence.** “He told a conference of seven thousand officials that the disaster was three parts natural and seven parts man-made.”

   The paragraph opens “By 1961,” but this conference took place in **January–February 1962**. A brief date is needed to separate Liu’s 1961 investigation from his subsequent report. Source: [*Selected Works of Liu Shaoqi*, report dated 27 January 1962](https://ebook.theorychina.org.cn/ebook/upload/storage/files/2022/08/04/a7155eae7c3331e4aaffd20cd343e39e56589/files/basic-html/page330.html).

9. **Publication chronology — high confidence about the dates; medium about the intended meaning.** “In August he wrote a short poster of his own and had it published.”

   Mao wrote and circulated the poster at the party plenum in **August 1966**; its official publication in *People’s Daily* followed on **5 August 1967**. “Published,” followed by “The country understood,” collapses those stages. Source: [“Liberating the Small Devils”: Red Guard Newspapers and Radical Publics](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/liberating-the-small-devils-red-guard-newspapers-and-radical-publics-19661968/1B64D41ADF7DC64E7E99543EE55975DC).

10. **Reader loses the chronology.** “Within weeks he reversed himself.”

    The paragraph’s only date is 1956, making the crackdown appear to occur that year. The invitation began in 1956; the decisive surge of criticism and the anti-rightist reversal occurred in **1957**. Insert that year before the reversal. Also explain “rightists” as people accused of opposing socialism or Communist rule: the label drives the following account of official fear. Source: [“A Fresh Look at China’s Hundred Flowers Period”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/000944557601200507). **Confidence: high.**

11. **Missing explanation of a central motive.** “He also remembered what Stalin’s heirs had done to Stalin three years after his death.”

    Chapter 78 ends in 1941; chapter 90 mentions Stalin’s death but does not explain his subsequent denunciation. Readers therefore lack the event that makes Mao’s fear intelligible: **Khrushchev’s 1956 denunciation of Stalin’s repression and personality cult**. State what happened rather than referring obliquely to it. Source: [US National Archives on the secret speech](https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2020/12/03/khrushchevs-secret-speech-1956/). **Confidence: high.**

12. **Missing bridge in the succession story.** “Deng Xiaoping, who had been purged twice, came back to lead the country.”

    The reader has seen only one purge. This sentence also follows the widow’s trial, obscuring when Deng returned and suggesting a direct succession from Mao. Briefly establish his earlier rehabilitation, second removal, and eventual ascendancy; Mao’s immediate successor was Hua Guofeng, whom Deng displaced as effective leader by late 1978. Source: [Oxford’s account of Deng’s rise](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62338/reference-article-abstract/571461493). **Confidence: high.**

13. **Anonymity makes a recurring character difficult to follow.** “The army chief who had made the cult of Mao was named in the party’s constitution as his successor.”

    This is the unnamed “new chief” introduced several sections earlier. Naming Lin Biao at his first appearance would help readers distinguish him from the dismissed defence minister and recognise the significance of the plane crash. The style guide explicitly permits names for recurring people; withholding this one creates unnecessary work.

14. **Absolute contradicted by the chapter itself.** “After that nobody objected to anything.”

    The next section describes Liu explicitly challenging the explanation of the famine and confronting Mao. The intended point—Peng’s punishment made criticism dangerous—is strong without claiming that all objection ceased. This breaks the rule against absolutes that one exception defeats.

15. **Absolute contradicted by the examples that follow.** “The worst killing took place in the countryside, away from any witness.”

    Rural killings had witnesses, including survivors, perpetrators and spectators. The following account even describes public feasts. If the intended distinction is lack of outside scrutiny or publicity, say that. “Away from any witness” is both inaccurate and a style violation.

16. **Unsupported universality.** “The lesson was learned by everyone.” “His portrait hung in every house, and people began each day by bowing to it and reporting their thoughts.”

    Both turn widespread behaviour into universal behaviour. The second also makes a particular devotional practice sound like everybody’s daily routine. Describe their prevalence without “everyone” or “every house”; this preserves the force while satisfying the rule on absolutes.

17. **Explicitly prohibited signpost.** “Mao had one more surprise.”

    This announces a surprise before delivering it—the exact construction prohibited by “No signpost sentences.” Begin with the border fighting or the diplomatic reversal itself.
