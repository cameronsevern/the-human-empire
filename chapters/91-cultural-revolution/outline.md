# Chapter 91 — What was the Cultural Revolution?

**Line under the title:** China, from 1956 to 1976.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The Cultural Revolution was a ten-year upheaval that Mao Zedong launched against his own party. He had lost authority after his attempt to industrialise China in a single bound caused the worst famine in history, in which some thirty million people starved because no official dared to report the truth. To get his power back, and to stop China from settling into rule by comfortable officials, he called on schoolchildren and students to attack their teachers, their parents and the government itself. More than a million people were killed and a generation lost its schooling. The men who rebuilt China afterwards were people who had been its victims.

## The story

1. **Opening: an armband.** Answer first. Beijing, 18 August 1966, and what had happened at one girls' school two weeks before.
2. **Let a hundred flowers bloom.** The farms collectivised; an invitation to criticise, and what happened to those who accepted.
3. **Backyard steel.** The Great Leap Forward of 1958: the communes, the canteens, the furnaces, the sparrows.
4. **Sputnik fields.** Why officials reported harvests that did not exist. One letter at a mountain resort. The famine.
5. **A black cat or a white cat.** Mao steps back; the pragmatists repair the damage; a warning about the history books. The quarrel with Moscow and Mao's fear of a Chinese Khrushchev.
6. **Bombard the headquarters.** 1966: a swim in the river, a little red book, schools closed, free trains.
7. **The four olds.** Red August: teachers, temples, books, a philosopher's grave. What was done to the head of state. A future president in a cave.
8. **Tanks in the streets.** The factions turn on each other; the army steps in; seventeen million sent to the villages.
9. **A plane in Mongolia.** The fall of the chosen successor. An American president in Beijing.
10. **Seventy per cent.** Mao's death, the arrest of his widow, the verdict, and what the years had and had not built.
11. **Ending: an apology.**
