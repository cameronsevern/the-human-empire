## Changed facts

No existing number, name, date or quotation was removed or changed. However, the revision adds factual assertions and strengthens some existing claims. Purely interpretive bridges and sentences merely split or moved are excluded below.

1. **Collective farming gains an explanation.**

   Old: “By 1956 the land that had been shared out among the peasants had been taken back into collective farms.”

   New: “By 1956 the land that had been shared out among the peasants had been taken back into collective farms, which meant that the villagers now worked the fields together under the party's direction.”

2. **The commune programme receives a new chronological position.**

   Old: No corresponding sentence.

   New: “The first step was to reorganise the villages on a vast scale.”

   “First” introduces a sequencing claim, rather than simply connecting paragraphs.

3. **The steel passage explicitly adds the destruction of usable tools.**

   Old: “The lumps of brittle iron that came out were useless.”

   New: “The lumps of brittle iron that came out were useless, and the tools that had gone into them were gone.”

   This makes an existing implication explicit.

4. **The farming passage adds an explanation of competition and a claim about the planners’ knowledge.**

   Old: “Seed was sown five or ten times more thickly than before, on the theory that plants of the same kind would not compete with one another, and the seedlings choked.”

   New: “Seed was sown five or ten times more thickly than before, on the theory that plants of the same kind would not compete with one another. They did compete, and the seedlings choked.”

   Old: No corresponding sentence about what planners knew.

   New: “What the planners had forgotten was that sparrows also eat insects.”

   The second addition is materially different from the existing statement that insects subsequently damaged crops.

5. **Grain confiscation gains an explicit consequence.**

   Old: “In many villages that meant the entire real harvest, seed grain included.”

   New: “In many villages that meant the entire real harvest, seed grain included, so there was nothing left to eat and nothing to plant.”

6. **Liu’s confrontation with Mao is explicitly placed in private.**

   Old: “To Mao's face he said that history would record the part that the two of them had played, and that the eating of human flesh would go into the books.”

   New: “In private he went further. To Mao's face he said that history would record the part that the two of them had played, and that the eating of human flesh would go into the books.”

7. **Confucius gains identifying historical information.**

   Old: “Guards travelled to the home town of Confucius, smashed the statues in his temple, and dug up his grave.”

   New: “Guards travelled to the home town of Confucius, the ancient sage whose teachings had shaped Chinese life for centuries, smashed the statues in his temple, and dug up his grave.”

   This is a useful addition for the intended reader.

8. **The army’s intervention gains a statement of purpose.**

   Old: “The army had been ordered in to support the left, and had ended by arming one faction against another.”

   New: “The army was supposed to calm things down, and at first it made them worse. It had been ordered in to support the left, and had ended by arming one faction against another.”

   Restoring order is now explicitly presented as its initial purpose.

9. **University admissions become an exclusive opposition between recommendation and merit.**

   Old: “The universities, when they reopened, took students by political recommendation.”

   New: “The universities, when they reopened, took students by political recommendation, not by merit.”

10. **The struggle over Mao’s legacy receives a duration.**

    Old: No corresponding sentence.

    New: “The struggle over his legacy began at once, and it was short.”

11. **Jiang Qing’s defence is explicitly summarised.**

    Old: “At her trial she was defiant.”

    New: “At her trial she was defiant, and her defence was that she had only done what Mao wanted.”

    This restates the substance of the unchanged quotation immediately following.

12. **The subsequent reformers are explicitly identified with Mao’s displaced officials.**

    Old: “It was also very poor, and the people who now set out to change that had spent the past decade in prisons, villages and tractor workshops.”

    New: “It was also very poor. The people who now set out to change that were the same ones Mao had pushed aside: they had spent the past decade in prisons, villages and tractor workshops.”

## Factual flags

1. **Major — existing error: Peng’s letter is misrepresented.**

   > “It was respectful, and it said that the Leap had cost more than it had gained.”

   Peng’s letter criticised waste, exaggeration and economic imbalances while explicitly affirming the Leap’s achievements and the correctness of its general direction. It did **not** deliver the net-negative verdict attributed to it here. The correct account is that he defended the programme’s accomplishments while urging correction of serious mistakes. This matters because Mao punished even that qualified criticism. Source: [Peng’s letter of 14 July 1959, reproduced in translation](https://braddelong.substack.com/p/document-1959-07-14-peng-dehuai-to).

2. **Minor — new error: the sparrow problem was debated, not simply forgotten.**

   > “What the planners had forgotten was that sparrows also eat insects.”

   Chinese scientists had investigated sparrows’ diets and publicly debated their benefits before the mass extermination of 1958. Scientific objections were raised in 1956–57; the campaign proceeded despite them. The correct distinction is between disregarding or overriding ecological evidence and forgetting an unknown consequence. Sources: [Chinese Academy of Sciences historical study](https://www.ihns.ac.cn/sourcedb/zh/fblw/202212/t20221209_6572842.html), [Zheng Zuoxin’s December 1957 discussion in *People’s Daily*](https://cn.govopendata.com/renminribao/1957/12/23/7/).

3. **Minor — existing numerical error: homes searched in Beijing.**

   > “In Beijing in August and September, Red Guards searched more than a hundred thousand homes.”

   The documented tally used in Andrew Walder’s research is **33,695 homes** for those months. “More than a hundred thousand” substantially exceeds that figure. “More than thirty thousand homes” would accurately convey the documented scale. Source: [Walder, “Beijing Red Guard Factionalism”](https://sociology.stanford.edu/sites/sociology/files/walder-jas.pdf).

4. **Minor — new overstatement: university selection did not exclude every academic consideration.**

   > “The universities, when they reopened, took students by political recommendation, not by merit.”

   Political recommendation dominated admissions, but the added exclusion is too broad. In April 1973, for example, the State Council required a limited examination of candidates’ educational level alongside political selection. The correct generalisation is that political criteria took precedence over academic attainment; the national competitive entrance examination returned in 1977. This also means the following “There was no entrance examination until 1977” needs “national” to distinguish it from these limited assessments. Source: [“Settling Accounts with the Cultural Revolution at Beijing University 1977–78,” note 10](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/abs/settling-accounts-with-the-cultural-revolution-at-beijing-university-197778/F96B27DB5CDFFEAE663EB35EE7DFDC5C).

5. **Major — new chronological distortion: the struggle over Mao’s legacy was not settled within a month.**

   > “The struggle over his legacy began at once, and it was short.”

   The arrest of the Gang of Four was swift. The struggle over Mao’s political legacy continued through Hua Guofeng’s leadership, Deng’s ascendancy and the 1981 historical resolution—which this chapter itself subsequently describes. The party’s 1981 resolution records that its 1977 congress had still reaffirmed Cultural Revolution doctrines. The sentence conflates a rapid arrest with a prolonged political settlement. Source: [CCP historical resolution, 27 June 1981, especially sections 30–31](https://china.usc.edu/node/20941).

## Clarity flags

1. **New signposts announce significance before supplying it.**

   > “That silence would matter enormously within a year.”  
   > “What makes it stranger still is the setting.”  
   > “The next shock came from the top of the army.”  
   > “The new leaders then faced an awkward question.”

   These advertise an impending consequence, surprise or question. They fall under the style sheet’s explicit prohibition on signpost sentences. The surrounding narrative already supplies the connections.

2. **Several additions duplicate the sentence beside them.**

   > “The meaning was unmistakable.”

   The next sentence explains precisely what people understood.

   > “It is easy to see the appeal.”

   The next sentence explains the teenagers’ excitement.

   > “The accusations themselves were impossible to answer.”

   The next sentence already says that an accused person had no defence and explains why.

   > “Even now, the past is not fully open for discussion. The subject is still handled with care in China.”

   These make essentially the same point twice.

3. **The opening bridge momentarily reverses the causal sequence.**

   > “In fact, at her own school they had already been acted out.”

   “They” means Mao’s words, but the violence happened thirteen days before he spoke them. The following sentence resolves the chronology, yet “acted out” momentarily suggests the pupils were carrying out that instruction.

4. **A new universal repeats the kind of problem the earlier check corrected.**

   > “The warning of 1957 had now been repeated at the very top, and everyone understood it.”

   “Everyone” makes an unnecessary claim about universal understanding. This is a newly introduced absolute, distinct from the earlier wording already repaired.

   Similarly:

   > “To anyone who knew the old beliefs, the timing was ominous.”

   Knowing a belief does not mean sharing its interpretation.

5. **The factionalism explanation conflicts with the authority exercised later.**

   > “Once every group could claim his blessing, there was no one to say which group was right.”

   The intended point appears to be that rival factions rejected one another’s legitimacy. Literally, however, Mao and the central leadership could—and later in this account do—decide which groups to support or suppress. “No one” obscures that distinction.

6. **The transition promises a descent in rank that its example does not provide.**

   > “The purge reached far below the very top, and into the next generation.”

   The next official is a deputy prime minister, still near the very top. His son illustrates persecution reaching the next generation; the example does not establish the first half of the sentence.

7. **One compressed comparison leaves its subject unclear.**

   > “Deng Xiaoping fared better, though not by much.”

   Compared with Liu’s death in confinement, Deng’s own survival and eventual return were substantially different. The following account of his son’s paralysis suggests the comparison concerns the whole family, but the sentence names Deng alone.

8. **Historical terminology remains unexplained.**

   > “A new purge of ‘right opportunists’ swept the party.”

   Unlike “rightists,” this label receives no explanation. The reader needs to know that it targeted people accused of opposing or weakening the Leap.

   > “For teenagers who had been raised on stories of the Long March and had never been allowed to decide anything, it was intoxicating.”

   “Long March” is also unexplained at first appearance, and “never been allowed to decide anything” is an unnecessary absolute.

I found no hedging stacks, chapter self-mentions or explicit chapter cross-references.

## Overall

The revision preserves the original numerical and quoted material, but it is not entirely fact-neutral. Correct the new claims about sparrows, admissions and Mao’s legacy, plus the existing Peng-letter and house-search errors. Several bridges help; others add repetition, signposts or absolutes. No files were edited, and resolved flags from the earlier check are not repeated.
