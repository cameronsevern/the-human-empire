1. **Misleading account of Marx’s economics — high confidence.**  
   > “As wages fall, the workers cannot afford to buy what the factories pour out, and the economy lurches into slumps, each worse than the one before.”

   This presents one disputed interpretation as Marx’s settled explanation. Marx explicitly observed that crises are commonly preceded by **rising wages**, and rejected inadequate consumption as a sufficient explanation. His account also involved investment, profitability and imbalances between industries. “Each worse than the one before” adds an unsupported, mechanically worsening sequence. [Source: *Capital*, volume II, chapter 20.](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1885-c2/ch20_01.htm)

2. **Incorrect explanation of surplus value — high confidence.**  
   > “The difference goes to the owner, and Marx argued that this difference, taken from millions of workers, is where all profit comes from.”

   The preceding sentence makes “the difference” mean the finished goods’ value minus wages. That leaves out raw materials and machinery used up in production. Marx distinguishes these transferred values from the **new value added by labour**; surplus value is the latter minus the cost of labour-power. This needs correcting for readers to understand the argument rather than mistake ordinary production costs for profit. [Source: *Capital*, volume I, chapter 8.](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch08.htm)

3. **Overstated interpretation of Marx’s expectations — high confidence about the qualification needed.**  
   > “Capitalism had bent where Marx had expected it to break, and one of the things that bent it was the fear he inspired.”

   The surrounding passage makes factory legislation and electoral reform sound like possibilities Marx had failed to recognise. He celebrated the Ten Hours Act as a major working-class victory and, in 1872, explicitly allowed that workers might achieve their objectives peacefully in England and America. The argument that capitalism proved more adaptable than he expected is defensible; the implied opposition between Marx and all effective reform is not. [Sources: Marx’s 1864 inaugural address](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1864/10/27.htm), [his 1872 Amsterdam speech.](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1872/09/08.htm)

4. **Wrong scope and misleading chronology of labour legislation — high confidence.**  
   > “Parliament limited the working day in factories and banned women and children from the mines.”

   The 1842 Act prohibited underground employment of women and girls, and **boys under ten**, not all children or all mining employment. The major factory restrictions initially applied to children, young people and women in specified industries. Moreover, the 1833, 1842 and 1847 measures precede the London exile introduced by “During those years.” [Sources: the 1842 Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/5-6/99/pdfs/ukpga_18420099_en.pdf), [National Archives’ factory-legislation chronology.](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/1833-factory-act/)

5. **False universal; also breaks the rule against exception-fragile absolutes — high confidence.**  
   > “In the 1880s he had the state insure every German worker against sickness, against accidents, and against poverty in old age.”

   Coverage differed between schemes and occupations. The initial sickness scheme covered specified groups of industrial and other wage workers, not every German worker. These were compulsory insurance arrangements involving contributions and existing insurance funds, rather than comprehensive insurance supplied directly by the state. [Sources: European Observatory’s German system history](https://eurohealthobservatory.who.int/monitors/health-systems-monitor/countries-hspm/hspm/germany-2020/organization-and-governance/historical-background), [Otto-von-Bismarck Foundation’s account.](https://www.bismarck-biografie.de/en/politik/bismarcks-sozialgesetzgebung-1881-1890)

6. **False claim about Parliament — high confidence.**  
   > “Each time the House of Commons refused even to discuss it.”

   MPs debated the Chartist petitions and rejected the requested action. Refusing their demands is different from refusing discussion. Parliament’s own account specifically records the debate led by Thomas Attwood in 1839; Hansard also records discussion when that petition was presented. [Sources: Parliament’s account](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/houseofcommons/reformacts/from-the-parliamentary-collections/collections-reform-acts/great-reform-act/chartist-petition-1839/), [Hansard, 14 June 1839.](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1839/jun/14/national-petition-the-chartists)

7. **Misleading compression of famine policy — high confidence on events; moderate on the weighting of motives.**  
   > “The officials in London who ran the relief effort believed that to give away food would upset the market and teach the Irish to depend on charity, and they closed the soup kitchens after six months.”

   Market ideology and hostility to dependency mattered, but this omits that the **British government established the emergency soup-kitchen scheme**, which fed millions. Its closure in late summer 1847 accompanied a transfer of responsibility to locally financed Poor Law relief and expectations surrounding the harvest. Explain that reversal; otherwise readers cannot understand why officials opposed to giving food were running soup kitchens. [Sources: historical accounts of the relief measures](https://www.rte.ie/history/famine-ireland/2020/1123/1180004-a-policy-to-disaster-british-famine-relief-measures/), [their chronology and financing.](https://www.rte.ie/history/the-great-irish-famine/2020/0629/1150367-the-great-irish-famine/)

8. **Misstatement of Smith; also an unsupported absolute — high confidence.**  
   > “He wanted the state to provide defence, justice, roads and schools, since no private person would.”

   Smith’s argument concerned socially useful institutions that private returns would not adequately support, not the absence of private roads or schools. He discussed fee-paying education, private endowments and toll-financed infrastructure in detail. “Since no private person would” substitutes an easily disproved claim for his actual reasoning. [Source: *The Wealth of Nations*, book V.](https://www.egyptarch.gov.eg/sites/default/files/pdf/Books/%5Bphilosophy%5D%20Smith_Adam%20-%20The%20Wealth%20Of%20Nations%20%281776%29%20---%20W.pdf)

9. **Overstatement about the defeat of 1848 — high confidence.**  
   > “By the end of the following year every one of these revolutions had been crushed or had fizzled out.”

   Armed radical movements were largely defeated, but France’s Second Republic survived 1849: Louis-Napoleon’s coup came in December 1851. Prussia also retained a constitution, albeit a restrictive one. Distinguish defeat of revolutionary movements from disappearance of their constitutional results. “Every one” breaks the style rule as well as obscuring the history. [Sources: French presidency’s constitutional history](https://www.elysee.fr/en/french-presidency/the-constitution-of-14-january-1852-and-its-modifications), [*Encyclopedia of 1848 Revolutions*, constitutional outcomes.](https://people.ohio.edu/chastain/ac/civillib.htm)

10. **Direct contradiction with chapter 61 — high confidence.**  
    > “Smith was writing just before the machines came.”

    Chapter 61 has already placed Newcomen’s engine in 1712, spinning inventions in the 1760s, and Arkwright’s Cromford mills in 1771–72. Smith published in 1776, during the early mechanisation already described. “Before mechanisation transformed most workers’ lives” would preserve the intended distinction. [Earlier chapter: “A river in a valley.”](chapters/61-coal/chapter.md)

11. **Reversed chronology — high confidence.**  
    > “A little later, in a café in Paris, he met Marx, and the two talked for ten days.”

    Following “He went home and wrote a furious book,” this puts the Paris meeting after composition of *The Condition of the Working Class in England*. Engels met Marx in Paris in **August 1844, on his way home**, then wrote the book between September 1844 and March 1845. [Sources: Engels’s October 1844 letter and editorial chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1844/letters/44_10_01.htm), [the book’s publication record.](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1845/condition-working-class/index.htm)

12. **Anachronism in the opening scene — high confidence.**  
    > “Most mornings he walked to the British Museum, sat at a desk under the great dome of the reading room, and worked until it closed.”

    The domed reading room opened in **1857**; Marx left Dean Street in September **1856**. He used the museum library earlier, but not that room while living in the Soho accommodation described. [Sources: British Museum chronology](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2019-09/Localhist_19thC_presnotes_KS2.pdf), [English Heritage’s Marx residence history.](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/blue-plaques/karl-marx/)

13. **Overbroad claim about income — high confidence.**  
    > “He had no regular income.”

    This cannot describe the 1850s generally. Marx earned money as a newspaper correspondent; Jenny Marx specifically described his *New-York Daily Tribune* work in 1853 as providing steady income while they still occupied the Dean Street flat. His earnings were inadequate and insecure, but not nonexistent. [Source: Jenny Marx’s recollection reproduced in the Marx–Engels biographical collection.](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/bio/marx-engels.pdf)

14. **Doubtful exact number supporting both heading and ending — high confidence that eleven is unsafe; moderate on a complete attendance count.**  
    > “Eleven people attended.”

    Research published by Highgate Cemetery identifies **thirteen named attendees** from contemporary accounts and notes that there may have been others. “A small group of mourners” is secure; the exact eleven should not carry the ending or the heading without better evidence. [Source: *Highgate Cemetery Newsletter*, April 2018, pp. 10–11.](https://ancestry.bartfeenstra.com/file/O0563/file/highgate-cemetery-newsletter-2018-04.pdf)

15. **Geographical error and contradiction with chapter 59 — high confidence.**  
    > “It made the breaking of a frame a crime punishable by death, and it sent twelve thousand soldiers into the northern counties, a larger force than it had first sent to fight Napoleon in Spain.”

    The initial force apparently intended by the comparison—Wellesley’s approximately 9,000 men in 1808—went to **Portugal**, as chapter 59 already says. “The Iberian Peninsula” would resolve this. The twelve-thousand figure should also identify the deployment being counted before making the comparison. [Source: *Dictionary of Irish Biography*, Wellesley](https://www.dib.ie/biography/wellesley-wesley-arthur-a8961); [earlier chapter, “The little war.”](chapters/59-napoleon/chapter.md)

16. **Reader loses track of the central book.**  
    > “He published the first volume of his great book in 1867 and never finished the rest.”

    The book is never named **Capital**. After the lengthy discussion of the *Communist Manifesto*, an inexperienced reader could understand this as a volume of the same work. Name *Capital* here and distinguish the long economic study from the earlier pamphlet.

17. **Missing legal bridge.**  
    > “Trade unions were made legal.”

    The reader last encountered the 1799 prohibition and the transportation of union members in 1834. The Combination Acts were repealed in 1824, restrictions followed in 1825, and the 1871 Act gave unions further legal recognition. Without that bridge, readers may infer an uninterrupted blanket prohibition until late in Marx’s life. The distinction also explains why the transported labourers were prosecuted for their oath rather than simply for belonging to a union. **Confidence: high.** [Source: Parliament’s history of trade unions.](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/tradeunions/)

18. **Unexplained destination for a general reader.**  
    > “When a revolution was finally made in his name, it happened in a country of peasants.”

    Readers without historical background have not been given enough to identify Russia or 1917. Naming the country would let the ending land without requiring a preview of the Russian Revolution.

19. **Clear break of the rule against absolutes.**  
    > “He was reading the reports of the British government's factory inspectors, shelf after shelf of them, and the works of every economist who had ever written.”

    “Every economist who had ever written” is an indefensible universal, not merely confident narration. “The major political economists” conveys the breadth of his reading without making an impossible completeness claim.
