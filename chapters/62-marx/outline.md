# Chapter 62 — What did Karl Marx want?

**Line under the title:** Britain and Europe, from 1776 to 1883.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Marx wanted the factories, mines and land to belong to everyone in common, and he thought that industrial capitalism would bring this about by itself. His was the most ambitious of several answers to the question the factories had raised: who should get the wealth the machines made? One answer, Adam Smith's, was to leave it to free bargaining in an open market, and it became the creed of the first industrial country. Workers answered by breaking machines, forming unions and demanding the vote. Reformers built model villages. Marx said that the system could not be reformed and would be overthrown by the class it had created. He was right about a good deal: slumps, giant firms, a world market. He was wrong about the ending, in the countries he was writing about, because wages rose, workers got the vote, and governments, partly out of fear of him, began to insure the poor.

## The story

1. **Opening: two rooms in Soho.** Answer first. A German exile in London in the 1850s, living on a friend's money, walking every day to a library.
2. **Eighteen operations.** A Scottish professor and a pin. The market as a way of getting strangers to serve each other. What the old view had been. What Smith said the state must still do.
3. **General Ludd.** The workers the machines replaced. Frames broken at night, soldiers in the north of England, hangings. Unions forbidden. A meeting in Manchester ridden down.
4. **A free bargain.** The mill owners' creed. Cheap bread and a famine. A mill owner's son walks the back streets of Manchester.
5. **A mill with a school.** A manufacturer shows that it can be done differently, and then crosses the ocean to found a perfect village.
6. **Six points.** The workers ask for the vote: three giant petitions. The year of revolutions, 1848, and a pamphlet nobody noticed.
7. **Nothing to lose.** What Marx actually argued, step by step.
8. **Ending: a dozen mourners.** What happened instead: wages, votes, unions, and a German chancellor's insurance. What Marx got right. The funeral.

## Left for later

The Russian revolution. The American Civil War. The welfare state. The return to free markets.
