## Factual flags

1. **Minor — newly added overgeneralisation about Stalin’s reputation.**

   > “None of this impressed the men at the top of the party.”

   Together with the following “grey mediocrity” sentence and the new explanation of his appointment, this presents leadership contempt as general. Lenin’s contemporary correspondence contradicts that picture: in February 1913 he approvingly described Stalin as a “marvellous Georgian” undertaking work on nationalities. The correct distinction is between Stalin’s standing among particular rivals and Lenin’s earlier appreciation of him. [Lenin, letter to Maxim Gorky, February 1913](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1913/feb/00mg.htm).

2. **Major — the geographical superlative excludes the population hit hardest proportionately.**

   > “The famine was worst in the richest farmland.”

   Ukraine suffered more deaths in absolute numbers, but Kazakhs suffered the highest proportional population loss. Their predominantly pastoral, partly arid and poor-soil environment does not fit “the richest farmland.” The chapter’s subsequent Kazakhstan paragraph makes the unrestricted superlative especially misleading. The correct distinction is between Ukraine’s immense absolute toll and the exceptionally severe proportional loss among Kazakhs. [Sarah Cameron, Library of Congress interview](https://blogs.loc.gov/kluge/2016/08/the-kazakh-famine-of-the-1930s/).

3. **Minor — the new genocide definition omits a consequential distinction.**

   > “Ukraine and more than thirty other countries recognise it as genocide, the deliberate destruction of a people.”

   The gloss can imply that destruction of an entire people is required. Genocide concerns specified acts committed with intent to destroy a protected group **in whole or in part**; successful destruction of the whole group is not required. That distinction matters directly to the dispute being explained. [United Nations, definition of genocide](https://www.un.org/en/genocide-prevention/definition).

4. **Minor — the prize is attributed to the newspaper.**

   > “The most famous foreign correspondent in Moscow, an Englishman who wrote for an American newspaper and had just won its highest prize, replied in print that there was no actual starvation.”

   Grammatically, “its” refers to the newspaper. Walter Duranty won the **1932 Pulitzer Prize for Correspondence**, not a prize awarded by *The New York Times*. Identify it as a Pulitzer or an American journalism prize. [Pulitzer’s record of Duranty’s award](https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/walter-duranty-new-york-times).
