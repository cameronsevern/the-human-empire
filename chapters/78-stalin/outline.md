# Chapter 78 — Why did so many people die under Stalin?

**Line under the title:** The Soviet Union, from 1924 to 1941.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Stalin set out to turn a country of peasants into an industrial power in ten years, and he paid for it with the peasants. The state seized their farms, merged them into collectives it could control, took the grain to feed the new factory towns and to sell abroad for machines, and deported or shot those who resisted. When the harvest fell short, it went on taking, and between five and eight million people starved, most of them in Ukraine and Kazakhstan. He then turned on his own party, army and people in a terror run by numerical quotas. The factories were built, and they armed the country that would break Hitler's army. Many of the people who carried all this out believed that they were building a just society, which is the hardest part of the story to face.

## The story

1. **Opening: ten years.** Answer first. February 1931: Stalin tells a conference of factory managers that the country is fifty years behind and has ten years to catch up.
2. **The man of steel.** A cobbler's son from Georgia, a job nobody wanted, a warning from the dying Lenin, and how the dull man beat the brilliant ones.
3. **The grain problem.** A workers' state in a country of peasants. The peasants hold back their grain.
4. **Fists.** The winter of 1929 to 1930: the farms are merged by force. Who counted as a rich peasant, and what happened to them. The animals.
5. **Steel city.** The five-year plan: a steelworks built on bare steppe, designed by Americans, by people living in tents. What was achieved.
6. **Death by hunger.** 1932 to 1933: the quotas, the searches, the law on ears of grain, the sealed borders. Ukraine and Kazakhstan. Two foreign reporters. Was it genocide?
7. **Quotas.** The terror of 1937 and 1938: the trials, the order that set numbers to be shot, the army, the camps, the retouched photograph.
8. **Believers.** Why people took part: faith, ambition, fear. What ordinary people gained: schools, literacy, a way up.
9. **Ending: ten years and four months.** June 1941.

## Left for later

The German invasion and the war. The camps after 1945. Khrushchev's denunciation of Stalin. Mao's famine.
