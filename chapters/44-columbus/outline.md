# Chapter 44 — Why did Columbus sail west?

**Line under the title:** China, Portugal, Spain and the oceans, from 1405 to 1502.
**Length:** 4,200 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Europeans were not the first or the best ocean sailors of the 1400s. Chinese fleets many times larger had already crossed the Indian Ocean, and Polynesians had settled the Pacific with no instruments at all. What Europeans had was a reason and a rivalry. The pepper and gold they wanted reached them through chains of merchants who each took a share, so there was a fortune for whoever found a way around. Portugal spent eighty years working down the coast of Africa and learned the secret of the Atlantic, which is that its winds turn in great circles and a ship must sail away from home to get back. Columbus used the same winds to go west, on arithmetic that was wrong by a factor of four, after the experts of one court had turned him down and he had tried another. In China one emperor's decision had ended the voyages. In Europe there was always another king to ask.

## The story

1. **Opening: a giraffe in Nanjing.** Answer first. The Chinese treasure fleets, their size, where they went and what they were for.
2. **The price of pepper.** What Europe wanted and whose hands it passed through. Gold from across the desert.
3. **The turn of the sea.** Portugal on the African coast: the cape nobody passed, the wind that would not let you home, and the loop out into the ocean. The small ship that made it possible. Sugar and the first slave cargoes.
4. **The height of the sun.** Finding latitude by star and sun; why longitude could not be found. The Polynesians, who needed none of it.
5. **A man with the wrong number.** Columbus's sums, the experts who were right, the courts he tried, and thirty-six days of open water.
6. **Washbasins for a king.** Da Gama's great loop, the pilot, the gifts that were laughed at, and the return with guns.
7. **Why the great fleet stayed home.** One court and many.
8. **Ending: a line nobody could find.**
