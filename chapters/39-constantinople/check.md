1. **Misleading answer to the title — high confidence.** “Because in 1453 a Turkish sultan broke its walls with cannon, filled the empty city with his own subjects and made it the capital of a Muslim empire.”

   The conquest explains the change of government, but it did not introduce the name Istanbul or produce an immediate official renaming. Forms of Istanbul predate 1453; Constantinople remained in official Ottoman use afterwards. The chapter’s later explanation is substantially better than its opening answer. [Account drawing on historian Christoph Herzog](https://www.livescience.com/istanbul-not-constantinople).

2. **Wrong geography underpinning the strategic explanation — high confidence.** “An Ottoman army moving from one half to the other had to be ferried across within sight of its walls, and Christian fleets could interrupt the ferry whenever they chose.”

   Ottoman armies could cross at the Dardanelles, far from Constantinople, as Murad II did in 1421 with Genoese assistance. Crossings were vulnerable to naval interference, but neither “had to” nor “whenever they chose” is defensible. Explain Constantinople as a strategic threat between Ottoman territories without making it the unavoidable crossing point. [Cristian Caselli, “Genoa, Genoese Merchants and the Ottoman Empire”](https://www.academia.edu/111854984/Caselli_C_Genoa_Genoese_Merchants_and_the_Ottoman_Empire).

3. **False technological first — high confidence.** “They had not yet been turned on the strongest city wall ever built.”

   Ottoman cannon had already been used against Constantinople in the siege of 1422. The difference in 1453 was the strength of the artillery train, not the first encounter between these walls and guns. “Strongest … ever built” also supplies an unmeasurable superlative. [Jonathan Harris, *The End of Byzantium*, discussion of the 1422 siege](https://dokumen.pub/the-end-of-byzantium-paperbacknbsped-0300187912-9780300187915.html).

4. **Doubtful projection of later religious government backwards — high confidence that qualification is needed.** “The Armenians and the Jews received similar arrangements.”

   This makes the preceding description of the patriarch’s authority sound like a uniform settlement bestowed on three communities immediately after the conquest. Gennadios’s installation is secure; equivalent, centrally organised Armenian and Jewish arrangements under Mehmed are disputed. Their institutions and jurisdictions developed unevenly over time. Retain the specific patriarch story without treating it as the inauguration of a complete system. [Benjamin Braude’s discussion of the millet system and its foundation traditions](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/millet-system).

5. **Wrong global comparison — high confidence.** “From this point on, the future belonged to large states with deep treasuries, and the Ottoman empire was the largest of them.”

   The Ottoman Empire in 1453 was not the world’s largest state: Ming China was substantially larger. The sentence needs a geographical scope or a different claim about Ottoman strength. The broader interpretation about fiscal capacity is legitimate; the objection is to the factual superlative. [*The Cambridge History of China*, volume 8, on the extent of Ming territory](https://djvu.online/file/JwWLOVX9X9DHJ).

6. **False exclusivity in the chapter’s concluding argument — high confidence.** “Only a ruler who taxed a large country could afford them in numbers.”

   Italian states, including Florence, manufactured and deployed substantial artillery. Resources and organisation mattered, but possession of a large country was not a prerequisite. This is precisely an “only” that counterexamples break under `style.md`. [Fabrizio Ansani’s research on Italian artillery production](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/this-french-artillery-is-very-good-and-very-effective-hypotheses-/).

7. **Misleading account of how siege weapons worked — high confidence.** “A catapult stone arrives in a slow arc and bounces off.”

   Mechanical artillery could damage fortifications; its projectiles did not characteristically bounce harmlessly away. Trebuchets were serious siege weapons before cannon. Explain the advantage of powerful guns without making their predecessors ineffective by definition. [Historic Environment Scotland on the siege engines used against Stirling Castle](https://blog.historicenvironment.scot/2024/04/the-war-wolf-at-stirling-castle/).

8. **Wrong architectural superlative — very high confidence.** “Its church of Holy Wisdom, Hagia Sophia, had the widest dome on earth.”

   Hagia Sophia’s dome spans approximately 31–32 metres; the much older Pantheon’s spans about 43 metres. Chapter 27 already gives Hagia Sophia’s width correctly as thirty-one metres, although it does not itself establish the comparative error. [Hagia Sophia’s dimensions](https://muze.gen.tr/muze-detay/ayasofya); [Oxford Classical Dictionary on the Pantheon](https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780199381135.013.4706).

9. **Wrong territorial absolute, also contradicted within this chapter — very high confidence.** “The city that Orban left was the last piece of the Roman empire.”

   The Byzantine Morea survived until 1460; the separate Byzantine successor empire of Trebizond survived until 1461. More immediately, the chapter itself subsequently says that Constantine ruled nearby towns and a corner of southern Greece. Constantinople was the remaining imperial capital, not literally the last territory. [*Oxford Handbook of Byzantine Studies*, political-historical survey](https://student.cc.uoc.gr/uploadFiles/177-%CE%92%CE%A5%CE%A6%CE%A6171/Laiou._Political-historical_survey._pages_from_OHBS._ss._280-294.pdf).

10. **Another territorial absolute contradicted by the narrative — high confidence.** “Everything around it for hundreds of kilometres belonged to the sultan.”

    Genoese Galata stood directly across the Golden Horn; the chapter later introduces it as such. Nearby Byzantine possessions are also acknowledged in the same paragraph. Ottoman territory surrounded Constantinople strategically, but “everything” is false. [Study of Genoese Pera/Galata and its position before the conquest](https://www.levantineheritage.com/pdf/Pera-Galata-neighbourhoods-and-churches-Somer.pdf).

11. **False absolute about the response to conquest — high confidence.** “Scholars mourned the loss of the last Greek empire, and popes called for a crusade that nobody joined.”

    Papal efforts failed to recover Constantinople, but they did attract participants. Pius II’s expedition gathered crusaders at Ancona in 1464, and a Venetian fleet arrived; his death helped terminate the enterprise. “Nobody joined” confuses failure with absence of participation. “Last Greek empire” also overlooks Trebizond’s survival until 1461. [Treccani’s account of Pius II](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pio-ii_%28Enciclopedia-dei-Papi%29/).

12. **Overstatement about Castillon — high confidence.** “In the same summer of 1453, at the other end of Europe, the king of France brought three hundred guns to a field near Bordeaux and blew apart the last English army on French soil, which ended a war that had lasted more than a century.”

    Castillon destroyed Talbot’s field army and was the last major battle of the Hundred Years’ War. It did not eliminate all English forces in France: Bordeaux held out until October, and Calais remained English for another century. Specify the field army or the decisive final campaign. [Historical Association’s account and contemporary sources](https://www.history.org.uk/files/download/19506/1512745789/Hundred_Years_War_Sources_14151453.pdf).

13. **Misleading accession chronology — very high confidence.** “Mehmed came to the throne in 1451, at nineteen.”

    He **returned** to the throne in 1451. His first reign was in 1444–46, beginning when he was twelve. One word would prevent readers from taking this as his first accession. [*Cambridge History of Turkey*, volume 2, chronology](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781316169131_A23896534/preview-9781316169131_A23896534.pdf).

14. **Internal chronological inconsistency — very high confidence.** “It began about three hours before sunrise on 29 May, at the place where the great gun had done the most damage.”

    Later: “The defenders had been fighting for four hours without relief.”

    Between these statements, the janissaries enter “as the sky was turning grey.” An attack beginning three hours before sunrise cannot already have lasted four hours at dawn. The approximate timings need to agree; the narrative does not need this much clock precision.

15. **A crucial character arrives too late for readers to understand his importance.** “At this moment the commander of the Italians, a soldier from Genoa who had been the heart of the defence, was hit by a bullet that pierced his armour.”

    His departure triggers the collapse, but readers have never met him or seen him direct the defence. “Had been the heart” supplies his importance retrospectively at the decisive moment. Introduce this commander during the preparations or nightly repairs so that his loss has an intelligible consequence. Naming him is optional; establishing his role is not.

16. **The physical explanation stops before the mechanism works.** “The powder was rammed down the barrel, followed by a wooden plug and then the stone ball, which masons had chipped round to fit.”

    The paragraph proceeds to recoil, cooling and firing frequency without explaining how the crew ignited the charge. Chapter 32 has already explained expanding gunpowder gases, so that need not be repeated. A brief account of ignition through the touch-hole would complete the sequence required by `style.md`.

I found no clear direct contradiction with chapters 27, 29, 32 or 34, and no clear hedging stacks, chapter self-reference, prohibited cross-references or answer-announcing signpost sentences. The strongest style violations are the falsifiable absolutes identified above. No files were edited.
