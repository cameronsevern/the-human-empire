# Chapter 39 — Why is Constantinople called Istanbul?

**Line under the title:** Constantinople and the Ottoman lands, from about 1300 to 1481.
**Length:** 4,000 to 4,800 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

In 1453 a young Ottoman sultan took the last city of the Roman empire. Cannon mattered: guns cast from bronze knocked down a wall that had stood for a thousand years. They were not enough by themselves. The sultan also had to control the water on both sides of the city, feed eighty thousand men, and wear down defenders who rebuilt the wall every night. Taking the city was the smaller half of the job. It was nearly empty, and he turned it into a capital by ordering families from all over his empire to move there, by giving each religious community its own leader, and by building a market. The name Istanbul is Greek, and the city's people had used it for centuries.
