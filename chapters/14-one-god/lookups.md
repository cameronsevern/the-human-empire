# Chapter 14 fact-check lookups

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. Bible quotations are checked against the NRSV (updated edition) unless marked KJV. Dates are converted from BCE to "years ago" from 2026.

## Opening (the ration tablets)

1. "German archaeologists digging in the ruins of Babylon ... cleared a row of vaulted cellars in a corner of the royal palace. In them lay about three hundred clay tablets."
   - Verdict: correct. Koldewey's excavation, 1899 to 1917; a barrel-vaulted underground building of fourteen rooms at the north-east corner of the Southern Palace, near the Ishtar Gate; about 300 tablets (Pedersén identifies 303).
   - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehoiachin%27s_Rations_Tablets ; https://www.livius.org/articles/person/jehoiachin-in-babylonia/ ; A. George paper via https://core.ac.uk/download/2788228.pdf

2. "they recorded how much oil and grain had been handed out"
   - Verdict: correct. The archive concerns the delivery and issue of oil, grain, dates and spices; the Jehoiachin lists are oil.
   - Source: George, as above.

3. "sat in a museum for thirty years ... a scholar finally worked through them in the 1930s"
   - Verdict: correct. Ernst Weidner published them in 1939.
   - Source: https://biblearchaeologyreport.com/2020/11/13/king-jehoiachin-an-archaeological-biography/

4. "sailors and carpenters from the coast of Lebanon, Egyptians, Greeks from the coast of Turkey, and men from Iran"
   - Verdict: correct. Carpenters from Arvad and Byblos, sailors from Tyre, Ionian craftsmen, Egyptians, Medes, Persians and Elamites; also the sons of the king of Ashkelon.
   - Sources: Wikipedia page above (Arvad, Byblos, Greek craftsmen); livius.org page above.

5. "on four different tablets ... oil ... to a man called Ya'u-kin, king of the land of Yahudu, and a smaller measure to the five sons of the king"
   - Verdict: correct. Four oil-ration lists name him; 10 sila to the king, 2.5 sila to the five sons.
   - Sources: Wikipedia page above; https://cojs.org/babylonian_ration_list-_king_jehoiakhin_in_exile-_592-1_bce/

6. "eighteen years old and had been on his throne for three months" / "The tablets were written about five years after that."
   - Verdict: correct. 2 Kings 24:8; the one dated tablet is Nebuchadnezzar year 13 (592/1 BCE), deportation 597.

7. "the God worshipped today by more than half the people on earth"
   - Verdict: correct. Pew (2025): Christians about 29 per cent and Muslims about 26 per cent of world population in 2020.
   - Source: https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/06/09/how-the-global-religious-landscape-changed-from-2010-to-2020/

## A god for each land

8. "A king of Moab ... About 2,850 years ago he set up a slab of black stone, a little over a metre tall"
   - Verdict: correct. Mesha Stele, basalt, about 840 BCE; height given as about 1 m (Wikipedia) to 1.15 to 1.24 m (Louvre).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesha_Stele

9. "Israel ... had oppressed Moab for many years, because Chemosh was angry with his land ... dragged the sacred vessels of Yahweh before Chemosh"
   - Verdict: correct. Lines 4 to 6 and 14 to 18; "dragging them before Chemosh" is the ANET (Albright) wording; the word "vessels" is a restoration that most editions accept.
   - Source: Mesha Stele page above.

10. "About 2,800 years ago ... blessings in ink on two big storage jars ... 'by Yahweh of Samaria and his Asherah'"
   - Verdict: correct. Kuntillet Ajrud, pithoi A and B, about 800 BCE (dating ranges from the late 10th to the late 8th century). Who the writers were is not known, so the text says "people at", not "travellers".
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuntillet_Ajrud_inscriptions

11. "Scholars argue over which of the two the writers had in mind." / "A tomb in the hills of Judah carries a similar line."
   - Verdict: correct. Goddess or cult object is debated; Khirbet el-Qom, near Hebron.
   - Source: as above; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirbet_el-Qom

12. "an Asherah stood inside the temple in Jerusalem itself, until a reforming king had it dragged out and burned"
   - Verdict: correct as a report of the text. 2 Kings 23:6 (Josiah).

13. "You shall have no other gods before me"
   - Verdict: correct. Exodus 20:3, NRSV.

14. "About 2,750 years ago ... Assyria ... took Israel's capital after a long siege ... more than twenty-seven thousand"
   - Verdict: correct. Samaria fell about 722 to 720 BCE after a three-year siege (2 Kings 17:5); Sargon II's inscriptions give 27,290 (one version 27,280).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_captivity

## Twenty-two letters

15. "twenty-two letters. Each letter stood for one consonant ... from right to left"
   - Verdict: correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Hebrew_alphabet

16. "a junior officer ... swears by Yahweh that nobody has ever had to read a letter to him" (first draft)
   - Verdict: correct (Lachish letter 3, Hoshaiah to Yaush), but cut for length.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachish_letters

16a. "For about a century, Assyrian records still mention people with names built on the name of Yahweh. After that, the deported Israelites can no longer be told apart from their neighbours."
   - Verdict: correct. The first draft said they had "married their neighbours and taken up the gods of the places where they lived", which goes beyond the evidence; replaced. Israelite deportees and descendants are documented in Assyria down to about 602 BCE.
   - Sources: https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/saao/aebp/Essentials/Countries/Israel/index.html ; R. Zadok, BASOR 374 (2015), https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/pdf/10.5615/bullamerschoorie.374.0159

17. Papyrus and skin sheets, glue and stitching, soot ink, columns.
   - Verdict: correct. Standard descriptions; Dead Sea Scrolls ink is carbon black with gum.
   - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea_Scrolls

18. "seventeen pieces of sheepskin sewn together ... more than seven metres long and holds the whole Book of Isaiah"
   - Verdict: correct. 1QIsa-a: 17 sheets, 734 cm, 54 columns, all 66 chapters; about 125 BCE. The Israel Museum describes the sheets as sheepskin.
   - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Scroll ; http://dss.collections.imj.org.il/isaiah

19. "In 1979 ... a tomb outside the walls of Jerusalem ... two tiny rolls of silver ... about 2,600 years ago ... the oldest lines of it that have ever been found"
   - Verdict: correct. Ketef Hinnom, Barkay, 1979; unrolled 27 by 97 mm and 11 by 39 mm; dated just before 586 BCE (a minority argues for a later date); the oldest known text also found in the Hebrew Bible (Numbers 6:24 to 26).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketef_Hinnom_scrolls

20. The burned scroll: "winter house beside a fire ... three or four columns ... cut them off with a knife ... dictated all of it again, and added more"
   - Verdict: correct. Jeremiah 36:22 to 23 and 36:32.

## We cannot see Azekah

21. "Assyria collapsed" about 2,635 years ago.
   - Verdict: correct. Nineveh fell in 612 BCE; the last Assyrian court ended by 609.

22a. "sets down the chief events of Nebuchadnezzar's early years, one year at a time"
   - Verdict: correct. The tablet covers 605 to 594 BCE; the first draft's "each year of Nebuchadnezzar's reign" was wrong.
   - Source: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1896-0409-51

22. "In his seventh year ... camped against 'the city of Judah' ... second day of the month of Adar, captured its king, appointed a king of his own choosing, and carried a heavy tribute back"
   - Verdict: correct. Babylonian Chronicle ABC 5, British Museum 21946.
   - Source: https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-5-jerusalem-chronicle/

23. "He was led away with his mother, his officials, his soldiers, his craftsmen and his smiths, and with the treasures of the temple."
   - Verdict: correct. 2 Kings 24:12 to 16.

24. "The man whom Nebuchadnezzar put in his place was Jehoiachin's uncle."
   - Verdict: correct. 2 Kings 24:17.

25. "In the burned gatehouse of ... Lachish, excavators found about twenty potsherds ... 'for we cannot see Azekah'"
   - Verdict: correct. Found 1935 (and three more in 1938), 21 in all, most in a guardroom of the city gate; letter 4.
   - Source: Lachish letters page above.

26. "only two fortified towns of Judah were still holding out ... Lachish and Azekah"
   - Verdict: correct. Jeremiah 34:7.

27. The siege, the breach, the king's flight, his sons and his blinding; "A month later" the burning.
   - Verdict: correct as a report of 2 Kings 25:1 to 10 (wall breached on the ninth day of the fourth month; the commander arrives on the seventh day of the fifth).

28. "about 2,610 years ago"
   - Verdict: correct. 587 or 586 BCE.

29. "a thick layer of ash and fallen stone, with the arrowheads of both armies ... fifty-one clay sealings ... Gemariah son of Shaphan"
   - Verdict: correct. Shiloh's excavation, 1982; 51 bullae (one source says 53); bronze trilobate and iron arrowheads in the destruction debris. Jeremiah 36:10.
   - Sources: https://timeline.cityofdavid.org.il/event/the-seal-of-gemaryhau-son-of-shaphan ; https://cojs.org/Gemaryahu_Son_of_Shaphan_Bulla-_586_BCE/

30. "The Book of Jeremiah counts 4,600 deportees in all." / "The poorest were left behind ..."
   - Verdict: correct. Jeremiah 52:30; 2 Kings 25:12.

## Whose god lost?

31. The Assyrian officer under the wall, speaking the language of Judah.
   - Verdict: correct. 2 Kings 18:26 to 35.

32. "Babylon was the largest city in the world ... blue glazed brick and rows of bulls and dragons ... the temple tower of Marduk"
   - Verdict: correct. Ishtar Gate (bulls and dragons); Etemenanki, about 90 m square at the base.
   - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishtar_Gate ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etemenanki

33. "How shall we sing the LORD's song in a strange land?"
   - Verdict: correct. Psalm 137:4, KJV.

34. Nebuchadnezzar as "my servant"; Jeremiah lowered into a muddy pit.
   - Verdict: correct. Jeremiah 25:9 and 27:6; 38:6.

35. "I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god." / the carpenter and the block of wood.
   - Verdict: correct. Isaiah 44:6; 44:13 to 19.

36. "Some eight centuries earlier, a king of Egypt had ordered his country to worship the disc of the sun alone"
   - Verdict: correct. Akhenaten, about 1350 BCE; the exilic chapters of Isaiah date to about 540.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atenism

36a. "The gods of great cities could survive a sack, because their people stayed and rebuilt the temple."
   - Verdict: correct. Marduk's worship survived Sennacherib's destruction of Babylon in 689 BCE; Ashur's temple was in use again centuries after 614 BCE. The first draft said that total defeat normally ended a god's worship, which these cases contradict; rewritten to contrast great cities with small deported peoples.
   - Sources: K. Radner, "Assur's 'Second Temple Period'" (2017), https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/36940/ ; https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/321907

36b. "his successors had undone the order within a few years of his death"
   - Verdict: correct. The old cults were restored under Tutankhamun. The first draft's "died with him" was imprecise.

## Judah-town

40a. "Business tablets cannot show how these farmers worshipped ... the writings of those years lay new weight on the customs that need no temple"
   - Verdict: correct. The first draft stated as fact that the exiles rested every seventh day and gathered to hear readings; the tablets do not show this (some Judean transactions are dated on the Sabbath). Rewritten to rest on the exilic writings (Ezekiel 20, Isaiah 56, Genesis 17) only.
   - Sources: https://thetorah.com/article/judean-life-in-babylonia ; https://anetoday.org/alstola-exile-babylon/

37. "About two hundred clay tablets ... Al-Yahudu ... The earliest tablet was written only fifteen years after Jerusalem burned."
   - Verdict: correct. About 200 tablets, 572 to 477 BCE. The village's exact site is not known (near Nippur or Borsippa), so the text says "on the plain of Iraq".
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Yahudu_Tablets

38. "a loan of barley, the lease of a grove of date palms, a tax payment, a marriage, the division of an inheritance"
   - Verdict: correct. Leases of date palms and barley land, credit, tax payments; one marriage agreement and one inheritance division.
   - Sources: as above; https://ancientjewreview.com/read/2015/2/18/pearce-and-wunsch-documents-of-judean-exiles-and-west-semites-in-babylonia-1

39. "The tablets follow one family through four generations."
   - Verdict: correct. Samak-Yama, his son, his grandson Ahiqam and Ahiqam's five sons.
   - Source: https://forward.com/fast-forward/214016/ancient-tablets-shed-light-on-exiled-jews-of-babyl

40. "Build houses and live in them; plant gardens and eat what they produce."
   - Verdict: correct. Jeremiah 29:5, with 29:6 to 7 paraphrased.

41. "The square letters in which Hebrew is written today are the letters of Aramaic" (the sentence on Babylonian month names was cut for length)
   - Verdict: correct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_calendar ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_alphabet

## The making of a book

42. Jehoiachin released in the thirty-seventh year, a seat at the table and an allowance.
   - Verdict: correct. 2 Kings 25:27 to 30 (561 BCE; the dated tablet is 592/1).

43. The two flood accounts (one pair; seven pairs of clean animals).
   - Verdict: correct. Genesis 6:19 to 20 and 7:2 to 3.

44. The goring ox in Hammurabi and Exodus (first draft).
   - Verdict: correct (Laws of Hammurabi 250 to 252; Exodus 21:28 to 32), but cut for length.

45. The dating debate and the linguistic argument.
   - Verdict: correct as a summary. Classical against Late Biblical Hebrew is the main argument for pre-exilic material; a minority disputes that the two can be dated.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dating_the_Bible ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_Hebrew

46. "requires any future king to write out a copy of the law for himself and read it all his life"
   - Verdict: correct. Deuteronomy 17:18 to 19.

## The king of Persia

47. "About 2,565 years ago ... his troops entered the city without a fight."
   - Verdict: correct. October 539 BCE, after the battle at Opis; Nabonidus Chronicle.

48. "a barrel of baked clay, about the size of a loaf of bread ... dug up in 1879"
   - Verdict: correct. Cyrus Cylinder, about 22 cm long and 10 cm across; found by Rassam in March 1879 as a foundation deposit.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Cylinder

49. Marduk searches all the lands, takes Cyrus by the hand; gods and peoples returned; Judah not mentioned.
   - Verdict: correct. Source as above; British Museum translation.

50. "has Yahweh take Cyrus by the right hand, and calls the Persian king Yahweh's 'anointed'"
   - Verdict: correct. Isaiah 45:1.

51. The decree of Cyrus allowing the return and the rebuilding.
   - Verdict: correct as a report of Ezra 1:1 to 4 and 6:3 to 5.

52. "A new temple was finished about 2,540 years ago" / the weeping and the shouting.
   - Verdict: correct. Sixth year of Darius, 515 BCE (Ezra 6:15); Ezra 3:12 to 13.

53. "the tablets from Judah-town run on for sixty years after Cyrus"
   - Verdict: correct. Latest tablet 477 BCE.

54. The garrison on the Nile island, its temple, and the other gods in its papers.
   - Verdict: correct. Elephantine; temple of Yahu destroyed 410 BCE, petition of 407; Anat-Yahu and others appear in oaths, lists and greetings.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephantine_papyri_and_ostraca

## At the Water Gate

55. The reading: first day of the seventh month, the square before the Water Gate, a wooden platform, from early morning until midday, helpers explaining, the people weeping.
   - Verdict: correct as a report of Nehemiah 8:1 to 9. The usual date is 444 BCE; Ezra's own dates are disputed (458 or 398), so "about 2,450 years ago".

55a. "Set beside the great temples of Babylon, it is a bare scene."
   - Note: the first draft said the scene "would have puzzled a priest of Marduk" and that the text was "not a secret kept by priests". Replaced: public reading of an authoritative text was not alien to Babylon (Hammurabi's epilogue invites the wronged man to have the stone read to him).

56. Scrolls "still copied by hand, in ink, on skin".
   - Verdict: correct. Torah scrolls are handwritten on parchment. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefer_Torah

## Cut after checking (for length)

Correct but no longer in the chapter: the thirty years the ration tablets waited in Berlin (item 3 now reads "a scholar in Berlin ... in the 1930s"); the Assyrian officer's speech (31); Jeremiah in the pit (34, second half); Jeremiah's letter to the exiles (40); the square script (41); the Gemariah sealing and the poorest left behind (29 and 30, second halves).
