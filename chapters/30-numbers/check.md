1. **Wrong historical contrast — high confidence.** “The Greeks had left a vast body of medical writing and no institution in which to practise it.” Hospitals existed in the Byzantine world and pre-Islamic Persia before the Abbasids. Baghdad developed an existing institutional tradition; it did not supply something previously absent. See [Timothy Miller on Byzantine hospitals](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11624568/) and [*Encyclopaedia Iranica* on Greek medicine in Persia](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/greece-x/).

2. **False impossibility claim — high confidence.** “There is no way to begin.” Roman numerals make modern column multiplication awkward, but multiplication remains possible through repeated addition or doubling and halving. The opening confuses an inconvenient notation with an impossible calculation. [This worked demonstration](https://bshelburne.wittenberguniversity.org/RomanNumeralMultiplicationAndDivision.pdf) shows multiplication directly in Roman numerals; it establishes possibility, not what ancient Romans routinely did.

3. **Overstates algebra’s scope and guarantees — high confidence.** “After these steps, any such problem comes down to one of six standard forms, and for each form he gives a recipe that always produces the answer, with a diagram to prove that it works.” His classification concerns linear and quadratic equations expressed with positive quantities, not arbitrary inheritance problems. Some cases have two answers; others have no admissible answer. Al-Khwarizmi explicitly explains when a case is impossible. See [his translated instructions](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Extras/Al-Khwarizmi_quadratics/). “Always produces the answer” also breaks the rule against exception-breaking absolutes.

4. **Misleading account of transmission — high confidence.** “In this way western Europe received Aristotle and Euclid from Arabic, a language into which they had been put by Christians of Iraq at the expense of a Muslim caliph.” Arabic transmission was crucial, but this packages several different routes, translators and patrons into one chain. Aristotle also reached medieval Latin readers directly from Greek, notably through James of Venice. Euclid’s early Arabic translations included those of al-Hajjaj. Acknowledge the parallel Greek route and avoid attributing both authors’ Arabic transmission wholesale to Christian translators. Sources: [Stanford’s account of Aristotle’s transmission](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-text/) and [McGill’s biography of al-Hajjaj](https://islamsci.mcgill.ca/RASI/BEA/Hajjaj_ibn_Yusuf_ibn_Matar_BEA.htm).

5. **Projects a mature hospital model backwards — medium-high confidence.** “It had separate wards for fevers, for eye diseases, for surgery and for the insane, with a pharmacy and a library.” Following the introduction of hospitals “from about the year 800,” this reads as the standard equipment of the early institutions. Their organisation varied, and evidence for early hospitals is often scant; elaborate later foundations cannot establish what every early hospital contained. Anchor this description to a named, dated institution. [*Iranica* distinguishes the small, poorly documented early hospitals from later developments](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bimarestan-hospital/).

6. **Wrong botanical claim — high confidence.** “The Romans and Greeks had written on papyrus, a sheet made from a reed that grows well only in Egypt.” Papyrus is a sedge native across much of Africa and parts of the eastern Mediterranean. Egypt’s importance as a manufacturing and exporting centre does not mean the plant grows well only there. [Kew’s distribution record](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A330934-2) supplies numerous counterexamples.

7. **Doubtful observational detail — medium confidence.** “On his orders, two teams of surveyors went out onto a flat plain in northern Iraq and walked north and south, measuring the ground with rods and cords, until the height of the Pole Star had changed by one degree.” The Paris Observatory’s account, citing al-Biruni, describes measuring the **noon Sun’s altitude**, with allowance for its changing declination. The underlying measurement of a degree of latitude is sound; the specific Pole Star detail needs a supporting source or replacement. See [“Mamun’s method”](https://dictionary.obspm.fr/terms/mamuns-method/).

8. **Likely anachronistic representation — medium-high confidence.** “He treated it as a number in its own right, on a line with numbers above it and numbers below it.” Brahmagupta’s arithmetic of zero, fortunes and debts is supported; attributing a number line to him is not. A historical study places an explicit interpretation of negatives as coordinates on a line later, with Bhaskara II. Present the line as a modern explanatory picture, if retained. See [*Studies in the History of Indian Mathematics*](https://www.ms.uky.edu/~sohum/ma330/files/chennai_talks/Seshadri%20%28ed%29%20-%20Studies%20in%20the%20History%20of%20Indian%20Mathematics%20%282010%29.pdf).

9. **The main explanation lacks an actual example.** “Suppose a man dies and leaves a sum to be divided according to the law, with one share depending on another.” No sum, shares or relationship follows, so the reader has nothing concrete to manipulate. “Restoring,” “balancing” and “six standard forms” then arrive without a worked calculation. Give one small problem and carry it through both operations. This is the clearest breach of the requirement that readers should be able to explain the method afterwards.

10. **An inference is left unexplained.** “The light from each lamp was therefore travelling through the hole in a straight line, without mixing with the light from the others, even though all the beams crossed in the same tiny opening.” Covering a lamp establishes which spot comes from it; the reader has not been shown why the paths must be straight. Explain the alignment of lamp, hole and spot, preferably with a diagram. Otherwise the conclusion arrives ahead of the evidence described.

11. **Discusses uncertainty about a dispensable anecdote.** “Whether or not prisoners had anything to do with it, the timing is right.” This is precisely the source-doubt discussion that *style.md* says to settle before writing. It also gives the Talas story more chronological support than it deserves: paper was known in Central Asia before the battle, and the prisoner account is much later. Keep the securely supported westward spread and omit the adjudication. **High confidence** on the evidential problem; see [Jonathan Bloom’s account](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/paper-iran-prior-printing/).

12. **Unsupported absolute.** “The scholars who wrote in Arabic respected the Greeks enormously, and the best of them refused to take the Greeks’ word for anything.” Critical examination of particular Greek claims does not establish refusal to accept *anything* on Greek authority. The ensuing examples support testing and correcting inherited knowledge. They do not support this universal description of the “best” scholars.

13. **Signpost sentence.** “The clearest case concerns a question that sounds childish: how do we see?” “The clearest case concerns” announces an example instead of entering it. The question itself supplies the transition and can stand without that framing.
