# Chapter 10 fact check (lookups)

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. Chapter file was not edited. Sources are listed per item. Research cutoff end of 2025.

## Section: Opening (title, epigraph, Ljubljana wheel)

1. "*Europe, the Middle East and the grasslands of Asia, from about 5,500 years ago to about 3,600 years ago.*" Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronocice_pot ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot . Evidence: earliest wheeled-vehicle evidence is about 3500 BC (Bronocice pot 3635-3370 BC); chariots reach Egypt around 1650 BC, about 3,600 years ago.

2. "Because a wheel is no use on its own. It needs an axle that fits it exactly, a metal tool to cut that fit, and a strong animal to pull the load." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/who-invented-the-wheel/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flintbek . Evidence: the Flintbek cart tracks and the Bronocice pot come from Funnel Beaker (Neolithic) communities in the north where copper was scarce; the Swiss National Museum says the Zurich wheels were joined with precisely fitted slotted battens and nothing is known of the tools. A metal tool is an advantage, not a proven requirement. Replacement: "Because a wheel is no use on its own. It needs an axle that fits it exactly, good tools to cut that fit, and a strong animal to pull the load."

3. "In 2002, archaeologists working in a marsh south of Ljubljana, in Slovenia, lifted a wooden wheel out of the wet ground." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marshes_Wheel ; https://rtvslo.si/news-in-english/slovenia-revealed/a-remarkable-wheel-was-found-buried-in-the-ljubljana-marshes/330614 . Evidence: found 2002 at Stare Gmajne, about 20 km south of Ljubljana, by the Institute of Archaeology team led by Anton Velusek.

4. "It is about 5,150 years old, which makes it the oldest wooden wheel that survives anywhere in the world." Verdict: correct (standard claim, with one neighbour). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marshes_Wheel ; https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/713722-oldest-wheel ; https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/who-invented-the-wheel/ . Evidence: VERA Vienna radiocarbon gives 5,100-5,350 BP (about 3150 BC) and it is catalogued as the oldest wooden wheel found; the oldest Zurich (Seefeld) wheel is dated about 3200 BC, so the two overlap within error. "Oldest known" is the usual wording and holds.

5. "It is a thick disc about seventy centimetres across, the size of a small café table." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://dlib.si/details/URN:NBN:SI:IMG-TJ61ZZV7 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marshes_Wheel . Evidence: diameter 72 cm (some sources round to 70).

6. "It is made of two planks of ash, laid side by side and held together by four strips of oak that slide into grooves cut across both of them." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://dlib.si/details/URN:NBN:SI:IMG-TJ61ZZV7 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marshes_Wheel . Evidence: Slovenian museum record: two ash boards joined by four oak wedges (braces) set in tenoned slots. (Note: "not cut from a single piece of wood" is true for this wheel; other wheels of the period in the Netherlands, Denmark and north Germany are one-piece planks with round holes.)

7. "The hole in the middle is square." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marshes_Wheel . Evidence: the axle aperture is square so wheel and axle turned together.

8. "Lying beside it was its axle, a rod of oak a little over a metre long." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://dlib.si/details/URN:NBN:SI:IMG-TJ61ZZV7 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marshes_Wheel . Evidence: oak axle 120-125 cm depending on source. The axle was found "not far away" in the same marsh (RTV Slovenija), so "lying beside it" is close enough but loose.

9. "The ends of the axle are squared off to fit the hole, so that the wheel and the axle were locked together and turned as one piece." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marshes_Wheel . Evidence: square aperture, oak wedges secured the axle; wheel and axle rotated together.

10. "People had been building houses, weaving cloth and smelting copper for thousands of years before anybody made one of these." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://dienekes.blogspot.com/2010/06/earliest-copper-smelting-from-balkans.html (summary of Radivojevic et al. 2010, J. Archaeol. Sci.). Evidence: copper smelting at Belovode, Serbia about 5000 BC, roughly 1,800 years before the wheel; houses and cloth far earlier.

11. "By the time this wheel was made, the first cities on the plain of Iraq were already centuries old." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel . Evidence: Uruk was a city by about 3500-3300 BC; the Ljubljana wheel is about 3150 BC (range 3340-3030 BC).

## Section: The axle is the hard part

12. "In the first, the wheel is fixed to the axle, as on the cart from the Slovenian marsh, and the whole axle turns in a pair of brackets under the cart." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://rtvslo.si/news-in-english/slovenia-revealed/a-remarkable-wheel-was-found-buried-in-the-ljubljana-marshes/330614 . Evidence: only one wheel and an axle were found; no cart. The design (fixed wheel, turning axle) is correctly inferred from the square hole. Replacement: "In the first, the wheel is fixed to the axle, as on the wheel and axle from the Slovenian marsh, and the whole axle turns in a pair of brackets under the cart."

13. "The obvious way to make one is to saw a slice off the end of a log... a slice of log cracks from the centre outwards as it dries." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/who-invented-the-wheel/ . Evidence: surviving early wheels are all built from lengthwise planks (two- or three-plank wheels, or one-piece plank wheels); end-grain discs split radially as they dry.

14. "Wood is weak across its grain." Verdict: correct. Source: standard wood mechanics (e.g. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood#Mechanical_properties ). Evidence: tensile strength across the grain is a small fraction of that along it.

15. "metal tools of that kind became common only when smiths learned to cast copper, a little over five and a half thousand years ago." Verdict: wrong. Sources: https://dienekes.blogspot.com/2010/06/earliest-copper-smelting-from-balkans.html ; https://core.ac.uk/download/323205862.pdf . Evidence: smelting at Belovode about 5000 BC (7,000 years ago); cast copper objects in the Balkans from about the same time; copper hammer-axes of Plocnik type about 4600 BC. By the time of the first wheels (about 3500-3150 BC) copper casting was 1,500 years old. Replacement: "For that a carpenter wants a chisel and a gouge of metal, and smiths in the Balkans had been casting copper tools for well over a thousand years by the time of the first wheels."

16. "A flint axe can fell a tree and rough out a beam. It cannot easily cut a clean, deep, square-sided slot." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/who-invented-the-wheel/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flintbek . Evidence: Neolithic carpenters cut mortise joints in stone-tool timber houses and trackways long before the wheel; the Funnel Beaker cart tracks and pot (Flintbek, Bronocice) come from stone-using communities. Replacement: "A flint axe can fell a tree and rough out a beam. A chisel and gouge of metal cut a clean, deep, square-sided slot far faster and with fewer ruined planks."

17. "The potters, it is true, already had a turning table... A potter's table spins on a single pivot and stays where it is." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter%27s_wheel . Evidence: slow wheels are known from the 4th millennium BC (oldest surviving, Ur, about 3100 BC; earlier ones in Trypillia, mid-5th millennium BC).

18. "Farmers had kept cattle for thousands of years by then." Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle#Domestication . Evidence: cattle domesticated about 10,500 years ago in the Near East.

19. "At about the same time as the wheel, they began to harness oxen in pairs to drag a plough." Verdict: correct (loose). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronocice_pot ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ard_(plough) . Evidence: ard-plough marks and draught-cattle bone deformities in Europe appear in the 4th millennium BC, matching the first wagons (Sherratt's "secondary products revolution"). Pairs of oxen under a yoke are shown in early Mesopotamian art by 3000 BC.

## Section: Everywhere at the same time

20. "In southern Poland, a farmer's pot was found with a pattern scratched round it before it was fired. The pattern shows a little four-wheeled wagon seen from above, repeated five times, with a pole at the front for the oxen." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronocice_pot ; https://ma.krakow.pl/en/tour/vase-from-bronocice/ . Evidence: Bronocice vessel from a Funnel Beaker pit; five incised representations of a wagon with four wheels, connecting lines as axles, and a shaft for a draught animal. (The "seen from above" reading is the standard schematic plan-view interpretation.)

21. "The pot is about 5,500 years old." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronocice_pot . Evidence: pit bones dated 3635-3370 BC, one animal bone about 3400 BC, so 5,400-5,650 years.

22. "In northern Germany, archaeologists took apart an ancient burial mound and found a pair of ruts running underneath it. They are the tracks of a cart that crossed the ground just before the mound was built, and they are close to the same age." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/neolithic-burial-sequence-at-flintbek-la-3-north-germany-and-its-cart-tracks-a-precise-chronology/760E97687C27589B1BD8C4C8E078FC78 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flintbek . Evidence: Mischka (Antiquity 2011) dates the tracks to 3420-3385 cal BC, beneath the final barrow stage of the long barrow LA 3 at Flintbek, which had earlier building phases. So the tracks lie under the last extension of a mound that already existed, and "close to the same age" as the Polish pot holds. Replacement: "In northern Germany, archaeologists took apart a long burial mound at Flintbek and found a pair of ruts running underneath its last extension. They are the tracks of a cart that crossed the ground about 5,400 years ago, close to the age of the Polish pot."

23. "On the plain of Iraq, meanwhile, the clerks of Uruk were pressing their first signs into clay. One of those signs is a drawing of a sledge... Another shows the same sledge with two small circles under it." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://www.academia.edu/39805158/Some_Notes_on_Pictograms_Interpreted_as_Sledges_and_Wheeled_Vehicles_in_the_Archaic_Texts_from_Uruk ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel . Evidence: Burmeister, Krispijn and Raulwing (BAR 2019) read the sledge signs and the "wagon" sign (a sledge with two round impressions under the runners) as wheeled-vehicle pictograms, from the Late Uruk period (c. 3300-3000 BC); the circles are an interpretation, and the wagon sign occurs only a handful of times against about 40 sledge signs. Replacement: "Another shows the same sledge with two small circles under it, which scholars read as wheels."

24. "Nobody can say for certain which of these places had the wheel first. The dates are too close together to separate." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/who-invented-the-wheel/ ; Bakker et al. 1999, Antiquity 73, "The earliest evidence of wheeled vehicles in Europe and the Near East" https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/earliest-evidence-of-wheeled-vehicles-in-europe-and-the-near-east/ . Evidence: Swiss National Museum: Northwest Europe, the Alps, southeast Europe and the Middle East have "an equal chance".

25. "the wheel was not invented again and again by different peoples. It was invented once, or very nearly, and then it spread from farm to farm faster than anything had spread before." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/who-invented-the-wheel/ . Evidence: researchers say there may be several winners; the chapter's own previous line says nobody can say where it began. Single invention is a hypothesis; fast spread is well supported. Replacement: "Where the wheel began is not known. What the record shows is that within a few centuries it was in use from the North Sea to the Persian Gulf, a faster spread than any earlier invention."

26. "Within three or four centuries it was in use from the North Sea to the Persian Gulf." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel . Evidence: Flintbek (north Germany) 3400 BC, Bronocice 3500-3400 BC, Uruk signs 3300 BC, Ljubljana and Zurich 3200-3100 BC.

27. "An ox walks at about three kilometres an hour, which is slower than a person." Verdict: could not verify (plausible). Sources: no source retrieved gave a figure in km/h (searches returned only m/s tables in draught-animal papers, e.g. https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08daae5274a27b200198d/R5198u.pdf). Evidence: working-ox pace is commonly given as 2-3.5 km/h against about 5 km/h for a person walking; the claim is almost certainly right but unsourced here.

28. "With a cart and a pair of oxen, one person could move in a morning what had taken a household several days." Verdict: could not verify. Evidence: rhetorical estimate with no source. Leave, or soften to a concrete figure from Anthony's comparisons if wanted.

## Section: Toys in Mexico

29. "In the east of Mexico, archaeologists have found dozens of small clay animals, mostly dogs and jaguars, made between about two thousand and one thousand years ago." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://uncoveredhistory.com/mesoamerica/wheeled-toys/ ; https://artmuseum.princeton.edu/collections/objects/42386 ; https://bowers.org/collections-blog/wheeled-jaguar-effigy-veracruz-mexico . Evidence: about 100 are known, found along the Gulf coast (Veracruz) and also in Guerrero, Michoacan, Teotihuacan and El Salvador; they date mainly to the Classic and Early Postclassic (about 300-1200 CE, 1,700-800 years ago); museum examples are 550-800 CE and 750-900 CE. Replacement: "Along the Gulf coast of Mexico, archaeologists have found about a hundred small clay animals, mostly dogs and jaguars, made between about 1,700 and 1,100 years ago."

30. "Each one stands on four clay discs. Thin rods once ran through the feet, from one side to the other, and the discs turned on the ends of them." Verdict: correct. Source: https://uncoveredhistory.com/mesoamerica/wheeled-toys/ . Evidence: axles run through loops or tubes on the legs, with a wheel at each end.

31. "They are toys or offerings on wheels, complete with axles." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://cabinetmagazine.org/issues/51/editorial2.php ; https://artmuseum.princeton.edu/art/stories-perspectives/wheeled-feline . Evidence: scholars debate toy versus ritual use; many come from burials and dedicatory caches.

32. "They were also fine engineers, who raised pyramids and laid out straight stone roads through the forest." Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacbe . Evidence: Maya sacbeob are raised, straight, paved limestone causeways in the forest, up to about 100 km long.

33. "The large animals of the Americas had died out soon after people arrived, and the horses and the wild cattle were among them." Verdict: wrong in part. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7682371 ; https://news.ucsb.edu/2001/011478/humans-hunted-mammals-extinction-north-america . Evidence: horses and camels died out; bison survived and are the living wild cattle of North America. Replacement: "The large animals of the Americas had died out soon after people arrived, and the horses and the camels were among them."

34. "The one beast of burden left in the whole hemisphere was the llama, far to the south in the Andes." Verdict: correct (alpaca is a fibre animal, not a pack animal). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pack_animal . Evidence: llama was the only pack animal of the pre-Columbian Americas.

35. "A llama can carry a load about as heavy as a small child, on mountain paths too steep for any wheel." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://backpackingguys.com/?p=7272 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pack_animal . Evidence: an adult llama carries about 20-30 percent of its weight, roughly 25-30 kg, the weight of a child of eight to ten. Replacement: "A llama can carry about thirty kilograms, the weight of a ten-year-old child, on mountain paths too steep for any wheel."

36. "The wheelbarrow... it does not appear until some three thousand years after the cart." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelbarrow . Evidence: possible Greek use at Eleusis, 408/407 BC (one-wheeler part in building inventories; evidence not clear); the first clear Chinese depictions are about the first and second centuries AD (the consensus among historians of technology is that it was invented in China around 100 AD), about 3,500 years after the first carts. Replacement: "Nevertheless, it does not appear until some three and a half thousand years after the cart: the first clear pictures are from Han China, around the year 100."

37. "It is a box with a single wheel, pushed by one person, and it roughly doubles what that person can move." Verdict: could not verify. Evidence: no source gives a figure; the often-quoted claim is that a barrow lets one person carry about twice to three times as much as on the back. Safer to drop the number or write "lets one person move far more than can be carried".

## Section: The sea of grass

38. "It begins near the mouth of the Danube and goes on eastward, past the Black Sea and the Caspian, across Kazakhstan, as far as Mongolia. From end to end it is about eight thousand kilometres long." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://www.britannica.com/place/the-Steppe ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe . Evidence: Britannica gives about 8,000 km (5,000 miles) from Hungary to Manchuria. The Danube mouth to Mongolia is only about 6,000-6,500 km, so the endpoints named do not match the length given. Replacement: "It begins in Hungary and goes on eastward, past the Black Sea and the Caspian, across Kazakhstan and Mongolia, as far as Manchuria. From end to end it is about eight thousand kilometres long."

39. "It is flat or gently rolling, almost treeless, hot in summer and savagely cold in winter. ... There is too little rain for wheat on most of it." Verdict: correct. Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/the-Steppe . Evidence: treeless grassland with continental climate and low rainfall.

40. "Before the wheel, people lived along the rivers that cross the grassland... The great spaces between the rivers were left almost empty." Verdict: correct. Sources: Wilkin et al. 2021, Nature 598, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03798-4 ; https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2023/03/first-human-horseback-riding . Evidence: Eneolithic steppe economy was river-based (fishing, gathering, some stock); Anthony credits wagons with opening the plateaus between river valleys, "the most mobile form of economy".

41. "About 5,300 years ago, the herders who lived along the rivers north of the Black Sea got hold of the wagon." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture ; Wilkin et al. 2021 (Early Bronze Age Yamnaya about 3300-2500 BC). Evidence: radiocarbon-dated wagon graves such as Ostannii (Ostanii) and Balki are about 3100 BC; Yamnaya horizon from about 3300 BC.

42. "They roofed it over with a hoop of bent poles covered in reed mats and felt. In effect they had built a house on wheels." Verdict: could not verify. Evidence: no Yamnaya wagon cover survives. The felt-covered wagon tent is a reconstruction from much later steppe peoples. Replacement: "Their wagons had four solid wheels and a pole for the oxen, and they carried their belongings and their water across the grass." (Later steppe herders did roof wagons with felt on bent poles.)

43. "When an important person died, these herders dug a pit, laid the body in it, roofed the pit with timber, and heaped a mound of earth over the top. Thousands of those mounds still stand on the steppe, and some are as high as a house." Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture . Evidence: pit graves under kurgans, bodies on the back with knees up and covered in ochre; kurgans number in the tens of thousands.

44. "In a few hundred of them, the dead person was buried with a wagon, taken to pieces, with the solid wheels laid at the corners of the pit." Verdict: correct on count; could not verify on wheel placement. Sources: https://journals.ur.edu.pl/anarres/article/view/10701 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture . Evidence: about 280 wagon burials are known from the Urals to the lower Danube (Kaiser 2007 as cited), highest concentration in the north-west Pontic region. Placement of wheels varies grave to grave; I found no source for "at the corners". Replacement: "In about three hundred of them, the dead person was buried with a wagon, often taken to pieces, with its solid wheels laid in the pit."

45. "They were tall and strongly built." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture ; Mathieson et al. 2015, Nature 528 (height selection). Evidence: the support is genetic (the highest genetically predicted height of any ancient group tested); "their bones show" and "strongly built" are not documented by the sources found. Replacement: "Their DNA shows they were among the tallest people of their age."

46. "The hardened deposit on their teeth still holds traces of what they ate, and it is full of the proteins of milk. They lived on dairy and meat, and they ate very little grain." Verdict: correct. Source: Wilkin et al. 2021, Nature 598:629-633, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03798-4 (text read from the PDF). Evidence: 56 steppe individuals tested; milk peptides in 15 of 16 (94%) Early Bronze Age (Yamnaya) individuals against 1 uncertain of 11 in the Eneolithic; species were cow, sheep and goat; paper says diet of Yamnaya focused on herd animals. Small wording point: "traces" is safer than "full of".

47. "Within a few generations, the wagon herders had spread across an immense stretch of country." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture . Evidence: Anthony dates the spread across the steppe to about 3400-3200 BC (a few centuries); the Danube valley and Hungary c. 3100-2600 BC. Replacement: "Within a few centuries, the wagon herders had spread across an immense stretch of country."

48. "Graves of the same kind, with the same pits, the same mounds and the same red ochre... from the plains of Hungary to the mountains on the edge of Mongolia. Those two ends are about five thousand kilometres apart." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture ; https://www.sciencenews.org/article/how-asian-nomadic-herders-built-new-bronze-age-cultures . Evidence: Yamnaya graves in Hungary; Afanasievo (genetically indistinguishable from Yamnaya) in the Altai; great-circle distance Hungary-Altai is about 4,700-5,000 km (my estimate, no source states it).

## Section: The horse

49. "About 5,500 years ago, in the north of Kazakhstan, one group began to do more than hunt. Their villages are full of horse bones, and their cooking pots carry traces of mare's milk. Nobody can milk a wild horse, so these people were keeping horses in some fashion." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/news-blog/first-domesticated-horses-in-centra-2009-03-05/ (Outram et al. 2009, Science) ; https://scholar.colorado.edu/concern/articles/9306t0641 (Taylor and Barron-Ortiz 2021, Sci. Rep.) ; Wilkin et al. 2021 (no milk peptides in two Botai individuals). Evidence: date (3500 BC) and horse-dominated bone assemblage are right; mare's-milk fat in nine pots is the 2009 finding. The domestication reading is contested: Taylor and Barron-Ortiz argue Botai people mass-hunted wild Przewalski horses and that the "bit wear" is natural; the genetic link to later Przewalski horses is firm. Replacement: "About 5,500 years ago, in the north of Kazakhstan, one group built its life around the horse. Their villages are full of horse bones, and their cooking pots carry the fat of mare's milk."

50. "Their horses were not the ancestors of the horses in a stable today." Verdict: correct. Source: Gaunitz et al. 2018, Science 360:111-114, https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02406833 . Evidence: modern domestic horses carry only about 2.7% Botai-related ancestry; Librado et al. 2021 confirm Botai is a separate lineage from DOM2.

51. "Instead, the breed they kept survives in the small, stocky wild horse of Mongolia." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02406833 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przewalski%27s_horse . Evidence: Gaunitz et al. conclude Przewalski's horses are the feral descendants of Botai horses, so "wild" is wrong in the usual sense and "breed" is loose; "of Mongolia" is the reintroduced range (the last wild animals died out in the 1960s). Replacement: "Their line survives in Przewalski's horse, the small, stocky horse of Mongolia, which descends from horses once kept at Botai."

52. "Geneticists have read the DNA in the bones of hundreds of ancient horses from all over Europe and Asia... Every modern horse descends from a population that lived on the steppe near the lower reaches of the Volga and the Don, north of the Caucasus mountains." Verdict: correct. Sources: Librado et al. 2021, Nature 598:634-640, https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/127531?show=full ; https://www.sci.news/paleontology/horse-domestication-western-eurasian-steppes-10191.html . Evidence: 273 ancient genomes; homeland of modern domestic horses (DOM2) is the lower Volga-Don region.

53. "About 4,200 years ago, those horses began to spread. Within a few centuries they had replaced almost every other kind of horse from Spain to Mongolia." Verdict: correct. Sources: Librado et al. 2024, Nature 631:819-825 (title: "Widespread horse-based mobility arose around 2200 BCE in Eurasia") https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11269178 ; https://le.ac.uk/news/2024/june/horses ; https://www.sci.news/paleontology/horse-domestication-western-eurasian-steppes-10191.html . Evidence: spread begins about 2200 BCE (4,200 years ago); the 2021 paper says DOM2 replaced local populations across Eurasia within roughly 500 years.

54. "The new horses carried one changed gene that is linked to a calmer temper, and another that is linked to a stronger back." Verdict: correct (as "linked"). Sources: Librado et al. 2021 (GSDMC and ZFPM1 selected) ; https://labroots.net/trending/genetics-and-genomics/29532/genetic-mutation-led-rideable-horses ; https://www.sci.news/paleontology/horse-domestication-western-eurasian-steppes-10191.html . Evidence: ZFPM1 is tied to fear and stress behaviour, docility; GSDMC to spine and body build; "variants of two genes", not one changed gene each, is more exact. Replacement: "The new horses carried changes in two genes, one linked to a calmer temper and the other to a stronger back."

55. "One person walking, with a good dog, can manage about two hundred sheep. On horseback, the same person can manage five hundred." Verdict: correct (secondary source for the figures). Sources: https://www.lyriahnam.com/reviews/nonfiction-the-horse-the-wheel-and-language-by-david-w-anthony (review of Anthony 2007, giving about 200 on foot with a dog and about 500 mounted) ; https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2023/03/first-human-horseback-riding (Anthony: riding made herding more efficient). Evidence: Anthony's book is the origin; I could not open the primary text.

56. "A rider can also cover in a day a distance that takes an ox wagon a week." Verdict: imprecise. Evidence: with the chapter's own ox speed (about 3 km/h, a working day of 6-8 hours, 20-25 km) a week is 150 km; a rider does not cover that in a day. A single rider covers 50-70 km a day, about three times an ox wagon. No source needed beyond the chapter's own figures. Replacement: "A rider can also cover in a day a distance that takes an ox wagon three or four days."

## Section: The language in the cart

57. "In the eighteenth century, scholars noticed that languages spoken very far apart resembled each other... The ancient language of India shared hundreds of words and whole patterns of grammar with Greek and Latin, and also with the old languages of the Germans, the Celts and the Slavs." Verdict: correct. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages (history; William Jones 1786, earlier Coeurdoux 1767). Evidence: Sanskrit-Greek-Latin resemblance recognised in the 1700s.

58. "In ancient Greek the word was *kyklos*, which gives English 'cycle'. In the ancient language of India it was *chakra*. These are the same word... The same holds for axle, for the yoke... and for a verb meaning to carry in a vehicle, which survives in English as both 'wagon' and 'way'." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Indo-European/k%CA%B7%C3%A9k%CA%B7los ; https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Indo-European/we%C7%B5%CA%B0- ; https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0526.05.pdf (Lubotsky). Evidence: PIE *kʷékʷlos (Greek kyklos, Sanskrit cakrá, English wheel), *h2eḱs- axle, *yugóm yoke, *weǵʰ- "to transport in a vehicle" (English way and wagon, Sanskrit vahati).

59. "A people cannot have a word for something they have never seen. So the speakers of that parent language had wheels, axles and yokes, and they were still one people, speaking one tongue, after the wagon was invented. That puts them less than 5,500 years ago." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0526.05.pdf (Lubotsky: Anatolian has the yoke and pole terms but no wheel word) ; https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3754407/view ; Kloekhorst and Pronk 2019 (Proto-Indo-Anatolian split about the end of the 5th millennium BC). Evidence: the wheel vocabulary belongs to the branches other than Anatolian; Hittite and its kin left before the wheel. Replacement: "So the speakers of that parent language, apart from the Anatolian branch, which had already left, had wheels, axles and yokes, and they were still one people, speaking one tongue, after the wagon was invented. That puts them less than 5,500 years ago."

60. "Their shared words also speak of sheep, cattle, horses, wool and milk, and of very few crops." Verdict: correct. Source: Anthony 2007, summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Horse,_the_Wheel,_and_Language . Evidence: reconstructed vocabulary is strongly pastoral, with sheep, cow, horse, wool and milk words and only a small farming vocabulary.

61. "Then, beginning in 2015, geneticists read the DNA of the people in the steppe grave mounds, and of the people who lived in Europe before and after them." Verdict: correct. Sources: Haak et al. 2015, Nature 522:207-211, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25731166/ ; Allentoft et al. 2015, Nature 522:167-172. Evidence: both published in 2015.

62. "About 4,800 years ago, the ancestry of northern and central Europe changed sharply. In Germany, about three quarters of the ancestry of the people buried after that date came from the steppe herders." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/entities/publication/01897a8e-b4cb-4621-90b0-4441285907ab ; https://www.mpg.de/9005184/humans-migration-indo-european-languages . Evidence: Haak 2015: Corded Ware people from Germany traced about 75% of their ancestry to the Yamnaya; Corded Ware appears by about 2900 BC (4,900 years ago).

63. "In Britain, a few centuries later, the change was closer to nine tenths." Verdict: imprecise. Source: Olalde et al. 2018, Nature 555:190-196, https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/nature25738_Olalde.pdf (text read from the PDF). Evidence: the 90% (minimum 90 ± 2%) is the share of Britain's gene pool replaced after the Beaker arrival (about 2450 BC), not the share of steppe ancestry; the newcomers were a mix of steppe and earlier European farmer ancestry, with individual Beaker people ranging from 0% to 75% steppe. Replacement: "In Britain, a few centuries later, newcomers who carried a large share of steppe ancestry replaced about nine tenths of the people's gene pool."

64. "A similar movement went east and south over the following thousand years, through Central Asia and into Iran and the north of India." Verdict: correct. Source: Narasimhan et al. 2019, Science 365:eaat7487, https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/2019_Science_NarasimhanPatterson_CentralSouthAsia_Supplement.pdf . Evidence: steppe ancestry reaches Central and South Asia in the 2nd millennium BC.

65. "Today, almost half of the human race speaks a language descended from the speech of those herders. English, Spanish, Russian, Persian and Hindi are among them. They are spoken on every continent." Verdict: correct (loose). Sources: https://humans-who-read-grammars.blogspot.com/2017/03/half-of-worlds-languages-are-not-indo.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages . Evidence: Ethnologue-based figures give about 46% (3.2 billion first-language speakers, 2017), and about 42% on the 3.4 billion figure against today's population. "Almost half" is fair for the older figure; "more than two in five" is safe for both.

66. "Their common ancestor was the language of a few tens of thousands of people who lived in wagons on the grass north of the Black Sea." Verdict: could not verify. Evidence: no source found for a population figure; the steppe homeland is the mainstream view but the people did not live in wagons full time and no estimate of speaker numbers exists. Replacement: "Their common ancestor was the language of herders who lived on the grass north of the Black Sea."

## Section: Spokes

67. "A pair of them, with their axle, can weigh more than the driver." Verdict: could not verify (plausible). Evidence: a 72 cm, 5 cm thick ash wheel like the Ljubljana one is about 10-15 kg; larger wagon wheels run heavier. Unsourced.

68. "So for more than a thousand years, the fastest animal that people kept and the wheel had little to do with each other." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11269178 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot . Evidence: wheeled vehicles from about 3500 BC were drawn by oxen; the earliest horse-drawn spoked vehicles are Sintashta, about 2000 BC.

69. "The people who brought them together lived about four thousand years ago, on the steppe just east of the Ural mountains. They were descendants of the wagon herders." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintashta_culture ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/chariots-in-the-eurasian-steppe-a-bayesian-approach-to-the-emergence-of-horsedrawn-transport-in-the-early-second-millennium-bc/C4FDF8C5E7D1D20A28BEB7F6C50A9AF4 (Lindner 2020). Evidence: Sintashta in the Trans-Urals, c. 2100-1800 BC (Sintashta stage c. 2040-1730 cal BC); Poltavka (a Yamnaya offshoot) is the local precursor, mixed with Corded Ware-related Abashevo people.

70. "They built round, walled settlements, with the houses packed together inside a bank and a ditch. In almost every house there was a furnace for smelting copper." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintashta_culture ; https://www.ancient-history-sites.com/?p=169874 . Evidence: circular timber-reinforced ramparts with V-shaped ditches; Anthony reports metallurgical remains (slag, ovens, hearths, copper) in every excavated house; some sites are oval or rectangular, but circular is typical (Sintashta, Arkaim).

71. "On the floor of the burial pit there are two narrow slots, cut side by side. In the earth at the sides of each slot are the faint prints of thin wooden rods... They are the marks of spoked wheels... They are the oldest spoked wheels known." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/chariots-in-the-eurasian-steppe-a-bayesian-approach-to-the-emergence-of-horsedrawn-transport-in-the-early-second-millennium-bc/C4FDF8C5E7D1D20A28BEB7F6C50A9AF4 ; https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Chariot . Evidence: only soil imprints of the lower wheel survive, in slots cut in the grave floor at Sintashta, Krivoe Ozero and Kamennyi Ambar 5; dates about 2000-1900 BC; "oldest known spoked wheels" is the standard claim (Raulwing and Burmeister dispute that the vehicles were chariots rather than carts, not that they had spokes).

72. "Between hub and rim run ten or a dozen slender spokes." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://www.ancient-origins.net/node/16210 ; summary of Anthony 2007. Evidence: Anthony: rim 1-1.2 m across, 10-12 spokes of square section; popular sources give 8-12.

73. "The rim is made of wood that has been soaked or steamed and bent into a circle... The whole thing weighs a fraction of what a solid wheel weighs, and it is just as strong, because every spoke shares the load." Verdict: imprecise. Evidence: the Sintashta imprints show the rim and spokes but not how the rim was made; the bent-rim description comes from later chariots. A spoked wheel is much lighter and strong enough for a chariot, not "just as strong" as a solid disc. Replacement: "The whole thing weighs a fraction of what a solid wheel weighs, and it is strong enough for the job, because every spoke shares the load."

74. "Two such wheels, a light axle, a platform of bent wood and leather straps, and a pole: that is a chariot. One man can pick it up." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/indoeuropean-puzzle-revisited/bronze-age-chariot-and-wool-horizons/EA263A952AD59E13C3AE0B55D143A027 ; https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Chariot . Evidence: only the wheels' imprints (and at Krivoe Ozero traces of axle and naves) survive; nothing is known of the platform or its leather, and reconstructions above axle height are uncertain. Replacement: "Two such wheels, a light axle, a platform and a pole: that is a chariot. A man could lift one."

75. "Hitch two of the new horses to it, and it can carry two people across open ground at more than thirty kilometres an hour." Verdict: could not verify. Evidence: no source found for a Sintashta speed; replica Bronze Age chariots are reported at roughly 30-40 km/h but I did not retrieve a source. Leave or cite a replica test.

76. "The chariot went into the ground with its wheels in their slots. The two horses were killed and laid beside it. With them went the bone cheek-pieces from their bridles, the dead man's spear, and his arrows." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintashta_culture ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/chariots-in-the-eurasian-steppe-a-bayesian-approach-to-the-emergence-of-horsedrawn-transport-in-the-early-second-millennium-bc/C4FDF8C5E7D1D20A28BEB7F6C50A9AF4 . Evidence: chariot graves hold horse sacrifices (usually pairs), disk-shaped bone and antler cheek-pieces and weapons (spearheads, trilobate arrowheads); weapons occur in over half the chariot graves.

77. "It was also a weapon. One man drives. The other stands beside him with a bow and a quiver, and shoots as they pass." Verdict: imprecise. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Chariot ; https://revistas.fcsh.unl.pt/res/article/view/573 . Evidence: the Sintashta graves contain spearheads and arrowheads but not the composite bow later used from chariots; whether the vehicles were fought from is argued (Raulwing and Burmeister read them as carts). Chariot archery is secure for the Near East and Egypt later. Replacement: "It was also a weapon. Its owners were buried with spears and arrows, and a fighter on a chariot had a speed that men on foot could not match."

78. "Within three centuries of those graves, chariots were being used in Turkey and Syria." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/transportation-land ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot . Evidence: the earliest true chariot images are cylinder seals from Kultepe (Kanesh), Anatolia, about 1900 BC, and shortly after in Syria; the Sintashta graves are about 2000-1900 BC, so the gap may be under one century. "Within three centuries" is safe; "within a century or two" is more exact.

79. "A century after that, they were in Egypt." Verdict: imprecise. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot . Evidence: chariots reached Egypt around 1650 BC with the Hyksos, 250 years after the Kultepe seals. Replacement: "Two or three centuries after that, around 1650 BC, they were in Egypt."

80. "In time they reached India, and at last China." Verdict: correct. Sources: https://www.history.com/articles/everyday-objects-shang-dynasty ; Wu 2013, Chariots in Early China (BAR 2457). Evidence: earliest Chinese chariots are Late Shang burials at Anyang, about 1200 BC.

81. "an enemy with a few hundred light carts and trained horses could ride round their armies and shoot them down." Verdict: could not verify. Evidence: narrative claim with no source; chariot armies of the later Bronze Age numbered in the hundreds to thousands (Kadesh, about 1274 BC). Fine as storytelling; tone down "shoot them down" if the replacement in item 77 is used.
