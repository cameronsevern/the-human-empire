# Chapter 11 — Why did the Bronze Age end?

**Line under the title:** The eastern Mediterranean, from about 3,900 years ago to about 3,100 years ago.
**Length:** 3,500 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Bronze is copper with a little tin, and tin is rare and far away. A kingdom that wanted bronze tools, bronze armour and a chariot force therefore depended on mines, ships, donkeys and foreign kings it did not control. For several centuries that dependence produced the first international world: kings who wrote to each other as brothers in a shared language, and ships carrying the goods of ten countries. It also meant that the palaces stood or fell together. About 3,200 years ago, drought, famine, earthquakes, raiders from the sea and revolt struck many of them within one lifetime, and most of the great palaces burned. No single cause explains it. The palaces that had gathered everything into their own hands had nothing to fall back on. What came through were the small, the flexible and the traders, and the metal that came next was one that needed no trade at all.

## The story

1. **The ship that did not arrive.** Answer first. The wreck off the Turkish coast and what was in it.
2. **Nine parts copper, one part tin.** What bronze is and why it is better. Copper is common enough. Tin is not.
3. **The donkeys of Ashur.** A family firm of tin merchants, six weeks on the road, and the letters their wives wrote. How many hands lie behind one bronze axe.
4. **Brothers.** The great kings write to each other. Gold like dust. One language for all of them.
5. **What a chariot costs.** Horses, trainers, wheelwrights, armour. Palace clerks counting wheels. The largest chariot battle.
6. **The enemy's ships have come.** One lifetime: the port in Syria, the capital in Turkey, the palaces of Greece, Egypt's last stand.
7. **One at a time, or all at once.** Drought, famine, earthquakes, raiders, revolt. Why none is enough alone, and why together they were fatal.
8. **Who came through.** Egypt shrunk, the coastal traders free of their overlords, and iron.
