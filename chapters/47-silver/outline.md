# Chapter 47 — Why didn't American silver make Spain rich?

**Line under the title:** Peru, Spain and China, from 1527 to about 1650.
**Length:** 4,200 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Spain took more silver out of the Americas than the world had ever seen, and it passed through the country like water through a pipe. The king had spent it before it arrived, on wars paid for with loans that were secured on the next fleet. Spaniards used the rest to buy what other countries made, while prices at home rose until Spanish goods could not compete. The silver went on to the king's bankers in Italy and Germany, to his soldiers in the Netherlands, and above all to China, whose huge economy had switched to silver and would pay twice the European price for it. A hill in Bolivia, worked by conscripted Andean villagers, became the first thing to link the economies of four continents.

## The story

1. **Opening: a line on a wall.** Answer first. The ransom room at Cajamarca, filled and melted.
2. **An empire without money.** What the Inca state was, the epidemic that ran ahead of the Spanish, the war between brothers, and the ambush.
3. **The rich hill.** Potosí: the find, the city above the clouds, and how mercury gets silver out of poor rock.
4. **One man in seven.** The labour draft: a conscript's journey, his week underground, and the mercury mine.
5. **Pieces of eight.** The coin and the fleets. The king's share and what he did with it. A banker's letter that moves money without moving metal. Borrowing against silver still in the ground. Four bankruptcies.
6. **The Indies of the foreigner.** Rising prices, idle workshops, and why a mountain of silver left its owner in debt.
7. **The galleon to Manila.** Why China wanted silver so badly, what it paid, and the ship that crossed the Pacific once a year. A merchant's sums.
8. **Ending: the sign for a dollar.**
