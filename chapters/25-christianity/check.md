Read the requested files; no files changed. Findings below are ordered by seriousness. Confidence refers to the finding, not to certainty about every ancient event.

1. **Factual overstatement — the central explanation needs firmer boundaries.**  
   > “Because for three hundred years the Christian churches gave ordinary people in Roman cities a family of strangers that fed its poor, nursed its sick and buried its dead, together with a promise of life after death that was open to anyone.”

   Christian mutual aid is well attested. Its importance relative to other causes of conversion is harder to establish. The subsequent argument repeatedly turns a plausible explanation into demonstrated population history. Keep the interpretation, but distinguish documented charitable practices from proposed mechanisms of growth. **Confidence: high.** See [Stark’s argument](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv10crfgh) and [research on the limitations of growth models](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9809.2009.00826.x).

2. **Factual error — government assistance was not confined to Rome’s grain dole.**  
   > “No pagan temple did anything of the kind, and neither did the government, whose free grain was a privilege of citizens in the capital.”

   The imperial *alimenta* subsidised children in Italian towns. Grain distributions also existed outside Rome, including at Oxyrhynchus. These were not identical to Christian poor relief, but they invalidate the categorical contrast. **Confidence: high.** Sources: [Cambridge study of the alimenta](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/40F7875AB803280B4904053A02A90370/9781009496964apxB_228-241.pdf/dataset-alimenta-pledges.pdf), [Oxyrhynchus application for grain](https://egyptianinscriptions.org/object/143018-application-for-admission-to-the-grain-dole-in-place-of-a-deceased-recipient).

3. **Doubtful inference presented as an established outcome.**  
   > “A group that nursed its sick came out of an epidemic with more survivors, and with pagan neighbours who owed their lives to it.”

   Nursing could improve survival; close contact could also increase exposure. We lack comparative mortality data establishing the net Christian advantage asserted here. Modern research tests this as a hypothesis using simulations, rather than measuring an ancient outcome. **Confidence: high about the evidentiary limitation.** [Everton and Schroeder’s study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jssr.12631).

4. **Factual overstatement — hypothetical figures become a count.**  
   > “Historians who have tried to count estimate that there were perhaps a few thousand Christians in the year 100 and several million by 300, about a tenth of the empire.”

   These figures closely reproduce Stark’s illustrative growth model. Its early totals are outputs of an assumed growth rate and starting population, not independent counts. A substantial Christian minority by 300 is defensible; this particular trajectory should be identified as a model. **Confidence: high.** [Stark](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv10crfgh); [quantitative modelling critique](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9809.2009.00826.x).

5. **Factual distortion — pagan religion also offered personal afterlife hopes.**  
   > “The old gods looked after cities and harvests and had little to say about what happened to an ordinary person after death.”

   Mystery cults, including those of Demeter and Persephone, explicitly addressed individual prospects after death. Christianity’s particular promise can be distinguished without implying an empty religious market. **Confidence: high.** [Metropolitan Museum account of mystery cults](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/mystery-cults-in-the-greek-and-roman-world).

6. **Factual overstatement — Paul did not originate the entire non-Jewish mission.**  
   > “The man who changed that never met Jesus.”

   In context, “changed that” makes Paul personally responsible for admitting non-Jews. Even Acts describes missionaries addressing Greeks at Antioch before Barnabas brings Paul there. Paul was a crucial advocate of membership without circumcision, not its sole originator. **Confidence: high.** [Acts 11:19–26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+11%3A19-26&version=NIV).

7. **Factual error — the legion survived.**  
   > “The rebels took Jerusalem and wiped out a Roman legion sent to retake it.”

   Cestius Gallus’s army suffered a disastrous defeat during its retreat in 66. The Twelfth Legion lost its eagle but survived and subsequently served in the war and elsewhere. “Defeated a Roman army” would preserve the point accurately. **Confidence: high.** [History of XII Fulminata](https://www.livius.org/articles/legion/legio-xii-fulminata/).

8. **Chronological problem — Jerusalem’s Roman refoundation is placed after the revolt.**  
   > “He rebuilt Jerusalem as a Roman town with a temple of Jupiter, forbade Jews to enter it, and renamed the province after their ancient enemies the Philistines.”

   The sentence follows the suppression of the revolt in 135. Cassius Dio instead makes the Roman city and Jupiter temple a cause of the revolt; the conventional foundation date is about 130. The exact sequence remains debated, so these measures should not be bundled into one securely dated aftermath. The motive behind the provincial renaming also needs qualification. **Confidence: high about the misleading sequence; medium about precise reconstruction.** [Cassius Dio 69.12](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/69%2A.html), [Hadrian chronology](https://www.livius.org/articles/person/hadrian/).

9. **Factual error — Christian teaching did sometimes urge widows to remarry.**  
   > “They also honoured widows and did not press them to remarry, and women appear as hosts and organisers in Paul's letters from the start.”

   The second half is sound. The first overlooks the distinction between older and younger widows: 1 Timothy explicitly recommends marriage and childbearing for younger widows. Christian practice cannot be described categorically here. **Confidence: high.** [1 Timothy 5:14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Timothy+5%3A14&version=NIV%3BESV).

10. **Factual overstatement — Stoicism is reduced to a misleading opposite of Christianity.**  
    > “Their philosophy offered no life after death, and it was not meant for people who could not read.”

    Some Stoics taught that souls survived bodily death, although not necessarily forever. The literacy exclusion is also unsupported: Stoicism was taught orally, and advanced literary study was not synonymous with practising its ethics. The defensible distinction is that it offered no equivalent of the Christian promise of universal resurrection and eternal life. **Confidence: high on survival doctrines; medium-high on the literacy claim.** [Diogenes Laertius 7.157](https://www.whatwouldstoicsdo.com/passage/lives-of-the-stoics/7.157), [Stanford account of Stoic teaching and sources](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/).

11. **Factual error and unsafe medical generalisation.**  
    > “Most victims of such a disease do not need medicine, which in any case did not exist.”

    Medicine and medicines certainly existed; Galen treated and described epidemic illness. What was lacking was an effective specific cure for these infections. Moreover, without identifying the disease, the claim about what “most victims” need cannot be established. **Confidence: high.** [Research on Galen and the plague](https://doi.org/10.1163%2F9789004383302_011).

12. **Factual error — persecution continued beyond 311 in parts of the East.**  
    > “It went on for eight years in the eastern provinces, and several thousand people died.”

    Galerius issued toleration in 311, but Maximinus renewed persecution, which continued into 313. The later banquet sentence—“Many of the men present had been through the persecution that ended fourteen years earlier”—repeats the same simplification. **Confidence: high.** [Eusebius, *Church History*, book IX](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250109.htm).

13. **Factual overstatement — bishops did not routinely recognise all one another’s decisions.**  
    > “The bishops wrote to one another, met in councils, and recognised one another's decisions from Spain to Syria.”

    Communication and councils are correct. Mutual recognition was contested: the third-century dispute over rebaptism divided Rome from bishops in Africa and Asia Minor, including threats to break communion. The network was substantial but neither uniformly united nor a single administrative hierarchy. **Confidence: high.** [Eusebius, book VII, chapters 3–5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250107.htm).

14. **Chronological misuse of evidence.**  
    > “The strongest evidence for all this comes from an enemy.”

    Julian’s letter supports Christian charitable activity in 362, after roughly fifty years of imperial patronage. It cannot by itself establish the preceding claims about pre-Constantinian survival rates, women’s recruitment or three centuries of growth. Introduce it as evidence that Christian charity remained conspicuous in Julian’s time. **Confidence: high.** [Julian’s letter to Arsacius](https://romanletters.org/letters/julian_emperor/22/).

15. **Factual error and exception-breaking absolute.**  
    > “It is the one physical trace of a man whose name is spoken aloud every week in churches all over the world.”

    Coins issued under Pilate survive. The stone is secure contemporary inscriptional evidence naming him, not his sole physical trace. **Confidence: high.** [University of Calgary’s Pilate coin](https://nickle.ucalgary.ca/online-resource/nickle-new-pontius-pilate-coin/).

16. **Earlier-chapter continuity problem — portability appears to be invented twice.**  
    > “At Yavneh and then in Galilee, teachers of the law rebuilt Judaism around the things that could be carried.”

    Chapter 14 already concludes: “A religion of this kind can live without its hill if it has to,” and explains portable observance, study and diaspora communities. This is not a strict contradiction, but chapter 25 makes the same transformation appear new after 70. Explain that the rabbis developed and reorganised existing resources after the loss of sacrifice. **Confidence: high about the continuity problem.** [Chapter 14](chapters/14-one-god/chapter.md).

17. **Factual overstatement — prescription becomes universal practice.**  
    > “The religion's daily worship had consisted of sacrifice, and sacrifice could be offered in one place only.”

    Temple sacrifice was central, but Jewish worship also included prayer and scripture outside Jerusalem. Moreover, the Jewish temple at Leontopolis in Egypt operated until the Romans closed it after Jerusalem’s fall. Chapter 14 has already introduced an earlier Jewish temple outside Jerusalem at Elephantine. Distinguish Jerusalem’s normative claim from all actual Jewish practice. **Confidence: high.** [Josephus, *Jewish War* VII.10](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Josephus/war-7.html); [chapter 14](chapters/14-one-god/chapter.md).

18. **Reader confusion — the gospels appear to be written after 132.**  
    > “They were composed in these years, by people who had to live under Roman rule, and they tend to shift the blame for the death of Jesus away from the Roman governor and onto the Jewish crowd.”

    The immediate narrative has reached the revolt of 132–135. “These years” therefore conflicts with the earlier dating of the gospels to roughly 70–100. Explicitly return to the decades around and after the first revolt. **Confidence: high about the ambiguity.** The chapter’s own earlier dates suffice; compare [this scholarly chronology](https://www.bartehrman.com/when-does-the-new-testament-start/).

19. **Factual precision — two versions of the creed are blurred.**  
    > “The council ruled that Christ was of one substance with the Father, and the words it agreed are still recited in churches.”

    The central formulation survives, but the creed normally recited today is the expanded Nicene-Constantinopolitan form associated with 381, not simply the entire text agreed in 325. A small clarification would prevent confusion. **Confidence: high.** [World Council of Churches explanation](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/nicene-constantinopolitan-creed).

20. **Reader comprehension — baptism arrives without an explanation.**  
    > “When he was about thirty, he was baptised in the river Jordan by a preacher named John, who was calling on people to repent before God's judgment arrived.”

    A reader without religious background is not told what John physically did or what the water signified. Later, “the price of a bath in water” depends on the same unexplained practice. Give the concrete action and its meaning at first mention. This also meets style.md’s requirement to explain historical terms.

21. **Reader comprehension — the political setting jumps.**  
    > “The next year, he and the emperor of the east agreed that everyone in the empire, Christians included, should be free to worship as they chose, and that confiscated church property should be returned.”

    Chapter 24 leaves the reader with Augustus’s personal supremacy. Here there are western and eastern emperors, following another rival emperor in Rome. One sentence explaining shared imperial rule would make Constantine’s position intelligible.

22. **Reader comprehension — a major theological term remains unexplained.**  
    > “The council ruled that Christ was of one substance with the Father, and the words it agreed are still recited in churches.”

    “One substance” is the technical conclusion, not an explanation for a general reader. Briefly explain that the council rejected the idea that the Son was a created being and affirmed that he shared the Father’s divine nature. Report this explicitly as the council’s position.
