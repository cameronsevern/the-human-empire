## Factual flags

1. **Major — the new transition misplaces substantial power-loom adoption.**
   
   > “Only then did the machine arrive in earnest.”
   
   The immediately preceding sentence ends in **1830**, making “only then” read as after 1830. Power looms were already spreading substantially during the 1820s: Baines records approximately **14,150 in England and Scotland in 1820 and 55,500 in 1829**. The defensible sequence is that handloom earnings began deteriorating before widespread mechanisation, which then accelerated during the 1820s—not that mechanisation arrived only after the decline described through 1830. [Edward Baines, *History of the Cotton Manufacture in Great Britain*, contemporary figures reproduced in the book excerpt](https://books.google.com/books/about/History_of_the_Cotton_Manufacture_in_Gre.html?id=9YjoLwEACAAJ).

2. **Minor — the new wording makes the calico restrictions sound comprehensive.**
   
   > “Instead, with the Indian cloth shut out and the customers still wanting it, the ban gave British makers a reason to try to produce it themselves.”
   
   Indian cloth as a whole was not shut out. The restrictions distinguished between kinds of cloth and uses; **muslins were exempt**, and Indian white cottons continued to enter trade under changing restrictions. The correct claim concerns the exclusion of prohibited printed or painted cloth from the domestic market. This also sits awkwardly with the chapter’s later account of substantial Indian cloth shipments to Britain in the 1790s. [Pat Hudson, *The Limits of Wool and the Potential of Cotton*, discussion of the Calico Acts](https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/Helsinki/HELSINKIHudson.pdf).

3. **Minor — wrong county in the engine caption; unchanged by this revision.**
   
   > “From a 1744 book, after an engraving of 1717 of an engine at Griff, Worcestershire.”
   
   Griff was in **Warwickshire**, near Nuneaton, not Worcestershire. The Science Museum identifies Beighton’s engraving as depicting the engine at Griff, Warwickshire. [Science Museum Group collection record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co50905/print-of-henry-beightons-engraving-of-a-newcomen-engine-print).
