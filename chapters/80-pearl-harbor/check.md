1. **Factual error — high confidence.** “The Chinese leader, Chiang Kai-shek, threw his best divisions into the defence of Shanghai, and a quarter of a million Chinese soldiers died there, street by street, before the line broke.”  
   The commonly cited quarter-million figure is **casualties, including wounded**, not deaths. Estimates vary, so replacing it with another precise death toll would require stronger evidence. [Battle of Shanghai, casualty estimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shanghai).

2. **Doubtful interpretation presented as settled fact — high confidence that qualification is needed.** “The armed forces therefore answered in practice to nobody, and each new step was presented to the country as the emperor's will.”  
   Civilian control was weak, but this erases the emperor’s participation and the senior leadership’s responsibility. Hirohito received briefings, questioned proposals and sanctioned decisions; historians disagree about how much independent influence he exercised. The chapter itself has just shown him successfully demanding suppression of the 1936 mutiny. [Peter Wetzler, *Hirohito and War*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt6wqzs6).

3. **Missing causal link for a general reader.** “They chose the oil, and since taking it meant war with America, they began by trying to sink the American fleet.”  
   Why would attacking **Dutch** possessions necessarily mean war with **America**? Explain that American-held Philippines lay beside Japan’s intended supply routes, and Japanese planners chose to eliminate that threat while anticipating American intervention. This was a strategic judgment, not an automatic consequence of ownership. [U.S. Army history of the Philippine campaign](https://ibiblio.net/hyperwar/USA/USA-C-Philippines/index.html).

4. **Factual overstatement and exception-breaking absolute — high confidence.** “The only force that could interfere with the seizure of the southern oilfields was the American fleet at Pearl Harbor.”  
   British, Dutch and American forces already in Southeast Asia could also interfere, including the U.S. Asiatic Fleet and aircraft in the Philippines. The Pacific Fleet was a major potential reinforcement and counterattack force, not the only opposition. [U.S. Army campaign history](https://ibiblio.net/hyperwar/USA/USA-CPhilippines/index.html) [correct campaign link](https://ibiblio.net/hyperwar/USA/USA-C-Philippines/index.html).

5. **Wrong causal chronology — high confidence.** “Washington had already protested about China for years without doing anything.” / “It now began to cut off supplies, starting with scrap iron and the high-grade fuel used in aircraft.”  
   These sentences make sanctions follow the September 1940 alliance with Hitler. Aviation-fuel restrictions began in **July 1940**; the broader scrap restriction was announced on **26 September**, before the pact was signed on 27 September. Washington had also already taken other economic and diplomatic measures. “Without doing anything” compounds the error. [Contemporary State Department documents](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1931-41v02/papers).

6. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.** “In five years this was destroyed, and it was done from below, by young men with pistols.”  
   “This” encompasses parties, elections, the press and party-led cabinets. Party cabinets ended in 1932, but competitive parliamentary elections continued in 1936 and 1937; the established parties dissolved in 1940. Assassination mattered, but institutional change also came through senior officers and civilian leaders. [National Diet Library’s election and party-dissolution documents](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/utility/list.html).

7. **Factual error and style-breaking absolute — high confidence.** “The Japanese took every large city on the coast and every railway, and found that they controlled nothing beyond the range of their guns.”  
   “Every railway” is false. Capturing and connecting important railway corridors remained an objective of the **1944 Ichi-Go offensive**. The defensible point is that occupation concentrated on cities and transport corridors while substantial territory remained outside Japanese control. [Japanese Army operational history, prepared for the U.S. Army](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/NHC/NewPDFs/JAPAN/IJA%20Mono%2076%20Air%20Ops%20China%201937-1945.PDF).

8. **Misleading implication — high confidence.** “The government intended to observe the forms, narrowly.”  
   The following account makes the embassy’s delay seem responsible for the absence of proper warning. But the fourteen-part message broke off negotiations; **even timely delivery would not have constituted a declaration of war**. The text should distinguish that substantive problem from the typing delay. [National Park Service timeline](https://www.nps.gov/perl/learn/historyculture/timeline.htm).

9. **Contradiction with chapter 75 — high confidence.** “Manchuria was turned into a state that was independent on paper, with the last emperor of China brought out of retirement to sit on its throne.”  
   Placed before the February 1933 League vote, this implies immediate enthronement. Puyi became chief executive in **1932** and emperor in **1934**, as [chapter 75](chapters/75-puyi/chapter.md) already explains. Separate the establishment of the puppet state from the later restoration of his imperial title. [Puyi chronology](https://www.cmcarchive.org/figures/puyi).

10. **Contradiction with earlier chapters and this chapter’s own setup.** “Japan would have to get its own.”  
    The referent is an empire, but Japan already possessed Taiwan and Korea. [Chapter 66](chapters/66-meiji/chapter.md) describes their acquisition, and [chapter 75](chapters/75-puyi/chapter.md) describes further expansion into China. The intended argument is that officers wanted a **larger, more economically self-sufficient empire**.

11. **Factual error — high confidence.** “The fleet's eight battleships were moored in a row beside an island in the middle of the harbour, and its sailors were at breakfast or getting ready for church.”  
    Seven battleships were beside Ford Island; **Pennsylvania was in dry dock across the channel**. [Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/p/the-pearl-harbor-attack-7-december-1941.html).

12. **Chronology reversed — high confidence.** “Two days later President Roosevelt froze every Japanese bank account and asset in the United States.”  
    Following the sentence about troops entering southern Indochina, “later” reverses the sequence. The freeze was **26 July 1941**; the troop advance began **28 July**, following advance diplomatic notification. [State Department chronology](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1941v05/ch3?start=31), [Japanese archival chronology](https://www.jacar.go.jp/english/exhibition/shuhou-english/timeline/nenpyo19410728.html).

13. **Factual overstatement — high confidence.** “The Japanese islands held about seventy million people and almost nothing that an industrial economy runs on.”  
    Japan lacked sufficient oil and several other strategic materials, but had a substantial domestic **coal industry**, central to its industrialization. The general resource claim obscures the particular dependence that matters here: imported oil. [U.S. Strategic Bombing Survey, *Coals and Metals in Japan’s War Economy*](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/NHC/NewPDFs/USAAF/United%20States%20Strategic%20Bombing%20Survey/USSBS%20Coals%20and%20Metals%20in%20Japan%27s%20War%20Economy.pdf).

14. **Factual imprecision — high confidence.** “A treaty on warships allowed Japan three large ships for every five built by Britain or America.”  
    The Washington treaty’s ratio governed **aggregate capital-ship tonnage**, not a rolling entitlement to build three ships whenever another country built five. It also imposed a construction holiday and replacement rules. [Treaty text](https://wwws.loc.gov/law/help/us-treaties/bevans/m-ust000002-0351.pdf).

15. **Factual error — high confidence.** “In May 1932 a group of young naval officers walked into the residence of his successor, an old man of seventy-six.”  
    Inukai was not Hamaguchi’s immediate successor: **Wakatsuki served between them**. “A later prime minister” would preserve the narrative without introducing another name. [Cabinet chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_cabinets).

16. **Missing transition from chapter 79.** “In the spring of 1940 Germany overran the Netherlands and France in six weeks, and Britain was left fighting for its life.”  
    [Chapter 79](chapters/79-hitler/chapter.md) has not narrated the outbreak of the European war. A reader needs a brief bridge explaining that Germany invaded Poland in 1939 and Britain and France entered the war. Otherwise the European catastrophe arrives without its initiating event.

17. **Style: too many numbers for one point.** “In two hours, in two waves, about three hundred and fifty aircraft sank four of the battleships and damaged the other four.”  
    Five numerical details arrive in one sentence, followed by several more casualty and aircraft-loss figures in the same paragraph. This directly conflicts with “one number per point.” Keep the figures that establish the scale and human cost; reduce the operational tally.
