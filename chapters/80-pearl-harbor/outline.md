# Chapter 80 — Why did Japan attack Pearl Harbor?

**Line under the title:** Japan, China and the Pacific, from 1929 to 1941.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Japan attacked because it was running out of oil. It had spent ten years building an empire on the mainland of Asia, driven by army officers whom no government in Tokyo could control, and by 1941 it was stuck in a war in China that it could neither win nor leave. When it moved towards the oilfields of South-East Asia, the United States, which sold it most of its oil, cut off the supply. Japan then had about two years of fuel. It could give up China or take the oil of the Dutch East Indies by force, and taking it meant war with America. The attack on Pearl Harbor was meant to buy six months by sinking the only fleet that could interfere. The men who ordered it knew the odds, and the admiral who planned it had said that he could promise nothing after the first year.

## The story

1. **Opening: a Sunday morning.** Answer first. Two soldiers at a radar set, 7 December 1941. The attack in brief.
2. **Silk.** Japan in the 1920s: a great power with no resources, slights from the West, and a countryside ruined when the price of silk collapsed.
3. **A length of railway track.** Manchuria, September 1931: an army acts without orders, and the country cheers. Japan leaves the League.
4. **Government by assassination.** Prime ministers shot, the mutiny of February 1936, and the rule that let the army bring down any cabinet. The emperor's place.
5. **The bridge.** July 1937 and the war in China. Shanghai, Nanjing, and a German businessman. A war with no end.
6. **Going south.** A defeat on the Mongolian border; the fall of France and the Netherlands; the alliance with Germany; Indochina.
7. **Four hundred tons an hour.** The oil embargo and the clock it started. The American terms. An emperor reads a poem. A general on jumping with one's eyes closed.
8. **The gambler.** The admiral who had seen Detroit, his warning, and his plan. Shallow-water torpedoes. The fleet's voyage. A message delivered late.
9. **What was not hit.** Six months of victories from Hong Kong to Singapore. The carriers, the oil tanks and the repair yards. America united in an afternoon, and Hitler's declaration.
10. **Ending: oil on the water.**
