1. **Misleading chronology — high confidence.** “At the end of November they attacked.” Chinese forces had already attacked on **25 October**, fought American troops in early November, and then withdrawn before their larger November offensive. Omitting that first offensive makes the disaster look like an entirely unexpected intervention, rather than an advance resumed despite direct evidence of Chinese involvement. Add that missing sequence. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/research/online-documents/korean-war)

2. **Unsupported precision in the central explanation — medium confidence.** “The line was a convenience for accepting the surrender of the Japanese army, and it was expected to last a few months.” The temporary surrender boundary is established; the specific expectation of “a few months” needs a source identifying whose expectation this was. American planning envisaged a trusteeship lasting years, although that does not establish how long the separate occupation zones were expected to remain. “A temporary boundary for accepting the Japanese surrender” is supportable without the invented-looking deadline. [Contemporary American trusteeship proposal](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945v07/d614)

3. **Factual error and contradiction with chapter 89 — high confidence.** “It was the first war of the nuclear age, and both sides had decided that they would rather not win it than risk the larger one.” Chapter 89 has just described the Chinese Civil War continuing after 1945, including its decisive battles in 1948. Korea cannot be the first war of the nuclear age. The second clause also blurs Washington and Moscow’s restraint with the wishes of the Korean governments: this chapter subsequently says Rhee wanted to continue fighting. Specify whose decision to limit the war is meant.

4. **Misleading conflation of private proposals and public statements — medium-high confidence.** “He said these things in public, and in a letter to a congressman that was read out in the House, he declared that there was no substitute for victory.” Because this immediately follows “He asked for atomic bombs,” it implies a public demand for atomic bombing before his dismissal. His December nuclear targeting proposals went to his superiors; the March letter endorsed using Chiang’s forces and criticised the limited-war policy, without demanding atomic bombs. Separate the confidential nuclear proposals from his public challenge to policy. [Nuclear proposals](https://www.ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/5742/1/58.pdf), [text of the Martin–MacArthur correspondence](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951v07p1/d206)

5. **Overgeneralised statistic — high confidence that qualification is needed.** “A southerner is now, on average, about eight centimetres taller than a northerner of the same age, and more than twenty times richer.” Height differences vary substantially by sex, generation and sample. Research comparing young adults has reported roughly **six to seven centimetres**, while studies of children and adolescents find different gaps. Those findings do not establish a current, population-wide eight-centimetre difference between age-matched Koreans. Specify the population and study date, or retain the simpler point that people raised in the South generally grow taller. “Richer” should also identify income per person rather than imply measured personal wealth. [Research on growth differences](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1570677X09000021), [review of the underlying studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5769832/)

6. **False absolute — high confidence.** “Every expert told him that it could not be done.” Admiral Doyle, a central amphibious specialist, expressly distinguished **not recommending** Inchon from considering it impossible. There was substantial opposition, but this sentence turns reservations into unanimous declarations of impossibility. It breaks the rule against absolutes that one exception defeats. [U.S. Army official history, “The Landing at Inch’on”](https://webdoc.sub.gwdg.de/ebook/p/2005/CMH_2/www.army.mil/cmh-pg/books/korea/20-2-1/sn25.htm)

7. **Misleading absolute — high confidence.** “The American defence budget was multiplied nearly fourfold in three years and never fell back.” Defence spending did fall after the Korean War; Eisenhower explicitly reduced it. The defensible point is that spending remained substantially above its prewar level, not that it never declined from its wartime peak. Say which baseline “fell back” refers to. [Eisenhower’s fiscal 1955 budget message](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/annual-budget-message-the-congress-fiscal-year-1955)

8. **Chronological and causal overstatement — high confidence.** “It paid with twenty years of American hostility and its exclusion from the United Nations.” The chapter itself has already explained that the Communist government was excluded from China’s UN seat **before** the invasion, prompting the Soviet boycott. The war entrenched American opposition to seating Beijing; it did not initiate that exclusion. Also distinguish the People’s Republic’s exclusion from **China’s**: China’s seat remained occupied by Chiang’s government. [U.S. State Department account of the dispute and eventual change in representation](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1969-1976/china)

9. **Death toll attached to too narrow a period — medium-high confidence.** “They burned most of the island’s inland villages and killed some thirty thousand people, a tenth of the population.” Following the previous sentence, this assigns the whole Jeju death toll to suppression over the year after April 1948. The commonly cited total concerns the broader **1947–1954** episode, although the worst killing occurred in 1948–1949. Keep the approximate total, but attach it to the full period rather than that single year. [Jeju’s official historical account](https://m.visitjeju.net/en/themtour/view?contentsid=CNTS_000000000021602&menuId=DOM_000004000000000221)

10. **False absolute — high confidence.** “The strip between them runs for two hundred and fifty kilometres from sea to sea, fenced and mined on both sides, and has been left to itself for seventy years.” Large areas have become wildlife refuges, but the DMZ has not been left entirely alone: it contains settlements, cultivated land and military activity. South Korea’s Daeseong-dong village lies inside it and has working farms. “Much of it” would preserve the image without erasing the exceptions. [National Atlas of Korea](https://nationalatlas.ngii.go.kr/pages/page_3860.php)

11. **Overbroad account of family separation — high confidence.** “No letters crossed it, and no telephone calls.” As an unqualified account of the decades since the war, this is false: an organised exchange in March 2001 carried letters between **600 families**. Routine private communication was prohibited; exceptional exchanges occurred. The following sentence about reunions “since 2000” is literally compatible with earlier meetings, but would also benefit from acknowledging the first reunion in **1985**. [Congressional Research Service chronology](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20020109_RL30811_c21ab7db71693a8ec1b7a84c32a737d1e5d8e171.html), [1985 reunion account](https://world.kbs.co.kr/english/event/nkorea_nuclear/general_02c.htm)

12. **Continuity gap with chapter 89, rather than a direct contradiction.** “In June 1950 Truman had sent a fleet into the strait between China and Taiwan, and Mao had to call off the invasion that he had been preparing.” Chapter 89 leaves the reader with “The Communists had no navy with which to follow him.” The new sentence needs a short bridge explaining that Mao was preparing an invasion despite the crossing problem. Otherwise an invasion previously presented as beyond his means suddenly appears ready enough for the American fleet to cancel.

13. **Reader loses the referent and the argument.** “Two governments were saved by it.” Taiwan’s government is identifiable and its danger is explained. The next sentence turns to Japan’s industrial recovery, without explaining what threatened its government or how war orders “saved” it. Name the two governments and establish the second claim, or describe the two different benefits directly.

14. **Missing explanation at a major turning point.** “Tens of thousands of the Chinese and northern prisoners held by the United Nations did not want to be sent home, and the communist side insisted on having them all back.” A general reader can understand the disagreement but not yet why it prolonged the war for two years. Explain why some prisoners feared returning and why the UN side refused compulsory repatriation. This is where *style.md*’s instruction to walk through the reasoning matters most.

15. **Unsupported absolute and style breach.** “The memory of those years is the foundation of everything that the northern state has told its people since.” “Everything” unnecessarily includes the regime’s claims about ancestry, economic achievement and the ruling family’s legitimacy. The bombing’s central place in the state’s account of American hostility is the strong, specific claim here; universal coverage is not needed.
