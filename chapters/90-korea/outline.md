# Chapter 90 — Why is Korea divided?

**Line under the title:** Korea, from 1945 to 1953.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Korea was divided in half an hour in August 1945, by two American officers with a magazine map, as a convenience for taking the surrender of the Japanese army. It was meant to last a few months. Each occupying power then built a government in its own image, and each of those governments claimed the whole country. In 1950 the North invaded to reunite it. The United States and then China came in, the armies went up and down the peninsula four times in a year, and after three years and about three million dead the line was almost where it had begun. No peace was ever signed. The war fixed the shape of the Cold War in Asia, and the two halves of one people have since become as different as any two countries on Earth.

## The story

1. **Opening: thirty minutes with a map.** Answer first. Washington, the night of 10 August 1945.
2. **Thirty-five years.** Korea under Japan, and what Koreans expected when it ended.
3. **Two governments.** The committees; the American general and the old police; the Soviet zone and a young guerrilla; an old exile from America. An island rising. Two states in 1948.
4. **Forty-eight telegrams.** Kim asks permission; why Stalin changed his mind.
5. **Sunday morning.** 25 June 1950. A bridge in Seoul. An empty chair at the United Nations.
6. **Up and down.** The corner at Pusan; the landing at Inchon; the decision to go north; the river; the Chinese in the snow.
7. **The general.** MacArthur, the bomb, and who commands in a republic.
8. **Caves.** Two years of talks and trenches. The bombing of the North. What was done to civilians by every side.
9. **A ceasefire.** July 1953: what was signed, by whom, and the strip between.
10. **What it settled.** Rearmament, Japan, Taiwan, China's standing. Families.
11. **Ending: a photograph at night.**
