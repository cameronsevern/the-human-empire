# Chapter 44 — Who built the Taj Mahal?

**Line under the title:** Turkey, Iran and India, from 1501 to 1707.
**Length:** 4,300 to 5,000 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

The Taj Mahal was built by a Muslim emperor with the taxes of a hundred million subjects, most of whom were Hindu, on land bought from a Hindu prince who was one of his generals. In the 1500s and 1600s three great Muslim empires, the Ottoman, the Safavid and the Mughal, covered the lands from Hungary to Bengal. All three had been won with cannon and muskets, which only a large treasury could afford, and all three ruled millions of people whose faith or sect was not the ruler's. Each found a different answer. The Ottomans left Christians and Jews to run their own affairs under their own leaders, for a tax, and took Christian boys to be their soldiers and ministers. The Safavids forced a Sunni country to become Shia. The Mughal emperor Akbar made partners of the Hindu princes and dropped the tax on unbelievers, and India under his family was among the richest countries on earth. His great-grandson reversed the policy, and the empire began to come apart.

## The story

1. **Opening: a death in childbirth.** Answer first. The tomb at Agra: who paid, who worked, where the stone and the land came from.
2. **The Tiger's guns.** A prince from Central Asia wins India in an afternoon with cannon. What guns cost, and the three empires they made.
3. **The boy from Bosnia.** The Ottomans under Suleiman: the levy of boys, a shepherd's son who became chief minister, and the communities that governed themselves.
4. **The shah who was fourteen.** Iran is made Shia by force. The battle that fixed a border. Isfahan and the Armenians marched there.
5. **Peace with all.** Akbar: the marriage, the tax he cancelled, the house of debates. The wandering teachers who had carried Islam ahead of him.
6. **A third of the harvest.** The land tax, from a measured field to the treasury. How rich India was.
7. **The emperor who copied the Koran.** Aurangzeb: the tax restored, the Sikhs take up the sword, the wars in the south.
8. **Ending: the one thing out of place.**
