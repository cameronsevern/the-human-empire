# Chapter 17 fact-check lookups

Verdict key: correct / wrong / imprecise / could not verify. "Fixed" means the chapter text was changed. Claims that were cut during tightening are listed at the end.

## Opening: Guodian and the bamboo book

1. "In 1993, robbers twice dug into an old tomb beside the village of Guodian... about eight hundred thin strips of bamboo."
   - Verdict: correct after fix (draft said "autumn" and one robbery). Tomb no. 1, Guodian, Jingmen, Hubei; opened by robbers in August and again in October 1993; excavated by Jingmen City Museum, October 1993; about 804 strips.
   - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guodian_Chu_Slips ; Bumbacher, Asiatische Studien 52 (1998), https://edoc-vmtest.ub.unibas.ch/8827/1/ast-002_1998_52__1316_d-1.pdf
2. "buried with their owner about 2,300 years ago"
   - Verdict: correct. Tomb dated to about 300 BCE.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guodian_Chu_Slips
3. "Each strip was narrower than a pencil and no longer than a forearm."
   - Verdict: correct for width (bamboo strips about 0.6 cm). Lengths (15 to 32.4 cm) are from the 1998 excavation report as I recall it; the web sources fetched did not state them. Imprecise but safe.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_and_wooden_slips
4. Brush and ink, one column per strip, mistakes shaved off with a knife, bound with cords, read right to left.
   - Verdict: correct. "a single column of several dozen brush-written characters"; knife used to scrape away mistakes; bound with hemp, silk or leather.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_and_wooden_slips
5. "dried over a fire, so that they would not rot or be eaten by insects"
   - Verdict: correct by the standard account (Tsien, Written on Bamboo and Silk: "killing the green"). Not confirmed on a web page in this pass.
6. "The Chinese character for a volume of a book, 冊, is still a drawing of strips with a cord through them."
   - Verdict: correct; standard etymology (Tsien; any character dictionary). Not fetched in this pass.
7. "One strip held two or three dozen characters at most"
   - Verdict: correct after fix (draft said "perhaps thirty"). About 12,000 to 13,000 characters on some 730 inscribed strips, an average under twenty.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_Texts_of_Guodian
8. "One scholar of the time was said to own enough writing to fill five carts."
   - Verdict: correct. Zhuangzi ch. 33 on Hui Shi (Watson: "his writings would fill five carriages"). Changed "was famous for owning" to "was said to own".
9. "more than a dozen short works. Three bundles held sayings... the oldest copy yet found of parts of a book"
   - Verdict: correct after fix (Codex 9: the draft called it one copy of the book). 18 sections by the editors' count; Laozi A, B, C are three bundles matching parts of the received text. Published 1998.
   - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guodian_Chu_Slips ; https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/laozi/
10. "the three sets of thinkers had never heard of each other"
   - Verdict: correct after fix (Codex 3: Pyrrho is reported to have met Indian thinkers with Alexander, after the traditions had formed). Narrowed to the founders.
   - Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pyrrho/

## China

11. Zhou conquest about 3,000 years ago; more than a hundred states; seven large ones by about 2,400 years ago; Warring States.
   - Verdict: correct. Conquest c. 1046 BCE; about 150 states in the early Spring and Autumn period; seven after the partition of Jin (453/403 BCE); period ends 221 BCE.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period
12. "In time, the king was left with his title and very little else."
   - Verdict: correct. Draft said the king "was driven out of his capital"; in 771 BCE the king was killed and the court moved east. Rewritten to avoid the detail.
13. Confucius: born about 2,550 years ago in Lu; father died when he was small; poor; left in his fifties; about fourteen years wandering; home in his late sixties.
   - Verdict: correct. c. 551 to 479 BCE; father died when he was three; left Lu 497 BCE, returned 484 BCE aged 68.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius
14. "European missionaries two thousand years later turned that into the Latin name Confucius."
   - Verdict: correct. Latinised from Kong Fuzi by Jesuits in the late 16th century.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius
15. Rule by punishment and rule by conduct (Analects 2.3), paraphrased.
   - Verdict: correct paraphrase (Lau translation).
16. "Do not impose on others what you yourself do not desire."
   - Verdict: correct. D. C. Lau, Analects 15.24, in answer to Zigong's request for a single word.
   - Source: https://wist.info/confucius/12270/
17. "The Chinese word for a gentleman meant, literally, the son of a lord."
   - Verdict: correct. Junzi, "son of a lord"; Confucius turned it into a moral term.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junzi
18. The duke who asked about battle formations; Confucius left next day.
   - Verdict: correct. Analects 15.1 (Duke Ling of Wei), Lau translation. Checked from memory of the text, not fetched.
19. Mozi: test of benefit; killing one man and killing ten thousand.
   - Verdict: correct. Mozi, "Against Music" and ch. 17 "Against Offensive War".
   - Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mohism/
20. Mozi walks ten days and ten nights to Chu; belt as wall; nine attacks; three hundred students on the wall; king calls off the attack.
   - Verdict: correct as a story in the Mozi (ch. 50, Gongshu). 行十日十夜而至於郢; 解帶為城; 九距之; 禽滑釐等三百人.
   - Source: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/墨子/公輸
21. Shang Yang: went to Qin a little under 2,400 years ago on the ruler's call for able men; chief minister.
   - Verdict: correct. Arrived c. 361 BCE; reforms 356 and 350 BCE.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_Yang
22. Groups of five and ten households; one step of rank per enemy head; heir's tutors punished.
   - Verdict: correct (Shiji 68; Book of Lord Shang ch. 19). Secondary sources differ on which tutor suffered which punishment, so the chapter does not say.
   - Source: https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/History/World_History/An_Outline_History_of_East_Asia_to_1200_(Schneewind)/03%3A_From_Warring_States_Two_Empire_(480_BC_-207_BC)/3.09%3A_Surveillance_and_Law
23. "Shang Yang was hunted down and killed, and his body was torn apart by chariots. The new ruler kept his laws."
   - Verdict: correct by the Shiji; Wikipedia notes the details of the death are partly legendary. The tradition is unanimous on the dismemberment.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_Yang
24. Zhuangzi and the tortoise; "Go away! I'll drag my tail in the mud!"
   - Verdict: correct. Zhuangzi ch. 17, Burton Watson's translation.
   - Sources: https://chinaheritage.net/journal/ill-drag-my-tail-in-the-mud-zhuangzi/ ; https://terebess.hu/english/tao/Zhuangzi-Burton-Watson.pdf
25. Laozi sayings: more laws, more thieves; the best ruler barely known.
   - Verdict: correct. Received chapters 57 and 17, both among the Guodian material (bundles A and C).
   - Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/laozi/
26. "seventy-six of them were given fine houses and high rank, with no duties except to discuss"
   - Verdict: correct. Shiji 46: 七十六人，皆賜列第，爲上大夫，不治而議論.
   - Source: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷046

## The Ganges plain

27. Sixteen states, some kingdoms and some ruled by councils; the Shakya among the latter; birthplace in Nepal.
   - Verdict: correct. Sixteen mahajanapadas in Buddhist lists; Shakya a gana-sangha; Lumbini.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapadas
28. "Siddhartha Gautama, who lived about 2,450 years ago"
   - Verdict: correct as a round figure. Scholars now put his death within a few decades of 400 BCE; older dating c. 480 BCE.
   - Source: https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/culture/religious-studies/the-dates-the-buddha
29. Left at twenty-nine; six years; awakening under a fig tree; forty-five years of teaching; died at eighty.
   - Verdict: correct as the tradition, and the chapter marks it as such.
30. The barber admitted ahead of the nobles.
   - Verdict: correct. Pali Vinaya, Cullavagga 7: the Sakyan princes ask that Upali be ordained first.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up%C4%81li
31. "Among the monks, precedence went by how long a man had been a member"; nuns "in an order of their own that was placed under the monks'"
   - Verdict: correct after fix (Codex 2: draft said rank depended on seniority "and on nothing else", which the rules for nuns contradict).
   - Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/book/anguttara-nikaya-numbered-discourses/d/doc1964794.html
32. Recited after his death; written down some four centuries later.
   - Verdict: correct by tradition. First council; Pali canon written in Sri Lanka in the 1st century BCE.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali_Canon

## Greece

33. Alphabet about 2,800 years ago, borrowed from the Lebanon coast, vowels added, fewer than thirty letters.
   - Verdict: correct. Earliest Greek inscriptions c. 770 to 750 BCE. Draft's "never the property of palace clerks" contradicted chapter 11 (Codex 5); fixed to "This time".
34. "about a thousand cities"
   - Verdict: correct after fix (Codex 4). Hansen and Nielsen list 1,035 poleis for about 650 to 325 BCE; draft tied the figure to the eighth century and called them all independent.
   - Source: https://academic.oup.com/book/47066
35. Thales: water; earth floats like a log; earthquakes from the water moving.
   - Verdict: correct. Aristotle, On the Heavens 294a; Seneca, Natural Questions 3.14 and 6.6.
   - Source: https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/heavens.2.ii.html
36. The younger Milesian: the earth rests on nothing and has no reason to move.
   - Verdict: correct after fix (Codex 1). Anaximander's view per Aristotle, On the Heavens 295b. The draft staged a public exchange and put Aristotle's objection in his mouth; both removed.
37. Democritus: atoms, "uncuttable"; "in reality" "atoms and void".
   - Verdict: correct. Fragment B9/B125. The halving argument was restated after Codex 10.
   - Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/democritus/
38. Socrates: charges, jury of five hundred, narrow margin, free dinners then a fine, death.
   - Verdict: correct. Plato, Apology 36 to 38; jury 500 or 501, about 280 to 220. The fine was added after Codex 6.
   - Source: https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/apology.html
39. "The unexamined life is not worth living."
   - Verdict: correct. Apology 38a (Fowler, Loeb).
40. Refused to escape; seventy years under the laws.
   - Verdict: correct. Plato, Crito 52e.
41. Plato wrote for fifty years. (The school sentence was later cut for length.)
   - Verdict: correct. Socrates died 399 BCE, Plato 347 BCE; the Academy ran until 86 BCE.

## The Axial Age

42. "In 1949, a German philosopher... from 2,800 to 2,200 years ago the Axial Age"; included the prophets.
   - Verdict: correct. Karl Jaspers, Vom Ursprung und Ziel der Geschichte (1949); 800 to 200 BCE; China, India, Iran, Palestine, Greece.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_Age
43. The 2018 comparison of ten regions.
   - Verdict: correct after fix (draft said "societies across the world"). Mullins et al., American Sociological Review 83(3): traits appeared across Afro-Eurasia hundreds, in some cases thousands, of years before the proposed age.
   - Sources: https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/36086 ; https://peterturchin.com/publications/a-systematic-assessment-of-axial-age-proposals-using-global-comparative-historical-evidence/
44. "Within about a century of the burial at Guodian, one of the warring states of China had conquered all the others, and most of the cities of Greece and the kingdoms of the Ganges had passed under empires too."
   - Verdict: correct after fix (Codex 8). Qin unification 221 BCE; Macedon 338 BCE; Mauryas c. 320 BCE. "Most" because some Greek cities, Rhodes for one, stayed independent.

## Cut from the chapter

- Anaxagoras prosecuted at Athens and honoured at Lampsacus: the accounts conflict (Codex 11; https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anaxagoras/). Cut.
- The Mohist listening jars: verified (墨子/備穴, https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/墨子/備穴) but cut for length.
- The Book of Lord Shang's "lice": could not verify the wording on the web. Cut.
- Mencius's train of carts (Mencius 3B.4), Xenophanes on the gods of oxen, Plato's frogs round a pond, the bundle of dried meat (Analects 7.7): true, cut for length.
