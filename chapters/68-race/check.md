I found no clear direct contradictions with chapters 52, 56 or 64, and no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-mentions or body cross-references. The findings below are ranked by seriousness. No files were edited.

1. **Wrong chronology undermines the Virginia explanation — high confidence.**  
   > “The planters drew the lesson.”  
   > “A child would be slave or free according to the condition of the mother, so that slavery passed down by birth.”  
   > “Baptism would no longer make a slave free.”

   Placed after the 1676 rebellion, these sentences make the laws consequences of it. The maternal-status law dates to **1662**, and the baptism law to **1667**. The latter resolved uncertainty; it does not establish that baptism previously freed slaves automatically. Racial slavery was being legally entrenched before the rebellion. Sources: the original [1662 statute](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/negro-womens-children-to-serve-according-to-the-condition-of-the-mother-1662/) and [1667 statute](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/an-act-declaring-that-baptisme-of-slaves-doth-not-exempt-them-from-bondage-1667/).

   > “Over the next generation they stopped importing English servants and bought Africans, who served for life.”

   “Stopped” is also wrong: indentured immigration declined rather than ended. The shift towards enslaved labour had already begun before 1676, and cannot securely be reduced to a lesson deliberately drawn from the rebellion. [Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/indentured-servants-in-colonial-virginia/).

2. **The account of prejudice before Spain makes an untenable absolute — high confidence.**  
   > “The idea that a person's worth was fixed for ever by ancestry, whatever he believed or did, was missing.”  
   > “It first appeared in Spain.”

   Hereditary rank and theories of natural human inequality long predated fifteenth-century Spain. Aristotle explicitly connected barbarians with natural slavery; the chapter itself subsequently invokes that theory to explain Sepúlveda. This does not make ancient prejudice identical to modern racial classification, but it defeats the claim that inherited or supposedly natural inferiority was previously absent. Toledo is a strong example of ancestry overriding conversion, not a defensible first appearance of the broader idea. This also breaks the rule against exception-vulnerable absolutes. [Aristotle, *Politics*, Book I](https://web.mit.edu/classics/Aristotle/politics.1.one.html).

3. **The scientific rebuttal of skull measurement contains a false claim — high confidence.**  
   > “His method could not show what he claimed, since a larger skull mostly indicates a larger body, and brain size within the normal range tells nothing about ability.”

   “Tells nothing” is incorrect. Modern studies find a modest positive association between brain volume and measured intelligence; a large meta-analysis estimated a correlation around **0.24**. That does not establish Morton’s racial hierarchy or make skull capacity a reliable measure of an individual’s abilities. The rebuttal should explain what his comparisons cannot establish, without denying an observed association. [Pietschnig and colleagues, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9096623/).

4. **The Darwin passage creates a misleading separation between Darwin and racial hierarchy — high confidence.**  
   > “His theory was nevertheless put to uses that he had not intended.”

   In context, readers are told that applying evolutionary struggle to human groups was something other people did to Darwin’s theory. Darwin himself discussed competition and extermination between human populations and described peoples in hierarchical terms. His opposition to slavery and defence of common ancestry were real, but did not amount to rejecting racial hierarchy. Distinguish later political programmes from ideas already present in his writings. Sources: [*The Descent of Man*](https://darwin-online.org.uk/converted/published/1871_Descent_F937/1871_Descent_F937.1.html) and the [Darwin Correspondence Project](https://www.darwinproject.ac.uk/learning/universities/darwin-and-human-nature/race-civilization-and-progress).

5. **Jefferson’s supposed resolution of the equality problem is misleading — high confidence.**  
   > “If that were true, the contradiction disappeared.”

   A claim about unequal abilities does not logically remove a contradiction about equal rights. More specifically, Jefferson explicitly distinguished the two: in 1809 he wrote that alleged differences in talent were no measure of rights. His racism helped sustain exclusion, but this sentence incorrectly presents it as a clean resolution of his professed principles. Chapter 56’s treatment of an unresolved contradiction is more accurate. [Jefferson to Henri Grégoire, 25 February 1809](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mtj1.043_0836_0836/?st=text).

6. **Valladolid is framed as the wrong question — high confidence about the misleading framing.**  
   > “Were the people of the new lands fully human?”  
   > “In 1550 the king of Spain suspended all further conquests and summoned a panel of theologians to hear the matter argued.”

   This invites readers to imagine a tribunal deciding whether Indigenous Americans belonged to humanity. The central dispute concerned the justice of conquest, compulsory subjection and the means of evangelisation. Sepúlveda’s arguments degraded Indigenous rational capacities, but the issue was more specific than human versus nonhuman. The papal declaration *Sublimis Deus* had already affirmed their humanity and capacity for Christianity in 1537. [Study of the debate’s framing](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=5781908); [historical research on its legal and missionary issues](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10609164.2026.2629185).

7. **The 1924 immigration law restricted these Europeans rather than excluding them — high confidence.**  
   > “Three years before that, Congress had rewritten the immigration law to shut out the peoples of southern and eastern Europe, whom the experts classed as inferior stock.”

   The law imposed sharply discriminatory national-origin quotas; it did not prohibit immigration from southern and eastern Europe. “Severely restrict” would state its effect accurately. The distinction matters because outright exclusion and restrictive quotas were different parts of American immigration policy. [US Holocaust Memorial Museum’s account of the quotas](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/united-states-immigration-and-refugee-law-1921-1980).

8. **The Herzl conversion scene presents a disputed causal story as settled — medium-high confidence.**  
   > “If it could happen there, the journalist concluded, Jews would not be safe anywhere as a minority.”

   The surrounding paragraph makes the Dreyfus degradation the decisive event producing Herzl’s Zionism. That familiar account is disputed: his concern with antisemitism preceded the affair, and his contemporary correspondence and diary do not document this sudden conclusion. His presence at the degradation is not the problem; the confidently reconstructed thought and immediate causal sequence are. [Ritchie Robertson, “Theodor Herzl et l’antisémitisme”](https://www.persee.fr/doc/austr_0396-4590_2003_num_57_1_4256).

9. **Boas’s result is presented as a stronger causal demonstration than the evidence warrants — medium-high confidence.**  
   > “The supposedly fixed mark of race changed in one generation with food and surroundings.”

   Boas interpreted differences between immigrants and their children as evidence of environmental plasticity. His observational study did not isolate food as the cause, and later reanalyses disagree about the size and interpretation of the environmental effect. Present this as Boas’s finding and interpretation, rather than an uncontested experiment proving the mechanism. Sources: [Sparks and Jantz’s reassessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC137471/) and [Gravlee and colleagues’ supporting reanalysis](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1525/aa.2003.105.1.125/full).

10. **The single ancestral population is too definite — medium-high confidence.**  
    > “All living people descend from a small population that lived in Africa within the last few hundred thousand years, which is a short time as species go.”

    African origins are well supported; a single small founding population is not an established summary of current evidence. Research supports models involving several connected ancestral populations within Africa, exchanging genes over long periods. Retain the shared African ancestry without presenting one particular demographic model as settled. [Ragsdale and colleagues, *A weakly structured stem for human origins in Africa*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10208968/).

11. **Skin pigmentation is not adequately described as controlled by a handful of genes — high confidence.**  
    > “It has darkened and lightened several times in different branches of the human family, and it is controlled by a handful of genes.”

    Some large pigmentation differences involve a relatively small number of influential variants, but pigmentation is a complex, polygenic trait. Research in African populations specifically challenges the simple few-genes account. The repeated evolution point can stand independently. [Martin and colleagues, *An Unexpectedly Complex Architecture for Skin Pigmentation in Africans*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29195075/).

12. **The genetic-similarity statistic needs its measurement specified — high confidence about the qualification.**  
    > “Any two humans, chosen from anywhere on Earth, are alike in about nine hundred and ninety-nine of every thousand letters of their genetic code.”

    Approximately 99.9% is the familiar estimate based on single-letter substitutions. It excludes other kinds of variation, including insertions, deletions and structural changes. NHGRI now explains that counting these gives an average similarity nearer **99.6%**. Specify what the figure measures, or state the broad similarity without a deceptively universal number. [NHGRI, *Human Genomic Variation*](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/educational-resources/fact-sheets/human-genomic-variation).

13. **The Roman emperor’s supposedly unremarked origin is unsupported — high confidence that the absolute should go.**  
    > “One Roman emperor was born in North Africa, and his subjects did not think his origin worth remarking.”

    Septimius Severus’s African origin and accent were remarked upon in ancient writing. The *Historia Augusta* is a later and problematic source, so it cannot establish what all his contemporary subjects thought—but neither can the chapter claim their collective indifference. His African birth did not prevent him becoming emperor; that is the secure point. [Ancient biography and source notes](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Historia_Augusta/Septimius_Severus%2A.html).

14. **“Volume for volume” gives the wrong bibliographic impression — high confidence.**  
    > “A Haitian lawyer answered the French count, volume for volume, in a book called *The Equality of the Human Races*.”

    Firmin’s 1885 work was a single substantial volume, not a matching four-volume reply to Gobineau. If “volume for volume” means equally substantial intellectual opposition, the phrasing obscures that meaning. [Digitised text and publication details](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/firmin_antenor/de_egalite_races_humaines/de_egalite_races_humaines.html).

15. **The opening compresses Ota Benga’s death into a misleading timeline — high confidence.**  
    > “He wanted to go home, and when the war of 1914 made that impossible, he shot himself.”

    He died in **1916**, not at the outbreak of war. The sentence encourages an immediate-event reading and reduces his death to a single confidently stated cause. A brief temporal bridge would keep the narrative clear while retaining the importance of his frustrated hopes of returning. [Encyclopedia Virginia’s biography](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/benga-ota-ca-1883-1916/).

16. **The central eugenics mechanism is left unexplained for the intended reader.**  
    > “In 1907 the American state of Indiana passed a law permitting the forced sterilisation of criminals and the ‘feeble-minded’.”

    “Sterilisation” needs a short explanation that it means making someone unable to have children. Otherwise a reader unfamiliar with the term can miss the connection between selective breeding and state coercion. Likewise, identify “feeble-minded” as the authorities’ broad historical classification, not an accepted medical description. This falls under *style.md*’s requirement to explain historical terms when introduced.

17. **The anonymous journalist becomes a major historical actor without enough orientation.**  
    > “The following year he published a pamphlet proposing that they found a state of their own, and in 1897 he called a congress to begin the work.”

    Readers are never given Herzl’s name, the term “Zionism,” or the proposed homeland in Palestine. Here the “few names” rule has been pushed past its purpose: this person’s identity and the movement he helped organise are needed to understand why the episode matters. One short identifying sentence would prevent the paragraph becoming an unexplained detour.

18. **A signpost replaces a substantive transition.**  
    > “That belief had a history, and it was not a long one.”

    This announces the historical explanation about to follow rather than supplying it. It is a mild but clear break of the “No signpost sentences” rule, and repeats the opening’s claim about recency. The next paragraph can begin the history directly.
