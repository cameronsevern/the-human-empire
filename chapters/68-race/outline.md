# Chapter 68 — Where did racism come from?

**Line under the title:** Europe and the Americas, from 1449 to 1906.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

Suspicion of outsiders is as old as humanity. The belief that mankind is divided by nature into a few "races", each with fixed abilities and a fixed rank, is about three or four hundred years old. It grew up in the Atlantic world alongside slavery, because people who enslaved Africans by the million, while proclaiming that all men were equal, needed a reason why some men did not count. Colonial laws invented "white" and "black" as legal categories. The naturalists of the 1700s sorted humanity as they sorted plants, and the scientists of the 1800s measured skulls, misread Darwin and proposed to breed better humans. Empires took up the idea as a duty to rule. It was false, as some said at the time and as genetics has since shown: the differences between human groups are shallow and recent.

## The story

1. **Opening: the monkey house.** Answer first. New York, September 1906: a man from the Congo is put on show at the zoo.
2. **Barbarians.** Before race: how the ancient and medieval worlds divided people, by language, religion and law, and whom they enslaved.
3. **Clean blood.** Spain, 1449: a prejudice that baptism cannot wash out. A debate in 1550 over whether the people of the Americas are fully human.
4. **The word "white".** Virginia in the 1600s: servants and slaves who drank and ran away together, a rebellion, and the laws that separated them.
5. **Five varieties.** The age of equality needs an exception. The naturalists classify mankind. Where "Caucasian" comes from.
6. **A thousand skulls.** The 1800s make it a science: measuring heads, separate creations, and a French count's theory of a master race.
7. **The fittest.** What Darwin said and what was done with it. "Survival of the fittest". The plan to breed better people, and the laws that followed.
8. **The burden.** Empire as a duty. People exhibited in zoos and fairs. Hatred of Jews recast as a matter of race, and one journalist's conclusion.
9. **Ending: the colour line.** The people who argued back, and what the reading of human DNA showed.
