## Factual flags

1. **Major — the new ancient-world generalisation contradicts evidence and the chapter itself.**

   > “In each case the insult was aimed at how the stranger spoke and lived, and not at what he had been born.”  
   > “A family that changed them stopped being barbarian.”

   Greek accounts did not invariably treat barbarian status as something removable by adopting customs. Aristotle explicitly connected barbarians with natural servitude. The chapter itself later acknowledges that philosophers thought some peoples were born to be slaves.

   **Correct fact:** Cultural assimilation could overcome some ancient distinctions, but ancient thought also included supposedly natural differences between peoples. This flags a newly introduced categorical claim, not the earlier wording already corrected. Source: [Aristotle, *Politics*, Book I](https://web.mit.edu/classics/Aristotle/politics.1.one.html).

2. **Major — converts continued to be attacked on religious grounds.**

   > “These families could no longer be attacked for their religion, since they shared it with their neighbours. They could be attacked only for their ancestors.”

   The very Toledo decree being discussed accused converts of religious unfaithfulness and continued Jewish observance. Conversion did not stop persecutors from alleging heresy or insincere Christianity.

   **Correct fact:** Ancestry became an additional basis for exclusion, intertwined with accusations about religious belief and practice; it did not replace religious accusations because those had become impossible. Source: [the 1449 *Sentencia-Estatuto*, translated extract hosted by CUNY](https://jjcweb.jjay.cuny.edu/history/worldspring09/document2.html).

3. **Minor — physical measurement did not begin with the nineteenth-century successors.**

   > “The naturalists of the previous century had described the varieties of mankind. Their successors set out to measure them.”

   As a contrast between centuries, this implies that eighteenth-century classification preceded measurement. Petrus Camper was already developing numerical facial-angle comparisons in the 1760s and lecturing on them in 1770.

   **Correct fact:** Nineteenth-century investigators expanded and systematised measurements already being made in the eighteenth century. Source: [Leiden University Libraries’ account of Camper’s manuscripts and publications](https://www.leidenspecialcollectionsblog.nl/articles/petrus-campers-facial-angle-theory-and-the-missing-monkey).

4. **Minor — the baptism transition contradicts the earlier Spanish example.**

   > “A hatred that baptism had once been able to end now had no exit.”

   The preceding sentence carefully says “at least in theory”; this addition turns that into a claim about hatred actually ending. Earlier, the chapter has already shown baptised families being persecuted centuries before 1879.

   **Correct fact:** Baptism offered religious incorporation in principle but did not reliably end hostility; ancestry-based exclusion long predated nineteenth-century racial antisemitism. The chapter’s Spanish episode and [the Toledo decree itself](https://jjcweb.jjay.cuny.edu/history/worldspring09/document2.html) establish the contradiction.

5. **Minor — Palestine was not a single Ottoman province.** *Unchanged by this revision; not flagged in the earlier check.*

   > “The movement was called Zionism, after a hill in Jerusalem, and the land it had in view was Palestine, then a province of the Ottoman empire.”

   “A province” gives an incorrect administrative description.

   **Correct fact:** Palestine was a region under Ottoman rule, divided between administrative units, including the separately administered Jerusalem district and districts belonging to Beirut province. Source: [*The Handbook of Palestine*, administrative divisions](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Handbook_of_Palestine/Part_5).

## Clarity flags

1. **New signposts announce the explanation instead of providing it.**

   > “The most famous case is the plainest.”  
   > “Help for that suspicion came from an unexpected direction.”  
   > “The reasoning ran as follows.”  
   > “What made the scene so alarming was where it took place.”

   These conflict with the explicit prohibition on signpost sentences. The concrete sentences immediately following them already do the work.

2. **A previously identified signpost has been reintroduced.**

   > “A belief like theirs has a history, and it is a short one.”

   This sentence is newly added in the diff. It restores substantially the same signpost identified in the earlier check and repeats the opening’s claim about recency.

3. **Some additions repeat rather than explain.**

   > “The privileges were small.”

   The preceding sentence already calls them “small privileges.”

   > “Even so, he was a man of his time.”

   The following sentence specifies exactly which assumptions Darwin shared; this intervening cliché adds little.

   > “A farmer breeds from his best animals and not from his worst.”

   This sits between the cattle-breeding analogy and its direct human application, explaining the same comparison a third time.

4. **An unclear backward reference blurs two different kinds of change.**

   > “The same was true of the harshest division of all.”

   “The same” follows cultural assimilation and an emperor’s African origin, then introduces slavery. It is unclear whether it means slavery was unrelated to ancestry, that enslaved people could change status, or that their children could enter civic life.

5. **The date range does not describe the chapter’s actual scope.**

   > “Europe and the Americas, from 1449 to 1906”

   The narrative includes the 1924 immigration law, the 1927 judgment, and later DNA research. The subtitle presents 1906 as an endpoint that the chapter repeatedly exceeds.

6. **The ending’s chronology can momentarily confuse.**

   > “Its effects, however, were as real as those of any other institution, and they did not end when the science behind them collapsed. In 1903 a Black American scholar named W. E. B. Du Bois…”

   Coming immediately after modern DNA evidence, the move to 1903 can make Du Bois’s prediction sound subsequent to that scientific collapse. The historical jump needs clearer orientation.
