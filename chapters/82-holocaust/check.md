I found the following issues, ranked by seriousness. I found no clear contradiction with chapters 68, 79, or 81, and no hedging stacks, explicit chapter self-reference, or cross-references. No files were edited.

1. **Misleading account of how the Holocaust developed — high confidence.**  
   > “The Jews of Europe were first registered, then robbed, then shut into ghettos, then shot beside pits, and finally carried by train to camps that had been built to kill them.”

   This makes different, overlapping routes to murder sound like successive stages experienced by everyone. Western Europe had no ghettos; many Soviet Jews were shot near their homes; shootings continued after killing centres opened. The opening needs to distinguish escalating persecution from a universal itinerary. [USHMM, Ghettos](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/ghettos); [USHMM, Final Solution](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/final-solution-overview?parent=en%2F35294).

2. **Hungary’s earlier persecution is erased — high confidence.**  
   > “Hungary, an ally, had so far protected its Jews.”

   Hungary had resisted German demands for wholesale deportation, but its government had also enacted antisemitic laws, imposed lethal forced labour, and participated in deportations and massacres. Between 44,000 and 63,000 Jews died through Hungarian actions before the German occupation. “Protected” without that distinction seriously misrepresents the preceding years. [USHMM, The Holocaust in Hungary](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-holocaust-in-hungary?parent=en%2F11710).

3. **An inferred cause is presented as an established decision — medium-high confidence.**  
   > “The regime learned that Germans would protest at the murder of their own relatives on German soil, and it concluded that its next and larger killing should be done far away, in the east.”

   The transfer of personnel and methods from the disability murders to the killing centres is established. This specific explanation for locating the Holocaust in the east is not. Mass shootings there had already begun before the August 1941 protests and suspension of centralised T4 killings. Geography, conquest, the location of Jewish populations, and developing extermination policy cannot be reduced to this asserted lesson. Keep the documented transfer; either substantiate or remove the claimed conclusion. [USHMM, Euthanasia Program](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/euthanasia-program?series=18); [USHMM, Final Solution](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/final-solution-in-depth).

4. **The killing centres did not all close — high confidence.**  
   > “It continued more quietly, by starvation and injection, but the centres were closed.”

   The centralised programme was formally halted, but some centres continued gassing concentration-camp prisoners. Bernburg, Sonnenstein and Hartheim were used for these murders after August 1941. The sentence incorrectly makes both closure and the abandonment of gas comprehensive. [Bernburg Memorial, Operation 14f13](https://www.gedenkstaette-bernburg.de/site/euthanasie/14f13.html).

5. **The decision-making chronology is more disputed than this allows — high confidence about the uncertainty.**  
   > “Some time in the autumn of 1941 the decision was taken at the top to kill every Jew within reach, and to find a better method.”

   Historians disagree about the timing and sequence of decisions extending murder to all European Jews. The development of killing methods also cannot safely be bundled into a single decision. This is a substantive uncertainty, for which `style.md` expressly permits a short explanation; it is not a reason to weaken otherwise well-supported statements. [Yad Vashem, decision-making overview](https://wwv.yadvashem.org/odot_pdf/Microsoft%20Word%20-%203222.pdf); [USHMM, Final Solution overview](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/final-solution-overview?parent=en%2F35294).

6. **The police battalion’s Nazi membership is understated — high confidence.**  
   > “Few were members of the Nazi party.”

   Browning found that approximately 25% of the sampled rank-and-file policemen were party members—a disproportionately high share, not merely a few. Most were not members, which preserves the chapter’s point without distorting the evidence. [Christopher Browning, *Ordinary Men*](https://www.academia.edu/36302824/BROWNING_Ordinary_Men_Reserve_Police_Battalion_101_and_the_Final_Solution_in_Poland_1992_).

7. **Anne Frank’s betrayal is unproved — high confidence.**  
   > “Someone informed.”

   Betrayal remains a hypothesis, not an established fact. The Anne Frank House says the circumstances leading to discovery remain uncertain. The safe fact is that the hiding place was raided and its occupants arrested on 4 August 1944. [Anne Frank House, Arrest and betrayal](https://research.annefrank.org/en/onderwerpen/2abdc9fe-b0e6-493d-a6cd-a8fd85baabd7/).

8. **The resistance explanation contains two unsustainable absolutes — high confidence.**  
   > “No one could at first believe the truth, since nothing like it had been done before.”

   Disbelief was widespread, but responses differed. By the turn of 1942, Abba Kovner was explicitly warning that Hitler intended to destroy European Jewry. Nor was the deliberate destruction of a people without precedent: the Armenian genocide was a major influence on Lemkin’s thinking. Describe the difficulty of recognising this particular extermination programme without claiming universal disbelief or the absence of comparable crimes. This also breaks the rule against exception-breaking absolutes. [USHMM, Abba Kovner](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/abba-kovner); [USHMM, Armenian genocide](https://www.ushmm.org/information/press/press-releases/museum-statement-on-the-100th-anniversary-of-the-armenian-genocide).

9. **The badge chronology runs backwards — high confidence.**  
   > “The first measures were the ones already tried in Germany.”  
   > “Jews were made to wear a badge with a six-pointed star.”

   Read together, these say that compulsory badges had already been imposed in Germany before their introduction in occupied Poland. They were introduced in Poland in 1939; the nationwide requirement in the German Reich followed in September 1941. Confiscation fits the introductory sentence; the badge does not. [USHMM, Jewish Badge](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/jewish-badge-during-the-nazi-era).

10. **Treblinka opened in summer — high confidence.**  
    > “In the spring of 1942 three more camps were opened in remote corners of eastern Poland, each beside a railway line.”

    Bełżec began killing in March, Sobibór in May, and Treblinka in July 1942. “Spring and summer” would give the correct sequence. [USHMM, Operation Reinhard](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/operation-reinhard-einsatz-reinhard).

11. **The Wannsee institutions are misidentified — high confidence.**  
    > “Some came from the security police and the party, and the others from the ministries of justice, the interior, foreign affairs and the economy, and from the governments of occupied Poland and Russia.”

    Erich Neumann represented the Four-Year Plan office, not the economics ministry. The Ministry for the Occupied Eastern Territories represented German administration of occupied Soviet territory, not a government of occupied “Russia.” The distinction matters after chapter 81’s account of the Soviet Union and Ukraine. [Wannsee Protocol, participant list](https://wwv.yadvashem.org/odot_pdf/microsoft%20word%20-%204017.pdf).

12. **The Polish death penalty is made automatic and uniform — high confidence.**  
    > “In Poland, where nine Jews in ten were murdered, the Germans ruled directly and punished any help to a Jew with the death of the helper’s whole family.”

    Helping Jews could bring execution, and Germans did murder entire rescuing families. But the rules and their enforcement varied by place and time; not every act of assistance resulted in the execution of a whole family. State the threat and documented collective reprisals without turning them into an invariant outcome. This is also an exception-breaking absolute under `style.md`. [POLIN Museum, The Death Penalty for Helping Jews](https://sprawiedliwi.org.pl/en/o-sprawiedliwych/kim-sa-sprawiedliwi/kara-smierci-za-udzielanie-pomocy-zydom).

13. **Two victim categories need precision — high confidence.**  
    > “They starved three million Soviet prisoners of war, and killed nearly two million Polish civilians.”

    More than three million Soviet POWs died in German custody, principally through starvation and exposure, but also disease, shootings and other killing methods. The Polish figure refers to **non-Jewish** Polish civilians; without that qualification, readers cannot tell how it relates to the roughly three million murdered Polish Jews discussed elsewhere. [USHMM, Treatment of Soviet POWs](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-treatment-of-soviet-pows-starvation-disease-and-shootings-june-1941january-1942); [National WWII Museum, victim categories](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/genocide-roma).

14. **The Tuesday timetable is overgeneralised — high confidence.**  
    > “From the transit camp a train left every Tuesday morning for the east.”

    The weekly rhythm developed in early 1943, rather than applying throughout deportations beginning in July 1942. Transports also departed on other days; Anne Frank’s Auschwitz transport left on Sunday, 3 September 1944. “Usually on Tuesdays” with an appropriate starting point would preserve the image. [Veterans Affairs Canada, Kamp Westerbork](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/remembrance/memorials/overseas/kamp-westerbork).

15. **Anne Frank’s death is placed too close to liberation — medium-high confidence.**  
    > “Anne Frank was put on the last train to leave that transit camp for Auschwitz, and died of typhus in another camp a few weeks before it was freed.”

    The last-Auschwitz-train detail is correct. The Anne Frank House now dates her death to February 1945; Bergen-Belsen was liberated on 15 April, roughly seven to ten weeks later. “A few weeks” perpetuates the older, later dating. Naming Bergen-Belsen would also help the reader follow the change of camp. [Anne Frank House, findings on her fate](https://www.annefrank.org/en/about-us/news-and-press/news/2024/8/1/arrest-of-anne-frank-80-years-ago/).

16. **The genocide paragraph temporarily reverses its own chronology — high confidence.**  
    > “There was no word for what had been done.”

    Coming immediately after liberation in January 1945, this implies the word did not yet exist. The next sentence correctly places Lemkin’s publication in 1944. Introduce the earlier date before making the claim about the missing term. More precisely, what was missing was this particular term and legal formulation, not language for describing mass murder. [USHMM, Raphael Lemkin](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/coining-a-word-and-championing-a-cause-the-story-of-raphael-lemkin).

17. **An essential institution is never explained.**  
    > “When a train arrived, an SS doctor stood on the platform and pointed to the left or the right.”

    This is the first appearance of “SS” in chapter 82, and the three earlier chapters supplied do not explain it. A general reader needs a brief identification of the Nazi organisation that controlled the camps and much of the machinery of persecution. This directly breaches the rule to explain historical terms on first appearance.

18. **Avoiding names makes the camp sequence difficult to follow.**  
    > “From one of them, where over four hundred thousand died, two survivors are known to have given evidence after the war.”  
    > “At another camp they killed eleven of the SS with axes and three hundred ran for the forest.”

    These refer to Bełżec and Sobibór respectively, but readers have no way to identify them. The earlier three-camp group names only Treblinka. A brief introduction naming all three would let the later passages refer back clearly; `style.md` permits names needed to follow the story.

19. **Explicit signposting replaces the opening of the story.**  
    > “To see how many hands the work passed through, it helps to follow one deportation from the beginning.”  
    > “The Netherlands will serve.”

    These announce the narrative’s method instead of beginning the Dutch example. They break the prohibition on signpost sentences. They also promise one identifiable deportation, whereas the passage supplies a composite account of the Dutch system. Starting with the registration order would make the scope clear.
