# Chapter 82 — How did the Holocaust happen?

**Line under the title:** Europe, from 1939 to 1945.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,600 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years. Plain and restrained throughout; the facts carry it.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

It happened in steps, and each step made the next one possible: Jews were first marked, then robbed, then shut into ghettos, then shot beside pits, and finally carried by train to camps built for killing. No step was the work of a few fanatics alone. It took civil servants to register people, bankers to take their savings, railwaymen to schedule the trains, policemen to do the shooting, and officials in every occupied country to hand people over. Where those people refused, as in Denmark, most Jews lived. Where they obeyed, as in the Netherlands, most died. Six million were murdered, two of every three Jews in Europe.

## The story

1. **Opening: a villa by a lake.** Answer first. Ninety minutes on 20 January 1942: fifteen officials, a table of figures, and lunch.
2. **Behind the wall.** Poland, 1939. The ghettos. Hunger in Warsaw. A historian begins to collect paper.
3. **The shower room.** The killing of the disabled in Germany: the method, the staff, and a bishop's protest.
4. **The pits.** June 1941: the shooting squads in the Soviet Union. A ravine at Kyiv. A battalion of middle-aged policemen and an offer from their major.
5. **A painted clock.** The decision of late 1941. The camps built only for killing. The larger camp that also worked people to death, and the firms that supplied it.
6. **Every Tuesday.** One deportation followed from beginning to end, in the Netherlands: the registration form, the bank, the tram, the train and its fare. A family in an attic.
7. **Fishing boats.** Where it was refused: Denmark, Bulgaria. The price of helping in Poland.
8. **Pistols.** Resistance: Warsaw in April 1943, the revolts in the camps.
9. **"I am unable to believe him."** What the outside world was told.
10. **Eight weeks.** Hungary, 1944. The other victims.
11. **Warehouses.** Liberation. A new word. How few were ever tried.
12. **Ending: a milk can.**
