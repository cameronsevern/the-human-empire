# Chapter 28 — How did Islam begin?

**Line under the title:** Western Arabia, from about 570 to 632.
**Length:** 4,600 to 5,200 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.
**Care:** Report what Muslims believe as their belief and what historians can establish as that. No picture of Muhammad is used.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

In Arabia a person's only protection was the clan, and a person without one was prey. A merchant of Mecca began to recite a message in which each soul answers to one God alone, with no clan beside it, and in which the orphan and the poor have a claim on the rich. Driven out of his own town, he built in another a community held together by belief instead of blood: a tribe that anyone could join. Because its members could not raid one another, each clan that joined made the peace larger, and within ten years it covered Arabia. Most of the story comes from accounts written down four or five generations later, but pages of the Qur'an, a receipt on papyrus and the notes of startled outsiders confirm its outline from within a few decades.

## The story

1. **Opening: two leaves in Birmingham.** Answer first. The misfiled pages, the radiocarbon date. What the evidence is: the book, the late biographies, the outsiders, the receipt for sheep.
2. **A man without a clan.** Arabia: desert, camels, no state. The clan as police and insurance; the feud. Mecca, its shrine and its truce. Two empires at the edges, and Jews and Christians with books.
3. **Recite.** An orphan raised by an uncle; the caravans; Khadija. The cave. What the first recitations say: one God, the Day, the orphan, the buried daughter, honest scales. The religion of Abraham.
4. **The uncle's protection.** Why Mecca's leaders objected. Followers without protectors are tortured; the Prophet is safe because his clan stands by him. The year both protectors die.
5. **The oasis.** Yathrib's feud and its invitation. The emigration of 622, later year one. The agreement: one community apart from all others. Alms as a duty. Prayer, fast and the turn towards Mecca.
6. **Caravans and a trench.** The war with Mecca as each side saw it. The Jewish clans of the oasis. The truce.
7. **The key of the shrine.** Mecca surrenders; the idols go, the pilgrimage stays, the old enemies are forgiven and given commands. The delegations. A peace that grows by joining.
8. **Ending.** The death in 632 and the sentence spoken at the door.
