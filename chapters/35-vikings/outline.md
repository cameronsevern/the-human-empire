# Chapter 35 — Who were the Vikings?

**Line under the title:** Scandinavia, and from Newfoundland to Baghdad, from 793 to 1066.
**Length:** 3,800 to 4,500 words.
**Voice:** `style.md`. Answer the question early. No hedging; replace weak examples. Lead with transitions. Give the reader room. Plain numbered years.

## The idea the chapter leaves the reader with

"Viking" was a job. The word meant a raider, and the people who did the raiding were farmers and fishermen from Denmark, Norway and Sweden who went to sea in the summer to get silver. What made them possible was a ship: light, shallow and fast, able to cross an ocean and then row up a river or land on any beach. For two and a half centuries those ships carried raiders to the monasteries of Britain and France, slave traders down the rivers of Russia to Constantinople and the caliphate, and settlers to Iceland, Greenland and, for a few years, America. The raids ended when the places they robbed built defences, and when their own chiefs became Christian kings who preferred taxing people to robbing them. The Vikings who stayed abroad turned into the English, the Russians and the Normans.

## The story

1. **Opening: the island of the monks.** Answer first. Lindisfarne, June 793, and why nobody thought it could happen.
2. **A ship that could land anywhere.** Split planks, overlapping and riveted; the keel and the sail; a metre of water. Why a monastery was the perfect target.
3. **Silver.** Why they went: poor farmland, chiefs who kept followers with gifts. From summer raids to armies. Paris bought off; England paying by the ton.
4. **The river road.** The Swedes go east: furs and slaves down the rivers for Arab silver. A Baghdad envoy meets them on the Volga. A Buddha in a Swedish field. The emperor's guard and a name scratched in the great church.
5. **Staying.** The army that conquered half of England, and the words it left. The chief who was given Normandy.
6. **A country without a king.** Iceland: an empty island, an assembly, a man who recited the law.
7. **The far shore.** Greenland and walrus ivory. Newfoundland, and a tree cut down in 1021.
8. **Bluetooth.** Kings and the cross. Iceland votes. Why the raids stopped. The helmets. Ending: 1066, two invasions of England, both led by men of Viking stock.
